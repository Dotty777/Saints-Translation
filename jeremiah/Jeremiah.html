Jeremiah 1

Read this chapter on staging

The Word That Came to Jeremiah

1:1The word of God that came upon Jeremiah son of Hilkiah, one of the priests, who lived in Anathoth in the land of Benjamin,

1:2which became God’s word to him in the days of Josiah son of Amos, king of Judah, in the thirteenth year of his reign.

1:3It also came in the days of Jehoiakim son of Josiah, king of Judah, until the eleventh year of Zedekiah son of Josiah, king of Judah, until Jerusalem’s captivity in the fifth month.

1:4The Lord’s word came to me, saying,

The Call of Jeremiah

1:5Before I formed you in the belly, I know you; before you came out of the womb, I have consecrated you. I have appointed you a prophet to nations. [1]

1:6I said, O Master Lord! Behold, I do not know how to speak, because I am young.

1:7The Lord said to me, Do not say, I am young, because you will go to everyone to whom I send you, and you will speak according to everything I command you.

1:8Do not fear them, because I am with you to rescue you, says the Lord.

1:9The Lord stretched out His hand toward me and touched my mouth. The Lord said to me, Behold, I have put My words into your mouth.

1:10Behold, today I have appointed you over nations and kingdoms, to uproot, demolish, destroy, build and plant.

What Do You See, Jeremiah?

1:11The Lord’s word came to me, saying, What do you see, Jeremiah? I said, A nut-wood staff.

1:12The Lord said to me, You have seen well, because I am watchful over My words to perform them.

The Second Word: From the North

1:13The Lord’s word came to me a second time, saying, What do you see? I said, A cauldron heated from beneath, its face away from the north.

1:14The Lord said to me, From the north calamities will be kindled upon all who inhabit the land.

1:15Because behold, I am summoning all the kingdoms from the north of the land, says the Lord. They will come and each set his throne at the forecourts of Jerusalem’s gates, against all her surrounding walls and against all Judah’s cities.

1:16I will speak to them with judgment concerning all their evil, because they abandoned Me, sacrificed to foreign gods and bowed down to the works of their hands.

Gird Your Waist and Rise

1:17But you, gird your waist, rise and tell them everything I command you. Do not fear them or be terrified before them, because I am with you to rescue you, says the Lord.

1:18Behold, on this day I have made you like a fortified city and like a strong bronze wall against all Judah’s kings, its rulers and the people of the land,

1:19and they will fight you, but they will certainly not prevail against you, because I am with you to rescue you, said the Lord.

Source and wording notes

Jeremiah 1:5 · Meaning and wording · consecrate

Set apart the addressed prophet for the stated vocation. Retain the completed consecration and the following appointment to the nations. Explain sanctify here as setting apart; the verb does not alone assert every possible claim about personal sinlessness.

Historical comparison. Saints uses consecrated; KJV, Brenton, and DRC use sanctified. The Greek's I know thee is present in the preceding clause, unlike their I knew thee.

Jeremiah 2

Read this chapter on staging

I Remembered the Mercy of Your Youth

2:2And He said, Thus says the Lord: I remembered the mercy of your youth and the love of your completion, when you followed the Holy One of Israel, says the Lord.

2:3Israel is holy. To the Lord he is the first of His produce. All who devour him will offend; calamities will come upon them, declares the Lord.

What Offense Did Your Fathers Find in Me?

2:4Hear the Lord’s word, house of Jacob and every clan of the house of Israel.

2:5Thus says the Lord: What offense did your fathers find in Me, that they withdrew far from Me, followed futile things and became futile?

2:6They did not say, Where is the Lord who brought us up from Egypt’s land, who guided us in the wilderness, in a boundless and impassable land, a waterless and fruitless land, a land through which nothing passed and where no human being lived?

2:7I brought you into Carmel to eat its fruits and good things, but you entered and polluted My land and made My inheritance an abomination.

2:8The priests did not say, Where is the Lord? Those holding to the law did not know Me; the shepherds acted ungodly toward Me; the prophets prophesied by Baal and followed what is useless.

2:9Therefore I will still enter judgment with you, says the Lord, and with your children’s children I will enter judgment.

The Spring of Life Abandoned for Broken Pits

2:10Cross over to the islands of Kittim and see; send to Kedar and consider carefully. See whether such things have happened.

2:11Will nations exchange their gods, though these are not gods? But My people exchanged their glory, from which they will not benefit.

2:12Heaven was astonished at this and shuddered exceedingly, says the Lord.

2:13For My people did two evil things: they abandoned Me, a spring of water of life, and dug broken cisterns for themselves that will not be able to hold water. [1]

Is Israel a Slave?

2:14Is Israel a slave, or is he house-born? Why has he become spoil?

2:15Lions roared over him and raised their voice. They made his land a wilderness, and his cities were demolished so that none inhabited them.

2:16The sons of Memphis and Tahpanhes knew you and mocked you.

2:17Has abandoning Me not brought these things upon you? says the Lord your God.

2:18Now what do you have to do with Egypt’s road, to drink Gihon’s water? And what do you have to do with the Assyrians’ road, to drink river waters?

2:19Your apostasy will discipline you, and your evil will rebuke you. Know and see that abandoning Me is bitter for you, says the Lord your God; and I took no pleasure in you, says the Lord your God.

Long Ago You Shattered Your Yoke

2:20For long ago you shattered your yoke and tore apart your bonds, and said, I will not be enslaved, but I will go upon every high hill and beneath every shady tree; there I will abandon myself to my prostitution.

2:21But I planted you as a fruitful vine, wholly genuine. How have you changed into bitterness, you foreign vine?

2:22Even if you wash with natron and multiply cleansing herb for yourself, you remain stained in your injustices before Me, says the Lord.

2:23How will you say, I was not polluted and did not follow Baal? See your ways in the burial ground and know what you did. Late her voice wailed; her ways

2:24she broadened upon wilderness waters. In her soul’s desires she was carried by the wind; she was handed over. Who will turn her back? All who seek her will not toil; in her humiliation they will find her.

2:25Turn your foot from a rough road and your throat from thirst. But she said, I will be strong, because she had loved strangers and went after them.

The Shame of the House of Israel

2:26As a thief is ashamed when caught, so the sons of Israel will be ashamed: they, their kings, their rulers, their priests and their prophets.

2:27They said to wood, You are my father, and to stone, You gave birth to me. They turned their backs toward Me, not their faces; and in the time of their calamities they will say, Rise and save us!

2:28Where are your gods that you made for yourself? Will they rise and save you in the time of your affliction? For your gods were as numerous as your cities, Judah, and according to the number of Jerusalem’s streets they sacrificed to Baal.

2:29Why do you speak to Me? All of you acted ungodly, and all of you acted lawlessly against Me, says the Lord.

2:30In vain I struck your children; you accepted no discipline. A sword devoured your prophets like a destroying lion, and you did not fear.

2:31Hear the Lord’s word. Thus says the Lord: Have I become a wilderness to Israel, or a land left barren? Why did My people say, We will not be ruled, and we will come to Thee no more?

2:32Will a bride forget her adornment, or a virgin her breastband? Yet My people forgot Me for days beyond counting.

2:33What good practice will you still pursue in your ways to seek love? Not so; you too acted wickedly to pollute your ways.

The Blood of Innocent Souls

2:34Even on your hands was found the blood of innocent lives. I did not find them in break-ins, but at every oak.

2:35And you said, I am innocent; let His fury turn away from me. Behold, I enter judgment with you because you say, I did not sin.

2:36Why did you show such contempt by repeating your ways? You will also be put to shame by Egypt, as you were put to shame by Assyria.

2:37For from here too you will go out with your hands on your head, because the Lord rejected your hope, and you will not prosper in it.

Jeremiah 2:13 · Meaning and wording · cistern

Jeremiah 3

Read this chapter on staging

If a Man Sends His Wife Away

3:1If a man sends his wife away, and she leaves him and becomes another man’s, surely she will not return to him again? Would not that woman be utterly polluted? And you prostituted yourself with many shepherds—and were you returning to Me? says the Lord.

3:2Lift your eyes straight ahead and see: where have you not been defiled? You sat for them on the roads like a solitary crow, and polluted the land with your prostitution and your evils.

3:3You had many shepherds as a stumbling block for yourself. You had a prostitute’s appearance; you were shameless toward everyone.

3:4Did you not call Me a household, a father and the leader of your virginity?

3:5Will He remain for ever, or will it be guarded always? Behold, you spoke and did these evil things and were able.

3:6The Lord said to me in the days of King Josiah, You saw what the dwelling of Israel did to Me. They went upon every high mountain and beneath every wooded tree and prostituted themselves there.

3:7After she prostituted herself in all these ways, I said, Return to Me. But she did not return, and faithless Judah saw her faithlessness.

3:8I saw that, concerning all the things in which the dwelling of Israel was caught committing adultery, I sent her away and gave a certificate of divorce into her hands. Yet faithless Judah did not fear; she too went and prostituted herself.

3:9Her prostitution came to be regarded as nothing, and she committed adultery with wood and stone.

3:10In all these things faithless Judah did not return to Me with her whole heart, but in falsehood.

Israel Has Justified Himself

3:11The Lord said to me, Israel has justified himself in comparison with faithless Judah.

3:12Go and read these words toward the north: Return to Me, dwelling of Israel, says the Lord. I will not set My face firmly against you, because I am merciful, says the Lord, and I will not be angry with you for ever.

3:13Only recognize your injustice: you acted ungodly toward the Lord your God and spread your ways to strangers beneath every wooded tree, but you did not obey My voice, says the Lord.

3:14Return, children who have withdrawn, says the Lord, because I will rule over you. I will take you, one from a city and two from a clan, and bring you into Zion,

Shepherds According to My Heart

3:15and I will give you shepherds according to My heart, and they will shepherd you, shepherding with knowledge.

3:16When you multiply and increase upon the land in those days, says the Lord, they will no longer say, The ark of the covenant of the Holy One of Israel. It will not come to mind, be named or be attended to, and it will not be made again.

3:17In those days and at that time they will call Jerusalem the Lord’s throne, and all the nations will gather to her. They will no longer follow the thoughts of their evil heart.

3:18In those days the house of Judah will join the house of Israel, and together they will come from the northern land and from all the regions to the land I gave their fathers as an inheritance.

3:19And I said, May it be, Lord! For I will place you among children and give you a chosen land, an inheritance of God Almighty of nations. I said, You will call Me Father and will not turn away from Me.

3:20But as a woman deals faithlessly with the one living with her, so the house of Israel dealt faithlessly with Me, says the Lord.

A Voice Heard from the Lips

3:21A voice was heard from lips, the weeping and supplication of Israel’s children, because they acted unjustly in their ways and forgot their holy God.

3:22Return, returning children, and I will heal your fractures. Behold, we will be Thy servants, because Thou art the Lord our God.

3:23Truly the hills and the power of the mountains were false; but through the Lord our God is Israel’s salvation.

3:24Shame consumed our fathers’ labors from our youth: their sheep and calves, their sons and daughters.

3:25We lay down in our shame, and our dishonor covered us, because we sinned before our God, we and our fathers, from our youth until this day, and did not obey the voice of the Lord our God.

Jeremiah 4

Read this chapter on staging

If Israel Returns

4:1If Israel returns, says the Lord, he will return to Me; if he removes his abominations from his mouth and shows reverence before Me,

4:2and swears, ‘The Lord lives,’ with truth and in judgment and righteousness, then nations will bless in her, and in Him they will praise God in Jerusalem.

4:3For thus says the Lord to the men of Judah and those dwelling in Jerusalem: Break up fallow ground for yourselves, and do not sow among thorns.

4:4Be circumcised to your God, and circumcise your hardness of heart, men of Judah and those dwelling in Jerusalem, lest My fury come out like fire and burn, and there be no one to extinguish it because of the evil of your practices.

Announce in Judah, Blow the Trumpet

4:5Announce in Judah, and let it be heard in Jerusalem; say: Signal with a trumpet over the land, and cry loudly! Say: Gather, and let us enter the walled cities!

4:6Take up and flee to Zion; hurry, do not stand still, for I am bringing evils from the north and great crushing.

4:7A lion has gone up from his enclosure; destroying nations, he has set out and left his place to make the land a desolation, and cities will be demolished so that they are not inhabited.

4:8For these things, gird yourselves with sackcloth, beat yourselves in mourning and wail, because the Lord’s fury has not turned away from you.

4:9And it will be on that day, says the Lord: the king’s heart and the rulers’ heart will perish, and the priests will be beside themselves and the prophets astonished.

4:10And I said: O Master, Lord, surely Thou hast indeed deceived this people and Jerusalem, saying, ‘You will have peace,’ and behold, the sword has reached to their soul!

A Burning Wind from the Wilderness

4:11At that time they will say to this people and Jerusalem: A spirit of wandering in the wilderness; the daughter of My people’s way is not toward purity or holiness.

4:12A spirit of filling will come to Me; but now I speak judgments against them.

4:13Behold, he will come up like a cloud, and his chariots like a storm; his horses are swifter than eagles. Woe to us, because we suffer misery!

4:14Wash your heart clean of evil, Jerusalem, so that you may be saved. How long will the schemes of your troubles exist within you?

4:15For the voice of one announcing will come from Dan, and trouble will be heard from Mount Ephraim.

4:16Remind the nations: Behold, they have come! Announce in Jerusalem: Bands come from a distant land and have raised their voice against the cities of Judah.

4:17Like those guarding a field they came against her all around, because you neglected Me, says the Lord.

4:18Your ways and your practices have done these things to you. This is your evil: it is bitter; it has reached to your heart.

My Belly, My Belly, I Am in Pain

4:19My belly, my belly, I am in pain, and the senses of my heart! My soul rages, my heart is torn; I will not be silent, because my soul heard the sound of a trumpet, a cry of war.

4:20It calls out misery and crushing, because the whole land has suffered misery; suddenly the tent has suffered misery, and my tent coverings have been torn apart.

4:21How long will I see people fleeing while hearing the sound of trumpets?

4:22For the leaders of My people did not know Me; they are foolish sons and not discerning. They are wise at doing evil, but did not learn to do well.

I Looked upon the Earth, and Look, Nothing

4:23I looked upon the earth, and behold, nothing; and into heaven, and its lights were not there.

4:24I saw the mountains, and they were trembling, and all the hills were being shaken.

4:25I looked, and behold, there was no human, and all the birds of heaven were terrified.

4:26I saw, and behold, Carmel was a wilderness, and all the cities were burned with fire before the Lord, and they vanished before His furious anger.

The Whole Land Will Be a Wilderness

4:27Thus says the Lord: The whole land will be a wilderness, but I will certainly not make a complete end.

4:28For these things let the earth mourn and let heaven above grow dark, because I have spoken and will not change My mind; I have set Myself in motion and will not turn back from it.

4:29At the sound of a horseman and a drawn bow the whole countryside withdrew; they entered caves and hid in groves and climbed onto the rocks. Every city was abandoned; no human dwells in them.

4:30And you, what will you do if you put on scarlet and adorn yourself with golden ornaments, and if you paint your eyes with antimony? Your adornment is in vain; your lovers have rejected you; they seek your soul.

4:31For I heard a voice like a woman in labor, your groaning like one giving birth for the first time, the voice of the daughter of Zion. She will grow faint and let her hands fall: Woe to me, because my soul fails over those killed!

Jeremiah 5

Read this chapter on staging

Run Through Jerusalem's Streets

5:1Run through Jerusalem’s streets and see and learn, and search her squares: if you find a man, if there is one doing justice and seeking faithfulness, then I will be merciful to them, says the Lord.

5:2They say, ‘The Lord lives’; do they not therefore swear on falsehoods?

5:3Lord, Thine eyes are toward faithfulness. Thou didst scourge them, and they did not suffer; Thou broughtest them to an end, and they would not accept instruction. They hardened their faces beyond rock and would not return.

5:4And I said: Perhaps they are poor, because they had no ability, because they did not know the Lord’s way and God’s judgment.

5:5I will go to the prosperous and speak to them, for they recognized the Lord’s way and God’s judgment. And behold, with one accord they broke the yoke and tore the bonds.

5:6Therefore a lion from the woodland struck them, and a wolf destroyed them as far as the houses, and a leopard kept watch over their cities. All who leave them will be hunted, because they multiplied their impieties and grew strong in their turnings away.

Why Should I Be Merciful to You?

5:7For which of these things should I be merciful to you? Your sons abandoned Me and swore by those who are not gods. And I fed them fully, and they committed adultery and lodged in prostitutes’ houses.

5:8They became horses mad for mares; each neighed after his neighbor’s wife.

5:9Shall I not visit for these things? says the Lord. Or will not My soul take vengeance on such a nation?

5:10Go up against her battlements and tear them down, but do not make a complete end. Leave her supports, because they belong to the Lord.

5:11For the house of Israel and the house of Judah have utterly rejected Me, says the Lord.

5:12They lied to their own Lord and said: These things are not so; evils will not come upon us, and we will not see sword or famine.

5:13Our prophets became wind, and the Lord’s word was not in them. Thus it will be for them.

5:14Therefore thus says the Lord Almighty: Because you spoke this utterance, behold, I have made My words fire in your mouth, and this people wood, and it will devour them.

A Nation from Far Away

5:15Behold, I am bringing a nation upon you from far away, house of Israel, says the Lord, a nation whose language’s sound you will not understand.

5:16All are strong,

5:17and they will devour your harvest and your bread, and devour your sons and daughters, and devour your sheep and calves, and devour your vineyards and fig orchards and olive groves; and with a sword they will thresh your fortified cities, in which you have trusted.

5:18And it will be in those days, says the Lord your God: I will certainly not bring you to a complete end.

5:19And when you say, ‘For what reason has the Lord our God done all these things to us?’ you will say to them: Because you served foreign gods in your land, so you will serve foreigners in a land not yours.

5:20Announce these things to the house of Jacob, and let it be heard in Judah.

Hear, Foolish and Senseless People

5:21Hear these things now, foolish and senseless people: they have eyes and do not see; they have ears and do not hear.

5:22Will you not fear Me? says the Lord. Or will you not show reverence before Me, who appointed sand as the sea’s boundary, an eternal command it will not cross? It will be stirred and will not prevail, and its waves will roar, and it will not cross it.

5:23But this people acquired an unheeding and disobedient heart, and they turned aside and went away.

5:24And they did not say in their heart: Let us fear the Lord our God, who gives us early and late rain at the time of fulfillment of the harvest’s command, and He kept it for us.

5:25Your lawless acts turned these things aside, and your sins removed good things from you.

5:26For impious people were found among My people, and they set traps to destroy men and were catching them.

Deceit in the Houses, Falsehood Among the Prophets

5:27Like a set trap full of birds, so their houses are full of deceit; therefore they became great and rich.

5:28And they transgressed judgment; they did not judge an orphan’s case, and they were not judging a widow’s case.

5:29Shall I not visit for these things? says the Lord. Or will not My soul take vengeance on such a nation?

5:30Astonishment and horrifying things happened upon the land:

5:31The prophets prophesy unjust things, and the priests applauded with their hands, and My people loved it so. And what will you do after these things?

Flee, Sons of Benjamin

6:1Take courage, sons of Benjamin, from within Jerusalem, and signal with a trumpet in Tekoa, and raise a sign above Beth-haccherem, because evils have looked out from the north, and great crushing is taking place,

6:2and your height will be taken away, daughter of Zion.

Zedekiah's Reign and the Siege

52:1Zedekiah was twenty-one years old when he became king, and he reigned eleven years in Jerusalem. His mother’s name was Amitaal daughter of Jeremiah, from Lobena.

52:4In the ninth year of his reign, in the tenth month, on the tenth of the month, Nebuchadnezzar king of Babylon and all his force came against Jerusalem. They enclosed her with a palisade and built around her with squared stones.

52:5The city was under siege until the eleventh year of King Zedekiah.

52:6On the ninth of the month, the famine grew severe in the city, and there were no loaves for the people of the land.

The City Breached

52:7The city was breached, and all the warriors went out at night by the gate between the wall and the outer wall beside the king’s garden, while the Chaldeans surrounded the city. They went by the road to Arabah,

52:8and the Chaldean force pursued the king and overtook him beyond Jericho. All his servants scattered away from him.

52:9They captured the king and brought him to the king of Babylon at Deblatha, and he spoke to him in judgment.

52:10The king of Babylon slaughtered Zedekiah’s sons before his eyes, and he slaughtered all Judah’s rulers at Deblatha.

52:11He blinded Zedekiah’s eyes and bound him in fetters. The king of Babylon brought him to Babylon and put him in a mill house until the day he died.

52:12In the fifth month, on the tenth of the month, Nebuzaradan, the chief guard who stood before the king of Babylon, came to Jerusalem.

The Burning of the House of the Lord

52:13He burned the Lord’s house, the king’s house, and all the city’s houses; every great house he burned with fire.

52:14The Chaldean force with the chief guard pulled down the whole wall of Jerusalem all around.

52:16The chief guard left the remaining people as vinedressers and farmers.

52:17The Chaldeans shattered the bronze pillars in the Lord’s house, the bases, and the bronze Sea in the Lord’s house. They took their bronze and carried it to Babylon.

The Vessels Carried Away

52:18And the rim, the bowls, the meat forks, and all the bronze utensils with which they performed service,

52:19and the saphphoth, the masmaroth, the pouring vessels, the lampstands, the incense bowls, and the ladles—those of gold, gold, and those of silver, silver—the chief guard took.

52:20The two pillars, the one Sea, and the twelve bronze calves beneath the Sea, which King Solomon made for the Lord’s house—their bronze was beyond weighing.

52:21As for the pillars, one pillar was thirty-five cubits high, and a cord of twelve cubits encircled it. Its thickness was four fingerbreadths all around,

52:22and a bronze cornice was on them. The projection of one cornice was five cubits in length, and a net and pomegranates surrounded the cornice, all of bronze. The second pillar had the same: eight pomegranates per cubit over the twelve cubits.

52:23There were ninety-six pomegranates on one side; all the pomegranates around the net numbered one hundred.

The Officers Taken to Riblah

52:24The chief guard took the first priest, the second priest, and the three men guarding the road,

52:25and one eunuch who supervised the warriors, seven prominent men in the king’s presence who were found in the city, the scribe of the forces who kept records for the people of the land, and sixty people from the people of the land found inside the city.

52:26Nebuzaradan, the chief guard, took them and brought them to the king of Babylon at Deblatha,

52:27and the king of Babylon struck them at Deblatha in Aimath’s land.

Joakim Released in Babylon

52:31In the thirty-seventh year after Ioakim king of Judah had been deported, in the twelfth month, on the twenty-fourth of the month, Oulaimaradach king of Babylon, in the year he became king, took up the head of Ioakim king of Judah and brought him out of the house where he was guarded.

52:32He spoke kindly to him and placed his throne above the thrones of the kings with him in Babylon.

52:33He changed his prison robe, and he ate bread continually before him all the days he lived.

52:34His allowance was given to him continually by the king of Babylon, from day to day until the day he died.
