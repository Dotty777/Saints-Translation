Daniel 12

Read this chapter on staging

Michael the Great Angel

12:1“At that hour Michael will pass by, the great angel who stands over the sons of your people. That will be a day of affliction such as has not occurred since they came into being until that day. On that day all the people found inscribed in the book will be exalted.

Many of Those Sleeping Will Arise

12:2Many of those sleeping in the breadth of the earth shall arise: some unto everlasting life, others unto reproach, and others unto dispersion and everlasting shame. [1]

12:3Those who understand will appear like heaven’s lights, and those who strengthen my words like the stars of heaven for the age of the age.

Seal the Book Until the Time

12:4But you, Daniel, cover the commands and seal the book until the time of completion, until the many go mad and the earth is filled with injustice.”

12:5I, Daniel, saw two others standing, one on this side of the river and one on the other.

12:6I said to the one clothed in fine linen above, “When, then, will the wonders you have told me reach completion, and these things be cleansed?”

12:7I heard the one clothed in fine linen above the river’s water say, “Until the time of completion.” He raised his right hand and his left to heaven and swore by God who lives for ever that in a time, times, and half a time would be the completion of the “hands of release” of a holy people, and all these things would be completed.

12:8I heard, but did not understand the time from him. I said, “My lord, what is the interpretation of this word, and what do these figures concern?”

12:9He said to me, “Go away, Daniel, for the commands are covered and sealed until

12:10many are tested and made holy, and sinners sin. None of the sinners will understand, but those who understand will pay attention.

The Days Numbered

12:11From when the sacrifice is removed for all time and preparations are made to set up the abomination of desolation: one thousand two hundred ninety days.

12:12Blessed is the one who perseveres and reaches a total of one thousand three hundred thirty-five days.

12:13But you, go and rest, for there are still days and hours until completion is fulfilled. You will rest and arise unto your glory at the completion of days.” [1]

Current Bespoke editorial notes

The newer Bespoke Saints English (1).pdf governs, with accepted Genesis 1–11 as the English benchmark. It supersedes the older Founding Style Guide where their pronoun rules conflict: Thou/Thee/Thy/Thine address God directly; ordinary address uses you/your.

Modernize ordinary address and third-person verbs, while retaining the measured shall arise/unto cadence at 12:2 and opening the continued speech at 12:1. Preserve Michael passing by, exaltation of those inscribed, breadth of the earth, three outcome clauses including dispersion, strengthening my words, the many going mad, both hands raised, the difficult hands of release phrase, 1,290 and 1,335 days, and rising unto glory rather than substituting lot.

Source and wording notes

Daniel 12:2 · Meaning and wording · arise

Rise from the sleep of death in the vision. Retain arise with the three stated outcome clauses in this Old Greek text, including dispersion. The division of these clauses and the extent of many require interpretation outside the verse.

Historical comparison. Saints preserves breadth of the earth and the additional dispersion clause. KJV, Brenton, and DRC have dust and a shorter two-part outcome statement.

Providence lexical record PRV-DAN-09, 2026-09-26. Source occurrence checked in the earlier study; interpretation remains provisional. Greek chapter

Daniel 12:13 · Meaning and wording · glory

The glory associated with Daniel's rising at the completion of days. Retain glory, the promised rest, and the rising verb. This Old Greek noun is not the word lot; the comparison should make that difference visible.

Historical comparison. Saints follows Old Greek glory. KJV, Brenton, and DRC have thy lot. Brenton also retains an expanded interval before the end, but agreement there does not erase the noun difference.
