Daniel 2

Read this chapter on staging

The King's Dream and the Chaldeans

2:1In the second year of Nebuchadnezzar’s reign, the king fell into visions and dreams and was disturbed in his dream, and his sleep left him.

2:2The king ordered the incantation-singers, magi, and sorcerers of the Chaldeans brought in to report his dreams to him. They arrived and stood beside the king.

2:3The king said to them, “I have seen a dream, and my spirit was stirred; therefore I wish to understand the dream.”

2:4The Chaldeans spoke to the king in the Syrian language: “My lord king, live for ever! Report your dream to your servants, and we will explain its interpretation to you.”

2:5The king answered the Chaldeans, “Unless you truthfully report the dream to me and disclose its interpretation, you will be made a public example, and your possessions will be taken into the royal treasury.

2:6But if you make the dream clear to me and report its interpretation, you will receive gifts of every kind and be honored by me. Disclose the dream to me and interpret it.”

2:7They answered a second time, “O king, state the vision, and your servants will interpret accordingly.”

2:8The king said to them, “I know for certain that you are buying time, since you have seen that the matter has departed from me. As I have commanded, so it will be.

2:9Unless you truthfully report the dream and disclose its interpretation, you will meet death. You have agreed together to speak false words before me until the time changes. Now, if you tell me what I saw during the night, I will know that you can also disclose its interpretation.”

2:10The Chaldeans answered before the king, “No one on earth can tell the king what he has seen, as you ask. No king and no ruler asks such a thing of any wise man, magus, or Chaldean.

2:11The matter you seek, O king, is weighty and extraordinary. No one can disclose these things to the king except perhaps an angel, whose dwelling is not with any flesh. Therefore it cannot happen as you suppose.”

The Decree to Kill the Wise Men

2:12Then the king became gloomy and deeply grieved and ordered all the wise men of Babylonia brought out.

2:13It was decreed that all should be killed, and Daniel and everyone with him were sought so that they might perish together.

2:14Then Daniel shared his counsel and judgment with Arioch, the king’s chief cook, who had been ordered to bring out Babylonia’s learned men,

2:15and questioned him, saying, “On what matter is such a bitter decree being issued by the king?” Then Arioch explained the command to Daniel.

2:16Daniel quickly went in to the king and petitioned for time from the king, so that he might disclose everything before him.

Daniel Seeks Mercy from the God of Heaven

2:17Then Daniel went to his house and explained everything to his companions Hananiah, Mishael, and Azariah.

2:18He called for fasting and supplication and for help to be sought from the Lord Most High concerning this mystery, so that Daniel and those with him would not be handed over to destruction with Babylon’s learned men.

2:19Then, in a vision that very night, the king’s mystery was plainly revealed to Daniel. Daniel blessed the Lord Most High,

Blessed Be the Name of the Great Lord

2:20and cried out, “The name of the great Lord will be blessed for ever, because wisdom and majesty belong to Him.

2:21He changes seasons and times, transfers kings and appoints them, gives wisdom to the wise and understanding to those who possess knowledge,

2:22reveals deep and dark things, knows what is in darkness and what is in light, and with Him is a dwelling. [1]

2:23To Thee, Lord of my fathers, I give thanks and praise, because Thou hast given me wisdom and prudence. Now Thou hast shown me everything I requested, to disclose these things to the king.”

Daniel Brought Before the King

2:24Daniel went to Arioch, whom the king had appointed to kill all Babylonia’s learned men, and said, “Do not destroy Babylonia’s learned men. Bring me to the king, and I will disclose every detail to him.”

2:25Then Arioch urgently brought Daniel to the king and said, “I have found a wise man from the captivity of the sons of Judea who will disclose every detail to the king.”

2:26The king answered Daniel, who was called Baltasar in Chaldean, “Will you be able to disclose to me the vision I saw and its interpretation?”

2:27Daniel spoke aloud before the king, “Disclosure of the mystery the king saw does not belong to wise men, sorcerers, incantation-singers, or gazarenes,

2:28but there is a God in heaven who uncovers mysteries, who has disclosed to King Nebuchadnezzar what must happen in the last days. O king, live for ever! This is the dream and the vision of your head upon your bed:

2:29You, O king, reclining upon your bed, saw everything that must happen in the last days, and the Revealer of mysteries disclosed to you what must happen.

2:30This mystery was revealed to me because of no wisdom in me beyond all people; rather, so that it might be disclosed to the king, what you conceived in your heart was made known to me.

The Image of Gold, Silver, Bronze and Iron

2:31You, O king, saw a single image. That image was exceedingly large, its appearance was extraordinary as it stood before you, and the image’s appearance was frightening.

2:32Its head was good gold, its chest and arms silver, and its belly and thighs bronze,

2:33its legs iron, and its feet partly iron and partly earthenware.

2:34You watched until a stone was cut from a mountain without hands. It struck the image on its feet of iron and earthenware and ground them down.

2:35Then the iron, earthenware, bronze, silver, and gold were ground fine together, becoming finer than chaff on a threshing floor. The wind scattered them so that nothing remained of them. The stone that struck the image became a great mountain and struck the whole earth.

The Interpretation of the Dream

2:36This was the vision; now we will state its interpretation before the king.

2:37You, O king, are king of kings, and the Lord of heaven has given you rule, kingdom, strength, honor, and glory.

2:38Throughout the inhabited world He has handed over into your hands human beings, wild beasts, birds of heaven, and fish of the sea, to rule them all. You are the golden head.

2:39After you a kingdom lesser than you will arise, and another, a third kingdom of bronze, which will rule the whole earth.

2:40A fourth kingdom will be strong like iron that subdues everything and cuts down every tree, and the whole earth will be shaken.

2:41As you saw its feet partly potter’s earthenware and partly iron, another kingdom, divided in two, will be within it, just as you saw iron mixed with clay earthenware.

2:42The toes of the feet were partly iron and partly earthenware; part of the kingdom will be strong and part shattered.

2:43As you saw iron mixed with clay earthenware, they will be mingled in human generation, but will neither agree nor have goodwill toward one another, just as iron cannot blend with earthenware.

The Kingdom That Will Never Be Destroyed

2:44In the times of these kings the God of heaven will establish another kingdom, which will exist for ever and will not be destroyed. This kingdom will not leave another nation; it will strike and obliterate these kingdoms and will itself stand for ever,

2:45just as you saw a stone cut from a mountain without hands, pounding together the earthenware, iron, bronze, silver, and gold. The great God has shown the king what will happen in the last days. The vision is exact, and its interpretation trustworthy.”

Nebuchadnezzar Exalts Daniel

2:46Then King Nebuchadnezzar fell face down on the ground and prostrated himself before Daniel. He ordered sacrifices and libations made to him.

2:47The king spoke aloud to Daniel, “Truly your God is God of gods and Lord of kings, the One who alone brings hidden mysteries to light, because you have been able to disclose this mystery.”

2:48Then King Nebuchadnezzar exalted Daniel, gave him many great gifts, placed him over Babylonia’s affairs, and appointed him ruler and leader of all Babylonia’s learned men.

2:49Daniel petitioned the king that Sedrach, Misach, and Abdenago be appointed over Babylonia’s affairs. Daniel remained in the royal court.

Source and wording notes

Daniel 2:22 · Meaning and wording · dwelling

A lodging or resting-place expression in a difficult Old Greek clause. Retain dwelling provisionally and record the Old Greek clause as such. Its subject and force require further review; do not replace it with the familiar light dwells with Him from a different textual form.

Historical comparison. Saints follows the Old Greek. KJV has the light dwelleth with him; Brenton and DRC the light is with him. The preceding Old Greek also names what is in light as an object of knowing.
