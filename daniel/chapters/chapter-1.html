Daniel 1

Read this chapter on staging

Nebuchadnezzar Takes the Vessels and the Young Men

1:1In the third year of King Joakim of Judea, Nebuchadnezzar king of Babylon arrived at Jerusalem and besieged it.

1:2The Lord handed the city into his hands, together with Joakim king of Judea and a portion of the Lord’s sacred vessels. He carried them away to Babylon and deposited them in his idol-temple.

1:3The king told Abiesdri, his chief eunuch, to bring him some of the sons of Israel’s magnates, of the royal family and of the chosen men:

1:4young men without blemish, handsome, knowledgeable in every kind of wisdom, literate, intelligent, wise, and strong enough to be in the king’s house, and to teach them letters and the Chaldean tongue,

1:5and to give them a ration from the king’s house each day, from the royal table and the wine the king drank, and to educate them for three years and place some of them before the king.

1:6Among the lineage of Israel’s sons from Judea were Daniel, Hananiah, Mishael, and Azariah.

1:7The chief eunuch assigned them names: Daniel, Baltasar; Hananiah, Sedrach; Mishael, Misach; and Azariah, Abdenago.

Daniel Resolves Not to Be Polluted

1:8Daniel considered in his heart how he might avoid being polluted by the king’s dinner and the wine he drank, and petitioned the chief eunuch that he might not be contaminated.

1:9The Lord gave Daniel honor and favor before the chief eunuch.

1:10The chief eunuch said to Daniel, “I am anxious about my lord the king, who prescribed your food and drink, lest he see your faces drawn and weak beside the foreign youths being reared with you, and I risk my own neck.”

1:11Daniel said to Abiesdri, appointed chief eunuch over Daniel, Hananiah, Mishael, and Azariah,

1:12“Test your servants for ten days, and let us be given legumes from the earth to eat, and water to drink. [1] [2]

1:13If our appearance looks drawn beside the other young men eating the royal dinner, then deal with your servants however you wish.”

1:14He dealt with them in this manner and tested them for ten days.

1:15After ten days their appearance was good and their bodily condition better than the other young men eating the royal dinner.

1:16Abiesdri kept removing their dinner and wine and giving them legumes in exchange.

Knowledge and Understanding Given to the Four

1:17The Lord gave the young men knowledge, understanding, and practical wisdom in every literary skill; to Daniel He gave understanding in every utterance, vision, dreams, and all wisdom.

1:18After these days the king ordered them brought in, and the chief eunuch brought them before King Nebuchadnezzar.

1:19The king conversed with them, and among the wise no one was found like Daniel, Hananiah, Mishael, and Azariah. They remained beside the king.

1:20In every matter of discourse, understanding, and education about which the king questioned them, he found them ten times wiser than the learned experts and philosophers throughout his kingdom. The king honored them, appointed them rulers, and declared them wiser than all his own men in affairs throughout his land and kingdom.

1:21Daniel remained until the first year of the reign of Cyrus king of the Persians.

Source and wording notes

Daniel 1:12 · Wording

Provisional trial of the checked Old Greek request. Pulse means legumes; ten days, earth, and water remain explicit. The English syntax of giving is smoothed without changing the requested food.

Isaiah and Providence reference studies, 26 September 2026; provisional editorial decisions. Greek chapter

Daniel 1:12 · Meaning and wording · pulse

Leguminous food requested for the ten-day trial. Use pulse with legumes as the plain explanation, retaining the ten days and water. The noun is food vocabulary here and does not denote heartbeat.

Historical comparison. All three historical witnesses use pulse; Saints uses legumes. This is a useful elevated word when its food sense is immediately accessible.
