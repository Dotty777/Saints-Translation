Job 1

Read this chapter on staging

Job of the Land of Ausitis

1:1A certain man lived in the land of Ausitis whose name was Job. That man was true, blameless, righteous, and God-fearing, keeping away from every evil deed.

1:2He had seven sons and three daughters.

1:3His livestock numbered seven thousand sheep, three thousand camels, five hundred pairs of oxen, and five hundred grazing female donkeys. He had a very large body of servants and great enterprises in the land. That man was of noble standing among those from the sunrise.

1:4His sons would gather with one another and hold a drinking feast each day, taking their three sisters along to eat and drink with them.

1:5When the days of the drinking feast had ended, Job would send for them and purify them, rising in the morning and offering sacrifices for them according to their number, and one calf as a sin offering for their lives. For Job said, “Perhaps my sons have conceived evil thoughts toward God in their minds.” So Job did this continually.

The Devil Before the Lord

1:6When that day came, behold, the angels of God came to stand before the Lord, and the devil came with them.

1:7The Lord said to the devil, “Where have you come from?” The devil answered the Lord, “I have come after going around the earth and walking through the world beneath heaven.”

1:8The Lord said to him, “Have you paid attention in your mind to My servant Job? For among those on earth there is no man like him—blameless, true, God-fearing, keeping away from every evil deed.”

1:9The devil answered before the Lord, “Does Job worship God for nothing?

1:10Hast Thou not fenced in what is outside him and what is inside his house, and everything around all that belongs to him? Thou hast blessed the works of his hands and made his livestock numerous in the land.

1:11But stretch out Thy hand and touch everything he has; surely he will curse Thee to Thy face.”

1:12Then the Lord said to the devil, “Behold, everything he has I give into your hand, but do not touch him.” And the devil went out from the Lord.

The Four Messengers

1:13When that day came, Job’s sons and his daughters were drinking wine in the house of their elder brother.

1:14Behold, a messenger came to Job and said to him, “The pairs of oxen were plowing, and the female donkeys were grazing beside them.

1:15The raiders came and carried them away, and killed the servants with swords. I alone was saved and came to report to you.”

1:16While this one was still speaking, another messenger came and said to Job, “Fire fell from heaven and burned up the sheep and likewise consumed the shepherds. I alone was saved and came to report to you.”

1:17While this one was still speaking, another messenger came and said to Job, “The horsemen formed three companies against us, surrounded the camels and carried them away, and killed the servants with swords. I alone was saved and came to report to you.”

1:18While this one was still speaking, another messenger came, saying to Job, “While your sons and daughters were eating and drinking with their elder brother,

1:19a great wind suddenly came from the wilderness and struck the four corners of the house. The house fell on your children, and they died. I alone was saved and came to report to you.”

The Lord Gave, the Lord Has Taken Away

1:20Then Job rose, tore his garments, and cut off the hair of his head. He fell to the ground, bowed in worship, and said:

1:21“I myself came out naked from my mother’s womb, and naked I shall depart there: the Lord gave, and the Lord took away; as it seemed good to the Lord, so it happened: blessed be the name of the Lord.”

1:22In all these things that happened to him, Job did not sin at all before the Lord or attribute foolishness to God.

Job 2

Read this chapter on staging

The Devil Before the Lord a Second Time

2:1When that day came, the angels of God came to stand before the Lord, and the devil came among them to stand before the Lord.

2:2The Lord said to the devil, “Where are you coming from?” Then the devil said before the Lord, “I have come after traveling through the world beneath heaven and walking through all of it.”

2:3The Lord said to the devil, “Have you then paid attention to My servant Job? For among those on earth there is no man like him—innocent, true, blameless, God-fearing, keeping away from every evil. He still holds to innocence, but you said to destroy his belongings for nothing.”

2:4The devil replied to the Lord, “Skin for skin! Whatever a person has, he will pay for his life.

2:5But indeed, stretch out Thy hand and touch his bones and his flesh; surely he will curse Thee to Thy face.”

2:6The Lord said to the devil, “Behold, I hand him over to you; only preserve his life.”

Job Struck with Sores

2:7The devil went out from the Lord and struck Job with a grievous ulcer from his feet to his head.

2:8He took a potsherd to scrape off the discharge, and sat on the dung heap outside the city.

The Words of Job's Wife

2:9After much time had passed, his wife said to him, “How long will you persist, saying,

2:9a‘Behold, I will wait a little while longer, expecting the hope of my deliverance’?

2:9bFor behold, your memorial has vanished from the earth: sons and daughters, the pangs and pains of my womb, for whom I labored in vain with hardships.

2:9cYou yourself sit amid the decay of worms, spending the night in the open air,

2:9dwhile I am a wanderer and a hired servant, going from place to place and from house to house, waiting for the sun to set so that I may rest from the hardships and pains that now hold me fast.

2:9eBut say some word to the Lord and die.”

2:10He looked at her and said, “You have spoken like one of the foolish women. If we received good things from the Lord’s hand, shall we not bear the bad things?” In all these things that happened to him, Job did not sin at all with his lips before God.

The Three Friends Come

2:11His three friends heard of all the bad things that had come upon him and came to him, each from his own country: Eliphaz king of the Temanites, Bildad ruler of the Shuhites, and Zophar king of the Minaeans. They came to him together to comfort him and visit him.

2:12When they saw him from far away, they did not recognize him. Crying out loudly, they wept, each tearing his own robe and sprinkling earth upon himself.

2:13They sat beside him seven days and seven nights, and none of them spoke, for they saw that his affliction was terrible and exceedingly great.

Job Curses His Day

3:1After this Job opened his mouth and cursed his day,

3:2saying:

3:3“May the day perish on which I was born,
and the night in which they said, ‘Behold, a male child!’

3:4May that day be darkness;
may the Lord above not seek it out,
and may no brightness come upon it.

3:5May darkness and death’s shadow take possession of it;
may gloom come upon it.

3:6May that day and night be cursed;
may darkness carry it away.
May it not belong among the days of the year
or be numbered among the days of the months.

3:7But may that night be pain,
and may neither gladness nor joy come upon it.

3:8But may the one who curses that day curse it,
the one who is about to subdue the great sea creature.

3:9May the stars of that night be darkened;
may it wait and not come into light,
and may it not see the morning star rising,

3:10because it did not close the gates of my mother’s womb;
for it would have removed pain from my eyes.

3:11For why did I not die in the womb,
come out from the belly and immediately perish?

3:12Why did knees meet me?
Why did I suck breasts?

3:13Now I would have lain down and been quiet;
I would have slept and rested

3:14with kings, counselors of the earth,
who exulted in swords,

3:15or with rulers who had much gold,
who filled their houses with silver,

3:16or like a miscarriage coming out of a mother’s womb,
or like infants who never saw light.

3:17There the ungodly kindled the fury of anger;
there those worn out in body rested.

3:18Together the age-old ones
did not hear a tax collector’s voice.

3:19Small and great are there,
and the servant does not fear his master.

3:20For why has light been given to those in bitterness,
and life to souls in pain,

3:21who long for death and do not obtain it,
digging for it as for treasures,

3:22and become overjoyed if they obtain it?

3:23Death is rest for a man,
for God has shut him in.

3:24For sighing comes to me before my food,
and I weep, gripped by fear.

3:25For the fear I thought about came upon me,
and what I had feared met me.

3:26I had no peace, no quiet, no rest;
yet wrath came upon me.”

The First Speech of Eliphaz

4:1Then Eliphaz the Temanite replied:

4:2“Have you not often been spoken to amid trouble?
Who will endure the force of your words?

4:3For if you admonished many
and encouraged a weak person’s hands,

4:4and raised up the weak with words
and put courage into powerless knees,

4:5yet now pain has come upon you and touched you, and you have become agitated.

4:6Is not your fear in foolishness,
and your hope and the innocence of your way as well?

4:7Remember, then: who perished while pure,
or when were true people utterly uprooted?

4:8As I have seen those who plow improper things,
those who sow them will reap pains for themselves.

4:9By the Lord’s command they will perish;
by the breath of His anger they will disappear.

4:10The lion’s strength, the lioness’s voice,
and the boasting of dragons were extinguished.

4:11The ant-lion perished for lack of food,
and lion cubs abandoned one another.

4:12But if some true word had been among your words,
none of these evils would have met you.
Will not my ear receive extraordinary things from Him?

4:13There were terrors and a nocturnal sound,
fear falling upon people;

4:14a shudder and trembling met me
and shook my bones greatly.

4:15A spirit came upon my face,
and my hair and flesh shuddered.

4:16I stood up and did not recognize it;
I looked, and there was no form before my eyes,
but I heard a breeze and a voice:

4:17‘What then? Can a mortal be pure before the Lord,
or a man blameless on account of his works?

4:18If He places no trust in His servants
and finds something crooked in His angels,

4:19as for those who inhabit houses of clay,
from which we ourselves also are, of the same clay,
He struck them as one does a moth.

4:20From morning until evening they are no more;
because they cannot help themselves, they perished.

4:21For He blew upon them, and they withered;
they perished because they had no wisdom.’

To Whom Will You Turn?

5:1“Call, then, to see whether anyone will answer you,
or whether you will see any of the holy angels.

5:2For anger kills a fool,
and jealousy puts one who has gone astray to death.

5:3I have seen fools taking root,
but immediately their way of life was devoured.

5:4May their sons be far from deliverance;
may they be battered at the doors of weaker people, with no one to rescue them.

5:5For the righteous will eat what they gathered,
and they themselves will not be rescued from evils;
may their strength be drained away.

5:6For toil will certainly not come out of the earth,
nor will pain sprout from mountains;

5:7but a human being is born to toil,
and a vulture’s young fly to the heights.

5:8But indeed, I will entreat the Lord;
I will call upon the Lord, the Master of all,

5:9who does great and unsearchable things,
glorious and extraordinary things beyond numbering,

5:10who gives rain upon the earth
and sends water upon the world beneath heaven,

5:11who raises the lowly to a height
and rouses those who have perished,

5:12who changes the counsels of the unscrupulous,
and their hands will certainly do nothing true.

5:13He catches the wise in their understanding
and throws the counsel of the devious into confusion.

5:14By day darkness will meet them;
may they grope at noon as in the night.

5:15May they perish in war,
but may the powerless escape from a ruler’s hand.

5:16May the powerless have hope,
and may an unjust person’s mouth be stopped.

5:17Happy is the person whom the Lord reproved;
do not refuse the Almighty’s admonition.

5:18For He causes pain and restores again;
He struck, and His hands healed.

5:19Six times He will rescue you from distress;
and the seventh time no evil will touch you.

5:20In famine He will rescue you from death,
and in war He will release you from the hand of iron.

5:21He will hide you from the lash of the tongue,
and you will not fear coming evils.

5:22You will laugh at unjust and lawless people;
you will not fear wild beasts,

5:23for wild beasts will be at peace with you.

5:24Then you will know that your house will be at peace,
and the livelihood of your tent will not fail.

5:25You will know that your offspring are many,
and your children will be like all the vegetation of the field.

5:26You will come to the grave like ripe grain harvested at the proper time,
or like a heap on the threshing floor gathered in its season.

5:27Behold, we have searched these things out in this way;
these are the things we have heard.
But know for yourself whether you have done anything.”

Job’s First Answer to Eliphaz

6:1Then Job replied:

6:2“If only someone would weigh my anger
and lift my pains together in a balance!

6:3It would indeed be heavier than the sand beside the sea;
but, it seems, my words are worthless.

6:4For the Lord’s arrows are in my body;
their fury drinks up my blood.
When I begin to speak, they stab me.

6:5What then? Does a wild donkey cry out for nothing, rather than seeking food?
Would an ox raise its voice at a manger when it has food?

6:6Will bread be eaten without salt?
Is there any taste in empty words?

6:7For my soul cannot cease;
I see my food as a stench, like the smell of a lion.

6:8If only He would grant it and my request would come,
and the Lord would grant my hope!

6:9Let the Lord begin and wound me,
but let Him not destroy me completely.

6:10May my city be a grave, on whose walls I used to leap; I will certainly not hold back.
For I have not falsified the holy words of my God.

6:11For what is my strength, that I endure?
What time remains to me, that my soul holds out?

6:12Is my strength the strength of stones?
Is my flesh made of bronze?

6:13Had I not trusted in Him?
But help is far from me.

6:14Mercy has disowned me,
and the Lord’s care has overlooked me.

6:15Those nearest me paid no attention to me;
like a failing winter torrent or a wave they passed me by.

6:16Those who regarded me with awe have now fallen upon me,
like snow or ice frozen solid.

6:17Just as snow melts when heat comes and is no longer recognized as what it was,

6:18so I too was abandoned by everyone;
I perished and became homeless.

6:19Look at the roads of the Temanites,
the paths of the Sabeans, you who see clearly!

6:20Those who trust in cities and possessions
will incur shame.

6:21But you too have come upon me without mercy,
so that on seeing my wound you became afraid.

6:22What then? Did I ask you for anything,
or do I need the strength you have,

6:23to save me from enemies
or rescue me from the hand of rulers?

6:24Teach me, and I will be silent;
if I have gone astray in anything, tell me.

6:25But, it seems, a truthful person’s words are worthless,
for I am not asking you for strength.

6:26Your reproof will not stop me with words,
for I will not endure even the sound of a word from you.

6:27Except that you fall upon an orphan
and leap upon your friend.

6:28But now, looking into your faces, I will not lie.

6:29Sit down, then, and let there be no injustice;
once again associate with the righteous person.

6:30For there is no injustice on my tongue;
does not my throat ponder understanding?

Is Not a Mortal's Life a Trial?

7:1“Is not a human being’s life on earth a trial,
and his existence like that of a worker hired for the day?

7:2Or like a servant fearing his master and obtaining shade,
or like a hired worker waiting for his wages,

7:3so I too endured empty months,
and nights of pain have been given to me.

7:4If I lie down, I say, ‘When will it be day?’
And when I rise, again, ‘When will it be evening?’
I am filled with pains from evening until morning.

7:5My body is fouled with the decay of worms;
I dissolve clods of earth as I scrape off the discharge.

7:6My life is lighter than chatter
and has perished in empty hope.

7:7Remember, then, that my life is a breath,
and my eye will no longer return to see good.

7:8The eye of the one who sees me will no longer look upon me;
Thine eyes will be upon me, and I will be no more,

7:9like a cloud cleared away from heaven.
For if a person goes down to Hades, he will never come up again,

7:10nor will he ever return again to his own house,
nor will his place recognize him any longer.

7:11So I too will not restrain my mouth;
I will speak in my distress;
held fast, I will disclose the bitterness of my soul.

7:12Am I a sea or a dragon,
that Thou didst post a guard over me?

7:13I said, ‘My bed will comfort me;
on my couch I will privately bring up a matter with myself,’

7:14Thou frightenest me with dreams
and overwhelmest me with visions.

7:15Thou wilt release my soul from my breath,
and my bones from death.

7:16For I will not live for ever, so as to keep enduring;
leave me alone, for my life is empty.

7:17For what is a human being, that Thou didst magnify him,
or that Thou givest Thy mind to him,

7:18or that Thou visitest him until morning
and judgest him for rest?

7:19How long wilt Thou not leave me alone,
nor let me go until I swallow my saliva in pain?

7:20If I sinned, what can I do to Thee,
Thou who understandest the human mind?
Why hast Thou made me Thine accuser,
and why am I a burden to Thee?

7:21Why hast Thou not brought about forgetfulness of my lawlessness
and cleansing of my sin?
But now I will depart into the earth;
at daybreak I am no more.”

The First Speech of Bildad

8:1Then Bildad the Shuhite replied:

8:2“How long will you speak these things?

8:9For we are of yesterday and do not know;

8:10Will these not teach you and tell you,

8:15If he props up his house, it will certainly not stand;

8:16For he is moist beneath the sun,

8:17He lies upon a heap of stones
and will live among pebbles.

8:18If it swallows him up, the place will disown him.

8:19For such is the overthrow of an ungodly person,

8:20For the Lord will certainly not reject the innocent,
but He will accept no gift of an ungodly person.

8:21He will fill the mouth of true people with laughter,
and their lips with thanksgiving.

8:22Their enemies will be clothed with shame,
and an ungodly person’s dwelling will be no more.”

Job’s First Answer to Bildad

9:1Then Job replied:

9:2“Truly I know that it is so;
for how can a mortal be righteous before the Lord?

9:3For if he wishes to enter judgment with Him, He certainly will not listen to him, lest he answer back to one of His words out of a thousand.

42:8Now take seven calves and seven rams and go to My servant Job, and he will make an offering on your behalf. My servant Job will pray for you, for I will surely accept his person. Were it not for him, I would have destroyed you; for you did not speak truth against My servant Job.”

42:9Eliphaz the Temanite, Bildad the Shuhite and Zophar the Minaean went and did as the Lord commanded them, and He released their sin through Job.

The Lord Gives Job Double

42:10The Lord increased Job. When he also prayed for his friends, He forgave their sin. The Lord gave double what Job had formerly, for a doubling.

42:11All his brothers and sisters heard everything that had happened to him and came to him, along with all who had known him from the beginning. They ate and drank with him and consoled him, and marveled at all the Lord had brought upon him. Each gave him one ewe lamb and an unstamped gold piece of four drachmas.

42:12The Lord blessed Job’s latter things more than the former. His livestock were fourteen thousand sheep, six thousand camels, one thousand yoke of oxen and one thousand grazing female donkeys.

42:13Seven sons and three daughters were born to him.

42:14He called the first Hemera, the second Casia and the third Amalthea’s Horn.

42:15None were found beneath heaven better than Job’s daughters, and their father gave them an inheritance among their brothers.

The Death of Job

42:16After the affliction, Job lived one hundred seventy years; in all, he lived two hundred forty-eight years. Job saw his sons and his sons’ sons, a fourth generation.

42:17Job died elderly and full of days.

He Will Rise Again with Those Whom the Lord Raises

42:17aIt is written that he will rise again with those whom the Lord raises.

From the Syriac Book: The Lineage of Job

42:17bThis is translated from the Syriac book: he lived in the land of Ausitis, on the borders of Idumea and Arabia, and his former name was Jobab.

42:17cHe took an Arabian wife and fathered a son named Ennon. His father was Zare, a son of the sons of Esau, and his mother was Bosorra, so that he was fifth from Abraham.

42:17dThese are the kings who reigned in Edom, the land he himself also ruled: first Balak son of Beor, whose city was called Dennaba; after Balak, Jobab, called Job; after him, Asom, a ruler from the land of Teman; after him, Adad son of Barad, who cut down Midian in the plain of Moab, whose city was called Geththaim.

42:17eThe friends who came to him were Eliphaz, from the sons of Esau, king of the Temanites; Bildad, ruler of the Shuhites; and Zophar, king of the Minaeans.
