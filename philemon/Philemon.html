Philemon 1

Philemon 1

Read this chapter on staging

Greeting

1:1Paul, a prisoner of Christ Jesus, and Timothy the brother, to Philemon, our beloved fellow worker,

1:2and to Apphia the sister, and Archippus our fellow soldier, and the Church in your house:

1:3Grace to you all and peace from God our Father and the Lord Jesus Christ.

Thanksgiving for Philemon’s Love

1:4I thank my God always, remembering you in my prayers,

1:5hearing of your love and the faith that you have toward the Lord Jesus and toward all the saints,

1:6that the fellowship of your faith may become effective through recognition of every good thing among us for Christ.

1:7For I had great joy and encouragement because of your love, because the hearts of the saints have been refreshed through you, brother.

Paul’s Appeal for Onesimus

1:8Therefore, although I have great boldness in Christ to command you to do what is fitting,

1:9I appeal instead because of love, being as I am, Paul, an old man and now also a prisoner of Christ Jesus—

1:10I appeal to you for my child Onesimus, whose father I became while in chains,

1:11who was once useless to you, but is now useful both to you and to me.

1:12I have sent him back to you—him, that is, my very heart.

1:13I wanted to keep him with me, so that in your place he might serve me in my chains for the gospel.

1:14But without your consent I did not want to do anything, so that your good deed might be voluntary, rather than as though compelled.

1:15For perhaps he was separated from you for a little while for this reason: that you might have him back forever,

1:16no longer as a slave, but more than a slave, a beloved brother, especially to me, but how much more to you, both in the flesh and in the Lord.

Receive Him as You Would Me

1:17So if you regard me as a partner, welcome him as you would me.

1:18And if he has wronged you in anything, or owes you anything, charge that to my account.

1:19I, Paul, write this with my own hand: I will repay it—not to mention that you owe me even your own self.

1:20Yes, brother, let me have this benefit from you in the Lord; refresh my heart in Christ.

1:21Confident of your obedience, I write to you, knowing that you will do even more than I say.

1:22At the same time, prepare lodging for me also, for I hope that through your prayers I will be graciously given to you.

Epaphras, My Fellow Prisoner, Greets You

1:23Epaphras, my fellow prisoner in Christ Jesus, greets you,

1:24as do Mark, Aristarchus, Demas, and Luke, my fellow workers.

1:25The grace of the Lord Jesus Christ be with your spirit.

Philemon 1:6 · participation

Meaning. Sharing or participation associated with Philemon's faith.

Decision. Retain the ambiguity of the sharing relation and explain the recognition clause. NA28 among us differs from the historical in you; preserve the pronoun actually consulted.

Comparison. Saints fellowship and among us; KJV and DRC communication and in you. Communication is broader than speaking here.

Excluded. modern publicity made the sole meaning

Philemon 1:11 · useful

Meaning. Usefulness contrasted with former uselessness.

Decision. Keep the once and now contrast and both recipients. A possible play on Onesimus's name belongs in a note, without changing the personal name or inventing his history.

Comparison. Saints useful and useless; KJV and DRC profitable and unprofitable.

Excluded. a specific prior offence inferred from the adjective

Philemon 1:12 · heart

Meaning. Deep personal affection expressed with a bodily term.

Decision. Use heart with bowels as the historical bodily connection. Retain I have sent him back; an epistolary present I am sending is an explained English option.

Comparison. Saints heart; KJV and DRC bowels. Their added receive him clause is absent from this consulted verse.

Excluded. the added receive command treated as NA28 wording

Philemon 1:16 · slave

Meaning. Enslaved status named alongside a transformed fraternal relation.

Decision. Retain slave twice and preserve as, more than, and both in flesh and in the Lord. Do not soften the social status into employment or add an explicit legal emancipation statement.

Comparison. Saints slave; KJV and DRC servant. The comparison concerns a real status distinction.

Excluded. employment substituted for slavery

Philemon 1:18 · charge to account

Meaning. Taking responsibility for a possible wrong or debt.

Decision. Preserve if and the alternative wrong or owes. The condition must not become an assertion that Onesimus stole a specified sum.

Comparison. All three preserve the condition and the account image.

Excluded. an unreported theft or debt amount supplied

Philemon 1:20 · refresh

Meaning. Relief or refreshment requested in the appeal.

Decision. Keep the singular request and the repeated bodily-affection image. The final in Christ is the consulted reading and differs from in the Lord in both historical versions.

Comparison. Saints heart in Christ; KJV and DRC bowels in the Lord.

Excluded. Christ and Lord treated as the same written word

Current Bespoke editorial notes

The newer Bespoke Saints English (1).pdf governs, with accepted Genesis 1–11 as the English benchmark. It supersedes the older Founding Style Guide where their pronoun rules conflict: Thou/Thee/Thy/Thine address God directly; ordinary address uses you/your.

Preserved every name, prisoner and fellow-soldier images, thanksgiving and prayer, love/faith, fellowship, repeated hearts/refreshment, old man, fatherhood in chains, useless/useful, voluntary rather than compelled action, perhaps and temporary/forever, slave/brother, the conditional wrong/debt and account, Paul’s own hand, lodging and final grace. Address to Philemon uses you/your. You all at 1:3 makes the plural greeting explicit. Both at 1:11 reflects the bracketed first kai in the consulted Greek. Among us at 1:6 and in Christ at 1:20 follow the consulted text; no final Amen is added.

Full UBS6/ECM and apparatus comparison, independent Greek and human read-aloud reviews remain pending.

The greeting at 1:3 addresses a plural audience, now explicit as you all. Most direct instructions address Philemon in the singular; your prayers and the final you in 1:22, and your in 1:25, are plural. Spirit remains singular at 1:25. Both at 1:11 reflects the bracketed first kai in NA28.

The account instruction at 1:18 remains conditional; it does not assert an unreported theft or amount. At 1:16, as a slave, more than a slave, brother, flesh and Lord all remain. The possible name-related wordplay around usefulness and benefit belongs to editorial discussion, without changing the name Onesimus.

The retained heart wording at 1:7,12,20 represents the bodily-affection image expressed by the Greek term traditionally rendered bowels. Among us at 1:6 and the final in Christ at 1:20 differ from wording in historical comparison translations.
