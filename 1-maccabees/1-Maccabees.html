1 Maccabees 1

Read this chapter on staging

Alexander and the Division of His Kingdom

1:1After Alexander son of Philip, the Macedonian who came from the land of Kittim, had defeated Darius king of the Persians and Medes, he became king in his place, having first ruled over Greece.

1:2He waged many wars, took possession of strongholds, and slaughtered kings of the earth.

1:3He passed through to the ends of the earth and took the spoils of a multitude of nations. The earth became quiet before him; he was exalted, and his heart was lifted up.

1:4He gathered an exceedingly strong army and ruled over countries, nations, and rulers; they became tributary to him.

1:5After this he fell upon his bed and knew that he was dying.

1:6He summoned his distinguished officers, who had been brought up with him from his youth, and divided his kingdom among them while he was still alive.

1:7Alexander reigned twelve years, and he died.

1:8His officers took power, each in his own place.

1:9They all put on diadems after his death, and their sons after them for many years; they multiplied evils on the earth.

Antiochus Epiphanes and the Lawless in Israel

1:10From them came a sinful root: Antiochus Epiphanes, son of King Antiochus, who had been a hostage in Rome. He became king in the one hundred and thirty-seventh year of the kingdom of the Greeks.

1:11In those days lawless sons came out of Israel and persuaded many, saying, “Let us go and make a covenant with the nations around us, because since we separated ourselves from them, many evils have overtaken us.”

1:12The proposal was good in their eyes.

1:13Some of the people eagerly went to the king, and he gave them authority to follow the ordinances of the nations.

1:14They built a gymnasium in Jerusalem according to the customs of the nations

1:15and made foreskins for themselves. They abandoned the holy covenant, yoked themselves to the nations, and sold themselves to do evil.

The Campaign Against Egypt

1:16The kingdom was established before Antiochus, and he planned to reign over the land of Egypt so that he might rule both kingdoms.

1:17He entered Egypt with a heavy force, with chariots, elephants, cavalry, and a great fleet,

1:18and waged war against Ptolemy king of Egypt. Ptolemy was driven back before him and fled, and many fell wounded.

1:19They seized the fortified cities in the land of Egypt, and he took the spoils of the land of Egypt.

The Plundering of the Sanctuary

1:20After striking Egypt, Antiochus returned in the one hundred and forty-third year. He went up against Israel and came up to Jerusalem with a heavy force.

1:21He arrogantly entered the sanctuary and took the golden altar, the lampstand for light and all its utensils,

1:22the table of presentation, the libation vessels, the bowls, the golden censers, the curtain, the crowns, and the gold ornament on the front of the temple. He stripped everything off.

1:23He took the silver, the gold, the precious vessels, and the hidden treasures that he found.

1:24Having taken everything, he went away to his own land. He committed bloodshed and spoke with great arrogance.

The Lament over Israel

1:25Great mourning came upon Israel in every place where they lived.

1:26Rulers and elders groaned; young women and young men grew weak, and the beauty of women was changed.

1:27Every bridegroom took up a lament, and the bride sitting in her bridal chamber mourned.

1:28The earth shook over its inhabitants, and the whole house of Jacob put on shame.

The Chief Collector and the Citadel

1:29After two full years, the king sent a chief collector of tribute to the cities of Judah. He came to Jerusalem with a heavy force.

1:30He spoke peaceful words to them deceitfully, and they trusted him. Then he suddenly fell upon the city, struck it a great blow, and destroyed many people of Israel.

1:31He took the spoils of the city and burned it with fire. He demolished its houses and its surrounding walls.

1:32They took the women and children captive and took possession of the livestock.

1:33They fortified the city of David with a great, strong wall and strong towers, and it became their citadel.

1:34They stationed there a sinful nation, lawless men, and strengthened themselves in it.

1:35They stored weapons and food, gathered the spoils of Jerusalem, and deposited them there. They became a great trap.

1:36It became an ambush against the sanctuary and an evil adversary to Israel continually.

1:37They poured out innocent blood around the sanctuary and defiled the sanctuary.

1:38Because of them the inhabitants of Jerusalem fled, and she became a dwelling of foreigners. She became foreign to those born in her, and her children abandoned her.

1:39Her sanctuary was laid waste like a wilderness; her festivals were turned into mourning, her sabbaths into reproach, and her honour into contempt.

1:40In proportion to her glory, her dishonour multiplied, and her exaltation was turned into mourning.

The Decree Against the Customs

1:41The king wrote to his whole kingdom that all should become one people

1:42and that each should abandon his own customs. All the nations accepted the king’s command.

1:43Many from Israel consented to his worship, sacrificed to idols, and profaned the sabbath.

1:44The king sent letters by messengers to Jerusalem and the cities of Judah, ordering them to follow customs foreign to the land,

1:45to prevent whole burnt offerings, sacrifice, and libation in the sanctuary, to profane sabbaths and festivals,

1:46to defile the sanctuary and holy people,

1:47to build pagan altars, sacred precincts, and shrines for idols, and to sacrifice pigs and unclean livestock,

1:48to leave their sons uncircumcised, and to make themselves abominable through every uncleanness and profanation,

1:49so that they would forget the law and change all the ordinances.

1:50Whoever did not act according to the king’s command was to die.

1:51In accordance with all these commands he wrote to his whole kingdom. He appointed overseers over all the people and commanded the cities of Judah to sacrifice, city by city.

1:52Many of the people gathered to them, everyone who abandoned the law, and they did evil in the land.

1:53They drove Israel into hiding in all their places of refuge.

The Desolating Abomination

1:54On the fifteenth day of Chislev, in the one hundred and forty-fifth year, he built an abomination of desolation upon the altar. They built pagan altars in the surrounding cities of Judah.

1:55At the doors of the houses and in the squares they burned incense.

1:56They tore apart the books of the law that they found and burned them with fire.

1:57Whenever a book of the covenant was found in someone’s possession, or anyone consented to the law, the king’s decree put him to death.

1:58By their power they dealt in this way with Israelites found in the cities, month after month.

1:59On the twenty-fifth day of the month they offered sacrifice on the pagan altar that stood upon the altar.

Those Who Chose to Die

1:60According to the decree, they put to death the women who had circumcised their children,

1:61hanging the infants from their necks; they also killed their households and those who had circumcised them.

1:62Many in Israel stood firm and fortified themselves against eating unclean things.

1:63They chose to die so that they would not be defiled by foods or profane the holy covenant, and they died.

1:64Very great wrath came upon Israel.

1 Maccabees 2

Read this chapter on staging

Mattathias and His Five Sons

2:1In those days Mattathias son of John son of Simeon, a priest of the sons of Joarib, arose from Jerusalem and settled in Modein.

2:2He had five sons: John, called Gaddi;

2:3Simon, called Thassi;

2:4Judas, called Maccabeus;

2:5Eleazar, called Avaran; and Jonathan, called Apphous.

The Lament of Mattathias

2:6He saw the blasphemies being committed in Judah and Jerusalem

2:7and said, “Alas for me! Why was I born to see the ruin of my people and the ruin of the holy city, and to sit there while she was given into the hand of enemies, the sanctuary into the hand of foreigners?

2:8Her temple has become like a man without honour.

2:9The vessels of her glory were carried away captive;
her little children were slain in her squares,
her young men by the sword of an enemy.

2:10What nation has not taken possession of her royal places and seized her spoils?

2:11All her adornment was taken away;
she that was free became a slave.

2:12Behold! Our holy places, our beauty, and our glory have been laid waste, and the nations have profaned them.

2:13Why should we live any longer?”

2:14Mattathias and his sons tore their garments, put on sackcloth, and mourned greatly.

The Zeal of Mattathias at Modein

2:15The king’s officials who were compelling apostasy came to the city of Modein to make the people sacrifice.

2:16Many from Israel came to them, and Mattathias and his sons gathered.

2:17The king’s officials addressed Mattathias, saying, “You are a ruler, honoured and great in this city, supported by sons and brothers.

2:18Now be the first to come forward and carry out the king’s decree, as all the nations, the men of Judah, and those left in Jerusalem have done. You and your sons shall be among the king’s Friends, and you and your sons shall be honoured with silver, gold, and many gifts.”

2:19Mattathias answered in a loud voice, “Even if all the nations within the king’s realm obey him, each abandoning the worship of his fathers, and have chosen his commands,

2:20I and my sons and my brothers will walk in the covenant of our fathers.

2:21Far be it from us to abandon the law and the ordinances!

2:22We will not obey the king’s words by turning aside from our worship to the right or to the left.”

2:23When he finished speaking these words, a Jewish man came forward in the sight of everyone to sacrifice upon the pagan altar in Modein, according to the king’s decree.

2:24Mattathias saw and burned with zeal; his inward parts trembled. He gave vent to anger according to the judgment, ran forward, and slaughtered him upon the altar.

2:25At that time he also killed the king’s officer who was compelling sacrifice, and he pulled down the altar.

2:26He was zealous for the law, just as Phinehas had acted toward Zimri son of Salom.

Flight to the Mountains

2:27Mattathias cried out in the city with a loud voice, “Let everyone who is zealous for the law and upholds the covenant come out after me!”

2:28He and his sons fled to the mountains and abandoned everything they had in the city.

Those Who Died on the Sabbath

2:29Then many who sought righteousness and judgment went down into the wilderness to settle there,

2:30with their sons, their wives, and their livestock, because the evils had become severe against them.

2:31It was reported to the king’s men and the forces in Jerusalem, the city of David, that men who had rejected the king’s command had gone down into hiding places in the wilderness.

2:32Many pursued them, overtook them, encamped against them, and prepared for battle against them on the sabbath day.

2:33They said to them, “Enough of this! Come out and act according to the king’s command, and you shall live.”

2:34They said, “We will not come out, nor will we carry out the king’s command by profaning the sabbath day.”

2:35They hurried into battle against them.

2:36But they did not answer them, throw a stone at them, or block the hiding places,

2:37saying, “Let us all die in our integrity. Heaven and earth bear witness concerning us that you are destroying us without judgment.”

2:38They attacked them in battle on the sabbath. They died, together with their wives, their children, and their livestock—about a thousand human lives.

The Resolve to Fight on the Sabbath

2:39Mattathias and his friends learned of it and mourned for them greatly.

2:40Each said to his neighbour, “If we all do as our brothers have done and do not fight the nations for our lives and our ordinances, they will now quickly destroy us from the earth.”

2:41That day they resolved, saying, “If anyone comes against us for battle on the sabbath day, we will fight against him, and we will certainly not all die as our brothers died in the hiding places.”

The Hasideans Join Them

2:42Then a company of Hasideans gathered to them, strong warriors from Israel, everyone who willingly offered himself for the law.

2:43All who were fleeing from the evils joined them and became their support.

2:44They organized an army and struck sinners in their anger and lawless men in their wrath. The rest fled to the nations for safety.

2:45Mattathias and his friends went around and pulled down the pagan altars.

2:46They forcibly circumcised all the uncircumcised boys whom they found within the borders of Israel.

2:47They pursued the sons of arrogance, and the work prospered in their hands.

2:48They took the law out of the hand of the nations and the kings, and they did not allow the sinner to gain a horn.

The Last Words of Mattathias

2:49When the days drew near for Mattathias to die, he said to his sons, “Now arrogance and rebuke have grown strong—a time of overthrow and furious wrath.

2:50Now, children, be zealous for the law and give your lives for the covenant of our fathers.

2:51Remember the deeds of the fathers that they did in their generations, and receive great glory and an everlasting name.

2:52Was not Abraham found faithful in testing, and was it not reckoned to him as righteousness?

2:53Joseph, in the time of his distress, kept the commandment and became lord of Egypt.

2:54Phinehas our father, by burning with zeal, received a covenant of everlasting priesthood.

2:55Joshua, by fulfilling the word, became a judge in Israel.

2:56Caleb, by bearing witness in the assembly, received an inheritance of land.

2:57David, through his mercy, inherited a royal throne for ever.

2:58Elijah, by burning with zeal for the law, was taken up into heaven.

2:59Hananiah, Azariah, and Mishael trusted and were saved from the flame.

2:60Daniel, in his integrity, was rescued from the mouth of lions.

2:61So consider it from generation to generation: all who hope in Him will not fail.

2:62Do not fear the words of a sinful man, for his glory will become dung and worms.

2:63Today he shall be exalted, and tomorrow he shall not be found, for he has returned to his dust, and his thought shall perish.

2:64Children, be courageous and be strong in the law, because through it you shall be glorified.

The Charge Concerning Simon and Judas

2:65Behold, Simon your brother—I know that he is a man of counsel. Listen to him all your days; he will be your father.

2:66Judas Maccabeus has been a strong warrior from his youth. He will be your army commander and will fight the battle of peoples.

2:67Gather to yourselves all who carry out the law, and exact vengeance for your people.

2:68Repay the nations what is due to them, and attend to the law’s command.”

The Death of Mattathias

2:69Then he blessed them and was gathered to his fathers.

2:70He died in the one hundred and forty-sixth year and was buried in the tombs of his fathers in Modein. All Israel lamented him with a great lament.

1 Maccabees 3

Read this chapter on staging

Judas Maccabeus Arises

3:1His son Judas, called Maccabeus, arose in his place.

3:2All his brothers and everyone who had joined his father helped him, and they fought Israel’s war with gladness.

3:3He spread glory for his people,
put on a breastplate like a giant,
and girded himself with his weapons of war;
he waged battles, shielding the army with his sword.

3:4In his deeds he was like a lion,
like a young lion roaring over prey.

3:5He searched out and pursued the lawless, and burned those who troubled his people.

3:6The lawless shrank back from fear of him;
all workers of lawlessness were shaken,
and deliverance prospered in his hand.

3:7He embittered many kings
and gladdened Jacob by his deeds;
his memory is a blessing for ever.

3:8He passed through the cities of Judah, destroyed the ungodly out of her, and turned wrath away from Israel.

3:9His name reached to the end of the earth, and he gathered those who were perishing.

Apollonius Defeated

3:10Apollonius gathered nations and a great force from Samaria to fight against Israel.

3:11Judas learned of it, went out to meet him, struck him, and killed him. Many fell wounded, and the survivors fled.

3:12They took their spoils. Judas took Apollonius’s sword and fought with it all his days.

Seron at the Ascent of Beth-horon

3:13Seron, commander of the Syrian force, heard that Judas had gathered a body of men, an assembly of faithful men who were with him and went out to war.

3:14He said, “I will make a name for myself and be honoured in the kingdom. I will fight Judas and those with him who despise the king’s command.”

3:15He set out again, and a strong army of ungodly men went up with him to help him take vengeance on the sons of Israel.

3:16He approached the ascent of Beth-horon, and Judas went out to meet him with only a few men.

3:17When they saw the army coming to meet them, they said to Judas, “How can we, being so few, fight such a strong multitude? We are exhausted, having eaten nothing today.”

3:18Judas said, “It is easy for many to be shut into the hands of a few. Before Heaven there is no difference between saving by many or by few.

3:19For victory in battle does not lie in the size of an army; strength comes from heaven.

3:20They come against us in an abundance of arrogance and lawlessness to destroy us, our wives, and our children, and to plunder us.

3:21But we are fighting for our lives and our customs.

3:22He Himself will crush them before us. Do not be afraid of them.”

3:23When he finished speaking, he rushed upon them suddenly, and Seron and his army were crushed before him.

3:24They pursued him down the descent of Beth-horon as far as the plain. About eight hundred of their men fell, and the rest fled into the land of the Philistines.

3:25Then fear of Judas and his brothers began, and terror fell upon the nations around them.

3:26His name reached the king, and the nations recounted the battles of Judas.

The Wrath of Antiochus

3:27When King Antiochus heard these reports, he became furious. He sent and gathered all the forces of his kingdom, an exceedingly strong army.

3:28He opened his treasury, gave the forces a year’s pay, and commanded them to be ready for every need.

3:29He saw that the money in the treasuries was exhausted and the revenues of the country were small because of the dissension and disaster he had brought upon the land by removing the customs that had existed from the earliest days.

3:30He feared that he would not have enough, as on former occasions, for expenses and for the gifts that he had previously given with a lavish hand, surpassing the kings before him.

3:31He was greatly perplexed in his soul and resolved to go to Persia, collect the revenues of the countries, and gather much silver.

Lysias Left in Charge

3:32He left Lysias, an honoured man of royal lineage, in charge of the king’s affairs from the Euphrates River to the borders of Egypt

3:33and to bring up his son Antiochus until he returned.

3:34He handed over to him half the forces and the elephants, and instructed him concerning everything he wished, including the inhabitants of Judea and Jerusalem:

3:35he was to send an army against them to crush and remove Israel’s strength and the remnant of Jerusalem, and to remove their memory from the place.

3:36He was to settle foreigners throughout all their territory and allot their land to them.

3:37The king took the remaining half of the forces and set out from Antioch, his royal city, in the one hundred and forty-seventh year. He crossed the Euphrates River and travelled through the upper countries.

The Force Sent into Judea

3:38Lysias selected Ptolemy son of Dorymenes, Nicanor, and Gorgias, capable men among the king’s Friends.

3:39He sent with them forty thousand men and seven thousand cavalry to enter the land of Judah and destroy it, according to the king’s command.

3:40He set out with all their force, and they came and encamped near Emmaus in the lowland.

3:41The merchants of the country heard their reputation, took a very great quantity of silver and gold, along with shackles, and came to the camp to buy the sons of Israel as slaves. A force from Syria and the land of foreigners joined them.

The Assembly at Mizpah

3:42Judas and his brothers saw that the evils had multiplied and that the forces were encamping within their borders. They learned the king’s commands to bring destruction and an end upon the people.

3:43Each said to his neighbour, “Let us restore our people’s fallen state and fight for our people and the holy places.”

3:44The assembly gathered to prepare for war, to pray, and to ask for mercy and compassion.

3:45Jerusalem was uninhabited like a wilderness. None of those born in her went in or out; the sanctuary was being trampled, and foreigners were in the citadel, a lodging for the nations. Delight was removed from Jacob, and flute and lyre ceased.

3:46They gathered and came to Mizpah opposite Jerusalem, because formerly Mizpah had been a place of prayer for Israel.

3:47They fasted that day, put on sackcloth, placed ashes on their heads, and tore their garments.

3:48They unrolled the book of the law concerning the matters for which the nations consulted the likenesses of their idols.

3:49They brought the priestly garments, the firstfruits, and the tithes, and brought forward the Nazirites who had completed their days.

3:50They cried aloud to Heaven, saying, “What shall we do with these, and where shall we take them,

3:51when Thy holy places have been trampled and profaned, and Thy priests are in mourning and humiliation?

3:52Behold! The nations have gathered against us to destroy us. Thou knowest what they are planning against us.

3:53How can we stand before them unless Thou help us?”

3:54They sounded the trumpets and cried out with a loud voice.

Leaders Appointed and the Camp at Emmaus

3:55After this Judas appointed leaders of the people: commanders of thousands, hundreds, fifties, and tens.

3:56He told those building houses, those betrothed to wives, those planting vineyards, and the fearful to return, each to his own house, according to the law.

3:57The army set out, and they encamped south of Emmaus.

3:58Judas said, “Gird yourselves and become men of strength. Be ready in the morning to fight these nations that have gathered against us to destroy us and our holy places.

3:59It is better for us to die in battle than to look upon the evils suffered by our nation and the holy places.

3:60But whatever the will in heaven may be, that He will do.”

1 Maccabees 4

Gorgias Sets Out by Night

4:1Gorgias took five thousand men and a thousand picked cavalry, and the army set out by night

4:2to fall upon the camp of the Jews and strike them suddenly. Men from the citadel served as his guides.

4:3Judas heard of it, and he and the mighty men set out to strike the king’s force at Emmaus,

4:4while the forces were still scattered away from the camp.

4:5Gorgias came to Judas’s camp by night and found no one. He searched for them in the mountains, saying, “They are fleeing from us.”

Judas Speaks to His Men

4:6At daybreak Judas appeared on the plain with three thousand men, though they did not have the protective equipment and swords they wanted.

4:7They saw a strong army of nations, armoured, with cavalry surrounding it; these men were trained for war.

4:8Judas said to the men with him, “Do not fear their multitude or lose courage before their assault.

4:9Remember how our fathers were saved at the Red Sea when Pharaoh pursued them with an army.

4:10Now let us cry to Heaven: perhaps He will favour us, remember the covenant of the fathers, and crush this army before us today.

4:11Then all the nations will know that there is One who redeems and saves Israel.”

The Rout of the Nations at Emmaus

4:12The foreigners raised their eyes and saw them coming from the opposite side.

4:13They went out of the camp for battle, and Judas’s men sounded the trumpet.

4:14They engaged, and the nations were crushed and fled into the plain.

4:15All who were at the rear fell by the sword. They pursued them as far as Gazera and the plains of Idumea, Azotus, and Jamnia; about three thousand of their men fell.

4:16Judas and his force returned from pursuing them,

4:17and he said to the people, “Do not covet the spoils, because a battle lies before us.

4:18Gorgias and his force are in the mountain near us. Stand now against our enemies and fight them; afterward take the spoils freely.”

4:19While Judas was finishing these words, a detachment appeared, looking out from the mountain.

4:20They saw that their men had been routed and that the camp was being burned, for the visible smoke made plain what had happened.

4:21Seeing these things, they became exceedingly afraid. When they also saw Judas’s army on the plain ready for battle,

4:22they all fled into the land of foreigners.

The Plunder and the Hymn

4:23Judas returned to plunder the camp, and they took much gold and silver, blue cloth, sea-purple cloth, and great wealth.

4:24On their return they sang hymns and blessed Heaven: “For it is good, for His mercy is for ever.”

4:25A great deliverance came to Israel that day.

Lysias at Beth-zur

4:26Those foreigners who escaped came and reported to Lysias everything that had happened.

4:27When he heard it, he was confused and disheartened, because what he had wished had not happened to Israel, and what the king had commanded him had not come about.

4:28In the following year he assembled sixty thousand picked men and five thousand cavalry to make war on them.

4:29They came into Idumea and encamped at Beth-zur. Judas met them with ten thousand men.

4:30He saw the strong army and prayed, saying, “Blessed art Thou, Saviour of Israel, who didst crush the mighty man’s assault by the hand of Thy servant David, and didst deliver the foreigners’ army into the hands of Jonathan son of Saul and his armour-bearer.

4:31So shut this army into the hand of Thy people Israel, and let them be ashamed of their force and their cavalry.

4:32Give them cowardice; melt the boldness of their strength, and let them be shaken by their collapse.

4:33Bring them down by the sword of those who love Thee, and let all who know Thy name praise Thee with hymns.”

4:34They joined battle with one another. About five thousand men from Lysias’s army fell; they fell before them.

4:35Lysias saw the rout of his own formation, the courage that had arisen in Judas’s men, and how they were ready either to live or to die nobly. He withdrew to Antioch and recruited mercenaries in greater numbers to come again into Judea.

The Cleansing of the Sanctuary

4:36Judas and his brothers said, “Behold! Our enemies have been crushed. Let us go up to cleanse the holy places and dedicate them anew.”

4:37The whole army gathered, and they went up to Mount Zion.

1 Maccabees 15

The Letter of Antiochus to Simon

15:1Antiochus son of King Demetrius sent letters from the islands of the sea to Simon, priest and ethnarch of the Jews, and to the whole nation.

15:2They contained the following: “King Antiochus to Simon the great priest and ethnarch, and to the nation of the Jews, greetings.

15:3Since certain pestilent men have seized the kingdom of our fathers, I wish to reclaim the kingdom and restore it as it was before. I have recruited a multitude of forces and prepared warships.

15:4I intend to land in the country to pursue those who have ruined our country and devastated many cities in my kingdom.

15:5Now, therefore, I confirm to you all the remissions that the kings before me granted you, and all the other gifts they granted you.

15:6I permit you to strike your own coinage as money for your country.

15:7Jerusalem and the holy places shall be free. All the weapons you have prepared and the strongholds you have built and hold shall remain yours.

15:8Every royal debt and all future royal dues shall be remitted to you from now and for all time.

15:9When we gain control of our kingdom, we will honour you, your nation, and the temple with great glory, so that your glory may become evident throughout the whole earth.”

Antiochus Besieges Tryphon at Dora

15:10In the one hundred and seventy-fourth year Antiochus went out into the land of his fathers. All the forces joined him, so that only a few remained with Tryphon.

15:11Antiochus pursued him, and he fled to Dora by the sea,

15:12for he knew that evils had gathered against him and that the forces had abandoned him.

15:13Antiochus encamped against Dora, with a hundred and twenty thousand warriors and eight thousand cavalry.

15:14He surrounded the city, and the ships joined the attack from the sea. He pressed the city from land and sea and allowed no one to go out or come in.

The Letter of the Romans

15:15Numenius and his companions came from Rome carrying letters to the kings and countries, in which the following was written:

15:16“Lucius, consul of the Romans, to King Ptolemy, greetings.

15:17The envoys of the Jews, our friends and allies, came to us to renew the friendship and alliance established from the beginning, sent by Simon the high priest and the Jewish people.

15:18They brought a golden shield of a thousand minas.

15:19We therefore resolved to write to the kings and countries that they should not seek their harm or make war on them, their cities, or their country, and should not ally themselves with those making war against them.

15:20We decided to accept the shield from them.

15:21If, therefore, any pestilent men have escaped from their country to you, hand them over to Simon the high priest, so that he may punish them according to their law.”

15:22He wrote the same things to King Demetrius, Attalus, Ariarathes, and Arsaces,

15:23and to all the countries: to Sampsame, the Spartans, Delos, Myndos, Sicyon, Caria, Samos, Pamphylia, Lycia, Halicarnassus, Rhodes, Phaselis, Cos, Side, Aradus, Gortyna, Cnidus, Cyprus, and Cyrene.

15:24They wrote a copy of these things to Simon the high priest.

Antiochus Turns Against Simon

15:25On the second day King Antiochus encamped against Dora, continually bringing his forces against it, making siege engines, and blocking Tryphon from going out or coming in.

15:26Simon sent him two thousand picked men to fight alongside him, along with silver, gold, and ample equipment.

15:27He would not receive them, but annulled everything he had previously agreed with Simon and became estranged from him.

15:28He sent Athenobius, one of his Friends, to confer with him, saying, “You hold Joppa, Gazara, and the citadel in Jerusalem, cities of my kingdom.

15:29You have devastated their territories, inflicted a great blow upon the land, and gained control of many places in my kingdom.

15:30Now, therefore, hand over the cities you have seized and the tribute of the places you have taken outside the borders of Judea.

15:31Otherwise, give five hundred talents of silver in their place, and another five hundred talents for the destruction you have caused and the tribute of the cities. If not, we will come and make war on you.”

The Answer of Simon to Athenobius

15:32Athenobius, the king’s Friend, came to Jerusalem. He saw Simon’s glory, the display of gold and silver vessels, and the large retinue, and was astonished. He reported the king’s words to him.

15:33Simon answered him, “We have neither taken foreign land nor gained control of others’ property, but the inheritance of our fathers, which our enemies unjustly seized at one time.

15:34Now that we have the opportunity, we are holding fast to the inheritance of our fathers.

15:35As for Joppa and Gazara, which you demand, these caused a great blow among the people and in our country. For these we will give a hundred talents.”

15:36He did not answer him a word, but returned angrily to the king and reported these words, Simon’s glory, and everything he had seen. The king became greatly angry.

15:37Tryphon boarded a ship and fled to Orthosia.

Cendebaeus Sent Against Judea

15:38The king appointed Cendebaeus chief commander of the coast and gave him infantry and cavalry forces.

15:39He commanded him to encamp opposite Judea, rebuild Kedron, fortify its gates, and fight the people. The king himself pursued Tryphon.

15:40Cendebaeus came to Jamnia and began provoking the people, invading Judea, taking the people captive, and killing them.

15:41He rebuilt Kedron and stationed cavalry and forces there, so that they might go out and make sorties along the roads of Judea, as the king had ordered him.

Simon Sends His Sons Against Cendebaeus

16:1John came up from Gazara and reported to Simon his father what Cendebaeus had done.

16:2Simon called his two elder sons, Judas and John, and said to them, “I, my brothers, and my father’s house have fought Israel’s wars from youth until this day, and it has prospered in our hands to rescue Israel many times.

16:3Now I have grown old, but through mercy you are mature in years. Take my place and my brother’s, go out and fight for our nation, and may help from Heaven be with you.”

16:4He chose from the country twenty thousand warriors and cavalry, and they marched against Cendebaeus and lodged in Modein.

The Battle in the Plain

16:5They arose in the morning and went into the plain. A large force of infantry and cavalry came to meet them, with a stream between them.

16:6He and his people encamped opposite them. He saw that the people were afraid to cross the stream, and he crossed first. The men saw him and crossed behind him.

16:7He divided the people, placing the cavalry among the infantry, for the adversaries had very many cavalry.

16:8They sounded the trumpets, and Cendebaeus and his army were routed. Many of them fell wounded, and the survivors fled into the stronghold.

16:9Then Judas, John’s brother, was wounded. John pursued them until he came to Kedron, which Cendebaeus had built.

16:10They fled into the towers in the countryside of Azotus, and he burned it with fire. About two thousand of their men fell. He returned to Judea in peace.

The Treachery of Ptolemy Son of Abubus

16:11Ptolemy son of Abubus had been appointed commander over the plain of Jericho and possessed much silver and gold,

16:12for he was the high priest’s son-in-law.

16:13His heart was lifted up, and he wished to gain control of the country. He plotted deceitfully against Simon and his sons to remove them.

16:14Simon was visiting the cities in the country and attending to their care. He came down to Jericho with his sons Mattathias and Judas in the one hundred and seventy-seventh year, in the eleventh month, which is the month of Shebat.

16:15The son of Abubus deceitfully received them in the small stronghold called Dok, which he had built. He made a great drinking feast for them and hid men there.

16:16When Simon and his sons had become drunk, Ptolemy and his men arose, took their weapons, entered the banquet room against Simon, and killed him, his two sons, and some of his attendants.

16:17He committed a great act of treachery and repaid evil for good.

16:18Ptolemy wrote these things and sent them to the king, asking him to send forces to help him and hand over their country and cities.

John Escapes at Gazara

16:19He sent others to Gazara to remove John, and sent letters to the commanders of thousands to come to him so that he might give them silver, gold, and gifts.

16:20He sent others to seize Jerusalem and the temple mountain.

16:21Someone ran ahead and reported to John at Gazara that his father and brothers had perished, and said, “He has also sent men to kill you.”

16:22When he heard, he was greatly astonished. He seized the men who had come to destroy him and killed them, for he had learned that they were seeking to destroy him.

16:23The rest of John’s deeds, his wars, the brave acts he performed, the building of the walls he built, and his actions—

16:24behold, these are written in the book of the days of his high priesthood, from the time he became high priest after his father.
