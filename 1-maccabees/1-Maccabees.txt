1 MACCABEES 1

1:1 After Alexander son of Philip, the Macedonian who came from the land of Kittim, had defeated Darius king of the Persians and Medes, he became king in his place, having first ruled over Greece.
1:2 He waged many wars, took possession of strongholds, and slaughtered kings of the earth.
1:3 He passed through to the ends of the earth and took the spoils of a multitude of nations. The earth became quiet before him; he was exalted, and his heart was lifted up.
1:4 He gathered an exceedingly strong army and ruled over countries, nations, and rulers; they became tributary to him.
1:5 After this he fell upon his bed and knew that he was dying.
1:6 He summoned his distinguished officers, who had been brought up with him from his youth, and divided his kingdom among them while he was still alive.
1:7 Alexander reigned twelve years, and he died.
1:8 His officers took power, each in his own place.
1:9 They all put on diadems after his death, and their sons after them for many years; they multiplied evils on the earth.
1:10 From them came a sinful root: Antiochus Epiphanes, son of King Antiochus, who had been a hostage in Rome. He became king in the one hundred and thirty-seventh year of the kingdom of the Greeks.
1:11 In those days lawless sons came out of Israel and persuaded many, saying, “Let us go and make a covenant with the nations around us, because since we separated ourselves from them, many evils have overtaken us.”
1:12 The proposal was good in their eyes.
1:13 Some of the people eagerly went to the king, and he gave them authority to follow the ordinances of the nations.
1:14 They built a gymnasium in Jerusalem according to the customs of the nations
1:15 and made foreskins for themselves. They abandoned the holy covenant, yoked themselves to the nations, and sold themselves to do evil.
1:16 The kingdom was established before Antiochus, and he planned to reign over the land of Egypt so that he might rule both kingdoms.
1:17 He entered Egypt with a heavy force, with chariots, elephants, cavalry, and a great fleet,
1:18 and waged war against Ptolemy king of Egypt. Ptolemy was driven back before him and fled, and many fell wounded.
1:19 They seized the fortified cities in the land of Egypt, and he took the spoils of the land of Egypt.
1:20 After striking Egypt, Antiochus returned in the one hundred and forty-third year. He went up against Israel and came up to Jerusalem with a heavy force.
1:21 He arrogantly entered the sanctuary and took the golden altar, the lampstand for light and all its utensils,
1:22 the table of presentation, the libation vessels, the bowls, the golden censers, the curtain, the crowns, and the gold ornament on the front of the temple. He stripped everything off.
1:23 He took the silver, the gold, the precious vessels, and the hidden treasures that he found.
1:24 Having taken everything, he went away to his own land. He committed bloodshed and spoke with great arrogance.
1:25 Great mourning came upon Israel in every place where they lived.
1:26 Rulers and elders groaned; young women and young men grew weak, and the beauty of women was changed.
1:27 Every bridegroom took up a lament, and the bride sitting in her bridal chamber mourned.
1:28 The earth shook over its inhabitants, and the whole house of Jacob put on shame.
1:29 After two full years, the king sent a chief collector of tribute to the cities of Judah. He came to Jerusalem with a heavy force.
1:30 He spoke peaceful words to them deceitfully, and they trusted him. Then he suddenly fell upon the city, struck it a great blow, and destroyed many people of Israel.
1:31 He took the spoils of the city and burned it with fire. He demolished its houses and its surrounding walls.
1:32 They took the women and children captive and took possession of the livestock.
1:33 They fortified the city of David with a great, strong wall and strong towers, and it became their citadel.
1:34 They stationed there a sinful nation, lawless men, and strengthened themselves in it.
1:35 They stored weapons and food, gathered the spoils of Jerusalem, and deposited them there. They became a great trap.
1:36 It became an ambush against the sanctuary and an evil adversary to Israel continually.
1:37 They poured out innocent blood around the sanctuary and defiled the sanctuary.
1:38 Because of them the inhabitants of Jerusalem fled, and she became a dwelling of foreigners. She became foreign to those born in her, and her children abandoned her.
1:39 Her sanctuary was laid waste like a wilderness; her festivals were turned into mourning, her sabbaths into reproach, and her honour into contempt.
1:40 In proportion to her glory, her dishonour multiplied, and her exaltation was turned into mourning.
1:41 The king wrote to his whole kingdom that all should become one people
1:42 and that each should abandon his own customs. All the nations accepted the king’s command.
1:43 Many from Israel consented to his worship, sacrificed to idols, and profaned the sabbath.
1:44 The king sent letters by messengers to Jerusalem and the cities of Judah, ordering them to follow customs foreign to the land,
1:45 to prevent whole burnt offerings, sacrifice, and libation in the sanctuary, to profane sabbaths and festivals,
1:46 to defile the sanctuary and holy people,
1:47 to build pagan altars, sacred precincts, and shrines for idols, and to sacrifice pigs and unclean livestock,
1:48 to leave their sons uncircumcised, and to make themselves abominable through every uncleanness and profanation,
1:49 so that they would forget the law and change all the ordinances.
1:50 Whoever did not act according to the king’s command was to die.
1:51 In accordance with all these commands he wrote to his whole kingdom. He appointed overseers over all the people and commanded the cities of Judah to sacrifice, city by city.
1:52 Many of the people gathered to them, everyone who abandoned the law, and they did evil in the land.
1:53 They drove Israel into hiding in all their places of refuge.
1:54 On the fifteenth day of Chislev, in the one hundred and forty-fifth year, he built an abomination of desolation upon the altar. They built pagan altars in the surrounding cities of Judah.
1:55 At the doors of the houses and in the squares they burned incense.
1:56 They tore apart the books of the law that they found and burned them with fire.
1:57 Whenever a book of the covenant was found in someone’s possession, or anyone consented to the law, the king’s decree put him to death.
1:58 By their power they dealt in this way with Israelites found in the cities, month after month.
1:59 On the twenty-fifth day of the month they offered sacrifice on the pagan altar that stood upon the altar.
1:60 According to the decree, they put to death the women who had circumcised their children,
1:61 hanging the infants from their necks; they also killed their households and those who had circumcised them.
1:62 Many in Israel stood firm and fortified themselves against eating unclean things.
1:63 They chose to die so that they would not be defiled by foods or profane the holy covenant, and they died.
1:64 Very great wrath came upon Israel.

1 MACCABEES 2

2:1 In those days Mattathias son of John son of Simeon, a priest of the sons of Joarib, arose from Jerusalem and settled in Modein.
2:2 He had five sons: John, called Gaddi;
2:3 Simon, called Thassi;
2:4 Judas, called Maccabeus;
2:5 Eleazar, called Avaran; and Jonathan, called Apphous.
2:6 He saw the blasphemies being committed in Judah and Jerusalem
2:7 and said, “Alas for me! Why was I born to see the ruin of my people and the ruin of the holy city, and to sit there while she was given into the hand of enemies, the sanctuary into the hand of foreigners?
2:8 Her temple has become like a man without honour.
2:9 The vessels of her glory were carried away captive;
her little children were slain in her squares,
her young men by the sword of an enemy.
2:10 What nation has not taken possession of her royal places and seized her spoils?
2:11 All her adornment was taken away;
she that was free became a slave.
2:12 Behold! Our holy places, our beauty, and our glory have been laid waste, and the nations have profaned them.
2:13 Why should we live any longer?”
2:14 Mattathias and his sons tore their garments, put on sackcloth, and mourned greatly.
2:15 The king’s officials who were compelling apostasy came to the city of Modein to make the people sacrifice.
2:16 Many from Israel came to them, and Mattathias and his sons gathered.
2:17 The king’s officials addressed Mattathias, saying, “You are a ruler, honoured and great in this city, supported by sons and brothers.
2:18 Now be the first to come forward and carry out the king’s decree, as all the nations, the men of Judah, and those left in Jerusalem have done. You and your sons shall be among the king’s Friends, and you and your sons shall be honoured with silver, gold, and many gifts.”
2:19 Mattathias answered in a loud voice, “Even if all the nations within the king’s realm obey him, each abandoning the worship of his fathers, and have chosen his commands,
2:20 I and my sons and my brothers will walk in the covenant of our fathers.
2:21 Far be it from us to abandon the law and the ordinances!
2:22 We will not obey the king’s words by turning aside from our worship to the right or to the left.”
2:23 When he finished speaking these words, a Jewish man came forward in the sight of everyone to sacrifice upon the pagan altar in Modein, according to the king’s decree.
2:24 Mattathias saw and burned with zeal; his inward parts trembled. He gave vent to anger according to the judgment, ran forward, and slaughtered him upon the altar.
2:25 At that time he also killed the king’s officer who was compelling sacrifice, and he pulled down the altar.
2:26 He was zealous for the law, just as Phinehas had acted toward Zimri son of Salom.
2:27 Mattathias cried out in the city with a loud voice, “Let everyone who is zealous for the law and upholds the covenant come out after me!”
2:28 He and his sons fled to the mountains and abandoned everything they had in the city.
2:29 Then many who sought righteousness and judgment went down into the wilderness to settle there,
2:30 with their sons, their wives, and their livestock, because the evils had become severe against them.
2:31 It was reported to the king’s men and the forces in Jerusalem, the city of David, that men who had rejected the king’s command had gone down into hiding places in the wilderness.
2:32 Many pursued them, overtook them, encamped against them, and prepared for battle against them on the sabbath day.
2:33 They said to them, “Enough of this! Come out and act according to the king’s command, and you shall live.”
2:34 They said, “We will not come out, nor will we carry out the king’s command by profaning the sabbath day.”
2:35 They hurried into battle against them.
2:36 But they did not answer them, throw a stone at them, or block the hiding places,
2:37 saying, “Let us all die in our integrity. Heaven and earth bear witness concerning us that you are destroying us without judgment.”
2:38 They attacked them in battle on the sabbath. They died, together with their wives, their children, and their livestock—about a thousand human lives.
2:39 Mattathias and his friends learned of it and mourned for them greatly.
2:40 Each said to his neighbour, “If we all do as our brothers have done and do not fight the nations for our lives and our ordinances, they will now quickly destroy us from the earth.”
2:41 That day they resolved, saying, “If anyone comes against us for battle on the sabbath day, we will fight against him, and we will certainly not all die as our brothers died in the hiding places.”
2:42 Then a company of Hasideans gathered to them, strong warriors from Israel, everyone who willingly offered himself for the law.
2:43 All who were fleeing from the evils joined them and became their support.
2:44 They organized an army and struck sinners in their anger and lawless men in their wrath. The rest fled to the nations for safety.
2:45 Mattathias and his friends went around and pulled down the pagan altars.
2:46 They forcibly circumcised all the uncircumcised boys whom they found within the borders of Israel.
2:47 They pursued the sons of arrogance, and the work prospered in their hands.
2:48 They took the law out of the hand of the nations and the kings, and they did not allow the sinner to gain a horn.
2:49 When the days drew near for Mattathias to die, he said to his sons, “Now arrogance and rebuke have grown strong—a time of overthrow and furious wrath.
2:50 Now, children, be zealous for the law and give your lives for the covenant of our fathers.
2:51 Remember the deeds of the fathers that they did in their generations, and receive great glory and an everlasting name.
2:52 Was not Abraham found faithful in testing, and was it not reckoned to him as righteousness?
2:53 Joseph, in the time of his distress, kept the commandment and became lord of Egypt.
2:54 Phinehas our father, by burning with zeal, received a covenant of everlasting priesthood.
2:55 Joshua, by fulfilling the word, became a judge in Israel.
2:56 Caleb, by bearing witness in the assembly, received an inheritance of land.
2:57 David, through his mercy, inherited a royal throne for ever.
2:58 Elijah, by burning with zeal for the law, was taken up into heaven.
2:59 Hananiah, Azariah, and Mishael trusted and were saved from the flame.
2:60 Daniel, in his integrity, was rescued from the mouth of lions.
2:61 So consider it from generation to generation: all who hope in Him will not fail.
2:62 Do not fear the words of a sinful man, for his glory will become dung and worms.
2:63 Today he shall be exalted, and tomorrow he shall not be found, for he has returned to his dust, and his thought shall perish.
2:64 Children, be courageous and be strong in the law, because through it you shall be glorified.
2:65 Behold, Simon your brother—I know that he is a man of counsel. Listen to him all your days; he will be your father.
2:66 Judas Maccabeus has been a strong warrior from his youth. He will be your army commander and will fight the battle of peoples.
2:67 Gather to yourselves all who carry out the law, and exact vengeance for your people.
2:68 Repay the nations what is due to them, and attend to the law’s command.”
2:69 Then he blessed them and was gathered to his fathers.
2:70 He died in the one hundred and forty-sixth year and was buried in the tombs of his fathers in Modein. All Israel lamented him with a great lament.

1 MACCABEES 3

3:1 His son Judas, called Maccabeus, arose in his place.
3:2 All his brothers and everyone who had joined his father helped him, and they fought Israel’s war with gladness.
3:3 He spread glory for his people,
put on a breastplate like a giant,
and girded himself with his weapons of war;
he waged battles, shielding the army with his sword.
3:4 In his deeds he was like a lion,
like a young lion roaring over prey.
3:5 He searched out and pursued the lawless, and burned those who troubled his people.
3:6 The lawless shrank back from fear of him;
all workers of lawlessness were shaken,
and deliverance prospered in his hand.
3:7 He embittered many kings
and gladdened Jacob by his deeds;
his memory is a blessing for ever.
3:8 He passed through the cities of Judah, destroyed the ungodly out of her, and turned wrath away from Israel.
3:9 His name reached to the end of the earth, and he gathered those who were perishing.
3:10 Apollonius gathered nations and a great force from Samaria to fight against Israel.
3:11 Judas learned of it, went out to meet him, struck him, and killed him. Many fell wounded, and the survivors fled.
3:12 They took their spoils. Judas took Apollonius’s sword and fought with it all his days.
3:13 Seron, commander of the Syrian force, heard that Judas had gathered a body of men, an assembly of faithful men who were with him and went out to war.
3:14 He said, “I will make a name for myself and be honoured in the kingdom. I will fight Judas and those with him who despise the king’s command.”
3:15 He set out again, and a strong army of ungodly men went up with him to help him take vengeance on the sons of Israel.
3:16 He approached the ascent of Beth-horon, and Judas went out to meet him with only a few men.
3:17 When they saw the army coming to meet them, they said to Judas, “How can we, being so few, fight such a strong multitude? We are exhausted, having eaten nothing today.”
3:18 Judas said, “It is easy for many to be shut into the hands of a few. Before Heaven there is no difference between saving by many or by few.
3:19 For victory in battle does not lie in the size of an army; strength comes from heaven.
3:20 They come against us in an abundance of arrogance and lawlessness to destroy us, our wives, and our children, and to plunder us.
3:21 But we are fighting for our lives and our customs.
3:22 He Himself will crush them before us. Do not be afraid of them.”
3:23 When he finished speaking, he rushed upon them suddenly, and Seron and his army were crushed before him.
3:24 They pursued him down the descent of Beth-horon as far as the plain. About eight hundred of their men fell, and the rest fled into the land of the Philistines.
3:25 Then fear of Judas and his brothers began, and terror fell upon the nations around them.
3:26 His name reached the king, and the nations recounted the battles of Judas.
3:27 When King Antiochus heard these reports, he became furious. He sent and gathered all the forces of his kingdom, an exceedingly strong army.
3:28 He opened his treasury, gave the forces a year’s pay, and commanded them to be ready for every need.
3:29 He saw that the money in the treasuries was exhausted and the revenues of the country were small because of the dissension and disaster he had brought upon the land by removing the customs that had existed from the earliest days.
3:30 He feared that he would not have enough, as on former occasions, for expenses and for the gifts that he had previously given with a lavish hand, surpassing the kings before him.
3:31 He was greatly perplexed in his soul and resolved to go to Persia, collect the revenues of the countries, and gather much silver.
3:32 He left Lysias, an honoured man of royal lineage, in charge of the king’s affairs from the Euphrates River to the borders of Egypt
3:33 and to bring up his son Antiochus until he returned.
3:34 He handed over to him half the forces and the elephants, and instructed him concerning everything he wished, including the inhabitants of Judea and Jerusalem:
3:35 he was to send an army against them to crush and remove Israel’s strength and the remnant of Jerusalem, and to remove their memory from the place.
3:36 He was to settle foreigners throughout all their territory and allot their land to them.
3:37 The king took the remaining half of the forces and set out from Antioch, his royal city, in the one hundred and forty-seventh year. He crossed the Euphrates River and travelled through the upper countries.
3:38 Lysias selected Ptolemy son of Dorymenes, Nicanor, and Gorgias, capable men among the king’s Friends.
3:39 He sent with them forty thousand men and seven thousand cavalry to enter the land of Judah and destroy it, according to the king’s command.
3:40 He set out with all their force, and they came and encamped near Emmaus in the lowland.
3:41 The merchants of the country heard their reputation, took a very great quantity of silver and gold, along with shackles, and came to the camp to buy the sons of Israel as slaves. A force from Syria and the land of foreigners joined them.
3:42 Judas and his brothers saw that the evils had multiplied and that the forces were encamping within their borders. They learned the king’s commands to bring destruction and an end upon the people.
3:43 Each said to his neighbour, “Let us restore our people’s fallen state and fight for our people and the holy places.”
3:44 The assembly gathered to prepare for war, to pray, and to ask for mercy and compassion.
3:45 Jerusalem was uninhabited like a wilderness. None of those born in her went in or out; the sanctuary was being trampled, and foreigners were in the citadel, a lodging for the nations. Delight was removed from Jacob, and flute and lyre ceased.
3:46 They gathered and came to Mizpah opposite Jerusalem, because formerly Mizpah had been a place of prayer for Israel.
3:47 They fasted that day, put on sackcloth, placed ashes on their heads, and tore their garments.
3:48 They unrolled the book of the law concerning the matters for which the nations consulted the likenesses of their idols.
3:49 They brought the priestly garments, the firstfruits, and the tithes, and brought forward the Nazirites who had completed their days.
3:50 They cried aloud to Heaven, saying, “What shall we do with these, and where shall we take them,
3:51 when Thy holy places have been trampled and profaned, and Thy priests are in mourning and humiliation?
3:52 Behold! The nations have gathered against us to destroy us. Thou knowest what they are planning against us.
3:53 How can we stand before them unless Thou help us?”
3:54 They sounded the trumpets and cried out with a loud voice.
3:55 After this Judas appointed leaders of the people: commanders of thousands, hundreds, fifties, and tens.
3:56 He told those building houses, those betrothed to wives, those planting vineyards, and the fearful to return, each to his own house, according to the law.
3:57 The army set out, and they encamped south of Emmaus.
3:58 Judas said, “Gird yourselves and become men of strength. Be ready in the morning to fight these nations that have gathered against us to destroy us and our holy places.
3:59 It is better for us to die in battle than to look upon the evils suffered by our nation and the holy places.
3:60 But whatever the will in heaven may be, that He will do.”

1 MACCABEES 4

4:1 Gorgias took five thousand men and a thousand picked cavalry, and the army set out by night
4:2 to fall upon the camp of the Jews and strike them suddenly. Men from the citadel served as his guides.
4:3 Judas heard of it, and he and the mighty men set out to strike the king’s force at Emmaus,
4:4 while the forces were still scattered away from the camp.
4:5 Gorgias came to Judas’s camp by night and found no one. He searched for them in the mountains, saying, “They are fleeing from us.”
4:6 At daybreak Judas appeared on the plain with three thousand men, though they did not have the protective equipment and swords they wanted.
4:7 They saw a strong army of nations, armoured, with cavalry surrounding it; these men were trained for war.
4:8 Judas said to the men with him, “Do not fear their multitude or lose courage before their assault.
4:9 Remember how our fathers were saved at the Red Sea when Pharaoh pursued them with an army.
4:10 Now let us cry to Heaven: perhaps He will favour us, remember the covenant of the fathers, and crush this army before us today.
4:11 Then all the nations will know that there is One who redeems and saves Israel.”
4:12 The foreigners raised their eyes and saw them coming from the opposite side.
4:13 They went out of the camp for battle, and Judas’s men sounded the trumpet.
4:14 They engaged, and the nations were crushed and fled into the plain.
4:15 All who were at the rear fell by the sword. They pursued them as far as Gazera and the plains of Idumea, Azotus, and Jamnia; about three thousand of their men fell.
4:16 Judas and his force returned from pursuing them,
4:17 and he said to the people, “Do not covet the spoils, because a battle lies before us.
4:18 Gorgias and his force are in the mountain near us. Stand now against our enemies and fight them; afterward take the spoils freely.”
4:19 While Judas was finishing these words, a detachment appeared, looking out from the mountain.
4:20 They saw that their men had been routed and that the camp was being burned, for the visible smoke made plain what had happened.
4:21 Seeing these things, they became exceedingly afraid. When they also saw Judas’s army on the plain ready for battle,
4:22 they all fled into the land of foreigners.
4:23 Judas returned to plunder the camp, and they took much gold and silver, blue cloth, sea-purple cloth, and great wealth.
4:24 On their return they sang hymns and blessed Heaven: “For it is good, for His mercy is for ever.”
4:25 A great deliverance came to Israel that day.
4:26 Those foreigners who escaped came and reported to Lysias everything that had happened.
4:27 When he heard it, he was confused and disheartened, because what he had wished had not happened to Israel, and what the king had commanded him had not come about.
4:28 In the following year he assembled sixty thousand picked men and five thousand cavalry to make war on them.
4:29 They came into Idumea and encamped at Beth-zur. Judas met them with ten thousand men.
4:30 He saw the strong army and prayed, saying, “Blessed art Thou, Saviour of Israel, who didst crush the mighty man’s assault by the hand of Thy servant David, and didst deliver the foreigners’ army into the hands of Jonathan son of Saul and his armour-bearer.
4:31 So shut this army into the hand of Thy people Israel, and let them be ashamed of their force and their cavalry.
4:32 Give them cowardice; melt the boldness of their strength, and let them be shaken by their collapse.
4:33 Bring them down by the sword of those who love Thee, and let all who know Thy name praise Thee with hymns.”
4:34 They joined battle with one another. About five thousand men from Lysias’s army fell; they fell before them.
4:35 Lysias saw the rout of his own formation, the courage that had arisen in Judas’s men, and how they were ready either to live or to die nobly. He withdrew to Antioch and recruited mercenaries in greater numbers to come again into Judea.
4:36 Judas and his brothers said, “Behold! Our enemies have been crushed. Let us go up to cleanse the holy places and dedicate them anew.”
4:37 The whole army gathered, and they went up to Mount Zion.
4:38 They saw the sanctuary desolate, the altar profaned, the gates burned down, plants growing in the courts as in a thicket or on one of the mountains, and the chambers demolished.
4:39 They tore their garments, lamented with a great lament, and put ashes on themselves.
4:40 They fell face down upon the ground, sounded the signal trumpets, and cried to Heaven.
4:41 Then Judas ordered men to fight those in the citadel until he had cleansed the holy places.
4:42 He chose blameless priests devoted to the law.
4:43 They cleansed the holy places and carried the stones of defilement to an unclean place.
4:44 They deliberated what to do with the altar of whole burnt offering, which had been profaned.
4:45 They considered it best to pull it down, so that it would not become a reproach to them because the nations had defiled it. They pulled down the altar
4:46 and placed the stones on the mountain of the house in a suitable place until a prophet should come to give an answer about them.
4:47 They took whole stones according to the law and built a new altar like the former one.
4:48 They rebuilt the holy places and the interior of the house, and consecrated the courts.
4:49 They made new holy vessels and brought the lampstand, the incense altar, and the table into the temple.
4:50 They burned incense upon the altar and lit the lamps on the lampstand, and they shone in the temple.
4:51 They placed loaves on the table and spread out the curtains. They completed all the works they had undertaken.
4:52 They rose early in the morning on the twenty-fifth day of the ninth month, which is the month of Chislev, in the one hundred and forty-eighth year.
4:53 They offered sacrifice according to the law upon the new altar of whole burnt offerings that they had made.
4:54 At the season and on the day on which the nations had profaned it, it was dedicated with songs, harps, lyres, and cymbals.
4:55 All the people fell face down, worshiped, and blessed toward heaven the One who had prospered them.
4:56 They celebrated the dedication of the altar for eight days, offered whole burnt offerings with gladness, and sacrificed a sacrifice of well-being and praise.
4:57 They adorned the front of the temple with golden crowns and small shields, renewed the gateways and chambers, and fitted them with doors.
4:58 There was exceedingly great gladness among the people, and the reproach of the nations was turned away.
4:59 Judas, his brothers, and the whole assembly of Israel established that the days of the altar’s dedication should be celebrated at their proper season, year by year, for eight days beginning on the twenty-fifth of the month of Chislev, with gladness and joy.
4:60 At that time they built high walls and strong towers around Mount Zion so that the nations could not come and trample them as they had done before.
4:61 He stationed a force there to guard it, and fortified it to guard Beth-zur, so that the people would have a stronghold facing Idumea.

1 MACCABEES 5

5:1 When the nations round about heard that the altar had been rebuilt and the sanctuary dedicated as before, they became exceedingly angry.
5:2 They resolved to remove the descendants of Jacob living among them, and began killing and destroying people.
5:3 Judas fought against the sons of Esau in Idumea, in Akrabattene, because they were besieging Israel. He struck them a great blow, subdued them, and took their spoils.
5:4 He remembered the evil of the sons of Baean, who had become a trap and a stumbling block for the people by ambushing them on the roads.
5:5 He shut them into the towers, encamped against them, devoted them to destruction, and burned the towers with everyone inside.
5:6 He crossed over against the sons of Ammon and found a strong force and many people, with Timothy as their leader.
5:7 He engaged them in many battles. They were crushed before him, and he struck them.
5:8 He seized Jazer and its daughter towns, then returned to Judea.
5:9 The nations in Gilead gathered against the Israelites living in their territory to destroy them, and they fled into the stronghold of Dathema.
5:10 They sent letters to Judas and his brothers, saying, “The nations around us have gathered against us to destroy us.
5:11 They are preparing to come and seize the stronghold into which we fled, and Timothy is leading their force.
5:12 Now, therefore, come and rescue us from their hand, because many of us have fallen.
5:13 All our brothers in the region of Tobiah have been put to death. They have taken their wives, children, and belongings captive, and have destroyed about a thousand men there.”
5:14 While the letters were still being read, other messengers arrived from Galilee with torn garments, reporting similar things.
5:15 They said that people from Ptolemais, Tyre, and Sidon, and all Galilee of foreigners, had gathered against them “to consume us completely.”
5:16 When Judas and the people heard these words, a great assembly gathered to deliberate what to do for their brothers who were in distress and under attack.
5:17 Judas said to Simon his brother, “Choose men for yourself and go and deliver your brothers in Galilee. I and Jonathan my brother will go to Gilead.”
5:18 He left Joseph son of Zechariah and Azariah, a leader of the people, with the remaining force in Judea to guard it.
5:19 He commanded them, saying, “Take charge of this people, and do not engage the nations in battle until we return.”
5:20 Three thousand men were assigned to Simon to go to Galilee, and eight thousand men to Judas for Gilead.
5:21 Simon went to Galilee and engaged the nations in many battles. The nations were crushed before him,
5:22 and he pursued them to the gate of Ptolemais. About three thousand men of the nations fell, and he took their spoils.
5:23 He took those from Galilee and Arbattos, with their wives, children, and everything they had, and brought them into Judea with great gladness.
5:24 Judas Maccabeus and Jonathan his brother crossed the Jordan and travelled a three-day journey in the wilderness.
5:25 They met the Nabateans, who received them peacefully and recounted everything that had happened to their brothers in Gilead.
5:26 Many of them had been confined in Bosora, Bosor in Alema, Chaspho, Maked, and Carnaim—all these cities were strong and large.
5:27 They were also confined in the other cities of Gilead. The enemy had arranged to attack the strongholds the next day, seize them, and destroy all these people in one day.
5:28 Judas and his army suddenly turned their route toward Bosora through the wilderness. He seized the city, killed every male with the edge of the sword, took all their spoils, and burned it with fire.
5:29 He set out from there by night, and they travelled as far as the stronghold.
5:30 In the morning they raised their eyes and saw a great people beyond counting, carrying ladders and siege engines to seize the stronghold and attacking its defenders.
5:31 Judas saw that the battle had begun and that the city’s cry rose to heaven with trumpets and a loud shout.
5:32 He said to the men of the force, “Fight today for our brothers!”
5:33 He came out behind them in three divisions. They sounded the trumpets and cried out in prayer.
5:34 Timothy’s army recognized that it was Maccabeus and fled before him. He struck them a great blow, and about eight thousand of their men fell that day.
5:35 He turned aside to Alema, attacked it, seized it, killed every male in it, took its spoils, and burned it with fire.
5:36 He set out from there and seized Chaspho, Maked, Bosor, and the other cities of Gilead.
5:37 After these events Timothy gathered another army and encamped opposite Raphon, beyond the stream.
5:38 Judas sent men to spy out the camp. They reported to him, saying, “All the nations around us have gathered to him, a very large force.
5:39 They have hired Arabs to help them and are encamped beyond the stream, ready to come against you in battle.” Judas went to meet them.
5:40 As Judas and his army approached the watercourse, Timothy said to his army commanders, “If he crosses over to us first, we will not be able to stand against him; he will surely prevail against us.
5:41 But if he becomes afraid and encamps beyond the river, we will cross over to him and prevail against him.”
5:42 When Judas approached the watercourse, he stationed the officers of the people at the stream and commanded them, saying, “Do not allow anyone to encamp; let everyone come into the battle.”
5:43 He crossed over against them first, with all the people behind him. All the nations were crushed before them; they threw away their weapons and fled to the sacred precinct of Carnaim.
5:44 They seized the city and burned the sacred precinct with everyone inside. Carnaim was overthrown, and they could no longer stand before Judas.
5:45 Judas gathered all Israel in Gilead, from small to great, with their wives, their children, and their belongings—a very great company—to go into the land of Judah.
5:46 They came as far as Ephron. It was a large, exceedingly strong city on the road, and there was no way to turn aside from it to the right or left, but only to pass through its midst.
5:47 The people of the city shut them out and blocked the gates with stones.
5:48 Judas sent to them with peaceful words, saying, “We will pass through your land to go to our land. No one will harm you; we will only pass through on foot.” But they would not open to him.
5:49 Judas ordered a proclamation through the company that each should encamp in the place where he was.
5:50 The men of the force encamped. He attacked the city all that day and all that night, and the city was given into his hand.
5:51 He destroyed every male with the edge of the sword, uprooted the city, took its spoils, and passed through the city over the slain.
5:52 They crossed the Jordan into the great plain opposite Beth-shan.
5:53 Judas gathered those who lagged behind and encouraged the people along the whole road until he came into the land of Judah.
5:54 They went up to Mount Zion with gladness and joy and presented whole burnt offerings, because none of them had fallen before they returned in peace.
5:55 In the days when Judas and Jonathan were in the land of Gilead, and Simon his brother was in Galilee opposite Ptolemais,
5:56 Joseph son of Zechariah and Azariah, commanders of the force, heard of their brave deeds and the warfare they had carried out.
5:57 They said, “Let us also make a name for ourselves. Let us go and fight the nations around us.”
5:58 They gave orders to the men of the force with them, and they marched against Jamnia.
5:59 Gorgias and his men came out of the city to meet them in battle.
5:60 Joseph and Azariah were routed and pursued to the borders of Judea. About two thousand men of the people of Israel fell that day.
5:61 A great rout occurred among the people because they had not listened to Judas and his brothers, thinking to perform brave deeds.
5:62 But they were not of the line of those men through whose hand the deliverance of Israel had been granted.
5:63 The man Judas and his brothers were greatly honoured before all Israel and all the nations wherever their name was heard.
5:64 People gathered to them, acclaiming them.
5:65 Judas and his brothers went out and fought the sons of Esau in the southern land. He struck Hebron and its daughter towns, demolished its fortifications, and burned its towers all around.
5:66 He set out to go into the land of foreigners and passed through Marisa.
5:67 That day priests fell in battle because they wished to perform brave deeds and went out to battle without counsel.
5:68 Judas turned aside to Azotus in the land of foreigners, pulled down their pagan altars, burned the carved images of their gods with fire, took the spoils of the cities, and returned to the land of Judah.

1 MACCABEES 6

6:1 King Antiochus was travelling through the upper countries when he heard that Elymais in Persia was a city renowned for wealth, silver, and gold.
6:2 Its temple was exceedingly rich, and there were golden coverings, breastplates, and weapons left there by Alexander son of Philip, the Macedonian king who first reigned among the Greeks.
6:3 He came and tried to seize the city and plunder it, but could not, because the people of the city learned of his plan.
6:4 They resisted him in battle, and he fled and withdrew from there in great sorrow to return to Babylon.
6:5 Someone came to Persia to report to him that the armies that had gone into the land of Judah had been routed;
6:6 that Lysias had gone at first with a strong army but had been driven back before them; and that they had grown strong in weapons, power, and the many spoils they had taken from the armies they had cut down.
6:7 They had pulled down the abomination he had built upon the altar in Jerusalem and surrounded the sanctuary with high walls as before, and also Beth-zur, his city.
6:8 When the king heard these reports, he was astonished and greatly shaken. He fell upon his bed and became ill from sorrow, because things had not happened to him as he had intended.
6:9 He remained there many days, for great sorrow came upon him again, and he judged that he was dying.
6:10 He called all his Friends and said to them, “Sleep is departing from my eyes, and my heart has collapsed from anxiety.
6:11 I said in my heart, ‘Into what distress and great storm have I come, in which I now find myself?’ For I was kind and beloved during my rule.
6:12 But now I remember the evils I committed in Jerusalem: I took all the silver and gold vessels in it and sent to destroy the inhabitants of Judah without cause.
6:13 I know that because of these things these evils have found me. Behold! I am perishing in great sorrow in a foreign land.”
6:14 He summoned Philip, one of his Friends, and appointed him over his whole kingdom.
6:15 He gave him the diadem, his robe, and his ring, to guide his son Antiochus and bring him up to reign.
6:16 King Antiochus died there in the one hundred and forty-ninth year.
6:17 Lysias learned that the king had died and appointed his son Antiochus, whom he had brought up from youth, to reign. He called his name Eupator.
6:18 Those in the citadel were hemming Israel in around the holy places, continually seeking harm, and providing support to the nations.
6:19 Judas determined to remove them and assembled all the people to besiege them.
6:20 They gathered together and besieged it in the one hundred and fiftieth year. He made artillery platforms and siege engines.
6:21 Some of them escaped from the blockade, and certain ungodly men from Israel joined them.
6:22 They went to the king and said, “How long will you refrain from giving judgment and avenging our brothers?
6:23 We are willing to serve your father, walk according to his words, and follow his decrees.
6:24 For this reason the sons of our people besieged it and became estranged from us. Moreover, as many of us as were found were put to death, and our inheritances were plundered.
6:25 They have stretched out their hand not only against us but also against all their territories.
6:26 Behold! Today they have encamped against the citadel in Jerusalem to seize it. They have fortified the sanctuary and Beth-zur.
6:27 Unless you act against them quickly, they will do greater things than these, and you shall not be able to restrain them.”
6:28 The king became angry when he heard this. He gathered all his Friends, his army commanders, and those in charge of the reins.
6:29 Mercenary forces came to him from other kingdoms and from islands of the seas.
6:30 His forces numbered a hundred thousand infantry, twenty thousand cavalry, and thirty-two elephants trained for war.
6:31 They came through Idumea, encamped against Beth-zur, and fought for many days, making siege engines. The defenders came out, burned them with fire, and fought courageously.
6:32 Judas withdrew from the citadel and encamped at Beth-zechariah opposite the king’s camp.
6:33 The king rose early in the morning and set his army moving rapidly along the road to Beth-zechariah. The forces prepared for battle and sounded the trumpets.
6:34 They showed the elephants the blood of grapes and mulberries to ready them for battle.
6:35 They divided the beasts among the phalanxes. Beside each elephant they stationed a thousand men armoured in chain mail, with bronze helmets on their heads, and five hundred picked cavalry were assigned to each beast.
6:36 They were in position beforehand wherever the beast was; wherever it went, they went together and did not leave it.
6:37 Strong, covered wooden towers were on them, secured by devices upon each beast. On each were four fighting men and its Indian driver.
6:38 He stationed the remaining cavalry on either side, on the two wings of the army, threatening the enemy and shielding the phalanxes.
6:39 When the sun flashed upon the golden and bronze shields, the mountains flashed from them and shone like torches of fire.
6:40 One part of the king’s army spread out on the high mountains and others on the low ground. They advanced securely and in order.
6:41 Everyone who heard the sound of their multitude, the march of the multitude, and the clash of weapons was shaken, for the army was exceedingly large and strong.
6:42 Judas and his army drew near for battle, and six hundred men of the king’s army fell.
6:43 Eleazar Avaran saw one of the beasts armoured with royal breastplates, towering above all the beasts, and thought that the king was on it.
6:44 He gave himself to save his people and to gain for himself an everlasting name.
6:45 He ran boldly toward it into the midst of the phalanx, killing to the right and left, and they parted before him on either side.
6:46 He slipped beneath the elephant, thrust under it, and killed it. It fell to the ground on top of him, and he died there.
6:47 They saw the strength of the kingdom and the onrush of the forces and withdrew from them.
6:48 The men of the king’s army went up to meet them at Jerusalem, and the king encamped in Judea and against Mount Zion.
6:49 He made peace with the people of Beth-zur, and they came out of the city, because they had no provisions there to sustain a siege, since it was a sabbath for the land.
6:50 The king seized Beth-zur and stationed a garrison there to guard it.
6:51 He encamped against the sanctuary for many days and set up artillery platforms, siege engines, fire-throwers, stone-throwers, small scorpions for shooting missiles, and slings.
6:52 The defenders also made engines to oppose their engines and fought for many days.
6:53 There was no food in the storage vessels, because it was the seventh year, and those who had escaped into Judea from the nations had consumed the remaining stores.
6:54 Only a few men were left in the holy places, because famine prevailed over them. They scattered, each to his own place.
6:55 Lysias heard that Philip, whom King Antiochus had appointed while still alive to bring up his son Antiochus to reign,
6:56 had returned from Persia and Media with the forces that had gone with the king, and that he was seeking to take control of affairs.
6:57 He hurried and pressed for departure, saying to the king, the commanders of the force, and the men, “We are dwindling day by day, our food is scarce, the place against which we are encamped is strong, and the affairs of the kingdom are pressing upon us.
6:58 Now, therefore, let us give these men our right hands and make peace with them and with their whole nation.
6:59 Let us establish their right to follow their customs as before. For it was because of their customs, which we abolished, that they became angry and did all these things.”
6:60 The proposal pleased the king and the commanders. He sent to make peace with them, and they accepted.
6:61 The king and the commanders swore to them, and on these terms they came out of the stronghold.
6:62 The king entered Mount Zion and saw the strength of the place. He rejected the oath he had sworn and ordered the surrounding wall to be pulled down.
6:63 He set out hurriedly and returned to Antioch. He found Philip in control of the city, fought against him, and seized the city by force.

1 MACCABEES 7

7:1 In the one hundred and fifty-first year, Demetrius son of Seleucus departed from Rome, came up with a few men to a coastal city, and reigned there.
7:2 As he was entering the royal house of his fathers, the forces seized Antiochus and Lysias to bring them to him.
7:3 The matter became known to him, and he said, “Do not show me their faces.”
7:4 The forces killed them, and Demetrius sat upon the throne of his kingdom.
7:5 All the lawless and ungodly men from Israel came to him. Alcimus, who wished to serve as priest, was their leader.
7:6 They accused the people before the king, saying, “Judas and his brothers have destroyed all your Friends and scattered us from our land.
7:7 Now, therefore, send a man whom you trust. Let him go and see all the devastation he has brought upon us and the king’s country, and let him punish them and all who help them.”
7:8 The king chose Bacchides, one of the king’s Friends, who ruled Beyond the River, was great in the kingdom, and was faithful to the king.
7:9 He sent him and the ungodly Alcimus, confirmed Alcimus in the priesthood, and commanded Bacchides to exact vengeance among the sons of Israel.
7:10 They set out and came with a large force into the land of Judah. He sent messengers to Judas and his brothers with peaceful words, deceitfully.
7:11 They paid no attention to their words, for they saw that they had come with a large force.
7:12 A company of scribes gathered to Alcimus and Bacchides to seek just terms.
7:13 The Hasideans were the first among the sons of Israel to seek peace from them,
7:14 for they said, “A man who is a priest of Aaron’s line has come with the forces, and he will not wrong us.”
7:15 He spoke peaceful words with them and swore to them, saying, “We will not seek harm for you or your friends.”
7:16 They trusted him. But he seized sixty men from among them and killed them in one day, according to the word that was written:
7:17 “They poured out the flesh of Thy holy ones and their blood around Jerusalem, and there was no one to bury them.”
7:18 Fear and trembling because of them fell upon all the people, for they said, “There is no truth or justice in them; they have violated the agreement and the oath they swore.”
7:19 Bacchides withdrew from Jerusalem and encamped at Beth-zaith. He sent and seized many of the men who had deserted to him and some of the people, and slaughtered them into the great pit.
7:20 He placed the country under Alcimus and left a force with him to help him. Then Bacchides went to the king.
7:21 Alcimus contended for the high priesthood.
7:22 All who troubled their people gathered to him, took control of the land of Judah, and inflicted a great blow upon Israel.
7:23 Judas saw all the evil that Alcimus and those with him had committed among the sons of Israel, beyond that of the nations.
7:24 He went out throughout all the territory of Judea and exacted vengeance on the deserters. They were prevented from going out into the country.
7:25 Alcimus saw that Judas and those with him had grown strong and knew that he could not stand against them. He returned to the king and brought evil accusations against them.
7:26 The king sent Nicanor, one of his distinguished commanders, who hated Israel and was hostile to them, and commanded him to destroy the people.
7:27 Nicanor came to Jerusalem with a large force. He sent deceitfully to Judas and his brothers with peaceful words, saying,
7:28 “Let there be no battle between me and you. I will come with a few men so that I may see your faces in peace.”
7:29 He came to Judas, and they greeted one another peacefully, but the enemies were ready to seize Judas.
7:30 Judas learned that he had come against him deceitfully. He became wary of him and would no longer see his face.
7:31 Nicanor knew that his plan had been revealed and went out to meet Judas in battle near Caphar-salama.
7:32 About five hundred of Nicanor’s men fell, and they fled into the city of David.
7:33 After these events Nicanor went up to Mount Zion. Some of the priests came out of the holy places, together with some elders of the people, to greet him peacefully and show him the whole burnt offering being presented for the king.
7:34 He sneered at them, mocked them, defiled them, and spoke arrogantly.
7:35 He swore in anger, saying, “Unless Judas and his army are handed over into my hands now, if I return safely, I will burn this house.” He went out in great anger.
7:36 The priests went in and stood before the altar and the temple. They wept and said,
7:37 “Thou didst choose this house for Thy name to be called upon it, to be a house of prayer and petition for Thy people.
7:38 Exact vengeance upon this man and his army, and let them fall by the sword. Remember their insults, and grant them no continuance.”
7:39 Nicanor went out from Jerusalem and encamped at Beth-horon, and a Syrian force met him.
7:40 Judas encamped at Adasa with three thousand men. Judas prayed and said,
7:41 “When the king’s men spoke blasphemously, Thine angel went out and struck a hundred and eighty-five thousand among them.
7:42 In this way crush this army before us today. Let the survivors know that he has spoken wickedly against Thy holy places, and judge him according to his wickedness.”
7:43 The armies joined battle on the thirteenth day of the month of Adar. Nicanor’s army was crushed, and he himself fell first in the battle.
7:44 When his army saw that Nicanor had fallen, they threw down their weapons and fled.
7:45 They pursued them a day’s journey from Adasa until they came to Gazera, sounding the signal trumpets behind them.
7:46 People came out from all the surrounding villages of Judea and outflanked them. They turned against one another and all fell by the sword; not even one of them remained.
7:47 They took the spoils and plunder, cut off Nicanor’s head and his right hand, which he had stretched out arrogantly, and brought and displayed them beside Jerusalem.
7:48 The people rejoiced greatly and celebrated that day as a day of great gladness.
7:49 They established that this day should be celebrated every year, on the thirteenth of Adar.
7:50 The land of Judah was quiet for a few days.

1 MACCABEES 8

8:1 Judas heard the reputation of the Romans: that they were mighty in strength, that they looked favourably on all who joined them, that they established friendship with whoever approached them, and that they were mighty in strength.
8:2 They recounted to him their wars, the brave deeds they were doing among the Galatians, and how they had subdued them and brought them under tribute;
8:3 also what they had done in the country of Spain to gain control of the silver and gold mines there.
8:4 By their counsel and perseverance they had gained control of the whole region, although it was very far from them. They had also overcome the kings who came against them from the end of the earth, crushing them and striking them a great blow; the survivors paid them tribute every year.
8:5 They had crushed in battle Philip, Perseus king of the Kittians, and those who rose against them, and had subdued them.
8:6 Antiochus the Great, king of Asia, had marched against them for battle with a hundred and twenty elephants, cavalry, chariots, and an exceedingly large force, but he was crushed by them.
8:7 They took him alive and required him and those reigning after him to pay a great tribute, give hostages, and make a territorial settlement,
8:8 including the country of India, Media, and Lydia, some of their finest regions. They took them from him and gave them to King Eumenes.
8:9 They also heard that the people of Greece had resolved to come and destroy them.
8:10 The Romans learned the plan and sent one general against them. They fought them, and many fell wounded; they took their wives and children captive, plundered them, subdued their land, demolished their strongholds, and enslaved them to this day.
8:11 The remaining kingdoms and islands, as many as ever resisted them, they destroyed and enslaved. But with their friends and those who relied on them they maintained friendship.
8:12 They subdued kings near and far, and all who heard their name feared them.
8:13 Those whom they wish to help and to have as kings do reign; those whom they wish to remove, they remove. They have been greatly exalted.
8:14 Yet in all these things not one of them has put on a diadem or clothed himself in purple to magnify himself by it.
8:15 They made a council chamber for themselves, and every day three hundred and twenty men deliberated, continually taking counsel concerning the people so that they might be well ordered.
8:16 Each year they entrust one man to rule them and govern their whole land. All obey the one, and there is no envy or rivalry among them.
8:17 Judas chose Eupolemus son of John son of Accos, and Jason son of Eleazar, and sent them to Rome to establish friendship and an alliance
8:18 and to remove the yoke from them, because they saw the kingdom of the Greeks enslaving Israel in servitude.
8:19 They went to Rome, a very long journey, entered the council chamber, and spoke, saying,
8:20 “Judas, also called Maccabeus, his brothers, and the people of the Jews have sent us to you to establish an alliance and peace with you, and to have us enrolled as your allies and friends.”
8:21 The proposal pleased them.
8:22 This is the copy of the letter they inscribed on bronze tablets and sent to Jerusalem to remain there with them as a memorial of peace and alliance:
8:23 “May it be well with the Romans and the nation of the Jews on sea and on dry land for ever, and may sword and enemy be far from them.
8:24 If war comes first upon Rome or upon any of their allies throughout their whole dominion,
8:25 the nation of the Jews shall fight alongside them wholeheartedly, as circumstances prescribe for them.
8:26 They shall neither give nor supply grain, weapons, money, or ships to those making war, as Rome has decided. They shall keep their obligations, receiving nothing.
8:27 Likewise, if war comes first upon the nation of the Jews, the Romans shall fight alongside them with all their soul, as circumstances prescribe for them.
8:28 To those acting as allies, grain, weapons, money, and ships shall not be given, as Rome has decided. They shall keep these obligations without deceit.
8:29 On these terms the Romans thus established an agreement with the Jewish people.
8:30 If after these terms both parties decide to add or remove anything, they shall do so by their own choice; whatever they add or remove shall be binding.
8:31 Concerning the evils that King Demetrius is committing against them, we have written to him, saying, ‘Why have you made your yoke heavy upon our friends and allies, the Jews?
8:32 If, therefore, they appeal against you again, we will give judgment for them and make war on you by sea and by land.’”

1 MACCABEES 9

9:1 Demetrius heard that Nicanor and his force had fallen in battle, and again sent Bacchides and Alcimus into the land of Judah, with the right wing of the army.
9:2 They travelled the road to Gilgal, encamped against Mesaloth in Arbela, seized it, and destroyed many human lives.
9:3 In the first month of the one hundred and fifty-second year they encamped against Jerusalem.
9:4 They set out and went to Berea with twenty thousand men and two thousand cavalry.
9:5 Judas was encamped at Elasa with three thousand picked men.
9:6 They saw the multitude of the forces, how many they were, and became exceedingly afraid. Many slipped away from the camp, and only eight hundred men were left.
9:7 Judas saw that his army had slipped away and that the battle was pressing upon him. His heart was crushed, because he had no time to gather them.
9:8 He grew faint and said to those remaining, “Let us arise and go up against our adversaries, if perhaps we can fight them.”
9:9 They tried to turn him back, saying, “We certainly cannot! Let us save our lives for now. We will return with our brothers and fight them, but we are few.”
9:10 Judas said, “Far be it from us to do this thing and flee from them! If our time has drawn near, let us die courageously for our brothers and leave no charge against our glory.”
9:11 The force moved out of the camp and took position to meet them. The cavalry was divided into two parts; the slingers and archers went ahead of the force, and all the foremost fighters were mighty men. Bacchides was on the right wing.
9:12 The phalanx approached from both sides, and they sounded the trumpets. Judas’s men also sounded their trumpets.
9:13 The earth was shaken by the sound of the armies, and battle was joined from morning until evening.
9:14 Judas saw that Bacchides and the strongest part of the army were on the right. All who were courageous in heart gathered to him,
9:15 and the right wing was crushed by them. He pursued them as far as Mount Azotus.
9:16 Those on the left wing saw that the right wing had been crushed, and turned to follow on the heels of Judas and those with him from behind.
9:17 The battle grew heavy, and many fell wounded on both sides.
9:18 Judas fell, and the rest fled.
9:19 Jonathan and Simon took up Judas their brother and buried him in the tomb of his fathers in Modein.
9:20 They wept for him, and all Israel lamented him with a great lament. They mourned for many days and said,
9:21 “How has the mighty one fallen, the saviour of Israel!”
9:22 The rest of the deeds of Judas, his battles, the brave deeds he performed, and his greatness were not written down, for they were exceedingly many.
9:23 After the death of Judas, the lawless emerged throughout all Israel’s territory, and all who practiced injustice rose up.
9:24 In those days an exceedingly great famine occurred, and the country went over to their side.
9:25 Bacchides chose the ungodly men and appointed them masters of the country.
9:26 They sought out and searched for Judas’s friends and brought them to Bacchides, who punished and mocked them.
9:27 Great distress came upon Israel, such as had not occurred since the day a prophet was no longer seen among them.
9:28 All Judas’s friends gathered and said to Jonathan,
9:29 “Since your brother Judas died, there is no man like him to go out and come in against our enemies, Bacchides, and those hostile to our nation.
9:30 Now, therefore, today we have chosen you to be our ruler and leader in his place, to fight our war.”
9:31 Jonathan accepted the leadership at that time and arose in place of Judas his brother.
9:32 Bacchides learned of it and sought to kill him.
9:33 Jonathan, Simon his brother, and all who were with him learned of it and fled into the wilderness of Tekoa. They encamped by the water of the pool of Asphar.
9:34 Bacchides learned of it on the sabbath day, and he and his whole army came beyond the Jordan.
9:35 Jonathan sent his brother, a leader of the company, and appealed to his friends the Nabateans to allow their many belongings to be deposited with them.
9:36 The sons of Jambri from Medaba came out, seized John and everything he had, and went away with it.
9:37 After these events they reported to Jonathan and Simon his brother, “The sons of Jambri are holding a great wedding and bringing the bride from Nadabath, the daughter of one of the great nobles of Canaan, with a great escort.”
9:38 They remembered the blood of John their brother, went up, and hid under the cover of the mountain.
9:39 They raised their eyes and saw a commotion and much baggage. The bridegroom, his friends, and his brothers came out to meet them with tambourines, musicians, and many weapons.
9:40 They rose against them from the ambush and killed them. Many fell wounded, and the survivors fled into the mountain. They took all their spoils.
9:41 The wedding was turned into mourning, and the sound of their musicians into lamentation.
9:42 They exacted vengeance for their brother’s blood and returned to the marsh of the Jordan.
9:43 Bacchides heard and came on the sabbath day to the banks of the Jordan with a large force.
9:44 Jonathan said to his men, “Come, let us arise and fight for our lives, for today is not like yesterday or the day before.
9:45 Behold! Battle is before us and behind us, the water of the Jordan is on either side, and there are marsh and thicket. There is nowhere to turn aside.
9:46 Now, therefore, cry out to Heaven so that you may be rescued from the hand of our enemies.”

10:18 “King Alexander to his brother Jonathan, greetings.
10:19 We have heard concerning you, that you are a man mighty in strength and suitable to be our friend.

10:28 We will grant you many remissions and give you gifts.

10:34 Let all festivals, sabbaths, new moons, and appointed days, and the three days before a festival and the three after it, all be days of exemption and release for all Jews in my kingdom.
10:35 No one shall have authority to exact payment from or trouble any of them concerning any matter.
10:36 Let up to thirty thousand men of the Jews be enrolled in the king’s forces, and let allowances be given to them, as is proper for all the king’s forces.
10:37 Some of them shall be stationed in the king’s great strongholds, and some shall be appointed to positions of trust in the kingdom. Their supervisors and commanders shall be from among them, and they shall follow their own laws, as the king has also decreed in the land of Judah.

10:41 All the excess that they did not pay from the revenues as in former years they shall from now on give for the works of the house.

1 MACCABEES 13

13:1 Simon heard that Tryphon had gathered a large force to enter the land of Judah and crush it.
13:2 He saw that the people were trembling and terrified. He went up to Jerusalem and gathered the people.

13:5 Now far be it from me to spare my life in any time of distress, for I am no better than my brothers.
13:6 I will avenge my nation, the holy places, your wives, and your children, for all the nations have gathered to crush us out of hatred.”

13:8 They answered in a loud voice, saying, “You are our leader in place of Judas and Jonathan your brother.
13:9 Fight our war, and we will do everything you tell us.”
13:10 He gathered all the warriors, hastened to complete Jerusalem’s walls, and fortified it all around.
13:11 He sent Jonathan son of Absalom and a sufficient force with him to Joppa. He expelled those who were in it and remained there.
13:12 Tryphon set out from Ptolemais with a large force to enter the land of Judah, with Jonathan under guard.
13:13 Simon encamped at Adida facing the plain.
13:14 Tryphon learned that Simon had arisen in place of Jonathan his brother and was about to join battle with him. He sent envoys to him, saying,
13:15 “We are holding Jonathan your brother because of money he owed to the royal treasury for the offices he held.
13:16 Now send a hundred talents of silver and two of his sons as hostages, so that he will not revolt against us when released, and we will release him.”
13:17 Simon knew that they were speaking deceitfully to him, yet sent to fetch the money and the children, lest he incur great hostility among the people
13:18 if they said, “Because I did not send him the money and the children, he perished.”
13:19 He sent the children and the hundred talents, but Tryphon proved false and did not release Jonathan.
13:20 After this Tryphon came to invade the country and crush it. They went around by the road to Adora. Simon and his army moved opposite him wherever he went.
13:21 Those in the citadel sent envoys to Tryphon, urging him to come to them through the wilderness and send them food.
13:22 Tryphon prepared all his cavalry to go, but that night there was exceedingly heavy snow, and he did not go because of the snow. He set out and came to Gilead.
13:23 When he approached Bascama, he killed Jonathan, and Jonathan was buried there.
13:24 Tryphon turned and went away to his own land.
13:25 Simon sent and took the bones of Jonathan his brother and buried him in Modein, the city of his fathers.
13:26 All Israel lamented him with a great lament and mourned him for many days.
13:27 Simon built over the tomb of his father and brothers and raised it high to be seen, with dressed stone at the back and front.
13:28 He erected seven pyramids, one opposite another, for his father, his mother, and his four brothers.
13:29 For these he made elaborate structures, setting great columns around them. On the columns he made full suits of armour for an everlasting name, and beside the armour he carved ships, to be seen by all who sailed the sea.
13:30 This is the tomb he made in Modein, remaining to this day.
13:31 Tryphon dealt deceitfully with the young King Antiochus and killed him.
13:32 He reigned in his place, put on the diadem of Asia, and inflicted a great blow upon the land.
13:33 Simon rebuilt Judea’s strongholds, enclosed them with high towers and great walls, gates, and bars, and placed food in the strongholds.
13:34 Simon chose men and sent them to King Demetrius to secure remission for the country, because all Tryphon’s acts were plunder.
13:35 King Demetrius sent to him in these words, replying with a letter of this form:
13:36 “King Demetrius to Simon, high priest and Friend of kings, and to the elders and nation of the Jews, greetings.
13:37 We have received the golden crown and the palm branch that you sent. We are ready to make a great peace with you and to write to the officials to grant you the remissions.
13:38 Everything we have established with you stands, and the strongholds you have built shall be yours.
13:39 We remit offences of ignorance and sins up to this day, and the crown payment you owed. If any other levy was collected in Jerusalem, let it be collected no longer.
13:40 If any of you are suitable to be enrolled among those around us, let them be enrolled, and let there be peace between us.”
13:41 In the one hundred and seventieth year, the yoke of the nations was removed from Israel.
13:42 The people began to write in their documents and contracts, “In the first year of Simon, great high priest, general, and leader of the Jews.”
13:43 In those days he encamped against Gazara, surrounded it with camps, made a siege tower, brought it against the city, struck one tower, and took it.
13:44 Those in the siege tower leaped into the city, and a great commotion arose in the city.
13:45 The people of the city, with their wives and children, went up onto the wall with torn garments and cried in a loud voice, asking Simon to give them his right hand.
13:46 They said, “Do not deal with us according to our wickedness, but according to your mercy.”
13:47 Simon came to terms with them and did not fight them. He expelled them from the city and cleansed the houses in which the idols had been. Then he entered it, singing hymns and blessings.
13:48 He removed every uncleanness from it, settled in it men who would carry out the law, further fortified it, and built himself a dwelling in it.
13:49 Those in the citadel in Jerusalem were prevented from going out and coming into the country to buy and sell. They became exceedingly hungry, and many of them perished from famine.
13:50 They cried to Simon to receive a pledge of peace, and he gave it to them. He expelled them from there and cleansed the citadel from its defilements.
13:51 They entered it on the twenty-third day of the second month in the one hundred and seventy-first year, with praise and palm branches, with lyres, cymbals, harps, hymns, and songs, because a great enemy had been crushed out of Israel.
13:52 He established that this day should be celebrated every year with gladness. He further fortified the temple mountain beside the citadel, and he and his men lived there.
13:53 Simon saw that his son John was a grown man, and appointed him commander of all the forces. He lived in Gazara.

1 MACCABEES 14

14:1 In the one hundred and seventy-second year, King Demetrius gathered his forces and went to Media to obtain help for himself so that he might fight Tryphon.
14:2 Arsaces king of Persia and Media heard that Demetrius had entered his territory, and sent one of his commanders to seize him alive.
14:3 He went, struck Demetrius’s army, seized him, and brought him to Arsaces, who put him under guard.
14:4 The land of Judah was quiet all the days of Simon. He sought good things for his nation, and his authority and glory pleased them all his days.
14:5 Among all his glorious deeds he took Joppa as a harbour and made an entrance to the islands of the sea.
14:6 He enlarged the borders of his nation and gained control of the country.
14:7 He gathered many who had been captive and gained control of Gazara, Beth-zur, and the citadel. He removed its uncleannesses, and there was no one to oppose him.
14:8 They tilled their land in peace;
the land yielded its produce,
and the trees of the plains their fruit.
14:9 Elders sat in the squares, all conversing about good things; the young men clothed themselves in splendour and garments of war.
14:10 He supplied food to the cities and equipped them with means of defence, until the fame of his glory reached the end of the earth.
14:11 He made peace upon the land,
and Israel rejoiced with great gladness.
14:12 Each sat beneath his vine and his fig tree,
and none made them afraid.
14:13 Those who fought them ceased from the land, and the kings were crushed in those days.
14:14 He strengthened all the lowly of his people. He sought out the law and removed every lawless and wicked person.
14:15 He glorified the holy places and multiplied the vessels of the holy places.
14:16 It was heard in Rome, and as far as Sparta, that Jonathan had died, and they were greatly grieved.
14:17 When they heard that Simon his brother had become high priest in his place and was in control of the country and its cities,
14:18 they wrote to him on bronze tablets to renew with him the friendship and alliance they had established with Judas and Jonathan his brothers.
14:19 The letters were read before the assembly in Jerusalem.
14:20 This is the copy of the letters the Spartans sent: “The rulers of the Spartans and the city to Simon the great priest, the elders, the priests, and the rest of the Jewish people, their brothers, greetings.
14:21 The envoys sent to our people reported to us concerning your glory and honour, and we rejoiced at their arrival.
14:22 We entered what they said in the resolutions of the people as follows: ‘Numenius son of Antiochus and Antipater son of Jason, envoys of the Jews, came to us to renew friendship with us.
14:23 The people resolved to receive the men with honour and to place a copy of their words in the designated public records, so that the Spartan people might have a memorial.’” They wrote a copy of these things to Simon the high priest.
14:24 After this Simon sent Numenius to Rome carrying a great golden shield weighing a thousand minas to establish the alliance with them.
14:25 When the people heard these things, they said, “What thanks shall we return to Simon and his sons?
14:26 For he, his brothers, and his father’s house stood firm, fought off Israel’s enemies, and established freedom for Israel.” They inscribed it on bronze tablets and placed them on pillars on Mount Zion.
14:27 This is the copy of the inscription: “On the eighteenth of Elul, in the one hundred and seventy-second year, which is the third year under Simon the great high priest, in Asaramel,
14:28 in a great assembly of priests, people, rulers of the nation, and elders of the country, a declaration was made to us:
14:29 ‘Since wars have often occurred in the country, Simon son of Mattathias, a priest of the sons of Joarib, and his brothers exposed themselves to danger and resisted their nation’s adversaries, so that their holy places and the law might stand. They honoured their nation with great glory.
14:30 Jonathan gathered their nation, became their high priest, and was gathered to his people.
14:31 Their enemies wished to invade their country and stretch out their hands against their holy places.
14:32 Then Simon resisted and fought for his nation. He spent much of his own money, supplied the men of his nation’s force with weapons, and gave them pay.
14:33 He fortified the cities of Judea and Beth-zur on Judea’s border, where the enemies’ weapons had formerly been, and stationed Jewish men there as a garrison.
14:34 He fortified Joppa by the sea and Gazara on the borders of Azotus, where the enemies had formerly lived. He settled Jews there and provided everything necessary for their restoration.
14:35 The people saw Simon’s faithfulness and the glory he had resolved to bring to his nation. They made him their leader and high priest because he had done all these things, because of the righteousness and faithfulness he maintained toward his nation, and because he sought in every way to exalt his people.
14:36 In his days it prospered in his hands to remove the nations from their country, including those in the city of David at Jerusalem who had made themselves a citadel. From it they used to go out and defile the surroundings of the holy places, inflicting a great blow upon purity.
14:37 He settled Jewish men in it, fortified it for the security of the country and city, and raised Jerusalem’s walls.
14:38 King Demetrius accordingly confirmed him in the high priesthood,
14:39 made him one of his Friends, and honoured him with great glory.
14:40 For he had heard that the Jews had been addressed by the Romans as friends, allies, and brothers, and that they had received Simon’s envoys with honour;
14:41 and that the Jews and priests had agreed that Simon should be their leader and high priest for ever, until a faithful prophet should arise;
14:42 that he should be their general, and take care of the holy places, appointing through his authority those in charge of their works, the country, the weapons, and the strongholds;
14:43 that he should take care of the holy places, be obeyed by everyone, have all documents in the country written in his name, and wear purple and gold ornaments;
14:44 and that no one among the people or priests should have authority to annul any of these things, contradict what he should say, convene a gathering in the country without him, wear purple, or fasten on a golden clasp.
14:45 Whoever acts contrary to these provisions or annuls any of them shall be liable.’
14:46 All the people agreed to establish Simon to act according to these terms.
14:47 Simon accepted and agreed to serve as high priest, to be general and ethnarch of the Jews and priests, and to preside over them all.
14:48 They directed that this inscription be placed on bronze tablets and that these be set up within the enclosure of the holy places in a conspicuous place,
14:49 and that copies be placed in the treasury so that Simon and his sons might possess them.”

1 MACCABEES 15

15:1 Antiochus son of King Demetrius sent letters from the islands of the sea to Simon, priest and ethnarch of the Jews, and to the whole nation.
15:2 They contained the following: “King Antiochus to Simon the great priest and ethnarch, and to the nation of the Jews, greetings.
15:3 Since certain pestilent men have seized the kingdom of our fathers, I wish to reclaim the kingdom and restore it as it was before. I have recruited a multitude of forces and prepared warships.
15:4 I intend to land in the country to pursue those who have ruined our country and devastated many cities in my kingdom.
15:5 Now, therefore, I confirm to you all the remissions that the kings before me granted you, and all the other gifts they granted you.
15:6 I permit you to strike your own coinage as money for your country.
15:7 Jerusalem and the holy places shall be free. All the weapons you have prepared and the strongholds you have built and hold shall remain yours.
15:8 Every royal debt and all future royal dues shall be remitted to you from now and for all time.
15:9 When we gain control of our kingdom, we will honour you, your nation, and the temple with great glory, so that your glory may become evident throughout the whole earth.”
15:10 In the one hundred and seventy-fourth year Antiochus went out into the land of his fathers. All the forces joined him, so that only a few remained with Tryphon.
15:11 Antiochus pursued him, and he fled to Dora by the sea,
15:12 for he knew that evils had gathered against him and that the forces had abandoned him.
15:13 Antiochus encamped against Dora, with a hundred and twenty thousand warriors and eight thousand cavalry.
15:14 He surrounded the city, and the ships joined the attack from the sea. He pressed the city from land and sea and allowed no one to go out or come in.
15:15 Numenius and his companions came from Rome carrying letters to the kings and countries, in which the following was written:
15:16 “Lucius, consul of the Romans, to King Ptolemy, greetings.
15:17 The envoys of the Jews, our friends and allies, came to us to renew the friendship and alliance established from the beginning, sent by Simon the high priest and the Jewish people.
15:18 They brought a golden shield of a thousand minas.
15:19 We therefore resolved to write to the kings and countries that they should not seek their harm or make war on them, their cities, or their country, and should not ally themselves with those making war against them.
15:20 We decided to accept the shield from them.
15:21 If, therefore, any pestilent men have escaped from their country to you, hand them over to Simon the high priest, so that he may punish them according to their law.”
15:22 He wrote the same things to King Demetrius, Attalus, Ariarathes, and Arsaces,
15:23 and to all the countries: to Sampsame, the Spartans, Delos, Myndos, Sicyon, Caria, Samos, Pamphylia, Lycia, Halicarnassus, Rhodes, Phaselis, Cos, Side, Aradus, Gortyna, Cnidus, Cyprus, and Cyrene.
15:24 They wrote a copy of these things to Simon the high priest.
15:25 On the second day King Antiochus encamped against Dora, continually bringing his forces against it, making siege engines, and blocking Tryphon from going out or coming in.
15:26 Simon sent him two thousand picked men to fight alongside him, along with silver, gold, and ample equipment.
15:27 He would not receive them, but annulled everything he had previously agreed with Simon and became estranged from him.
15:28 He sent Athenobius, one of his Friends, to confer with him, saying, “You hold Joppa, Gazara, and the citadel in Jerusalem, cities of my kingdom.
15:29 You have devastated their territories, inflicted a great blow upon the land, and gained control of many places in my kingdom.
15:30 Now, therefore, hand over the cities you have seized and the tribute of the places you have taken outside the borders of Judea.
15:31 Otherwise, give five hundred talents of silver in their place, and another five hundred talents for the destruction you have caused and the tribute of the cities. If not, we will come and make war on you.”
15:32 Athenobius, the king’s Friend, came to Jerusalem. He saw Simon’s glory, the display of gold and silver vessels, and the large retinue, and was astonished. He reported the king’s words to him.
15:33 Simon answered him, “We have neither taken foreign land nor gained control of others’ property, but the inheritance of our fathers, which our enemies unjustly seized at one time.
15:34 Now that we have the opportunity, we are holding fast to the inheritance of our fathers.
15:35 As for Joppa and Gazara, which you demand, these caused a great blow among the people and in our country. For these we will give a hundred talents.”
15:36 He did not answer him a word, but returned angrily to the king and reported these words, Simon’s glory, and everything he had seen. The king became greatly angry.
15:37 Tryphon boarded a ship and fled to Orthosia.
15:38 The king appointed Cendebaeus chief commander of the coast and gave him infantry and cavalry forces.
15:39 He commanded him to encamp opposite Judea, rebuild Kedron, fortify its gates, and fight the people. The king himself pursued Tryphon.
15:40 Cendebaeus came to Jamnia and began provoking the people, invading Judea, taking the people captive, and killing them.
15:41 He rebuilt Kedron and stationed cavalry and forces there, so that they might go out and make sorties along the roads of Judea, as the king had ordered him.

1 MACCABEES 16

16:1 John came up from Gazara and reported to Simon his father what Cendebaeus had done.
16:2 Simon called his two elder sons, Judas and John, and said to them, “I, my brothers, and my father’s house have fought Israel’s wars from youth until this day, and it has prospered in our hands to rescue Israel many times.
16:3 Now I have grown old, but through mercy you are mature in years. Take my place and my brother’s, go out and fight for our nation, and may help from Heaven be with you.”
16:4 He chose from the country twenty thousand warriors and cavalry, and they marched against Cendebaeus and lodged in Modein.
16:5 They arose in the morning and went into the plain. A large force of infantry and cavalry came to meet them, with a stream between them.
16:6 He and his people encamped opposite them. He saw that the people were afraid to cross the stream, and he crossed first. The men saw him and crossed behind him.
16:7 He divided the people, placing the cavalry among the infantry, for the adversaries had very many cavalry.
16:8 They sounded the trumpets, and Cendebaeus and his army were routed. Many of them fell wounded, and the survivors fled into the stronghold.
16:9 Then Judas, John’s brother, was wounded. John pursued them until he came to Kedron, which Cendebaeus had built.
16:10 They fled into the towers in the countryside of Azotus, and he burned it with fire. About two thousand of their men fell. He returned to Judea in peace.
16:11 Ptolemy son of Abubus had been appointed commander over the plain of Jericho and possessed much silver and gold,
16:12 for he was the high priest’s son-in-law.
16:13 His heart was lifted up, and he wished to gain control of the country. He plotted deceitfully against Simon and his sons to remove them.
16:14 Simon was visiting the cities in the country and attending to their care. He came down to Jericho with his sons Mattathias and Judas in the one hundred and seventy-seventh year, in the eleventh month, which is the month of Shebat.
16:15 The son of Abubus deceitfully received them in the small stronghold called Dok, which he had built. He made a great drinking feast for them and hid men there.
16:16 When Simon and his sons had become drunk, Ptolemy and his men arose, took their weapons, entered the banquet room against Simon, and killed him, his two sons, and some of his attendants.
16:17 He committed a great act of treachery and repaid evil for good.
16:18 Ptolemy wrote these things and sent them to the king, asking him to send forces to help him and hand over their country and cities.
16:19 He sent others to Gazara to remove John, and sent letters to the commanders of thousands to come to him so that he might give them silver, gold, and gifts.
16:20 He sent others to seize Jerusalem and the temple mountain.
16:21 Someone ran ahead and reported to John at Gazara that his father and brothers had perished, and said, “He has also sent men to kill you.”
16:22 When he heard, he was greatly astonished. He seized the men who had come to destroy him and killed them, for he had learned that they were seeking to destroy him.
16:23 The rest of John’s deeds, his wars, the brave acts he performed, the building of the walls he built, and his actions—
16:24 behold, these are written in the book of the days of his high priesthood, from the time he became high priest after his father.
