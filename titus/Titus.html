Titus 1

Read this chapter on staging

Greeting

1:1Paul, a slave of God and an apostle of Jesus Christ, for the faith of God’s chosen ones and the knowledge of the truth that accords with godliness,

1:2in hope of eternal life, which God, who does not lie, promised before the ages,

1:3and in His own appointed times He revealed His word through proclamation, with which I was entrusted by the command of God our Savior,

1:4to Titus, a genuine child in a shared faith: grace and peace from God the Father and Christ Jesus our Savior.

Elders Appointed in Crete

1:5For this reason I left you in Crete, so that you might put in order what remained unfinished and appoint elders in every city, as I directed you—

1:6if anyone is beyond reproach, the husband of one wife, having faithful children who are not accused of reckless living or insubordinate.

1:7For the overseer must be beyond reproach as God’s steward, not self-willed, not quick-tempered, not given to wine, not violent, not greedy for shameful gain,

1:8but hospitable, loving what is good, self-controlled, just, devout, disciplined,

1:9holding firmly to the trustworthy word in accordance with the teaching, so that he may be able both to encourage through sound teaching and to refute those who contradict it.

Rebuking the Insubordinate

1:10For there are also many insubordinate people, empty talkers and deceivers, especially those of the circumcision,

1:11whose mouths must be stopped. They overturn whole households, teaching what they ought not to teach for the sake of shameful gain.

1:12One of their own, a prophet of theirs, said, “Cretans are always liars, evil beasts, idle bellies.”

1:13This testimony is true. For that reason, rebuke them sharply, so that they may be sound in the faith,

1:14not paying attention to Jewish myths and commands of people who turn away from the truth.

1:15To the pure, everything is pure; but to those who are defiled and unbelieving, nothing is pure. Instead, both their mind and their conscience are defiled.

1:16They profess to know God, but by their deeds they deny Him, being detestable and disobedient and unfit for every good work.

Titus 2

Read this chapter on staging

Sound Teaching for Every Age

2:1But you, speak what is fitting for sound teaching.

2:2Older men are to be sober, dignified, self-controlled, sound in faith, in love, and in endurance.

2:3Older women likewise are to be reverent in their conduct, not slanderers or enslaved to much wine, teachers of what is good,

2:4so that they may train the younger women to love their husbands, to love their children,

2:5to be self-controlled, pure, working at home, good, subject to their own husbands, so that God’s word may not be reviled.

2:6Likewise, encourage the younger men to be self-controlled

2:7in everything. Present yourself as a model of good works, showing integrity in teaching, dignity,

2:8and sound speech that cannot be condemned, so that the opponent may be put to shame, having nothing bad to say about us.

2:9Slaves are to be subject to their own masters in everything, to be pleasing to them, not contradicting them,

2:10not pilfering, but showing complete good faith, so that in everything they may adorn the teaching of God our Savior.

The Grace of God Has Appeared

2:11For the grace of God has appeared, bringing salvation for all people,

2:12training us so that, denying ungodliness and worldly desires, we may live self-controlled, just, and godly lives in the present age,

2:13awaiting the blessed hope and the appearing of the glory of our great God and Savior Jesus Christ,

2:14who gave Himself for us, so that He might ransom us from all lawlessness and purify for Himself a people for His own possession, eager for good works.

2:15Speak these things, encourage, and rebuke with all authority. Let no one disregard you.

Titus 2:11 · saving

Meaning. Grace characterized as saving in its appearing.

Decision. Retain the saving adjective and all people. Connecting for all with saving is the chosen syntax; note the alternative attachment to appeared. Do not replace the adjective with the title Saviour.

Comparison. Saints bringing salvation for all; KJV bringeth salvation and appeared to all; DRC God our Saviour.

Excluded. a universal outcome asserted solely from the adjective

Titus 3

Read this chapter on staging

Ready for Every Good Work

3:1Remind them to be subject to rulers and authorities, to obey, and to be ready for every good work,

3:2to slander no one, to be peaceable, considerate, showing complete gentleness toward all people.

3:3For we too were once foolish, disobedient, led astray, enslaved to various desires and pleasures, living in malice and envy, hateful, hating one another.

3:4But when the kindness of God our Savior and His love for humanity appeared,

3:5He saved us, not on the basis of works done by us in righteousness, but according to His mercy, through the washing of regeneration and renewal by the Holy Spirit,

3:6whom He poured out richly upon us through Jesus Christ our Savior,

3:7so that, justified by His grace, we might become heirs in accordance with the hope of eternal life.

Avoiding Foolish Controversies

3:8The saying is trustworthy, and I want you to insist on these things, so that those who have believed God may take care to devote themselves to good works. These things are good and beneficial to people.

3:9But avoid foolish controversies, genealogies, disputes, and quarrels about the law, for they are unprofitable and futile.

3:10After a first and a second warning, have nothing further to do with a divisive person,

3:11knowing that such a person has become perverted and is sinning, being self-condemned.

Final Instructions and Greetings

3:12When I send Artemas or Tychicus to you, make every effort to come to me at Nicopolis, for I have decided to spend the winter there.

3:13Make every effort to send Zenas the legal expert and Apollos on their way, so that they lack nothing.

3:14Let our people also learn to devote themselves to good works to meet essential needs, so that they may not be unfruitful.

3:15All those with me greet you. Greet those who love us in faith. Grace be with all of you.

Titus 3:5 · washing of regeneration

Meaning. Washing associated with new birth and renewal.

Decision. Retain mercy, the denial of our works as the basis, washing, regeneration, and the Holy Spirit. Sacramental interpretation belongs in commentary without replacing the expressed washing.

Comparison. Saints and KJV washing; DRC laver. All retain regeneration.

Excluded. washing erased or a sacramental mechanism added
