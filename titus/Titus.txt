TITUS 1

1:1 Paul, a slave of God and an apostle of Jesus Christ, for the faith of God’s chosen ones and the knowledge of the truth that accords with godliness,
1:2 in hope of eternal life, which God, who does not lie, promised before the ages,
1:3 and in His own appointed times He revealed His word through proclamation, with which I was entrusted by the command of God our Savior,
1:4 to Titus, a genuine child in a shared faith: grace and peace from God the Father and Christ Jesus our Savior.
1:5 For this reason I left you in Crete, so that you might put in order what remained unfinished and appoint elders in every city, as I directed you—
1:6 if anyone is beyond reproach, the husband of one wife, having faithful children who are not accused of reckless living or insubordinate.
1:7 For the overseer must be beyond reproach as God’s steward, not self-willed, not quick-tempered, not given to wine, not violent, not greedy for shameful gain,
1:8 but hospitable, loving what is good, self-controlled, just, devout, disciplined,
1:9 holding firmly to the trustworthy word in accordance with the teaching, so that he may be able both to encourage through sound teaching and to refute those who contradict it.
1:10 For there are also many insubordinate people, empty talkers and deceivers, especially those of the circumcision,
1:11 whose mouths must be stopped. They overturn whole households, teaching what they ought not to teach for the sake of shameful gain.
1:12 One of their own, a prophet of theirs, said, “Cretans are always liars, evil beasts, idle bellies.”
1:13 This testimony is true. For that reason, rebuke them sharply, so that they may be sound in the faith,
1:14 not paying attention to Jewish myths and commands of people who turn away from the truth.
1:15 To the pure, everything is pure; but to those who are defiled and unbelieving, nothing is pure. Instead, both their mind and their conscience are defiled.
1:16 They profess to know God, but by their deeds they deny Him, being detestable and disobedient and unfit for every good work.

TITUS 2

2:1 But you, speak what is fitting for sound teaching.
2:2 Older men are to be sober, dignified, self-controlled, sound in faith, in love, and in endurance.
2:3 Older women likewise are to be reverent in their conduct, not slanderers or enslaved to much wine, teachers of what is good,
2:4 so that they may train the younger women to love their husbands, to love their children,
2:5 to be self-controlled, pure, working at home, good, subject to their own husbands, so that God’s word may not be reviled.
2:6 Likewise, encourage the younger men to be self-controlled
2:7 in everything. Present yourself as a model of good works, showing integrity in teaching, dignity,
2:8 and sound speech that cannot be condemned, so that the opponent may be put to shame, having nothing bad to say about us.
2:9 Slaves are to be subject to their own masters in everything, to be pleasing to them, not contradicting them,
2:10 not pilfering, but showing complete good faith, so that in everything they may adorn the teaching of God our Savior.
2:11 For the grace of God has appeared, bringing salvation for all people,
2:12 training us so that, denying ungodliness and worldly desires, we may live self-controlled, just, and godly lives in the present age,
2:13 awaiting the blessed hope and the appearing of the glory of our great God and Savior Jesus Christ,
2:14 who gave Himself for us, so that He might ransom us from all lawlessness and purify for Himself a people for His own possession, eager for good works.
2:15 Speak these things, encourage, and rebuke with all authority. Let no one disregard you.

TITUS 3

3:1 Remind them to be subject to rulers and authorities, to obey, and to be ready for every good work,
3:2 to slander no one, to be peaceable, considerate, showing complete gentleness toward all people.
3:3 For we too were once foolish, disobedient, led astray, enslaved to various desires and pleasures, living in malice and envy, hateful, hating one another.
3:4 But when the kindness of God our Savior and His love for humanity appeared,
3:5 He saved us, not on the basis of works done by us in righteousness, but according to His mercy, through the washing of regeneration and renewal by the Holy Spirit,
3:6 whom He poured out richly upon us through Jesus Christ our Savior,
3:7 so that, justified by His grace, we might become heirs in accordance with the hope of eternal life.
3:8 The saying is trustworthy, and I want you to insist on these things, so that those who have believed God may take care to devote themselves to good works. These things are good and beneficial to people.
3:9 But avoid foolish controversies, genealogies, disputes, and quarrels about the law, for they are unprofitable and futile.
3:10 After a first and a second warning, have nothing further to do with a divisive person,
3:11 knowing that such a person has become perverted and is sinning, being self-condemned.
3:12 When I send Artemas or Tychicus to you, make every effort to come to me at Nicopolis, for I have decided to spend the winter there.
3:13 Make every effort to send Zenas the legal expert and Apollos on their way, so that they lack nothing.
3:14 Let our people also learn to devote themselves to good works to meet essential needs, so that they may not be unfruitful.
3:15 All those with me greet you. Greet those who love us in faith. Grace be with all of you.
