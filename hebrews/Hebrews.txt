NA28 is the user-authorized provisional New Testament base. Focused checks of the German Bible Society’s Greek running text are recorded by chapter. This is an English editorial revision, not complete critical-text or apparatus verification. The newer Bespoke PDF’s preferred UBS6 text has not been obtained; UBS6/ECM comparison, full apparatus, independent Greek and human read-aloud reviews remain pending.

HEBREWS 1

1:1 In many portions and in many ways, God spoke long ago to the fathers in the prophets,
1:2 but in these last days He spoke to us in a Son, whom He appointed heir of all things, through whom He also made the ages.
1:3 He is the radiance of His glory and the imprint of His being, upholding all things by the word of His power. Having made purification of sins, He sat down at the right hand of the Majesty on high,
1:4 having become so much greater than the angels as the name He has inherited is more excellent than theirs.
1:5 For to which of the angels did He ever say, “Thou art My Son; today I have begotten Thee”? And again, “I will be a Father to Him, and He will be a Son to Me”?
1:6 And when He again brings the firstborn into the inhabited world, He says, “And let all God’s angels worship Him.”
1:7 And concerning the angels He says, “He makes His angels spirits, and His ministers a flame of fire.”
1:8 But concerning the Son, “Thy throne, O God, is for the age of the age, and the scepter of uprightness is the scepter of Thy kingdom.
1:9 Thou didst love righteousness and hate lawlessness; for this reason God, Thy God, anointed Thee with the oil of exultation beyond Thy companions.”
1:10 And, “Thou, Lord, didst lay the foundation of the earth in the beginning, and the heavens are the works of Thy hands.
1:11 They will perish, but Thou remainest; and all will grow old like a garment,
1:12 and like a cloak Thou wilt roll them up; like a garment they will also be changed. But Thou art the same, and Thy years will not fail.”
1:13 But to which of the angels has He ever said, “Sit at My right hand until I make Thine enemies a footstool for Thy feet”?
1:14 Are they not all ministering spirits, sent out for service for the sake of those who are to inherit salvation?

HEBREWS 2

2:1 For this reason we must pay all the more attention to what we have heard, so that we do not drift away.
2:2 For if the word spoken through angels became firm, and every transgression and disobedience received a just recompense,
2:3 how will we escape if we neglect so great a salvation? It was first spoken through the Lord and was confirmed to us by those who heard,
2:4 God bearing witness together with signs and wonders and various mighty deeds and distributions of the Holy Spirit according to His own will.
2:5 For He did not subject to angels the inhabited world to come, of which we speak.
2:6 But someone testified somewhere, saying, “What is a human being, that Thou rememberest him, or a son of man, that Thou carest for him?
2:7 Thou didst make him a little lower than angels; Thou didst crown him with glory and honor;
2:8 Thou didst subject all things beneath his feet.” For in subjecting all things to him, He left nothing unsubjected to him. But now we do not yet see all things subjected to him.
2:9 But we see Jesus, who was made a little lower than angels, crowned with glory and honor because of the suffering of death, so that by the grace of God He might taste death on behalf of everyone.
2:10 For it was fitting for Him, for whom are all things and through whom are all things, in bringing many sons into glory, to perfect the pioneer of their salvation through sufferings.
2:11 For both the One who sanctifies and those being sanctified are all from one. For this reason He is not ashamed to call them brothers,
2:12 saying, “I will announce Thy name to My brothers; in the midst of the assembly I will sing praise to Thee.”
2:13 And again, “I will put My trust in Him.” And again, “Behold, I and the children whom God gave Me.”
2:14 Therefore, since the children have shared in blood and flesh, He Himself likewise partook of the same, so that through death He might render powerless the one who has the power of death, that is, the devil,
2:15 and release those who through fear of death were subject to slavery throughout their whole lives.
2:16 For surely it is not angels He takes hold of, but He takes hold of Abraham’s seed.
2:17 Therefore He had to be made like His brothers in all things, so that He might become a merciful and faithful high priest in matters concerning God, to make propitiation for the sins of the people.
2:18 For because He Himself suffered when tested, He is able to help those who are being tested.

HEBREWS 3

3:1 Therefore, holy brothers, partakers of a heavenly calling, consider the apostle and high priest of our confession, Jesus,
3:2 who is faithful to the One who appointed Him, as Moses also was in all His house.
3:3 For He has been considered worthy of greater glory than Moses, just as the one who builds a house has greater honor than the house.
3:4 For every house is built by someone, but the One who built all things is God.
3:5 And Moses indeed was faithful in all His house as an attendant, for a testimony of the things to be spoken,
3:6 but Christ as a Son over His house. We are that house if indeed we hold fast our confidence and the boast of our hope.
3:7 Therefore, just as the Holy Spirit says, “Today, if you hear His voice,
3:8 do not harden your hearts as in the provocation, on the day of testing in the wilderness,
3:9 where your fathers tested Me with a trial and saw My works
3:10 for forty years. Therefore I was indignant with this generation and said, ‘They always wander in their heart, and they did not know My ways,’
3:11 as I swore in My wrath, ‘They shall certainly not enter My rest.’”
3:12 Watch out, brothers, so that none of you has an evil, unbelieving heart that turns away from the living God.
3:13 But encourage one another each day, as long as it is called “Today,” so that none of you may be hardened by the deceit of sin.
3:14 For we have become partakers of Christ if indeed we hold firm the beginning of our confidence until the end,
3:15 while it is said, “Today, if you hear His voice, do not harden your hearts as in the provocation.”
3:16 For who, having heard, provoked Him? Was it not all who came out of Egypt through Moses?
3:17 And with whom was He indignant for forty years? Was it not with those who sinned, whose bodies fell in the wilderness?
3:18 And to whom did He swear that they would not enter His rest, except those who disobeyed?
3:19 And we see that they could not enter because of unbelief.

HEBREWS 4

4:1 Therefore let us fear, lest, while a promise remains of entering His rest, any of you should seem to have fallen short.
4:2 For we too have received good news just as they did, but the word they heard did not benefit them, because they had not been united by faith with those who heard.
4:3 For we who believed enter the rest, just as He has said, “As I swore in My wrath, ‘They shall certainly not enter My rest,’” although the works came into being from the foundation of the world.
4:4 For He has said somewhere about the seventh day in this way, “And God rested on the seventh day from all His works.”
4:5 And in this passage again, “They shall certainly not enter My rest.”
4:6 Since, then, it remains for some to enter it, and those who formerly received the good news did not enter because of disobedience,
4:7 He again appoints a certain day, “Today,” saying in David after so long a time, just as has been said before, “Today, if you hear His voice, do not harden your hearts.”
4:8 For if Joshua had given them rest, He would not have spoken after these things about another day.
4:9 So then, a Sabbath rest remains for the people of God.
4:10 For the one who entered His rest also rested from his own works, just as God did from His own.
4:11 Therefore let us be diligent to enter that rest, so that no one may fall in the same pattern of disobedience.
4:12 For the word of God is living and active and sharper than any two-edged sword, penetrating as far as the division of soul and spirit, of joints and marrow, and able to judge the thoughts and intentions of the heart.
4:13 And no created thing is hidden before Him, but all things are naked and laid open to His eyes, to whom we must give account.
4:14 Therefore, having a great high priest who has passed through the heavens, Jesus the Son of God, let us hold fast the confession.
4:15 For we do not have a high priest unable to sympathize with our weaknesses, but One who has been tested in every way as we are, yet without sin.
4:16 Therefore let us approach the throne of grace with boldness, so that we may receive mercy and find grace for help at the right time.

HEBREWS 5

5:1 For every high priest taken from among people is appointed on behalf of people in matters concerning God, to offer both gifts and sacrifices for sins.
5:2 He can deal gently with those who are ignorant and wandering, since he too is surrounded by weakness,
5:3 and because of it he is obliged to make offerings for sins on his own behalf, just as on behalf of the people.
5:4 And no one takes the honor for himself, but receives it when called by God, just as Aaron was.
5:5 So also Christ did not glorify Himself to become high priest, but the One who said to Him, “Thou art My Son; today I have begotten Thee,”
5:6 as He also says in another place, “Thou art a priest for the age, according to the order of Melchizedek.”
5:7 In the days of His flesh, He offered petitions and supplications, with loud crying and tears, to the One able to save Him out of death, and was heard because of His reverent fear.
5:8 Although He was a Son, He learned obedience from the things He suffered,
5:9 and, having been perfected, He became the source of eternal salvation for all who obey Him,
5:10 having been designated by God high priest according to the order of Melchizedek.
5:11 Concerning this we have much to say, and it is hard to explain, since you have become slow to hear.
5:12 For although by this time you ought to be teachers, you again need someone to teach you the first elements of God’s oracles; you have come to need milk and not solid food.
5:13 For everyone who partakes of milk is inexperienced in the word of righteousness, for he is an infant.
5:14 But solid food belongs to the mature, whose faculties have been trained by practice to distinguish both good and evil.

HEBREWS 6

6:1 Therefore, leaving the elementary teaching about Christ, let us be carried on to maturity, without laying again a foundation of repentance from dead works and faith toward God,
6:2 of instruction about washings and the laying on of hands, the resurrection of the dead, and eternal judgment.
6:3 And this we will do, if indeed God permits.
6:4 For it is impossible to renew again to repentance those who were once enlightened, tasted the heavenly gift, became partakers of the Holy Spirit,
6:5 and tasted God’s good word and the powers of the coming age,
6:6 and fell away, since they are crucifying the Son of God again for themselves and exposing Him to public disgrace.
6:7 For land that drinks the rain often coming upon it, and produces vegetation useful to those for whose sake it is cultivated, shares in blessing from God.
6:8 But if it bears thorns and thistles, it is rejected and near a curse; its end is burning.
6:9 But concerning you, beloved, we are convinced of better things—things accompanying salvation—even though we speak this way.
6:10 For God is not unjust so as to forget your work and the love you showed toward His name by serving the saints, as you still serve them.
6:11 And we desire each of you to show the same diligence toward the full assurance of hope until the end,
6:12 so that you may not become sluggish, but imitators of those who through faith and patience inherit the promises.
6:13 For when God made a promise to Abraham, since He had no one greater by whom to swear, He swore by Himself,
6:14 saying, “Surely, blessing I will bless you, and multiplying I will multiply you.”
6:15 And so, having waited patiently, he obtained the promise.
6:16 For people swear by one greater, and among them an oath for confirmation ends every dispute.
6:17 In this, God, wishing to show more fully to the heirs of the promise the unchanging nature of His purpose, intervened with an oath,
6:18 so that through two unchangeable things, in which it is impossible for God to lie, we who have fled for refuge might have strong encouragement to hold fast the hope set before us.
6:19 We have this hope as an anchor of the soul, both secure and firm, entering the inner place behind the veil,
6:20 where Jesus entered as a forerunner on our behalf, having become high priest for the age according to the order of Melchizedek.

HEBREWS 7

7:1 For this Melchizedek, king of Salem, priest of God Most High, met Abraham returning from the slaughter of the kings and blessed him.
7:2 Abraham also apportioned to him a tenth of everything. His name is interpreted first as king of righteousness, and then also king of Salem, which is king of peace.
7:3 Without father, without mother, without genealogy, having neither a beginning of days nor an end of life, but made like the Son of God, he remains a priest continually.
7:4 Consider how great this man was, to whom even Abraham the patriarch gave a tenth from the choice spoils.
7:5 Those from the sons of Levi who receive the priestly office have a commandment, according to the law, to collect a tenth from the people—that is, from their brothers—although they too have come from Abraham’s loins.
7:6 But the one whose genealogy is not traced from them has received a tenth from Abraham and has blessed the one who had the promises.
7:7 And beyond all dispute, the lesser is blessed by the greater.
7:8 Here, people who die receive tenths; but there, one of whom it is testified that he lives.
7:9 And, so to speak, through Abraham even Levi, who receives tenths, has paid a tenth,
7:10 for he was still in his father’s loins when Melchizedek met him.
7:11 Now if perfection came through the Levitical priesthood—for on its basis the people received the law—what further need was there for another priest to arise according to the order of Melchizedek, and not to be spoken of according to the order of Aaron?
7:12 For when the priesthood is changed, a change of law necessarily takes place as well.
7:13 For the One concerning whom these things are said belongs to another tribe, from which no one has served at the altar.
7:14 For it is clear that our Lord has sprung from Judah, a tribe about which Moses said nothing concerning priests.
7:15 And it is still more evident if another priest arises according to the likeness of Melchizedek,
7:16 who has become a priest, not according to a law of a fleshly commandment, but according to the power of an indestructible life.
7:17 For it is testified, “Thou art a priest for the age, according to the order of Melchizedek.”
7:18 For there is a setting aside of the preceding commandment because of its weakness and uselessness,
7:19 for the law perfected nothing; and there is the introduction of a better hope, through which we draw near to God.
7:20 And insofar as this was not without an oath—for those became priests without an oath,
7:21 but He with an oath through the One who said to Him, “The Lord swore and will not change His mind: Thou art a priest for the age”—
7:22 to that extent Jesus has also become the guarantor of a better covenant.
7:23 And there were many of those priests, because death prevented them from continuing,
7:24 but He, because He remains for the age, has a permanent priesthood.
7:25 Therefore He is also able to save completely those who approach God through Him, since He always lives to intercede on their behalf.
7:26 For such a high priest was indeed fitting for us: holy, innocent, undefiled, separated from sinners, and having become higher than the heavens.
7:27 He has no daily need, as the high priests do, to offer sacrifices first for His own sins and then for those of the people; for He did this once for all when He offered Himself.
7:28 For the law appoints people subject to weakness as high priests, but the word of the oath, which came after the law, appoints a Son, perfected for the age.

HEBREWS 8

8:1 Now the main point in what is being said is this: we have such a high priest, who sat down at the right hand of the throne of the Majesty in the heavens,
8:2 a minister of the holy places and of the true tent, which the Lord pitched, not a human being.
8:3 For every high priest is appointed to offer both gifts and sacrifices; therefore this One too must have something to offer.
8:4 Now if He were on earth, He would not even be a priest, since there are those who offer the gifts according to law.
8:5 They serve a copy and shadow of the heavenly things, just as Moses was instructed when he was about to complete the tent. For He says, “See that you make everything according to the pattern shown to you on the mountain.”
8:6 But now He has obtained a more excellent ministry, insofar as He is also mediator of a better covenant, which has been established as law upon better promises.
8:7 For if that first covenant had been faultless, no place would have been sought for a second.
8:8 For, finding fault with them, He says, “Look, days are coming, says the Lord, and I will conclude a new covenant with the house of Israel and with the house of Judah,
8:9 not according to the covenant I made with their fathers on the day I took hold of their hand to lead them out of the land of Egypt, because they did not remain in My covenant, and I disregarded them, says the Lord.
8:10 For this is the covenant I will make with the house of Israel after those days, says the Lord: putting My laws into their mind, I will also inscribe them upon their hearts, and I will be their God, and they will be My people.
8:11 And they shall certainly not teach, each his fellow citizen and each his brother, saying, ‘Know the Lord,’ because all will know Me, from the least of them to the greatest.
8:12 For I will be merciful toward their wrongdoings, and I will certainly remember their sins no more.”
8:13 By saying “new,” He has made the first old. And what is growing old and aging is close to disappearing.

HEBREWS 9

9:1 Now the first covenant also had regulations for worship and an earthly sanctuary.
9:2 For a tent was prepared, the first, in which were the lampstand, the table, and the presentation of the loaves; it is called the Holy Place.
9:3 And behind the second curtain was a tent called the Holy of Holies,
9:4 having a golden incense altar and the ark of the covenant, covered on every side with gold. In it were a golden jar containing the manna, Aaron’s rod that had sprouted, and the tablets of the covenant.
9:5 Above it were cherubim of glory overshadowing the atonement cover. We cannot now speak of these things in detail.
9:6 With these things so arranged, the priests enter the first tent continually, carrying out the acts of worship.
9:7 But into the second only the high priest enters, once in the year, not without blood, which he offers for himself and for the people’s sins of ignorance.
9:8 By this the Holy Spirit indicates that the way into the holy places has not yet been disclosed while the first tent still has its standing.
9:9 This is a figure for the present time, according to which both gifts and sacrifices are offered that cannot perfect the worshiper with respect to conscience.
9:10 They concern only foods, drinks, and various washings: regulations for the flesh, imposed until a time of setting things right.
9:11 But Christ came as high priest of the good things that have come to be, through the greater and more perfect tent, not made by hands—that is, not of this creation—
9:12 and entered the holy places once for all, not through the blood of goats and calves but through His own blood, having obtained eternal redemption.
9:13 For if the blood of goats and bulls, and the ashes of a heifer sprinkling those who have been defiled, sanctify for the cleanness of the flesh,
9:14 how much more will the blood of Christ, who through an eternal Spirit offered Himself unblemished to God, cleanse our conscience from dead works to serve the living God!
9:15 And for this reason He is mediator of a new covenant, so that those who have been called might receive the promise of the eternal inheritance, since a death has occurred for redemption from the transgressions under the first covenant.
9:16 For where there is a testament, the death of the one who made it must be established.
9:17 For a testament takes effect at death, since it is never in force while the one who made it lives.
9:18 Therefore even the first covenant was not inaugurated without blood.
9:19 For when Moses had spoken every commandment according to the law to all the people, he took the blood of the calves and the goats, with water, scarlet wool, and hyssop, and sprinkled both the book itself and all the people,
9:20 saying, “This is the blood of the covenant that God commanded for you.”
9:21 And he likewise sprinkled the tent and all the vessels of ministry with the blood.
9:22 And according to the law almost everything is cleansed with blood, and without the shedding of blood no forgiveness takes place.
9:23 Therefore it was necessary for the copies of the things in the heavens to be cleansed with these, but the heavenly things themselves with better sacrifices than these.
9:24 For Christ did not enter holy places made by hands, counterparts of the true ones, but heaven itself, now to appear before the face of God on our behalf.
9:25 Nor did He enter to offer Himself many times, as the high priest enters the holy places year by year with another’s blood,
9:26 for then He would have had to suffer many times since the foundation of the world. But now, once at the end of the ages, He has appeared to set aside sin through His sacrifice.
9:27 And just as it is appointed for people to die once, and after this judgment,
9:28 so also Christ, having been offered once to bear the sins of many, will appear a second time, apart from sin, to those eagerly awaiting Him, for salvation.

HEBREWS 10

10:1 For the law, having a shadow of the good things to come, not the very image of the realities, can never perfect those who approach by the same sacrifices offered continually year by year.
10:2 Otherwise, would they not have ceased to be offered, because the worshipers, once cleansed, would no longer have any consciousness of sins?
10:3 But in them there is a reminder of sins year by year,
10:4 for it is impossible for the blood of bulls and goats to take away sins.
10:5 Therefore, coming into the world, He says, “Sacrifice and offering Thou didst not desire, but a body Thou didst prepare for Me.
10:6 In whole burnt offerings and offerings for sin Thou didst take no pleasure.
10:7 Then I said, ‘Look, I have come—in the scroll of the book it is written about Me—to do Thy will, O God.’”
10:8 After saying above, “Sacrifices and offerings and whole burnt offerings and offerings for sin Thou didst not desire or take pleasure in”—which are offered according to law—
10:9 He then said, “Look, I have come to do Thy will.” He takes away the first in order to establish the second.
10:10 By this will we have been sanctified through the offering of the body of Jesus Christ once for all.
10:11 And every priest stands daily, ministering and often offering the same sacrifices, which can never remove sins.
10:12 But this One, having offered one sacrifice for sins for all time, sat down at the right hand of God,
10:13 from then on waiting until His enemies are made a footstool for His feet.
10:14 For by one offering He has perfected for all time those who are being sanctified.
10:15 And the Holy Spirit also testifies to us; for after saying,
10:16 “This is the covenant I will make with them after those days, says the Lord: putting My laws upon their hearts, I will also inscribe them upon their mind,”
10:17 He adds, “And their sins and their lawless deeds I will certainly remember no more.”
10:18 Now where there is forgiveness of these, there is no longer an offering for sin.
10:19 Therefore, brothers, since we have confidence for entry into the holy places in the blood of Jesus,
10:20 by the new and living way He inaugurated for us through the curtain—that is, His flesh—
10:21 and since we have a great priest over the house of God,
10:22 let us approach with a true heart in full assurance of faith, our hearts sprinkled clean from an evil conscience and our body washed with pure water.
10:23 Let us hold fast the confession of our hope without wavering, for the One who promised is faithful.
10:24 And let us consider one another, to stir up love and good works,
10:25 not abandoning our own gathering together, as is the habit of some, but encouraging one another, and all the more as you see the day drawing near.
10:26 For if we sin deliberately after receiving the knowledge of the truth, no sacrifice for sins remains,
10:27 but a fearful expectation of judgment and a fury of fire about to consume the adversaries.
10:28 Anyone who has set aside the law of Moses dies without compassion on the testimony of two or three witnesses.
10:29 How much worse punishment do you think will be deserved by the one who has trampled the Son of God underfoot, regarded as common the blood of the covenant by which he was sanctified, and insulted the Spirit of grace?
10:30 For we know the One who said, “Vengeance belongs to Me; I will repay.” And again, “The Lord will judge His people.”
10:31 It is a fearful thing to fall into the hands of the living God.
10:32 But remember the earlier days, in which, after being enlightened, you endured a great contest of sufferings,
10:33 at one time publicly exposed to reproaches and afflictions, at another becoming companions of those treated in this way.
10:34 For you sympathized with the prisoners, and you accepted the seizure of your possessions with joy, knowing that you yourselves have a better possession, and a lasting one.
10:35 Therefore do not throw away your confidence, which has a great reward.
10:36 For you need endurance, so that, having done the will of God, you may receive the promise.
10:37 “For yet a very little while, the coming One will come and will not delay.
10:38 But My righteous one will live by faith; and if he draws back, My soul takes no pleasure in him.”
10:39 But we are not people who draw back to destruction, but people of faith for the preservation of the soul.

HEBREWS 11

11:1 Now faith is the assurance of things hoped for, the conviction of realities not seen.
11:2 For by it the elders received testimony.
11:3 By faith we understand that the ages were formed by God’s word, so that what is seen did not come into being from visible things.
11:4 By faith Abel offered God a greater sacrifice than Cain, through which he received testimony that he was righteous, God testifying concerning his gifts; and through it, though he died, he still speaks.
11:5 By faith Enoch was taken away so as not to see death, and he was not found, because God took him away. For before his taking away he had received testimony that he had pleased God.
11:6 And without faith it is impossible to please Him, for the one approaching God must believe that He is and that He becomes a rewarder of those who seek Him.
11:7 By faith Noah, instructed concerning things not yet seen, acted with reverent fear and prepared an ark for the salvation of his household. Through this he condemned the world and became heir of the righteousness according to faith.
11:8 By faith Abraham, when called, obeyed by going out to a place he was to receive as an inheritance; and he went out without knowing where he was going.
11:9 By faith he lived as a foreigner in the land of the promise as in a foreign land, dwelling in tents with Isaac and Jacob, fellow heirs of the same promise.
11:10 For he was awaiting the city that has foundations, whose craftsman and maker is God.
11:11 By faith even Sarah herself, though barren, received power to conceive offspring even beyond the proper age, since she considered the One who promised faithful.
11:12 Therefore from one man, and that one as good as dead, there were born as many as the stars of heaven in multitude and as the innumerable sand along the seashore.
11:13 All these died in faith, not having received the promises, but having seen and greeted them from afar and confessed that they were strangers and sojourners upon the earth.
11:14 For those who say such things make clear that they are seeking a homeland.
11:15 And if they had been thinking of that land from which they went out, they would have had opportunity to return.
11:16 But now they desire a better one, that is, a heavenly one. Therefore God is not ashamed of them, to be called their God, for He has prepared a city for them.
11:17 By faith Abraham, when tested, offered Isaac; and the one who had welcomed the promises was offering his only son,
11:18 to whom it had been said, “In Isaac offspring will be named for you.”
11:19 He reasoned that God is able even to raise the dead; from there he also received him back, figuratively speaking.
11:20 By faith Isaac blessed Jacob and Esau also concerning things to come.
11:21 By faith Jacob, when dying, blessed each of Joseph’s sons, and worshiped over the top of his staff.
11:22 By faith Joseph, when nearing his end, spoke of the exodus of the sons of Israel and gave instructions concerning his bones.
11:23 By faith Moses, when born, was hidden for three months by his parents, because they saw that the child was beautiful and did not fear the king’s decree.
11:24 By faith Moses, when grown, refused to be called a son of Pharaoh’s daughter,
11:25 choosing rather to be mistreated with the people of God than to have the temporary enjoyment of sin.
11:26 He considered the reproach of Christ greater wealth than the treasures of Egypt, for he was looking toward the reward.
11:27 By faith he left Egypt, not fearing the king’s anger, for he endured as though seeing the invisible One.
11:28 By faith he kept the Passover and the sprinkling of the blood, so that the destroyer of the firstborn might not touch them.
11:29 By faith they crossed the Red Sea as through dry land; when the Egyptians attempted it, they were swallowed up.
11:30 By faith the walls of Jericho fell after being encircled for seven days.
11:31 By faith Rahab the prostitute did not perish with those who disobeyed, having received the spies in peace.
11:32 And what more shall I say? For time will fail me as I recount Gideon, Barak, Samson, Jephthah, David and Samuel and the prophets,
11:33 who through faith subdued kingdoms, worked righteousness, obtained promises, shut the mouths of lions,
11:34 extinguished the power of fire, escaped the edges of the sword, were strengthened from weakness, became mighty in battle, and turned back the armies of foreigners.
11:35 Women received their dead through resurrection. But others were tortured, not accepting release, so that they might obtain a better resurrection.
11:36 And others experienced mockery and flogging, and also chains and imprisonment.
11:37 They were stoned, sawn apart, and died by slaughter with the sword. They went about in sheepskins, in goatskins, being destitute, afflicted, and mistreated—
11:38 of whom the world was not worthy—wandering in deserts and mountains and caves and the holes of the earth.
11:39 And all these, though they received testimony through faith, did not receive the promise,
11:40 since God had provided something better concerning us, so that they should not be perfected apart from us.

HEBREWS 12

12:1 Therefore, since we too have so great a cloud of witnesses surrounding us, let us lay aside every weight and the sin that readily entangles us, and run with endurance the race set before us,
12:2 looking away to Jesus, the pioneer and perfecter of faith. For the joy set before Him He endured a cross, despising its shame, and has sat down at the right hand of the throne of God.
12:3 For consider the One who has endured such opposition from sinners against Himself, so that you may not grow weary, fainting in your souls.
12:4 You have not yet resisted to the point of blood in your struggle against sin,
12:5 and you have forgotten the encouragement that addresses you as sons: “My son, do not make light of the Lord’s discipline, nor faint when reproved by Him.
12:6 For the Lord disciplines the one He loves, and scourges every son He receives.”
12:7 Endure for discipline. God deals with you as sons; for what son is there whom a father does not discipline?
12:8 But if you are without discipline, in which all have become partakers, then you are illegitimate children and not sons.
12:9 Furthermore, we had our fathers in the flesh to discipline us, and we respected them. But shall we not much more submit to the Father of spirits and live?
12:10 For they disciplined us for a few days according to what seemed right to them, but He for our benefit, so that we may share in His holiness.
12:11 Now every discipline seems for the present to be a matter of sorrow, not joy, but afterward it yields the peaceful fruit of righteousness to those trained through it.
12:12 Therefore straighten the drooping hands and the weakened knees,
12:13 and make straight tracks for your feet, so that what is lame may not be dislocated, but rather healed.
12:14 Pursue peace with everyone, and sanctification, without which no one will see the Lord.
12:15 Watch lest anyone fall short of the grace of God, lest any root of bitterness grow up and cause trouble, and through it many be defiled,
12:16 lest anyone be sexually immoral or profane like Esau, who sold his own birthright for a single meal.
12:17 For you know that even afterward, when he desired to inherit the blessing, he was rejected, for he found no place for repentance, although he sought it with tears.
12:18 For you have not approached what can be touched, and a blazing fire, and gloom and darkness and storm,
12:19 and the sound of a trumpet and a voice speaking words, whose hearers begged that no further word be spoken to them.
12:20 For they could not bear the command: “Even if an animal touches the mountain, it shall be stoned.”
12:21 And so fearful was the sight that Moses said, “I am terrified and trembling.”
12:22 But you have approached Mount Zion and the city of the living God, heavenly Jerusalem, and myriads of angels in festive gathering,
12:23 and the assembly of firstborn ones enrolled in the heavens, and God the judge of all, and the spirits of righteous ones who have been perfected,
12:24 and Jesus the mediator of a new covenant, and sprinkled blood that speaks better than Abel.
12:25 See that you do not refuse the One who speaks. For if those who refused the One instructing them on earth did not escape, much less shall we, if we turn away from the One from the heavens.
12:26 His voice shook the earth then, but now He has promised, saying, “Yet once more I will shake not only the earth but also the heaven.”
12:27 Now “yet once more” indicates the removal of the things shaken, as things that have been made, so that the things not shaken may remain.
12:28 Therefore, since we are receiving an unshakable kingdom, let us be grateful, and through this gratitude serve God acceptably with reverence and awe,
12:29 for indeed our God is a consuming fire.

HEBREWS 13

13:1 Let brotherly love remain.
13:2 Do not forget hospitality, for through it some unknowingly entertained angels.
13:3 Remember the prisoners as fellow prisoners, and those being mistreated, as you yourselves also are in a body.
13:4 Let marriage be honored among all, and the marriage bed undefiled, for God will judge the sexually immoral and adulterers.
13:5 Let your way of life be free from the love of money, being content with what you have; for He Himself has said, “I will certainly not leave you, nor will I ever forsake you.”
13:6 So we may say with confidence, “The Lord is my helper, and I will not fear. What will a person do to me?”
13:7 Remember your leaders, who spoke the word of God to you. As you consider the outcome of their way of life, imitate their faith.
13:8 Jesus Christ is the same yesterday and today and for the ages.
13:9 Do not be carried away by varied and strange teachings, for it is good for the heart to be strengthened by grace, not by foods, which have not benefited those occupied with them.
13:10 We have an altar from which those serving the tent have no right to eat.
13:11 For the bodies of those animals whose blood the high priest brings into the holy places for sin are burned outside the camp.
13:12 Therefore Jesus also suffered outside the gate, so that He might sanctify the people through His own blood.
13:13 So let us go out to Him outside the camp, bearing His reproach.
13:14 For here we have no lasting city, but we seek the one to come.
13:15 Therefore through Him let us continually offer God a sacrifice of praise, that is, the fruit of lips confessing His name.
13:16 And do not forget to do good and to share, for God is pleased with such sacrifices.
13:17 Obey your leaders and yield to them, for they keep watch over your souls as those who will give an account, so that they may do this with joy and not groaning, for that would be unprofitable for you.
13:18 Pray for us, for we are convinced that we have a good conscience, wishing to conduct ourselves well in everything.
13:19 And I urge you all the more to do this, so that I may be restored to you sooner.
13:20 Now may the God of peace, who brought up from the dead the great shepherd of the sheep, our Lord Jesus, in the blood of an eternal covenant,
13:21 equip you with every good thing to do His will, working in us what is pleasing in His sight, through Jesus Christ, to whom be glory for the ages of ages. Amen.
13:22 And I urge you, brothers, bear with the word of encouragement, for I have written to you briefly.
13:23 Know that our brother Timothy has been released; if he comes soon, I will see you with him.
13:24 Greet all your leaders and all the saints. Those from Italy greet you.
13:25 Grace be with you all.
