Galatians 1

Read this chapter on staging

Paul, an Apostle, Not from Men

1:1Paul, an apostle, not from human beings nor through a human being, but through Jesus Christ and God the Father, who raised Him from the dead,

1:2and all the brothers with me, to the Churches of Galatia:

1:3grace to you and peace from God our Father and the Lord Jesus Christ,

1:4who gave Himself for our sins, to rescue us from the present evil age, according to the will of our God and Father,

1:5to whom be the glory for the ages of ages. Amen.

I Am Astonished That You Are Turning Away

1:6I am astonished that you are so quickly deserting the One who called you in the grace of Christ for a different gospel,

1:7which is not another gospel, except that there are some who trouble you and want to distort the gospel of Christ.

1:8But even if we or an angel from heaven should proclaim to you a gospel contrary to what we proclaimed to you, let him be accursed.

1:9As we have said before, so now I say again: if anyone proclaims to you a gospel contrary to what you received, let him be accursed.

1:10For am I now seeking the approval of human beings, or of God? Or am I seeking to please human beings? If I were still pleasing human beings, I would not be Christ’s slave.

The Gospel Received by Revelation

1:11For I make known to you, brothers, that the gospel proclaimed by me is not of human origin.

1:12For I neither received it from a human being nor was taught it, but received it through a revelation of Jesus Christ.

1:13For you have heard of my former conduct in Judaism, how I persecuted the Church of God beyond measure and ravaged it,

1:14and I advanced in Judaism beyond many of my own age among my people, being far more zealous for the traditions of my ancestors.

1:15But when God, who set me apart from my mother’s womb and called me through His grace, was pleased

1:16to reveal His Son in me, so that I might proclaim Him among the nations, I did not immediately consult flesh and blood,

1:17nor did I go up to Jerusalem to those who were apostles before me, but I went away to Arabia and returned again to Damascus.

Paul’s First Visit to Jerusalem

1:18Then, after three years, I went up to Jerusalem to become acquainted with Cephas, and I stayed with him fifteen days.

1:19But I saw none of the other apostles except James, the Lord’s brother.

1:20Now in what I am writing to you, behold, before God, I am not lying.

1:21Then I went into the regions of Syria and Cilicia.

1:22But I was unknown by sight to the Churches of Judea that are in Christ.

1:23They only kept hearing, “The one who once persecuted us now proclaims the faith he once ravaged.”

1:24And they glorified God because of me.

Galatians 2

Read this chapter on staging

Paul Accepted by the Apostles

2:1Then, after fourteen years, I went up again to Jerusalem with Barnabas, taking Titus along as well.

2:2I went up in accordance with a revelation, and I set before them the gospel I proclaim among the nations, privately before those held in esteem, lest somehow I was running, or had run, in vain.

2:3But not even Titus, who was with me, was compelled to be circumcised, though he was a Greek.

2:4Yet because of the false brothers secretly brought in, who slipped in to spy out our freedom which we have in Christ Jesus, so that they might enslave us—

2:5we did not yield to them in submission even for an hour, so that the truth of the gospel might remain with you.

2:6But as for those held to be something—whatever they once were makes no difference to me; God does not favor a person’s outward standing—those held in esteem added nothing to me.

2:7On the contrary, seeing that I had been entrusted with the gospel for the uncircumcised, just as Peter with that for the circumcised,

2:8for the One who worked through Peter for an apostleship to the circumcised worked also through me for the nations,

2:9and recognizing the grace given to me, James and Cephas and John, who were regarded as pillars, gave me and Barnabas their right hands in fellowship, so that we should go to the nations and they to the circumcised.

2:10Only we were to remember the poor, the very thing I was also eager to do.

Paul Opposes Cephas

2:11But when Cephas came to Antioch, I opposed him to his face, because he stood condemned.

2:12For before certain people came from James, he used to eat with the Gentiles. But when they came, he began drawing back and separating himself, fearing those of the circumcision.

2:13And the other Jews also joined him in hypocrisy, so that even Barnabas was carried away by their hypocrisy.

2:14But when I saw that they were not walking straight in line with the truth of the gospel, I said to Cephas before them all, “If you, though a Jew, live like the Gentiles and not like the Jews, how can you compel the Gentiles to live as Jews?

Justified by Faith

2:15“We are Jews by birth and not sinners from the Gentiles,

2:16yet knowing that a human being is not justified by works of law but through faith in Jesus Christ, we too believed in Christ Jesus, so that we might be justified by faith in Christ and not by works of law, because by works of law no flesh will be justified.

2:17But if, in seeking to be justified in Christ, we ourselves were also found to be sinners, is Christ then a servant of sin? May it never be!

2:18For if I rebuild the things I tore down, I show myself to be a transgressor.

2:19For through law I died to law, so that I might live to God. I have been crucified with Christ.

2:20And I no longer live, but Christ lives in me. The life I now live in the flesh, I live by faith in the Son of God, who loved me and gave Himself for me.

2:21I do not set aside the grace of God. For if righteousness comes through law, then Christ died for nothing.”

Galatians 3

Read this chapter on staging

Law or Faith

3:1O foolish Galatians, who bewitched you, before whose eyes Jesus Christ was publicly portrayed as crucified?

3:2This alone I want to learn from you: did you receive the Spirit by works of law or by hearing with faith?

3:3Are you so foolish? Having begun by the Spirit, are you now being brought to completion by the flesh?

3:4Did you suffer so much in vain—if indeed it was in vain?

3:5So then, does the One who supplies you with the Spirit and performs acts of power among you do so by works of law or by hearing with faith?

The Blessing of Abraham

3:6Just as Abraham believed God, and it was credited to him as righteousness.

3:7Know, then, that those who are of faith are the sons of Abraham.

3:8And Scripture, foreseeing that God justifies the nations by faith, proclaimed the gospel beforehand to Abraham: “In you all the nations will be blessed.”

3:9So then, those who are of faith are blessed with believing Abraham.

3:10For all who are of works of law are under a curse, for it is written, “Accursed is everyone who does not abide by all the things written in the book of the law, to do them.”

3:11Now it is clear that no one is justified before God by law, because “The righteous one will live by faith.”

3:12But the law is not of faith; rather, “The one who does these things will live by them.”

3:13Christ bought us free from the curse of the law by becoming a curse for us, because it is written, “Accursed is everyone who hangs upon a tree,”

3:14so that in Christ Jesus the blessing of Abraham might come to the nations, so that we might receive the promise of the Spirit through faith.

The Promise and the Law

3:15Brothers, I speak in human terms: even when a human covenant has been ratified, no one sets it aside or adds provisions to it.

3:16Now the promises were spoken to Abraham and to his seed. It does not say, “And to seeds,” as concerning many, but as concerning one, “And to your seed,” who is Christ.

3:17What I mean is this: the law, which came four hundred and thirty years later, does not invalidate a covenant previously ratified by God, so as to nullify the promise.

3:18For if the inheritance is from law, it is no longer from promise. But God has graciously given it to Abraham through promise.

The Purpose of the Law

3:19Why, then, the law? It was added because of transgressions, until the seed should come to whom the promise had been made, and it was ordained through angels by the hand of a mediator.

3:20Now a mediator is not for one alone, but God is one.

3:21Is the law, then, against the promises of God? May it never be! For if a law had been given that could give life, righteousness really would have come from law.

3:22But Scripture confined everything under sin, so that the promise by faith in Jesus Christ might be given to those who believe.

Sons Through Faith

3:23Now before faith came, we were guarded under law, confined until the faith that was to be revealed.

3:24So the law became our guardian until Christ, so that we might be justified by faith.

3:25But now that faith has come, we are no longer under a guardian.

3:26For you are all sons of God through faith in Christ Jesus.

3:27For as many of you as were baptized into Christ have put on Christ.

3:28There is neither Jew nor Greek; there is neither slave nor free; there is no male and female, for you are all one in Christ Jesus.

3:29And if you belong to Christ, then you are Abraham’s seed, heirs according to promise.

Galatians 4

Read this chapter on staging

Heirs and Sons

4:1Now I say that as long as the heir is a child, he differs in no way from a slave, though he is master of everything.

4:2Rather, he is under guardians and stewards until the date appointed by his father.

4:3So also we, when we were children, were enslaved under the elements of the world.

4:4But when the fullness of time came, God sent forth His Son, born of a woman, born under law,

4:5to redeem those under law, that we might receive adoption as sons.

4:6And because you are sons, God sent forth the Spirit of His Son into our hearts, crying, “Abba, Father!”

4:7So you are no longer a slave but a son; and if a son, then also an heir through God.

Turning Back to Weak Elements

4:8But at that time, not knowing God, you were enslaved to those that by nature are not gods.

4:9Now, however, having come to know God—or rather, having been known by God—how do you turn back again to the weak and impoverished elements, to which you want to be enslaved all over again?

4:10You carefully observe days and months and seasons and years.

4:11I fear for you, lest somehow I have labored over you in vain.

Paul’s Appeal

4:12Become as I am, because I too became as you are, brothers; I beg you. You did me no wrong.

4:13And you know that it was because of a weakness of the flesh that I first proclaimed the gospel to you.

4:14And though my flesh was a trial for you, you did not despise or reject me, but you welcomed me as an angel of God, as Christ Jesus.

4:15Where, then, is your sense of blessedness? For I testify to you that, if possible, you would have dug out your eyes and given them to me.

4:16So have I become your enemy by telling you the truth?

4:17They are zealous for you, but not for good; they want to shut you out, so that you may be zealous for them.

4:18But it is good to be sought zealously for a good purpose at all times, and not only when I am present with you,

4:19my children, for whom I am again in labor until Christ is formed in you.

4:20I wish I could be present with you now and change my tone, because I am perplexed about you.

Hagar and Sarah

4:21Tell me, you who want to be under law, do you not listen to the law?

4:22For it is written that Abraham had two sons, one by the slave woman and one by the free woman.

4:23But the one by the slave woman was born according to the flesh, and the one by the free woman through promise.

4:24These things are being interpreted allegorically, for these women are two covenants: one from Mount Sinai, bearing children into slavery, which is Hagar.

4:25Now Hagar is Mount Sinai in Arabia, and corresponds to the present Jerusalem, for she is enslaved with her children.

4:26But the Jerusalem above is free, and she is our mother.

4:27For it is written, “Rejoice, barren woman who does not bear; break out and cry aloud, you who are not in labor, because the children of the deserted woman are more numerous than those of the woman who has a husband.”

4:28Now you, brothers, are children of promise, like Isaac.

4:29But just as then the one born according to the flesh persecuted the one born according to the Spirit, so it is also now.

4:30But what does Scripture say? “Cast out the slave woman and her son, for the son of the slave woman shall certainly not inherit with the son of the free woman.”

4:31Therefore, brothers, we are not children of a slave woman but of the free woman.

Galatians 5

Read this chapter on staging

Stand Firm in Freedom

5:1For freedom Christ set us free. Stand firm, therefore, and do not be held again under a yoke of slavery.

5:2Look, I, Paul, tell you that if you receive circumcision, Christ will be of no benefit to you.

5:3And I testify again to every man who receives circumcision that he is obligated to do the whole law.

5:4You who seek to be justified by law have been severed from Christ; you have fallen from grace.

5:5For we, by the Spirit, through faith, eagerly await the hope of righteousness.

5:6For in Christ Jesus neither circumcision nor uncircumcision has any strength, but faith working through love.

A Little Leaven

5:7You were running well. Who hindered you from obeying the truth?

5:8This persuasion is not from the One who calls you.

5:9A little leaven leavens the whole batch of dough.

5:10I am confident about you in the Lord that you will think nothing else. But the one troubling you will bear the judgment, whoever he may be.

5:11But I, brothers, if I still proclaim circumcision, why am I still persecuted? Then the stumbling block of the cross has been abolished.

5:12I wish those who unsettle you would even castrate themselves!

Freedom to Serve in Love

5:13For you were called to freedom, brothers. Only do not make freedom a base of operations for the flesh, but through love serve one another as slaves.

5:14For the whole law is fulfilled in one saying: “You shall love your neighbor as yourself.”

5:15But if you bite and devour one another, watch out that you are not consumed by one another.

Walk by the Spirit

5:16But I say, walk by the Spirit, and you will certainly not fulfill the desire of the flesh.

5:17For the flesh desires what is against the Spirit, and the Spirit what is against the flesh; for these oppose one another, so that you do not do the things you want.

5:18But if you are led by the Spirit, you are not under law.

Brothers, Even If a Person Is Caught

6:1Brothers, even if a person is overtaken in some transgression, you who are spiritual are to restore such a person in a spirit of gentleness, watching yourself, lest you too be tempted.

6:2Carry one another’s burdens, and in this way you will fulfill the law of Christ.

6:3For if anyone thinks he is something when he is nothing, he deceives himself.

6:4But let each test his own work, and then he will have his reason for boasting in relation to himself alone, and not in relation to someone else.

6:5For each will carry his own load.

Let the One Instructed Share

6:6Let the one instructed in the word share all good things with the one who instructs.

6:7Do not be deceived; God is not mocked. For whatever a person sows, that he will also reap.

6:8For the one who sows into his own flesh will reap corruption from the flesh, but the one who sows into the Spirit will reap eternal life from the Spirit.

6:9And let us not grow weary in doing good, for in the proper season we will reap, if we do not give up.

6:10So then, as we have opportunity, let us do good to everyone, especially to those of the household of faith.

The Cross and the New Creation

6:11See with what large letters I have written to you in my own hand.

6:15For neither circumcision nor uncircumcision counts for anything, but a new creation does.

6:17From now on, let no one cause me trouble, for I carry the marks of Jesus in my body.

6:18The grace of our Lord Jesus Christ be with your spirit, brothers. Amen.
