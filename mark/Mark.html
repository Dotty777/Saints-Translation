Mark 1

Read this chapter on staging

The Beginning of the Good News

1:1The beginning of the gospel of Jesus Christ, Son of God. †

1:2As it is written in Isaiah the prophet: “Look, I send My messenger before Thy face, who will prepare Thy way;

1:3a voice of one crying in the wilderness, ‘Prepare the way of the Lord; make His paths straight.’”

John the Baptist in the Wilderness

1:4John appeared, baptizing in the wilderness and proclaiming a baptism of repentance for the forgiveness of sins.

1:5The whole Judean countryside and all the people of Jerusalem were going out to him. They were being baptized by him in the Jordan River, confessing their sins.

1:6John was clothed in camel’s hair, with a leather belt around his waist, and ate locusts and wild honey.

1:7He proclaimed, saying, “The One stronger than I is coming after me. I am not worthy to stoop down and untie the strap of His sandals.

1:8I baptized you with water, but He will baptize you in the Holy Spirit.”

The Baptism of Jesus

1:9In those days Jesus came from Nazareth in Galilee and was baptized in the Jordan by John.

1:10Immediately as He came up out of the water, He saw the heavens being torn open and the Spirit descending like a dove into Him.

1:11A voice came from the heavens: “Thou art My beloved Son; in Thee I am well pleased.”

The Temptation in the Wilderness

1:12Immediately the Spirit drove Him out into the wilderness.

1:13He was in the wilderness forty days, being tempted by Satan. He was with the wild animals, and the angels ministered to Him.

Jesus Proclaims the Good News in Galilee

1:14And after John was handed over, Jesus came into Galilee, proclaiming the gospel of God, †

1:15and saying, “The appointed time is fulfilled, and the kingdom of God has drawn near; repent, and believe in the gospel.” †

The Calling of the First Disciples

1:16And passing beside the Sea of Galilee, He saw Simon and Andrew the brother of Simon casting a net in the sea, for they were fishermen. †

1:17And He said to them, “Come after Me, and I will make you become fishers of men.” †

1:18Immediately they left their nets and followed Him.

1:19Going a little farther, He saw James son of Zebedee and John his brother, who were also in the boat, putting the nets in order.

1:20Immediately He called them. They left their father Zebedee in the boat with the hired workers and went after Him.

An Unclean Spirit in the Synagogue

1:21They entered Capernaum, and immediately on the Sabbath He went into the synagogue and taught.

1:22They were astonished at His teaching, for He taught them as one having authority, and not as the scribes.

1:23Immediately there was in their synagogue a man with an unclean spirit, and he cried out,

1:24“What have we to do with Thee, Jesus of Nazareth? Hast Thou come to destroy us? I know who Thou art—the Holy One of God!”

1:25Jesus rebuked him, saying, “Be muzzled, and come out of him!”

1:26The unclean spirit convulsed him, cried out with a loud voice, and came out of him.

1:27They were all amazed, so that they discussed among themselves, saying, “What is this? A new teaching! With authority He commands even the unclean spirits, and they obey Him.”

1:28Immediately the report about Him went out everywhere through the whole surrounding region of Galilee.

Simon’s Mother-in-Law Healed

1:29Immediately after leaving the synagogue, they went into the house of Simon and Andrew, with James and John.

1:30Simon’s mother-in-law was lying down with a fever, and immediately they told Him about her.

1:31He came to her, took her by the hand, and raised her up. The fever left her, and she served them.

Healings at Sundown

1:32When evening came, after the sun had set, they brought to Him all who were ill and those possessed by demons.

1:33The whole city had gathered at the door.

1:34He healed many who were ill with various diseases and cast out many demons. He would not allow the demons to speak, because they knew Him.

Prayer in a Deserted Place

1:35Very early, while it was still dark, He arose, went out, and departed to a deserted place, and there He prayed.

1:36Simon and those with him searched for Him

1:37and found Him. They said to Him, “Everyone is looking for Thee.”

1:38He said to them, “Let us go elsewhere, into the neighboring towns, so that I may proclaim there also, for that is why I came out.”

1:39He went throughout all Galilee, proclaiming in their synagogues and casting out the demons.

A Leper Cleansed

1:40A leper came to Him, pleading with Him, kneeling, and saying, “If Thou art willing, Thou canst cleanse me.”

1:41And moved with compassion, He stretched out His hand and touched him, and says to him, “I will; be cleansed.” †

1:42Immediately the leprosy departed from him, and he was cleansed.

1:43Sternly warning him, He immediately drove him away

1:44and said to him, “See that you say nothing to anyone. But go, show yourself to the priest, and offer for your cleansing what Moses commanded, as a testimony to them.”

1:45But he went out and began proclaiming it widely and spreading the report, so that Jesus could no longer enter a city openly. He remained outside in deserted places, and they came to Him from every direction.

The Paralytic Forgiven and Healed

2:1When He entered Capernaum again after some days, it was heard that He was at home.

2:2Many gathered, so that there was no longer room, not even near the door, and He was speaking the word to them.

2:3They came, bringing to Him a paralyzed man carried by four men.

2:4Since they could not bring him to Jesus because of the crowd, they removed the roof above the place where He was. After digging through it, they lowered the mat on which the paralyzed man lay.

2:5Jesus saw their faith and said to the paralyzed man, “Child, your sins are forgiven.”

2:6Some of the scribes were sitting there and reasoning in their hearts,

2:7“Why does this man speak this way? He is blaspheming! Who can forgive sins except the one God?”

2:8Immediately Jesus knew in His spirit that they were reasoning this way within themselves. He said to them, “Why are you reasoning these things in your hearts?

2:9Which is easier: to say to the paralyzed man, ‘Your sins are forgiven,’ or to say, ‘Arise, take your mat, and walk’?

2:10But so that you may know that the Son of Man has authority on earth to forgive sins”—He said to the paralyzed man—

2:11“I say to you, arise, take your mat, and go to your house.”

2:12He arose, immediately took up the mat, and went out before everyone. They were all amazed and glorified God, saying, “We have never seen anything like this!”

The Calling of Levi

2:13He went out again beside the sea. The whole crowd came to Him, and He taught them.

2:14As He passed by, He saw Levi son of Alphaeus sitting at the tax station. He said to him, “Follow Me.” He arose and followed Him.

2:15As He reclined at table in his house, many tax collectors and sinners reclined with Jesus and His disciples, for there were many who followed Him.

2:16The scribes of the Pharisees saw that He was eating with sinners and tax collectors and said to His disciples, “Why does He eat with tax collectors and sinners?”

2:17Jesus heard and said to them, “The strong have no need of a physician, but those who are ill do. I did not come to call the righteous, but sinners.”

The Question About Fasting

2:18John’s disciples and the Pharisees were fasting. They came and said to Him, “Why do John’s disciples and the disciples of the Pharisees fast, but Thy disciples do not fast?”

2:19Jesus said to them, “Can the bridegroom’s companions fast while the bridegroom is with them? As long as they have the bridegroom with them, they cannot fast.

2:20But days will come when the bridegroom is taken away from them, and then they will fast on that day.

2:21No one sews a patch of unshrunk cloth onto an old garment. Otherwise the new patch pulls away from the old, and a worse tear results.

2:22No one puts new wine into old wineskins. Otherwise the wine will burst the skins, and the wine is lost along with the skins. New wine belongs in fresh wineskins.”

Lord of the Sabbath

2:23He was passing through the grainfields on the Sabbath, and His disciples began to make their way, plucking the heads of grain.

2:24The Pharisees said to Him, “Look! Why are they doing what is not lawful on the Sabbath?”

2:25He said to them, “Have you never read what David did when he was in need and hungry, he and those with him:

2:26how he entered God’s house in the time of Abiathar the high priest and ate the loaves of presentation, which only priests may lawfully eat, and also gave them to those who were with him?”

2:27And He said to them, “The Sabbath was made for man, and not man for the Sabbath. †

2:28So the Son of Man is Lord even of the Sabbath.”

Mark 3

The Man with the Withered Hand

3:1He entered a synagogue again, and a man with a withered hand was there.

3:2They watched Him closely to see whether He would heal him on the Sabbath, so that they might accuse Him.

3:3He said to the man with the withered hand, “Arise and come into the midst.”

3:4He said to them, “Is it lawful on the Sabbath to do good or to do evil, to save a life or to kill?” But they remained silent.

3:5He looked around at them with anger, grieving at the hardness of their hearts, and said to the man, “Stretch out your hand.” He stretched it out, and his hand was restored.

3:6The Pharisees went out and immediately took counsel with the Herodians against Him, as to how they might destroy Him.

Crowds by the Sea

3:7Jesus withdrew with His disciples to the sea, and a great multitude from Galilee followed; and from Judea,

3:8Jerusalem, Idumea, beyond the Jordan, and the region of Tyre and Sidon, a great multitude heard all He was doing and came to Him.

3:9He told His disciples to have a small boat ready for Him because of the crowd, so that they would not crush Him.

3:10For He healed many, so that all who had afflictions pressed upon Him to touch Him.

3:11Whenever the unclean spirits saw Him, they fell before Him and cried, “Thou art the Son of God!”

3:12He sternly warned them not to make Him known.

The Appointing of the Twelve

3:13He went up the mountain and called to Himself those He wanted, and they came to Him.

3:14He appointed twelve, whom He also named apostles, to be with Him and to be sent out by Him to proclaim

3:15and to have authority to cast out the demons.

3:16He appointed the Twelve: Simon, to whom He gave the name Peter;

3:17James son of Zebedee and John, James’s brother, to whom He gave the name Boanerges, that is, Sons of Thunder;

3:18Andrew, Philip, Bartholomew, Matthew, Thomas, James son of Alphaeus, Thaddaeus, Simon the Cananaean,

3:19and Judas Iscariot, who also handed Him over.

His Own People Seek to Seize Him

3:20He came into a house, and the crowd gathered again, so that they could not even eat bread.

3:21When His own people heard, they went out to take hold of Him, for they were saying, “He is out of His mind.”

A House Divided

3:22The scribes who had come down from Jerusalem were saying, “He has Beelzebul,” and, “By the ruler of the demons He casts out the demons.”

3:23He called them to Himself and spoke to them in parables: “How can Satan cast out Satan?

3:24If a kingdom is divided against itself, that kingdom cannot stand.

3:25If a house is divided against itself, that house will not be able to stand.

3:26If Satan has risen against himself and is divided, he cannot stand, but has come to an end.

3:27No one can enter the strong man’s house and plunder his belongings unless he first binds the strong man. Then he will plunder his house.

3:28Truly I tell you, people will be forgiven everything—their sins and whatever blasphemies they may utter.

3:29But whoever blasphemes against the Holy Spirit has no forgiveness for ever, but is liable for an eternal sin.” †

3:30For they were saying, “He has an unclean spirit.”

The Mother and Brothers of Jesus

3:31His mother and His brothers came. Standing outside, they sent to Him and called Him.

3:32A crowd was sitting around Him. They said to Him, “Look! Thy mother and Thy brothers and Thy sisters are outside looking for Thee.”

3:33He answered them, “Who is My mother, or who are My brothers?”

3:34He looked around at those sitting in a circle about Him and said, “Look—My mother and My brothers!

3:35For whoever does the will of God is My brother and sister and mother.”

Mark 4

Read this chapter on staging

The Parable of the Sower

4:1Again He began teaching beside the sea. A very great crowd gathered around Him, so that He got into a boat on the sea and sat down, while the whole crowd was on the land beside the sea.

4:2He taught them many things in parables, and in His teaching He said to them:

4:3“Listen! Look, the sower went out to sow.

4:4As he sowed, some fell beside the road, and the birds came and ate it up.

4:5Other seed fell on rocky ground, where it had little soil, and immediately sprang up because the soil had no depth.

4:6When the sun rose, it was scorched, and because it had no root, it withered.

4:7Other seed fell among the thorns. The thorns grew up and choked it, and it gave no fruit.

4:8Other seeds fell into the good soil and gave fruit, growing up and increasing; they yielded thirtyfold, sixtyfold, and a hundredfold.”

4:9He said, “Whoever has ears to hear, let him hear.”

The Purpose of the Parables

4:10When He was alone, those around Him, together with the Twelve, asked Him about the parables.

4:11He said to them, “To you the mystery of the kingdom of God has been given. But for those outside, everything comes in parables,

4:12so that, looking, they may look and not see, and hearing, they may hear and not understand, lest they turn back and be forgiven.”

Do You Not Understand This Parable?

4:13He said to them, “Do you not understand this parable? Then how will you understand all the parables?

4:14The sower sows the word.

4:15These are the ones beside the road where the word is sown: whenever they hear, Satan immediately comes and takes away the word sown into them.

4:16Likewise, these are the ones sown on the rocky places: whenever they hear the word, they immediately receive it with joy,

4:17but they have no root in themselves and last only for a time. Then, when distress or persecution comes because of the word, they immediately stumble.

4:18Others are the ones sown among the thorns. These are the ones who have heard the word,

4:19but the cares of the age, the deceitfulness of riches, and desires for other things enter and choke the word, and it becomes unfruitful.

4:20Those sown on the good soil are the ones who hear the word, accept it, and bear fruit: thirtyfold, sixtyfold, and a hundredfold.”

Does the Lamp Come to Be Put Under a Basket?

4:21He said to them, “Does the lamp come to be put under the modius basket, or under the bed? Is it not to be put on the lampstand?

4:22For nothing is hidden except to be revealed, nor has anything become secret except to come into the open.

4:23If anyone has ears to hear, let him hear.”

4:24He said to them, “Pay attention to what you hear. With the measure you use, it will be measured to you, and more will be added to you.

4:25For whoever has will be given more; and whoever does not have, even what he has will be taken from him.”

The Kingdom of God Is Like Scattered Seed

4:26He said, “The kingdom of God is like this: a man casts seed on the ground,

4:27and sleeps and rises, night and day, while the seed sprouts and grows—he himself does not know how.

4:28The earth bears fruit of itself: first the blade, then the ear, then the full grain in the ear.

4:29When the crop is ready, he immediately sends in the sickle, because the harvest has arrived.”

How Shall We Compare the Kingdom of God?

4:30He said, “How shall we compare the kingdom of God, or in what parable shall we set it out?

4:31It is like a mustard seed, which, when sown on the ground, is smaller than all the seeds on the earth.

4:32Yet when sown, it grows up and becomes larger than all the garden plants, and makes great branches, so that the birds of the sky can shelter under its shade.”

With Many Such Parables He Spoke to Them

4:33With many such parables He spoke the word to them, as they were able to hear.

4:34He did not speak to them without a parable, but privately He explained everything to His own disciples.

The Storm Stilled

4:35On that day, when evening came, He said to them, “Let us cross to the other side.”

4:36Leaving the crowd, they took Him with them, just as He was, in the boat. Other boats were with Him.

4:37A great windstorm arose, and the waves broke into the boat, so that the boat was already filling.

4:38He was in the stern, asleep on the cushion. They woke Him and said to Him, “Teacher, dost Thou not care that we are perishing?”

4:39And He awoke, rebuked the wind, and said to the sea, “Be silent; be still.” And the wind ceased, and there was a great calm. †

4:40He said to them, “Why are you cowardly? Do you still have no faith?”

4:41They were greatly afraid and said to one another, “Who then is this, that even the wind and the sea obey Him?”

The Man with the Legion

5:1They came to the other side of the sea, to the country of the Gerasenes.

5:2As He stepped out of the boat, a man with an unclean spirit immediately met Him from the tombs.

5:3He made his dwelling among the tombs, and no one could bind him anymore, even with a chain.

5:4He had often been bound with fetters and chains, but he had torn the chains apart and shattered the fetters. No one had the strength to subdue him.

5:5Continually, night and day, among the tombs and on the mountains, he cried out and cut himself with stones.

5:6Seeing Jesus from far away, he ran and bowed before Him.

5:7He cried out with a loud voice and said, “What hast Thou to do with me, Jesus, Son of God Most High? I adjure Thee by God: do not torment me!”

5:8For He had been saying to him, “Come out of the man, unclean spirit!”

5:9He asked him, “What is your name?” He said to Him, “Legion is my name, because we are many.”

5:10He begged Him earnestly not to send them out of the country.

5:11Now a large herd of pigs was feeding there near the mountain.

5:12They begged Him, saying, “Send us into the pigs, so that we may enter them.”

5:13He allowed them. The unclean spirits came out and entered the pigs, and the herd rushed down the steep bank into the sea—about two thousand—and they drowned in the sea.

5:14Those tending them fled and reported it in the city and in the fields. People came to see what had happened.

5:15They came to Jesus and saw the demon-possessed man sitting, clothed and in his right mind—the one who had had the legion—and they were afraid.

5:16Those who had seen it told them what had happened to the demon-possessed man and about the pigs.

5:17They began begging Him to leave their territory.

5:18As He was boarding the boat, the man who had been demon-possessed begged Him to let him be with Him.

5:19He did not allow him, but said to him, “Go home to your own people and report to them how much the Lord has done for you and how He has had mercy on you.”

5:20He went away and began proclaiming in the Decapolis how much Jesus had done for him, and everyone marveled.

Jairus Pleads for His Daughter

5:21When Jesus had crossed again in the boat to the other side, a large crowd gathered around Him, and He was beside the sea.

5:22One of the synagogue rulers came, named Jairus. Seeing Him, he fell at His feet

5:23and begged Him earnestly, saying, “My little daughter is at the point of death. Please come and lay Thy hands on her, so that she may be saved and live.”

5:24He went with him. A large crowd followed Him and pressed against Him.

The Woman with the Flow of Blood

5:25A woman who had had a flow of blood for twelve years,

5:26and had suffered much under many physicians and spent everything she had, without any benefit but instead growing worse,

5:27heard about Jesus. She came behind Him in the crowd and touched His garment.

5:28For she was saying, “If I touch even His clothes, I will be saved.”

5:29Immediately the source of her blood dried up, and she knew in her body that she was healed of the affliction.

5:30Immediately Jesus, recognizing in Himself that power had gone out from Him, turned around in the crowd and said, “Who touched My clothes?”
