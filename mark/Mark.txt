MARK 1

1:1 The beginning of the gospel of Jesus Christ, Son of God.
1:2 As it is written in Isaiah the prophet: “Look, I send My messenger before Thy face, who will prepare Thy way;
1:3 a voice of one crying in the wilderness, ‘Prepare the way of the Lord; make His paths straight.’”
1:4 John appeared, baptizing in the wilderness and proclaiming a baptism of repentance for the forgiveness of sins.
1:5 The whole Judean countryside and all the people of Jerusalem were going out to him. They were being baptized by him in the Jordan River, confessing their sins.
1:6 John was clothed in camel’s hair, with a leather belt around his waist, and ate locusts and wild honey.
1:7 He proclaimed, saying, “The One stronger than I is coming after me. I am not worthy to stoop down and untie the strap of His sandals.
1:8 I baptized you with water, but He will baptize you in the Holy Spirit.”
1:9 In those days Jesus came from Nazareth in Galilee and was baptized in the Jordan by John.
1:10 Immediately as He came up out of the water, He saw the heavens being torn open and the Spirit descending like a dove into Him.
1:11 A voice came from the heavens: “Thou art My beloved Son; in Thee I am well pleased.”
1:12 Immediately the Spirit drove Him out into the wilderness.
1:13 He was in the wilderness forty days, being tempted by Satan. He was with the wild animals, and the angels ministered to Him.
1:14 And after John was handed over, Jesus came into Galilee, proclaiming the gospel of God,
1:15 and saying, “The appointed time is fulfilled, and the kingdom of God has drawn near; repent, and believe in the gospel.”
1:16 And passing beside the Sea of Galilee, He saw Simon and Andrew the brother of Simon casting a net in the sea, for they were fishermen.
1:17 And He said to them, “Come after Me, and I will make you become fishers of men.”
1:18 Immediately they left their nets and followed Him.
1:19 Going a little farther, He saw James son of Zebedee and John his brother, who were also in the boat, putting the nets in order.
1:20 Immediately He called them. They left their father Zebedee in the boat with the hired workers and went after Him.
1:21 They entered Capernaum, and immediately on the Sabbath He went into the synagogue and taught.
1:22 They were astonished at His teaching, for He taught them as one having authority, and not as the scribes.
1:23 Immediately there was in their synagogue a man with an unclean spirit, and he cried out,
1:24 “What have we to do with Thee, Jesus of Nazareth? Hast Thou come to destroy us? I know who Thou art—the Holy One of God!”
1:25 Jesus rebuked him, saying, “Be muzzled, and come out of him!”
1:26 The unclean spirit convulsed him, cried out with a loud voice, and came out of him.
1:27 They were all amazed, so that they discussed among themselves, saying, “What is this? A new teaching! With authority He commands even the unclean spirits, and they obey Him.”
1:28 Immediately the report about Him went out everywhere through the whole surrounding region of Galilee.
1:29 Immediately after leaving the synagogue, they went into the house of Simon and Andrew, with James and John.
1:30 Simon’s mother-in-law was lying down with a fever, and immediately they told Him about her.
1:31 He came to her, took her by the hand, and raised her up. The fever left her, and she served them.
1:32 When evening came, after the sun had set, they brought to Him all who were ill and those possessed by demons.
1:33 The whole city had gathered at the door.
1:34 He healed many who were ill with various diseases and cast out many demons. He would not allow the demons to speak, because they knew Him.
1:35 Very early, while it was still dark, He arose, went out, and departed to a deserted place, and there He prayed.
1:36 Simon and those with him searched for Him
1:37 and found Him. They said to Him, “Everyone is looking for Thee.”
1:38 He said to them, “Let us go elsewhere, into the neighboring towns, so that I may proclaim there also, for that is why I came out.”
1:39 He went throughout all Galilee, proclaiming in their synagogues and casting out the demons.
1:40 A leper came to Him, pleading with Him, kneeling, and saying, “If Thou art willing, Thou canst cleanse me.”
1:41 And moved with compassion, He stretched out His hand and touched him, and says to him, “I will; be cleansed.”
1:42 Immediately the leprosy departed from him, and he was cleansed.
1:43 Sternly warning him, He immediately drove him away
1:44 and said to him, “See that you say nothing to anyone. But go, show yourself to the priest, and offer for your cleansing what Moses commanded, as a testimony to them.”
1:45 But he went out and began proclaiming it widely and spreading the report, so that Jesus could no longer enter a city openly. He remained outside in deserted places, and they came to Him from every direction.

MARK 2

2:1 When He entered Capernaum again after some days, it was heard that He was at home.
2:2 Many gathered, so that there was no longer room, not even near the door, and He was speaking the word to them.
2:3 They came, bringing to Him a paralyzed man carried by four men.
2:4 Since they could not bring him to Jesus because of the crowd, they removed the roof above the place where He was. After digging through it, they lowered the mat on which the paralyzed man lay.
2:5 Jesus saw their faith and said to the paralyzed man, “Child, your sins are forgiven.”
2:6 Some of the scribes were sitting there and reasoning in their hearts,
2:7 “Why does this man speak this way? He is blaspheming! Who can forgive sins except the one God?”
2:8 Immediately Jesus knew in His spirit that they were reasoning this way within themselves. He said to them, “Why are you reasoning these things in your hearts?
2:9 Which is easier: to say to the paralyzed man, ‘Your sins are forgiven,’ or to say, ‘Arise, take your mat, and walk’?
2:10 But so that you may know that the Son of Man has authority on earth to forgive sins”—He said to the paralyzed man—
2:11 “I say to you, arise, take your mat, and go to your house.”
2:12 He arose, immediately took up the mat, and went out before everyone. They were all amazed and glorified God, saying, “We have never seen anything like this!”
2:13 He went out again beside the sea. The whole crowd came to Him, and He taught them.
2:14 As He passed by, He saw Levi son of Alphaeus sitting at the tax station. He said to him, “Follow Me.” He arose and followed Him.
2:15 As He reclined at table in his house, many tax collectors and sinners reclined with Jesus and His disciples, for there were many who followed Him.
2:16 The scribes of the Pharisees saw that He was eating with sinners and tax collectors and said to His disciples, “Why does He eat with tax collectors and sinners?”
2:17 Jesus heard and said to them, “The strong have no need of a physician, but those who are ill do. I did not come to call the righteous, but sinners.”
2:18 John’s disciples and the Pharisees were fasting. They came and said to Him, “Why do John’s disciples and the disciples of the Pharisees fast, but Thy disciples do not fast?”
2:19 Jesus said to them, “Can the bridegroom’s companions fast while the bridegroom is with them? As long as they have the bridegroom with them, they cannot fast.
2:20 But days will come when the bridegroom is taken away from them, and then they will fast on that day.
2:21 No one sews a patch of unshrunk cloth onto an old garment. Otherwise the new patch pulls away from the old, and a worse tear results.
2:22 No one puts new wine into old wineskins. Otherwise the wine will burst the skins, and the wine is lost along with the skins. New wine belongs in fresh wineskins.”
2:23 He was passing through the grainfields on the Sabbath, and His disciples began to make their way, plucking the heads of grain.
2:24 The Pharisees said to Him, “Look! Why are they doing what is not lawful on the Sabbath?”
2:25 He said to them, “Have you never read what David did when he was in need and hungry, he and those with him:
2:26 how he entered God’s house in the time of Abiathar the high priest and ate the loaves of presentation, which only priests may lawfully eat, and also gave them to those who were with him?”
2:27 And He said to them, “The Sabbath was made for man, and not man for the Sabbath.
2:28 So the Son of Man is Lord even of the Sabbath.”

MARK 3

3:1 He entered a synagogue again, and a man with a withered hand was there.
3:2 They watched Him closely to see whether He would heal him on the Sabbath, so that they might accuse Him.
3:3 He said to the man with the withered hand, “Arise and come into the midst.”
3:4 He said to them, “Is it lawful on the Sabbath to do good or to do evil, to save a life or to kill?” But they remained silent.
3:5 He looked around at them with anger, grieving at the hardness of their hearts, and said to the man, “Stretch out your hand.” He stretched it out, and his hand was restored.
3:6 The Pharisees went out and immediately took counsel with the Herodians against Him, as to how they might destroy Him.
3:7 Jesus withdrew with His disciples to the sea, and a great multitude from Galilee followed; and from Judea,
3:8 Jerusalem, Idumea, beyond the Jordan, and the region of Tyre and Sidon, a great multitude heard all He was doing and came to Him.
3:9 He told His disciples to have a small boat ready for Him because of the crowd, so that they would not crush Him.
3:10 For He healed many, so that all who had afflictions pressed upon Him to touch Him.
3:11 Whenever the unclean spirits saw Him, they fell before Him and cried, “Thou art the Son of God!”
3:12 He sternly warned them not to make Him known.
3:13 He went up the mountain and called to Himself those He wanted, and they came to Him.
3:14 He appointed twelve, whom He also named apostles, to be with Him and to be sent out by Him to proclaim
3:15 and to have authority to cast out the demons.
3:16 He appointed the Twelve: Simon, to whom He gave the name Peter;
3:17 James son of Zebedee and John, James’s brother, to whom He gave the name Boanerges, that is, Sons of Thunder;
3:18 Andrew, Philip, Bartholomew, Matthew, Thomas, James son of Alphaeus, Thaddaeus, Simon the Cananaean,
3:19 and Judas Iscariot, who also handed Him over.
3:20 He came into a house, and the crowd gathered again, so that they could not even eat bread.
3:21 When His own people heard, they went out to take hold of Him, for they were saying, “He is out of His mind.”
3:22 The scribes who had come down from Jerusalem were saying, “He has Beelzebul,” and, “By the ruler of the demons He casts out the demons.”
3:23 He called them to Himself and spoke to them in parables: “How can Satan cast out Satan?
3:24 If a kingdom is divided against itself, that kingdom cannot stand.
3:25 If a house is divided against itself, that house will not be able to stand.
3:26 If Satan has risen against himself and is divided, he cannot stand, but has come to an end.
3:27 No one can enter the strong man’s house and plunder his belongings unless he first binds the strong man. Then he will plunder his house.
3:28 Truly I tell you, people will be forgiven everything—their sins and whatever blasphemies they may utter.
3:29 But whoever blasphemes against the Holy Spirit has no forgiveness for ever, but is liable for an eternal sin.”
3:30 For they were saying, “He has an unclean spirit.”
3:31 His mother and His brothers came. Standing outside, they sent to Him and called Him.
3:32 A crowd was sitting around Him. They said to Him, “Look! Thy mother and Thy brothers and Thy sisters are outside looking for Thee.”
3:33 He answered them, “Who is My mother, or who are My brothers?”
3:34 He looked around at those sitting in a circle about Him and said, “Look—My mother and My brothers!
3:35 For whoever does the will of God is My brother and sister and mother.”

MARK 4

4:1 Again He began teaching beside the sea. A very great crowd gathered around Him, so that He got into a boat on the sea and sat down, while the whole crowd was on the land beside the sea.
4:2 He taught them many things in parables, and in His teaching He said to them:
4:3 “Listen! Look, the sower went out to sow.
4:4 As he sowed, some fell beside the road, and the birds came and ate it up.
4:5 Other seed fell on rocky ground, where it had little soil, and immediately sprang up because the soil had no depth.
4:6 When the sun rose, it was scorched, and because it had no root, it withered.
4:7 Other seed fell among the thorns. The thorns grew up and choked it, and it gave no fruit.
4:8 Other seeds fell into the good soil and gave fruit, growing up and increasing; they yielded thirtyfold, sixtyfold, and a hundredfold.”
4:9 He said, “Whoever has ears to hear, let him hear.”
4:10 When He was alone, those around Him, together with the Twelve, asked Him about the parables.
4:11 He said to them, “To you the mystery of the kingdom of God has been given. But for those outside, everything comes in parables,
4:12 so that, looking, they may look and not see, and hearing, they may hear and not understand, lest they turn back and be forgiven.”
4:13 He said to them, “Do you not understand this parable? Then how will you understand all the parables?
4:14 The sower sows the word.
4:15 These are the ones beside the road where the word is sown: whenever they hear, Satan immediately comes and takes away the word sown into them.
4:16 Likewise, these are the ones sown on the rocky places: whenever they hear the word, they immediately receive it with joy,
4:17 but they have no root in themselves and last only for a time. Then, when distress or persecution comes because of the word, they immediately stumble.
4:18 Others are the ones sown among the thorns. These are the ones who have heard the word,
4:19 but the cares of the age, the deceitfulness of riches, and desires for other things enter and choke the word, and it becomes unfruitful.
4:20 Those sown on the good soil are the ones who hear the word, accept it, and bear fruit: thirtyfold, sixtyfold, and a hundredfold.”
4:21 He said to them, “Does the lamp come to be put under the modius basket, or under the bed? Is it not to be put on the lampstand?
4:22 For nothing is hidden except to be revealed, nor has anything become secret except to come into the open.
4:23 If anyone has ears to hear, let him hear.”
4:24 He said to them, “Pay attention to what you hear. With the measure you use, it will be measured to you, and more will be added to you.
4:25 For whoever has will be given more; and whoever does not have, even what he has will be taken from him.”
4:26 He said, “The kingdom of God is like this: a man casts seed on the ground,
4:27 and sleeps and rises, night and day, while the seed sprouts and grows—he himself does not know how.
4:28 The earth bears fruit of itself: first the blade, then the ear, then the full grain in the ear.
4:29 When the crop is ready, he immediately sends in the sickle, because the harvest has arrived.”
4:30 He said, “How shall we compare the kingdom of God, or in what parable shall we set it out?
4:31 It is like a mustard seed, which, when sown on the ground, is smaller than all the seeds on the earth.
4:32 Yet when sown, it grows up and becomes larger than all the garden plants, and makes great branches, so that the birds of the sky can shelter under its shade.”
4:33 With many such parables He spoke the word to them, as they were able to hear.
4:34 He did not speak to them without a parable, but privately He explained everything to His own disciples.
4:35 On that day, when evening came, He said to them, “Let us cross to the other side.”
4:36 Leaving the crowd, they took Him with them, just as He was, in the boat. Other boats were with Him.
4:37 A great windstorm arose, and the waves broke into the boat, so that the boat was already filling.
4:38 He was in the stern, asleep on the cushion. They woke Him and said to Him, “Teacher, dost Thou not care that we are perishing?”
4:39 And He awoke, rebuked the wind, and said to the sea, “Be silent; be still.” And the wind ceased, and there was a great calm.
4:40 He said to them, “Why are you cowardly? Do you still have no faith?”
4:41 They were greatly afraid and said to one another, “Who then is this, that even the wind and the sea obey Him?”

MARK 5

5:1 They came to the other side of the sea, to the country of the Gerasenes.
5:2 As He stepped out of the boat, a man with an unclean spirit immediately met Him from the tombs.
5:3 He made his dwelling among the tombs, and no one could bind him anymore, even with a chain.
5:4 He had often been bound with fetters and chains, but he had torn the chains apart and shattered the fetters. No one had the strength to subdue him.
5:5 Continually, night and day, among the tombs and on the mountains, he cried out and cut himself with stones.
5:6 Seeing Jesus from far away, he ran and bowed before Him.
5:7 He cried out with a loud voice and said, “What hast Thou to do with me, Jesus, Son of God Most High? I adjure Thee by God: do not torment me!”
5:8 For He had been saying to him, “Come out of the man, unclean spirit!”
5:9 He asked him, “What is your name?” He said to Him, “Legion is my name, because we are many.”
5:10 He begged Him earnestly not to send them out of the country.
5:11 Now a large herd of pigs was feeding there near the mountain.
5:12 They begged Him, saying, “Send us into the pigs, so that we may enter them.”
5:13 He allowed them. The unclean spirits came out and entered the pigs, and the herd rushed down the steep bank into the sea—about two thousand—and they drowned in the sea.
5:14 Those tending them fled and reported it in the city and in the fields. People came to see what had happened.
5:15 They came to Jesus and saw the demon-possessed man sitting, clothed and in his right mind—the one who had had the legion—and they were afraid.
5:16 Those who had seen it told them what had happened to the demon-possessed man and about the pigs.
5:17 They began begging Him to leave their territory.
5:18 As He was boarding the boat, the man who had been demon-possessed begged Him to let him be with Him.
5:19 He did not allow him, but said to him, “Go home to your own people and report to them how much the Lord has done for you and how He has had mercy on you.”
5:20 He went away and began proclaiming in the Decapolis how much Jesus had done for him, and everyone marveled.
5:21 When Jesus had crossed again in the boat to the other side, a large crowd gathered around Him, and He was beside the sea.
5:22 One of the synagogue rulers came, named Jairus. Seeing Him, he fell at His feet
5:23 and begged Him earnestly, saying, “My little daughter is at the point of death. Please come and lay Thy hands on her, so that she may be saved and live.”
5:24 He went with him. A large crowd followed Him and pressed against Him.
5:25 A woman who had had a flow of blood for twelve years,
5:26 and had suffered much under many physicians and spent everything she had, without any benefit but instead growing worse,
5:27 heard about Jesus. She came behind Him in the crowd and touched His garment.
5:28 For she was saying, “If I touch even His clothes, I will be saved.”
5:29 Immediately the source of her blood dried up, and she knew in her body that she was healed of the affliction.
5:30 Immediately Jesus, recognizing in Himself that power had gone out from Him, turned around in the crowd and said, “Who touched My clothes?”
5:31 His disciples said to Him, “Thou seest the crowd pressing against Thee, and Thou sayest, ‘Who touched Me?’”
5:32 He kept looking around to see the woman who had done this.
5:33 The woman, afraid and trembling, knowing what had happened to her, came and fell before Him and told Him the whole truth.
5:34 And He said to her, “Daughter, your faith has saved you; go in peace, and be well, free from your affliction.”
5:35 While He was still speaking, people came from the synagogue ruler’s house, saying, “Your daughter has died. Why trouble the Teacher any further?”
5:36 Jesus, overhearing the word being spoken, said to the synagogue ruler, “Do not fear; only believe.”
5:37 He allowed no one to accompany Him except Peter, James, and John the brother of James.
5:38 They came to the synagogue ruler’s house, and He saw a commotion, with people weeping and wailing loudly.
5:39 He went in and said to them, “Why are you making a commotion and weeping? The child has not died but is sleeping.”
5:40 They laughed at Him. But He put them all outside, took the child’s father and mother and those with Him, and went in where the child was.
5:41 Taking the child’s hand, He said to her, “Talitha koum,” which translated means, “Little girl, I say to you, arise!”
5:42 Immediately the little girl rose and walked around, for she was twelve years old. Immediately they were overcome with great amazement.
5:43 He gave them strict orders that no one should know this, and said that she should be given something to eat.

MARK 6

6:1 He left there and came to His hometown, and His disciples followed Him.
6:2 When the Sabbath came, He began teaching in the synagogue. The many who heard Him were astonished, saying, “Where did this Man get these things? What is this wisdom given to Him? And such mighty works taking place through His hands!
6:3 Is this not the carpenter, the Son of Mary and brother of James, Joses, Judas, and Simon? Are not His sisters here with us?” They took offense at Him.
6:4 Jesus said to them, “A prophet is not without honor except in his hometown, among his relatives, and in his own house.”
6:5 He could do no mighty work there, except that He laid His hands on a few sick people and healed them.
6:6 He marveled at their unbelief. Then He went around the surrounding villages, teaching.
6:7 He called the Twelve to Him and began sending them out two by two, giving them authority over the unclean spirits.
6:8 He instructed them to take nothing for the journey except a staff alone: no bread, no bag, no copper money in their belt,
6:9 but to wear sandals, and, “Do not put on two tunics.”
6:10 He said to them, “Wherever you enter a house, remain there until you leave that place.
6:11 Whatever place does not receive you, and whose people do not listen to you, shake off the dust beneath your feet as you leave there, as a testimony to them.”
6:12 They went out and proclaimed that people should repent.
6:13 They cast out many demons, and anointed many sick people with oil and healed them.
6:14 King Herod heard of it, for His name had become known. People were saying, “John the baptizer has been raised from the dead, and that is why mighty powers are at work in Him.”
6:15 Others said, “He is Elijah.” Still others said, “He is a prophet, like one of the prophets.”
6:16 But when Herod heard, he said, “John, whom I beheaded—he has been raised!”
6:17 For Herod himself had sent men to seize John and bind him in prison because of Herodias, the wife of his brother Philip, because he had married her.
6:18 For John had been saying to Herod, “It is not lawful for you to have your brother’s wife.”
6:19 Herodias held a grudge against him and wanted to kill him, but she could not.
6:20 For Herod feared John, knowing him to be a righteous and holy man, and kept him safe. When he heard him, he was greatly perplexed, yet he listened to him gladly.
6:21 An opportune day came when Herod gave a dinner on his birthday for his dignitaries, commanders of thousands, and the leading men of Galilee.
6:22 His daughter Herodias came in and danced, pleasing Herod and those reclining with him. The king said to the girl, “Ask me for whatever you want, and I will give it to you.”
6:23 He swore to her repeatedly, “Whatever you ask of me, I will give you, up to half my kingdom.”
6:24 She went out and said to her mother, “What should I ask for?” She said, “The head of John the baptizer.”
6:25 Immediately she hurried in to the king and made her request, saying, “I want you to give me at once, on a platter, the head of John the Baptist.”
6:26 The king became deeply grieved, but because of his oaths and those reclining at table, he did not want to refuse her.
6:27 Immediately the king sent an executioner and ordered him to bring John’s head. He went and beheaded him in prison,
6:28 brought his head on a platter, and gave it to the girl; the girl gave it to her mother.
6:29 When his disciples heard, they came and took his corpse and laid it in a tomb.
6:30 The apostles gathered around Jesus and reported to Him everything they had done and taught.
6:31 He said to them, “Come away by yourselves to a deserted place and rest a little.” For many were coming and going, and they did not even have an opportunity to eat.
6:32 They went away privately in the boat to a deserted place.
6:33 People saw them leaving, and many recognized them. They ran there together on foot from all the cities and arrived ahead of them.
6:34 As He stepped out, He saw a large crowd and was moved with compassion for them, because they were like sheep without a shepherd. He began teaching them many things.
6:35 When the hour was already late, His disciples came to Him and said, “The place is deserted, and the hour is already late.
6:36 Dismiss them, so that they can go into the surrounding fields and villages and buy themselves something to eat.”
6:37 He answered them, “You give them something to eat.” They said to Him, “Should we go and buy two hundred denarii worth of bread and give them something to eat?”
6:38 He said to them, “How many loaves do you have? Go and see.” When they found out, they said, “Five, and two fish.”
6:39 He ordered them to have everyone recline in dining groups on the green grass.
6:40 They reclined in rows, by hundreds and by fifties.
6:41 He took the five loaves and the two fish, looked up to heaven, pronounced a blessing, and broke the loaves. He kept giving them to His disciples to set before the people, and He divided the two fish among them all.
6:42 They all ate and were satisfied.
6:43 They took up broken pieces, twelve baskets full, and some of the fish.
6:44 Those who had eaten the loaves were five thousand men.
6:45 Immediately He compelled His disciples to board the boat and go ahead to the other side, toward Bethsaida, while He Himself dismissed the crowd.
6:46 After saying farewell to them, He went away to the mountain to pray.
6:47 When evening came, the boat was in the middle of the sea, and He was alone on the land.
6:48 Seeing them straining at the rowing, for the wind was against them, He came toward them about the fourth watch of the night, walking on the sea. He intended to pass by them.
6:49 When they saw Him walking on the sea, they thought it was a phantom and cried out,
6:50 for they all saw Him and were troubled. Immediately He spoke with them and said to them, “Take courage! It is I; do not fear.”
6:51 He climbed into the boat with them, and the wind ceased. They were utterly astounded within themselves,
6:52 for they had not understood about the loaves, but their heart was hardened.
6:53 When they crossed over to land, they came to Gennesaret and moored there.
6:54 As they stepped out of the boat, people immediately recognized Him
6:55 and ran through that whole country. They began carrying the sick around on their mats to wherever they heard He was.
6:56 Wherever He entered villages, cities, or fields, they laid the sick in the marketplaces and begged Him to let them touch even the fringe of His garment. As many as touched Him were saved.

MARK 7

7:1 The Pharisees and some of the scribes who had come from Jerusalem gathered around Him.
7:2 They saw that some of His disciples were eating their bread with common hands—that is, unwashed.
7:3 For the Pharisees and all the Jews do not eat unless they wash their hands with the fist, holding to the tradition of the elders.
7:4 On returning from the marketplace, they do not eat unless they wash themselves. There are many other things they received to observe: washings of cups, jugs, copper vessels, and couches.
7:5 The Pharisees and the scribes asked Him, “Why do Thy disciples not walk according to the tradition of the elders, but eat bread with common hands?”
7:6 He said to them, “Isaiah prophesied well about you hypocrites, as it is written: ‘This people honors Me with its lips, but their heart is far from Me.
7:7 In vain do they worship Me, teaching human commandments as teachings.’
7:8 You abandon God’s commandment and hold to human tradition.”
7:9 He said to them, “You set aside God’s commandment very well indeed, so that you may establish your tradition!
7:10 For Moses said, ‘Honor your father and your mother,’ and, ‘Let whoever speaks evil of father or mother surely die.’
7:11 But you say, ‘If a person says to his father or mother, “Whatever benefit you might receive from me is Corban”—that is, a gift—’
7:12 you no longer allow him to do anything for his father or mother,
7:13 making void the word of God by your tradition that you have handed down. You do many similar things.”
7:14 Calling the crowd to Him again, He said to them, “Listen to Me, all of you, and understand.
7:15 There is nothing outside a person that, by entering him, can defile him. Rather, the things coming out of a person are what defile the person.”
7:17 When He entered a house away from the crowd, His disciples asked Him about the parable.
7:18 He said to them, “Are you also so lacking in understanding? Do you not grasp that everything entering a person from outside cannot defile him,
7:19 because it does not enter his heart but his stomach, and goes out into the latrine?”—thus cleansing all foods.
7:20 He said, “What comes out of a person is what defiles the person.

7:24 He rose from there and went away into the territory of Tyre. He entered a house and wanted no one to know, but He could not remain hidden.

9:45 If your foot causes you to stumble, cut it off. It is better for you to enter life lame than to have two feet and be thrown into Gehenna.
9:47 If your eye causes you to stumble, cast it out. It is better for you to enter the kingdom of God with one eye than to have two eyes and be thrown into Gehenna,
9:48 where their worm does not die and the fire is not extinguished.

10:7 ‘For this reason a man will leave his father and mother and be joined to his wife,

10:44 and whoever wants to be first among you shall be slave of all.

10:51 Jesus answered him, “What do you want Me to do for you?” The blind man said to Him, “Rabbouni, that I may see again.”

13:30 Truly I tell you, this generation will certainly not pass away until all these things happen.
13:31 Heaven and earth will pass away, but My words will certainly not pass away.
13:32 But concerning that day or hour, no one knows—not even the angels in heaven, nor the Son—except the Father.
13:33 Watch out; stay awake, for you do not know when the time is.
13:34 It is like a man going abroad who left his house and gave his slaves authority, each his own work, and commanded the doorkeeper to keep watch.
13:35 Therefore keep watch, for you do not know when the master of the house is coming: in the evening, at midnight, at cockcrow, or in the morning.
13:36 Otherwise he may come suddenly and find you sleeping.
13:37 What I say to you, I say to all: keep watch!”

MARK 14

14:1 The Passover and the Feast of Unleavened Bread were two days away. The chief priests and the scribes sought how to seize Him by deceit and kill Him.
14:2 For they said, “Not during the feast, lest there be a disturbance among the people.”
14:3 While He was in Bethany, reclining in the house of Simon the leper, a woman came with an alabaster jar of very costly ointment of pure nard. She broke the alabaster jar and poured it over His head.
14:4 Some were indignant among themselves: “Why has this ointment been wasted?
14:5 For this ointment could have been sold for more than three hundred denarii and given to the poor.” They scolded her harshly.
14:6 Jesus said, “Leave her alone. Why are you troubling her? She has done a good work for Me.
14:7 For you always have the poor with you, and whenever you want, you can do good to them; but you do not always have Me.
14:8 She did what she could. She has anointed My body beforehand for burial.
14:9 Truly I tell you, wherever the gospel is proclaimed in the whole world, what she has done will also be told in remembrance of her.”
14:10 Judas Iscariot, one of the Twelve, went away to the chief priests to hand Him over to them.
14:11 When they heard, they rejoiced and promised to give him silver money. He sought how to hand Him over at a suitable time.
14:12 On the first day of Unleavened Bread, when they sacrificed the Passover, His disciples said to Him, “Where dost Thou want us to go and prepare the Passover for Thee to eat?”
14:13 He sent two of His disciples and said to them, “Go into the city, and a man carrying an earthenware jar of water will meet you. Follow him.
14:14 Wherever he enters, say to the master of the house, ‘The Teacher says: Where is My guest room, where I may eat the Passover with My disciples?’
14:15 He will show you a large upper room, furnished and ready. Prepare for us there.”
14:16 The disciples went out, came to the city, and found it just as He had told them. They prepared the Passover.
14:17 When evening came, He arrived with the Twelve.
14:18 As they reclined and ate, Jesus said, “Truly I tell you, one of you will hand Me over—one who is eating with Me.”
14:19 They began to be grieved and said to Him one by one, “Surely not I?”
14:20 He said to them, “One of the Twelve, the one dipping with Me into the dish.
14:21 For the Son of Man goes just as it is written about Him, but woe to that man through whom the Son of Man is handed over! It would have been good for that man if he had not been born.”
14:22 As they ate, He took bread, pronounced a blessing, broke it, and gave it to them, saying, “Take; this is My body.”
14:23 He took a cup, gave thanks, and gave it to them, and they all drank from it.
14:24 He said to them, “This is My blood of the covenant, poured out for many.
14:25 Truly I tell you, I will certainly no longer drink of the fruit of the vine until that day when I drink it new in the kingdom of God.”
14:26 After singing a hymn, they went out to the Mount of Olives.
14:27 Jesus said to them, “You will all stumble, because it is written, ‘I will strike the shepherd, and the sheep will be scattered.’
14:28 But after I am raised, I will go ahead of you into Galilee.”
14:29 Peter said to Him, “Even if all stumble, I will not.”
14:30 Jesus said to him, “Truly I tell you, today, this very night, before a rooster crows twice, you will deny Me three times.”
14:31 He spoke all the more insistently, “Even if I must die with Thee, I will certainly not deny Thee.” They all said the same.
14:32 They came to a place whose name was Gethsemane. He said to His disciples, “Sit here while I pray.”
14:33 He took Peter, James, and John with Him, and began to be deeply alarmed and distressed.
14:34 He said to them, “My soul is deeply grieved, to the point of death. Remain here and keep watch.”
14:35 Going forward a little, He fell on the ground and prayed that, if possible, the hour might pass from Him.
14:36 He said, “Abba, Father, all things are possible for Thee. Take this cup away from Me. Yet not what I will, but what Thou wilt.”
14:37 He came and found them sleeping, and said to Peter, “Simon, are you sleeping? Could you not keep watch for one hour?
14:38 Keep watch and pray, so that you may not enter into temptation. The spirit indeed is willing, but the flesh is weak.”
14:39 Again He went away and prayed, saying the same word.
14:40 Again He came and found them sleeping, for their eyes were weighed down, and they did not know what to answer Him.
14:41 He came the third time and said to them, “Sleep on now and rest. It is enough. The hour has come. Look, the Son of Man is being handed over into the hands of sinners.
14:42 Rise; let us go. Look, the one handing Me over has drawn near.”
14:43 Immediately, while He was still speaking, Judas, one of the Twelve, arrived, and with him a crowd with swords and clubs from the chief priests, the scribes, and the elders.
14:44 The one handing Him over had given them an agreed sign, saying, “The One I kiss is He. Seize Him and lead Him away securely.”
14:45 He came and immediately approached Him, saying, “Rabbi!” Then he kissed Him.
14:46 They laid their hands on Him and seized Him.
14:47 One of those standing nearby drew his sword, struck the high priest’s slave, and cut off his ear.
14:48 Jesus answered them, “Have you come out with swords and clubs to arrest Me as though against a robber?
14:49 Day after day I was with you in the temple, teaching, and you did not seize Me. But this is so that the Scriptures may be fulfilled.”
14:50 They all left Him and fled.
14:51 A certain young man was following Him, wearing a linen cloth over his naked body. They seized him,
14:52 but he left the linen cloth behind and fled naked.
14:53 They led Jesus away to the high priest, and all the chief priests, the elders, and the scribes assembled.
14:54 Peter followed Him from a distance, as far as inside the high priest’s courtyard. He sat with the attendants and warmed himself at the firelight.
14:55 The chief priests and the whole council sought testimony against Jesus to put Him to death, but found none.
14:56 For many bore false witness against Him, but their testimonies did not agree.
14:57 Some stood up and bore false witness against Him, saying,
14:58 “We heard Him say, ‘I will tear down this sanctuary made by hands, and in three days I will build another not made by hands.’”
14:59 Yet even in this their testimony did not agree.
14:60 The high priest stood up in the midst and questioned Jesus, saying, “Dost Thou answer nothing? What is it these men testify against Thee?”
14:61 He remained silent and answered nothing. Again the high priest questioned Him and said to Him, “Art Thou the Christ, the Son of the Blessed One?”
14:62 Jesus said, “I am. And you will see the Son of Man sitting at the right of the Power and coming with the clouds of heaven.”
14:63 The high priest tore his tunics and said, “What further need have we of witnesses?
14:64 You heard the blasphemy. What is your judgment?” They all condemned Him as deserving death.
14:65 Some began spitting on Him, covering His face, striking Him with their fists, and saying to Him, “Prophesy!” The attendants received Him with slaps.
14:66 While Peter was below in the courtyard, one of the high priest’s female slaves came.
14:67 Seeing Peter warming himself, she looked at him and said, “You too were with Jesus the Nazarene.”
14:68 He denied it, saying, “I neither know nor understand what you are saying.” He went outside into the forecourt, and a rooster crowed.
14:69 The female slave saw him and again began saying to those standing nearby, “This man is one of them.”
14:70 Again he denied it. After a little while, those standing nearby again said to Peter, “Truly you are one of them, for you are a Galilean.”
14:71 He began invoking a curse and swearing, “I do not know this Man you are speaking of.”
14:72 Immediately a rooster crowed a second time. Peter remembered the word Jesus had spoken to him: “Before a rooster crows twice, you will deny Me three times.” He broke down and wept.

MARK 15

15:1 Immediately in the morning, the chief priests held a consultation with the elders, the scribes, and the whole council. They bound Jesus, led Him away, and handed Him over to Pilate.
15:2 Pilate questioned Him, “Art Thou the King of the Jews?” He answered him, “You say so.”
15:3 The chief priests accused Him of many things.
15:4 Pilate questioned Him again, saying, “Dost Thou answer nothing? Look how many accusations they bring against Thee.”
15:5 But Jesus gave no further answer, so that Pilate marveled.
15:6 At the feast he used to release for them one prisoner whom they requested.
15:7 The man called Barabbas was imprisoned with the rebels who had committed murder in the uprising.
15:8 The crowd came up and began asking him to do as he usually did for them.
15:9 Pilate answered them, “Do you want me to release for you the King of the Jews?”
15:10 For he knew that the chief priests had handed Him over out of envy.
15:11 But the chief priests stirred up the crowd to have him release Barabbas for them instead.
15:12 Pilate answered them again, “Then what do you want me to do with the One you call the King of the Jews?”
15:13 They cried out again, “Crucify Him!”
15:14 Pilate said to them, “Why, what evil has He done?” They cried out all the more, “Crucify Him!”
15:15 Pilate, wanting to satisfy the crowd, released Barabbas for them. After having Jesus scourged, he handed Him over to be crucified.
15:16 The soldiers led Him inside the courtyard—that is, the praetorium—and called together the whole cohort.
15:17 They clothed Him in purple and, after weaving a crown of thorns, put it on Him.
15:18 They began saluting Him, “Hail, King of the Jews!”
15:19 They kept striking His head with a reed and spitting on Him, and knelt down to bow before Him.
15:20 After they had mocked Him, they stripped off the purple and put His own garments on Him. They led Him out to crucify Him.
15:21 They compelled a passerby, Simon of Cyrene, coming from the country, the father of Alexander and Rufus, to carry His cross.
15:22 They brought Him to the place Golgotha, which translated means Place of a Skull.
15:23 They offered Him wine mixed with myrrh, but He did not take it.
15:24 They crucified Him and divided His garments, casting a lot over them to determine who would take what.
15:25 It was the third hour when they crucified Him.
15:26 The inscription of the charge against Him was written: “The King of the Jews.”
15:27 They crucified two robbers with Him, one at His right and one at His left.
15:29 Those passing by reviled Him, shaking their heads and saying, “Aha! Thou who dost tear down the sanctuary and build it in three days,
15:30 save Thyself by coming down from the cross!”
15:31 Likewise the chief priests, mocking Him among themselves with the scribes, said, “He saved others; He cannot save Himself.
15:32 Let the Christ, the King of Israel, come down now from the cross, so that we may see and believe.” Those crucified with Him also reproached Him.
15:33 When the sixth hour came, darkness came over the whole land until the ninth hour.
15:34 At the ninth hour, Jesus cried out with a loud voice, “Eloi, Eloi, lema sabachthani?” which translated means, “My God, My God, why hast Thou forsaken Me?”
15:35 Some of those standing nearby heard and said, “Look, He is calling Elijah.”
15:36 Someone ran, filled a sponge with sour wine, placed it on a reed, and gave Him a drink, saying, “Leave Him; let us see whether Elijah comes to take Him down.”
15:37 Jesus let out a loud cry and breathed His last.
15:38 The curtain of the sanctuary was torn in two from top to bottom.
15:39 When the centurion standing opposite Him saw that He breathed His last in this way, he said, “Truly this Man was God’s Son.”
15:40 There were also women watching from a distance, among them Mary Magdalene, Mary the mother of James the Little and Joses, and Salome.
15:41 When He was in Galilee, they had followed Him and served Him; there were also many other women who had come up with Him to Jerusalem.
15:42 Evening had already come, and since it was Preparation—that is, the day before the Sabbath—
15:43 Joseph of Arimathea, a respected council member who himself was also awaiting the kingdom of God, came and dared to enter before Pilate and ask for Jesus’ body.
15:44 Pilate marveled that He was already dead. Calling the centurion to him, he asked whether He had died some time ago.
15:45 When he learned from the centurion, he granted the corpse to Joseph.
15:46 Joseph bought a linen cloth, took Him down, wrapped Him in the cloth, and laid Him in a tomb cut out of rock. He rolled a stone against the entrance of the tomb.
15:47 Mary Magdalene and Mary the mother of Joses watched where He was laid.

MARK 16

16:1 When the Sabbath had passed, Mary Magdalene, Mary the mother of James, and Salome bought spices so that they might come and anoint Him.
16:2 Very early on the first day of the week, they came to the tomb when the sun had risen.
16:3 They said to one another, “Who will roll the stone away from the entrance of the tomb for us?”
16:4 Looking up, they saw that the stone had been rolled away, for it was very large.
16:5 They entered the tomb and saw a young man sitting on the right, clothed in a white robe, and they were greatly alarmed.
16:6 He said to them, “Do not be alarmed. You seek Jesus the Nazarene, who was crucified. He has been raised; He is not here. Look, the place where they laid Him.
16:7 But go, tell His disciples and Peter, ‘He is going ahead of you into Galilee. There you will see Him, just as He told you.’”
16:8 They went out and fled from the tomb, for trembling and astonishment held them. They said nothing to anyone, for they were afraid.
16:9 ⟦After He had risen early on the first day of the week, He appeared first to Mary Magdalene, from whom He had cast out seven demons.
16:10 She went and reported to those who had been with Him, as they mourned and wept.
16:11 When they heard that He was alive and had been seen by her, they disbelieved.
16:12 After these things, He appeared in another form to two of them as they were walking, going into the country.
16:13 They went away and reported to the rest; they did not believe them either.
16:14 Later He appeared to the Eleven themselves as they reclined at table, and reproached their unbelief and hardness of heart because they had not believed those who had seen Him raised.
16:15 He said to them, “Go into the whole world and proclaim the gospel to all creation.
16:16 The one who believes and is baptized will be saved; the one who disbelieves will be condemned.
16:17 These signs will accompany those who believe: in My name they will cast out demons; they will speak in new tongues;
16:18 and with their hands they will pick up snakes; and if they drink anything deadly, it will certainly not harm them; they will lay hands on the sick, and they will be well.”
16:19 So the Lord Jesus, after speaking to them, was taken up into heaven and sat at the right of God.
16:20 They went out and proclaimed everywhere, while the Lord worked with them and confirmed the word through the accompanying signs.⟧
