Genesis 1

Read this chapter on staging · Book beginning

1:1 In the beginning God made heaven and earth.

1:2 Now the earth was unseen and unformed, and darkness was over the deep; and the Spirit of God was moving over the water. 1

1:3 And God said, “Let there be light.” And there was light.

1:4 And God saw that the light was good, and God divided the light from the darkness.

1:5 And God called the light Day, and the darkness He called Night. And there was evening, and there was morning: one day. 2

1:6 And God said, “Let there be a firmament in the midst of the water, and let it divide water from water.” And it was so. 3

1:7 And God made the firmament, and God divided the water beneath the firmament from the water above the firmament.

1:8 And God called the firmament Heaven. And God saw that it was good. And there was evening, and there was morning: a second day.

1:9 And God said, “Let the water beneath heaven be gathered into one place, and let the dry land appear.” And it was so. And the water beneath heaven was gathered into its gathering places, and the dry land appeared.

1:10 And God called the dry land Earth, and the gathered waters He called Seas. And God saw that it was good.

1:11 And God said, “Let the earth bring forth vegetation sowing seed according to its kind and likeness, and fruit-bearing trees upon the earth, each yielding fruit with its seed within it, according to its kind.” And it was so.

1:12 And the earth brought forth vegetation sowing seed according to its kind and likeness, and fruit-bearing trees upon the earth, each yielding fruit with its seed within it, according to its kind. And God saw that it was good.

1:13 And there was evening, and there was morning: a third day.

1:14 And God said, “Let there be lights in the firmament of heaven to give light upon the earth and to divide the day from the night. Let them serve for signs and seasons, for days and years.

1:15 And let them be lights in the firmament of heaven, to shine upon the earth.” And it was so.

1:16 And God made the two great lights: the greater light to rule the day, and the lesser light to rule the night; He made the stars also.

1:17 And God set them in the firmament of heaven to shine upon the earth,

1:18 to rule the day and the night, and to divide the light from the darkness. And God saw that it was good.

1:19 And there was evening, and there was morning: a fourth day.

1:20 And God said, “Let the waters bring forth living creatures that move, and birds that fly above the earth across the firmament of heaven.” And it was so. 4

1:21 And God made the great sea creatures, and every living creature that moves, which the waters brought forth according to their kinds, and every winged bird according to its kind. And God saw that they were good.

1:22 And God blessed them, saying, “Grow and multiply, and fill the waters in the seas; and let the birds multiply upon the earth.”

1:23 And there was evening, and there was morning: a fifth day.

1:24 And God said, “Let the earth bring forth living creatures according to their kind: four-footed animals, creeping things, and wild beasts of the earth according to their kind.” And it was so.

1:25 And God made the wild beasts of the earth according to their kind, and the cattle according to their kind, and all the creeping things of the earth according to their kinds. And God saw that they were good.

1:26 And God said, “Let Us make man in Our image and likeness; and let them rule over the fish of the sea, the birds of heaven, the cattle, all the earth, and every creeping thing that creeps upon the earth.” 5

1:27 And God made man; in the image of God He made him; male and female He made them.

1:28 And God blessed them, saying, “Grow and multiply, and fill the earth and subdue it. Rule over the fish of the sea, the birds of heaven, all the cattle, all the earth, and every creeping thing that creeps upon the earth.”

1:29 And God said, “Behold, I have given you every plant sowing seed upon all the earth, and every tree that bears fruit containing seed. These shall be yours for food.

1:30 And to every wild beast of the earth, every bird of heaven, and every creeping thing upon the earth that has life within it, I have given every green plant for food.” And it was so. 6

1:31 And God saw all that He had made, and behold, it was very good. And there was evening, and there was morning: a sixth day.

Translation notes

Current Bespoke benchmark notes

These notes accompany the accepted PDF rendering. Their source observations are retained from that document; they are not claims of a new collation.

1:2; 7:11; 8:2 — “The deep.” The shared rendering preserves the connection between the creation and flood accounts. “Was moving” in 1:2 retains the continuing action, and “the water” retains the Greek singular. “The Spirit of God” is the adopted interpretation; “a wind from God” remains a possible alternative.

1:11–12 — Vegetation and trees. “Sowing seed” preserves the Greek image. “Each” clarifies the singular description of a fruit-bearing tree within the English plural construction.

Relation to the earlier draft. The accepted benchmark uses cattle at 1:25–28, in Our image and likeness at 1:26, and it was very good at 1:31. These supersede the earlier draft wording described below; the older lexical and number questions remain available for specialist review.

Earlier Saints English notes — retained reference

These unchanged notes document the earlier draft. Their descriptions of English wording may have been superseded by the accepted Bespoke text and notes above. Earlier source-check statements report earlier work. Existing note numbers and links are preserved.

1. Genesis 1:2 Meaning. Unseen and unformed retain the two Greek descriptions. Spirit is the contextual rendering of pneuma; wind remains a lexical alternative. Moving preserves the continuing action. The capital letter is an editorial choice.

2. Genesis 1:5 Wording. One day retains the cardinal number in the Greek. The following creation days use ordinal numbers. Significant repetitions, including evening and morning, remain in the reading text.

3. Genesis 1:6 Meaning. Firmament names the source’s division between waters. No modern astronomical identification is added. The vegetation and tree-and-fruit phrasing in verses 11–12 now follows the supplied Bespoke Saints English guide provisionally. This English choice has not been freshly collated against the Greek. Kind is not a modern taxonomic rank.

4. Genesis 1:20 Meaning. Creeping things here includes aquatic life. Great sea creatures in verse 21 does not specify whales. Livestock in verses 25–28 keeps the wider ancient category and replaces cattle in the earlier Saints English trial, whose modern sense can suggest bovines alone.

5. Genesis 1:26 Meaning. Man is collective, including male and female in verse 27. The shift from singular man to plural let them have dominion is retained. The repeated according to is restored from the Greek. Capitalized Us and Our do not add an explanation of the divine plural.

6. Genesis 1:30 Wording. I have given is understood from verse 29 and repeated to complete the English sentence. The Greek life or soul expression also applies to animals; it is not restricted to human beings.

2:24 For this reason a man shall leave his father and his mother and cleave to his wife, and the two shall become one flesh.

2:25 And the two were naked, both Adam and his wife, and they were not ashamed.

7. Genesis 2:2 Text. The governing running text places completion on the sixth day and cessation on the seventh. Sixth is retained. This identifies the consulted edition’s reading, not the agreement of every Septuagint witness.

8. Genesis 2:9 Meaning. The unusual tree of knowing what is knowable of good and evil retains the fuller wording here. Verse 17 uses a shorter construction. Garden renders paradeisos throughout Genesis; garden of delight retains the additional noun at 3:23–24.

9. Genesis 2:10 Wording. Four branches renders the river’s division into four archai, literally beginnings or heads. The inherited draft’s four beginnings is preserved here in the note. The translation describes the division without supplying a geographical reconstruction.

10. Genesis 2:12 Meaning. Carbuncle and green stone preserve the inherited rendering of the two Greek stone terms. Their precise mineral identification remains uncertain. Ethiopia in verse 13 is the source’s ancient geographical designation; it is not a statement of modern national borders.

11. Genesis 2:16 Grammar. Verses 16–17 now follow the supplied guide in using you when God addresses people. The earlier draft used thou for the permission in verse 16 and ye for the prohibition and consequence in verse 17, reporting a singular-to-plural distinction even though Adam is the named recipient. That Greek-number claim remains pending selected-source verification and is not erased by the shared English pronoun. Freely eat and surely die are retained; their Greek constructions also remain subject to that review.

12. Genesis 2:18 Meaning. Let Us make is plural in the consulted Greek. A helper corresponding to him preserves the relationship expressed; verse 20 separately uses like unto him. Neither expression is expanded into a theological account of rank.

13. Genesis 2:21 Meaning. Deep sleep renders ekstasis in this sleeping context. The rib is built into a woman in verse 22, preserving the construction verb. Her husband in verse 23 retains the earlier draft’s stated possessive choice; its Greek basis remains subject to the pending source check. Woman and man express the earlier note’s naming explanation without manufacturing an English etymology. This, at last in verse 23 is the supplied guide’s contextual English rendering, not a claim that a separate Greek word expressly adds at last.

57. Genesis 15:11–17 Abram sat down with them follows the Greek verb; drove the birds away is not substituted. Trance renders ekstasis here, where the verse does not explicitly add falling asleep as 2:21 does. A smoking furnace and plural torches of fire retain the Greek scene. The exact character of the trance remains an interpretive question.
