ESTHER 1

1:1 In the second year of the reign of Artaxerxes the Great, on the first of Nisa, Mordecai son of Jair, son of Shimei, son of Kish, of the tribe of Benjamin, saw a dream.
1:1b He was a Jewish man living in the city of Susa, a great man serving in the king’s court.
1:1c He was among the captives whom Nebuchadnezzar king of Babylon had taken captive from Jerusalem with Jeconiah king of Judea.
1:1d This was his dream: Look! Voices and tumult, thunders and an earthquake, disturbance upon the earth!
1:1e Look! Two great dragons came forward, both ready to wrestle, and their cry became loud.
1:1f At their cry every nation prepared for war, to wage war against a nation of righteous people.
1:1g Look! A day of darkness and gloom, affliction and distress, suffering and great disturbance upon the earth!
1:1h The whole righteous nation was troubled, fearing its own calamities. They prepared to perish and cried out to God.
1:1i From their cry there came, as though from a small spring, a great river, much water.
1:1k Light and the sun rose, and the lowly were exalted and devoured the glorious.
1:1l When Mordecai awoke, having seen this dream and what God had resolved to do, he kept it in his heart and sought in every way to understand it until night.
1:1m Mordecai stayed quietly in the court with Gabatha and Tharra, the king’s two eunuchs who guarded the court.
1:1n He heard their discussions, investigated their intentions, and learned that they were preparing to lay hands on King Artaxerxes. He informed the king about them.
1:1o The king examined the two eunuchs, and after they confessed, they were led away.
1:1p The king wrote these matters down as a memorial, and Mordecai wrote about these matters.
1:1q The king ordered Mordecai to serve in the court and gave him gifts for these things.
1:1r Haman son of Hammedatha, a Bougaean, was held in honor before the king. He sought to harm Mordecai and his people on account of the king’s two eunuchs.
1:1s After these events, in the days of Artaxerxes—this Artaxerxes ruled a hundred and twenty-seven territories from India—
1:2 in those very days, when King Artaxerxes was enthroned in the city of Susa,
1:3 in the third year of his reign he held a reception for his friends and the other nations, the nobles of Persia and Media, and the rulers of the satraps.
1:4 After these things, after he had shown them the wealth of his kingdom and the glory of the rejoicing in his wealth for a hundred and eighty days,
1:5 when the days of the wedding were completed, the king held a drinking feast for the nations present in the city for six days, in the court of the king’s house,
1:6 which was adorned with fine linen and cotton cloths stretched on fine linen and purple cords upon gold and silver blocks, upon pillars of Parian marble and stone. There were gold and silver couches on a pavement of emerald stone, pearly stone, and Parian stone, and transparent coverings adorned with varied flowers, with roses scattered all around.
1:7 There were gold and silver drinking vessels and a garnet cup set out, reckoned at thirty thousand talents. There was abundant sweet wine, which the king himself drank.
1:8 This drinking feast did not take place according to an established law; rather, the king wished it so and ordered the stewards to carry out his will and that of the people.
1:9 Queen Astin also held a drinking feast for the women in the royal quarters, where King Artaxerxes was.
1:10 On the seventh day, when the king had become merry, he told Haman, Bazan, Tharra, Boraze, Zatholtha, Abataza, and Tharaba, the seven eunuchs who attended King Artaxerxes,
1:11 to bring the queen before him, that she might act as queen, to put the diadem on her, and to show all the rulers and the nations her beauty, because she was beautiful.
1:12 But Queen Astin did not obey his command to come with the eunuchs. The king was grieved and became angry.
1:13 He said to his friends, “Astin has spoken in this way; therefore establish law and judgment concerning this.”
1:14 Arcesaeus, Sarsathaeus, and Malesehar, the rulers of Persia and Media who were nearest the king and sat in the first places beside him, approached him
1:15 and reported to him, according to the laws, how Queen Astin must be dealt with, because she had not done what the king commanded through the eunuchs.
1:16 Muchaeus said to the king and the rulers, “Queen Astin has wronged not only the king, but also all the rulers and the king’s leaders
1:17 (for he had recounted to them the queen’s words and how she had contradicted the king). Just as she contradicted King Artaxerxes,
1:18 so today the other noblewomen, wives of the rulers of Persia and Media, on hearing what she said to the king, will likewise dare to dishonor their husbands.
1:19 If, therefore, it pleases the king, let him issue a royal command, and let it be written according to the laws of Media and Persia. Let him not act otherwise, and let the queen no longer enter his presence; let the king give her royal position to a woman better than she.
1:20 Let the law that the king makes be heard in his kingdom, and thus all the women will bestow honor on their own husbands, from poor to rich.”
1:21 The proposal pleased the king and the rulers, and the king did as Muchaeus said.
1:22 He sent throughout his kingdom, territory by territory, according to their own language, so that they would have fear in their own households.

ESTHER 2

2:1 After these events the king’s anger subsided, and he no longer remembered Astin, remembering what she had said and how he had condemned her.
2:2 The king’s attendants said, “Let young women who have not been defiled, beautiful in appearance, be sought for the king.
2:3 The king shall appoint village rulers in all the territories of his kingdom, and let them select beautiful young virgins for the women’s quarters in the city of Susa. Let them be handed over to the king’s eunuch, the guardian of the women, and let cleansing ointment and the rest of their care be provided.
2:4 The woman who pleases the king shall reign as queen in Astin’s place.” The matter pleased the king, and he did so.
2:5 There was a Jewish man in the city of Susa named Mordecai son of Jair, son of Shimei, son of Kish, of the tribe of Benjamin,
2:6 who was a captive from Jerusalem, which Nebuchadnezzar king of Babylon had taken captive.
2:7 He had a foster child, the daughter of Aminadab, his father’s brother, and her name was Esther. When her parents died, he brought her up for himself as a wife. The young woman was beautiful in appearance.
2:8 When the king’s command was heard, many young women were gathered into the care of Gai in the city of Susa, and Esther was brought to Gai, the guardian of the women.
2:9 The young woman pleased him and found favor before him. He hurried to give her the cleansing ointment, her portion of food, and the seven young women assigned to her from the royal household. He treated her and her maids well in the women’s quarters.
2:10 Esther did not disclose her ancestry or her homeland, for Mordecai had commanded her not to report them.
2:11 Every day Mordecai walked about the women’s court, watching to see what would happen to Esther.
2:12 This was the time for a young woman to enter the king’s presence: when she had completed twelve months. For thus the days of treatment were completed, with the women anointing themselves for six months with myrrh oil and for six months with spices and women’s cleansing ointments.
2:13 Then she entered the king’s presence. Whatever she requested would be handed over to her to accompany her from the women’s quarters to the royal quarters.
2:14 In the evening she entered, and toward daybreak she went back to the second women’s quarters, where Gai, the king’s eunuch and guardian of the women, was. She did not enter the king’s presence again unless summoned by name.
2:15 When the time was completed for Esther daughter of Aminadab, the brother of Mordecai’s father, to enter the king’s presence, she disregarded none of the instructions the eunuch, the guardian of the women, gave her. For Esther was finding favor with everyone who saw her.
2:16 Esther entered the presence of King Artaxerxes in the twelfth month, which is Adar, in the seventh year of his reign.
2:17 The king fell in love with Esther, and she found favor above all the virgins. He put the queen’s diadem on her.
2:18 The king held a drinking feast for all his friends and his forces for seven days. He celebrated Esther’s marriage splendidly and granted a remission to those under his rule.
2:19 Mordecai was serving in the court.
2:20 Esther did not disclose her homeland. For Mordecai had instructed her in this way: to fear God and carry out His commands, as when she was with him. Esther did not change her way of life.
2:21 The king’s two eunuchs who were chief bodyguards were grieved because Mordecai had been promoted, and they sought to kill King Artaxerxes.
2:22 The matter was made known to Mordecai, and he informed Esther; she disclosed the plot to the king.
2:23 The king examined the two eunuchs and hung them. The king ordered a commendation of Mordecai’s loyalty to be entered as a memorial in the royal library.

ESTHER 3

3:1 After these things King Artaxerxes honored Haman son of Hammedatha, a Bougaean, and exalted him; he held the first rank among all the king’s friends.
3:2 Everyone in the court bowed down before him, for the king had commanded them to do so. But Mordecai did not bow down before him.
3:3 Those in the king’s court said to Mordecai, “Mordecai, why do you disobey what the king says?”
3:4 They spoke to him every day, but he did not listen to them. They pointed out to Haman that Mordecai was opposing the king’s words. Mordecai had told them that he was a Jew.
3:5 When Haman learned that Mordecai did not bow before him, he became exceedingly angry
3:6 and resolved to wipe out all the Jews under Artaxerxes’s rule.
3:7 He made a decree in the twelfth year of Artaxerxes’s reign and cast lots, day by day and month by month, to destroy Mordecai’s race in a single day. The lot fell on the fourteenth of the month, which is Adar.
3:8 He spoke to King Artaxerxes, saying, “There is a nation scattered among the nations throughout your kingdom. Their laws are unlike those of all the nations; they disobey the king’s laws, and it is not advantageous for the king to let them be.
3:9 If it pleases the king, let him decree their destruction, and I will pay ten thousand talents of silver into the king’s treasury.”
3:10 The king removed his ring and put it into Haman’s hand to seal the written measures against the Jews.
3:11 The king said to Haman, “Keep the silver; deal with the nation as you wish.”
3:12 The king’s scribes were summoned on the thirteenth of the first month. As Haman commanded, they wrote to the generals and rulers in every territory from India to Ethiopia, the hundred and twenty-seven territories, and to the rulers of the nations in their own language, by authority of King Artaxerxes.
3:13 Letters were sent by couriers into Artaxerxes’s kingdom to wipe out the race of the Jews in a single day of the twelfth month, which is Adar, and to plunder their possessions.
3:13a This is the copy of the letter: “The great King Artaxerxes writes the following to the rulers of the hundred and twenty-seven territories from India to Ethiopia and to the district governors subject to them:
3:13b Having ruled many nations and gained control of the whole inhabited world, I have wished—not exalting myself through the boldness of authority, but always conducting myself with greater fairness and gentleness—to establish the lives of my subjects in perpetual calm, to provide a civilized kingdom open to travel to its furthest limits, and to renew the peace desired by all people.
3:13c When I asked my counselors how this might be brought to completion, Haman, who excels among us in sound judgment, has demonstrated unchanging goodwill and firm loyalty, and has received the second honor in the kingdom,
3:13d pointed out to us that among all the tribes throughout the inhabited world a certain hostile people is intermingled, opposed in its laws to every nation, continually disregarding the kings’ decrees so as not to accept the shared administration that we direct blamelessly.
3:13e We have therefore understood that this nation alone stands in continual opposition to every person, differing through its strange observance of laws, showing ill will toward our affairs, and accomplishing the worst evils so that the kingdom may not enjoy stability.
3:13f We have therefore commanded that all those indicated to you in the writings of Haman, who is appointed over our affairs and is our second father, be destroyed root and branch, with wives and children, by the swords of their enemies, without any pity or sparing, on the fourteenth of the twelfth month, Adar, of the present year,
3:13g so that those hostile both formerly and now may go down violently to Hades in a single day and thereafter leave our affairs stable and undisturbed for all time.”
3:14 The copies of the letters were posted in every territory, and all the nations were ordered to be ready for that day.
3:15 The matter was also hurried forward in Susa. The king and Haman were drinking heavily, while the city was troubled.

ESTHER 4

4:1 When Mordecai learned what was being accomplished, he tore his clothes, put on sackcloth, and sprinkled himself with ashes. Rushing out through the city square, he cried with a loud voice, “A nation that has done no wrong is being swept away!”
4:2 He came as far as the king’s gate and stood there, for he was not permitted to enter the court wearing sackcloth and ashes.
4:3 In every territory where the letters were posted, there was shouting, lamentation, and great mourning among the Jews; they spread sackcloth and ashes for themselves.
4:4 The queen’s maids and eunuchs came in and reported to her. On hearing what had happened, she was troubled and sent to clothe Mordecai and take away his sackcloth, but he would not be persuaded.
4:5 Esther summoned her eunuch Achrathaeus, who attended her, and sent him to learn the exact matter for her from Mordecai.
4:7 Mordecai told him what had happened and the promise Haman had made to the king of ten thousand talents for the treasury, in order to destroy the Jews.
4:8 He gave him the copy posted in Susa for their destruction, to show Esther. He told him to instruct her to go in, appeal to the king, and petition him concerning the people: “Remember the days of your lowliness, how you were brought up in my care. Haman, who is second to the king, has spoken against us for our death. Call upon the Lord, speak to the king concerning us, and rescue us from death.”
4:9 Achrathaeus came in and spoke all these words to her.
4:10 Esther said to Achrathaeus, “Go to Mordecai and say:
4:11 ‘All the nations of the kingdom know that any man or woman who enters the king’s inner court uninvited has no deliverance. Only the one to whom the king extends the golden sceptre will be spared. I have not been called to enter the king’s presence for these thirty days.’”
4:12 Achrathaeus reported all Esther’s words to Mordecai.
4:13 Mordecai said to Achrathaeus, “Go and tell her, ‘Esther, do not say to yourself that you alone in the kingdom will be spared beyond all the Jews.
4:14 For if you refuse to listen at this time, help and shelter will come to the Jews from elsewhere, but you and your father’s house will perish. Who knows whether you became queen for this time?’”
4:15 Esther sent the man who had come to her back to Mordecai, saying:
4:16 “Go, assemble the Jews in Susa, and all of you fast for me. Neither eat nor drink for three days, night and day; I and my maids will also go without food. Then I will enter the king’s presence contrary to the law, even if I must perish.”
4:17 Mordecai went and did everything Esther commanded him,
4:17a and he implored the Lord, remembering all the Lord’s works, and said:
4:17b “Lord, Lord, King who dost rule over all, everything is under Thine authority, and no one can oppose Thee when Thou wishest to save Israel.
4:17c For Thou madest heaven and earth and every wonderful thing beneath heaven. Thou art Lord of all, and no one can stand against Thee, the Lord.
4:17d Thou knowest all things. Thou knowest, Lord, that it was not in insolence, arrogance, or love of glory that I did this, refusing to bow before arrogant Haman. For I would gladly have kissed the soles of his feet for Israel’s salvation.
4:17e But I did this so that I would not set a human being’s glory above God’s glory. I will bow to no one except Thee, my Lord, and I will not do these things in arrogance.
4:17f Now, Lord God, King, God of Abraham, spare Thy people, for they look upon us to destroy us and have desired to destroy Thine inheritance from the beginning.
4:17g Do not overlook Thy portion, which Thou didst redeem for Thyself from the land of Egypt.
4:17h Hear my supplication, be gracious to Thine allotted people, and turn our mourning into feasting, so that while living we may sing hymns to Thy name, Lord. Do not wipe out the mouth of those who praise Thee.”
4:17i All Israel cried out with all their strength, because their death was before their eyes.
4:17k Queen Esther took refuge in the Lord, gripped by mortal anguish. She took off her glorious garments and put on garments of distress and mourning. Instead of splendid perfumes she filled her head with ashes and dung, greatly humbled her body, and filled every place of her joyful adornment with her tangled hair. She implored the Lord God of Israel and said:
4:17l “My Lord, our King, Thou art the only one. Help me, who am alone and have no helper except Thee, for my danger is at hand.
4:17m From my birth I have heard within my ancestral tribe that Thou, Lord, didst take Israel from all the nations and our fathers from all their ancestors as an everlasting inheritance, and do for them everything Thou saidst.
4:17n Now we have sinned before Thee, and Thou hast handed us over to our enemies because we glorified their gods. Thou art righteous, Lord.
4:17o Now they are not satisfied with the bitterness of our slavery, but have placed their hands upon the hands of their idols, to abolish the decree of Thy mouth, wipe out Thine inheritance, close the mouth of those who praise Thee, and extinguish the glory of Thy house and Thine altar,
4:17p and to open the mouth of nations to praise worthless things, and that a king of flesh might be admired for ever.
4:17q Do not hand over Thy sceptre, Lord, to those who do not exist. Do not let them mock us at our downfall, but turn their plan back upon them, and make an example of the one who began this against us.
4:17r Remember, Lord; make Thyself known in the time of our affliction. Give me courage, King of the gods, who dost hold sway over every dominion.
4:17s Put well-ordered speech in my mouth before the lion, and turn his heart to hatred of the one who wages war against us, to bring him and those of one mind with him to an end.
4:17t But rescue us by Thy hand, and help me, who am alone and have no one except Thee, Lord.
4:17u Thou hast knowledge of all things, and Thou knowest that I have hated the glory of the lawless and abhor the bed of the uncircumcised and of every foreigner.
4:17w Thou knowest my necessity: I abhor the sign of my exalted position upon my head on the days when I appear. I abhor it like a menstrual cloth, and I do not wear it in my days of quiet.
4:17x Thy slave woman has not eaten at Haman’s table; I have not honored the king’s drinking feast or drunk wine of libations.
4:17y Thy slave woman has had no joy from the day of my change until now except in Thee, Lord, God of Abraham.
4:17z God who is mighty over all, hear the voice of those who have lost hope, and rescue us from the hand of evildoers. Rescue me from my fear.”

ESTHER 5

5:1–1c On the third day, when she had ceased praying, she took off the garments of her devotion and clothed herself in her glory.
Having made herself splendid and called upon God, the watcher over all and Savior, she took her two maids. She leaned on one as though being pampered, while the other followed, lifting her clothing.
She herself was radiant in the bloom of her beauty, and her face was cheerful and lovely, but her heart was constricted with fear.
Passing through all the doors, she stood before the king. He was sitting on his royal throne, clothed in all his splendid apparel, entirely covered with gold and precious stones, and was exceedingly fearsome.
5:1d–1f; 5:2 He raised his face, ablaze with glory, and looked at her in the height of anger. The queen fell, her color changed as she fainted, and she bent over upon the head of the maid who went before her.
God changed the king’s spirit to gentleness. In alarm he leaped from his throne and took her into his arms until she recovered. He comforted her with peaceful words and said to her:
“What is it, Esther? I am your brother. Take courage; you shall certainly not die, for our decree is held in common. Come near.”
He raised the golden rod and laid it upon her neck. He kissed her and said, “Speak to me.”
5:2a She said to him, “I saw you, lord, like an angel of God, and my heart was troubled with fear of your glory. For you are marvelous, lord, and your face is full of grace.”
5:2b While she was speaking, she fell in her faintness. The king was troubled, and all his attendants comforted her.
5:3 The king said, “What do you want, Esther, and what is your petition? Even up to half my kingdom, it shall be yours.”
5:4 Esther said, “Today is a special day for me. If, therefore, it pleases the king, let him and Haman come to the reception I will hold today.”
5:5 The king said, “Hurry Haman here, so that we may carry out Esther’s word.” Both came to the reception Esther had spoken of.
5:6 During the drinking feast the king said to Esther, “What is it, Queen Esther? Whatever you ask shall be yours.”
5:7 She said, “My request and my petition are this:
5:8 If I have found favor before the king, let the king and Haman come tomorrow to the reception I will hold for them, and tomorrow I will do the same.”
5:9 Haman went out from the king overjoyed and rejoicing. But when Haman saw Mordecai the Jew in the court, he became exceedingly angry.
5:10 He went into his own house and summoned his friends and his wife Zosara.
5:11 He showed them his wealth and the glory the king had bestowed on him, and how he had made him first and a leader of the kingdom.
5:12 Haman said, “The queen has invited no one but me with the king to the reception, and I am invited for tomorrow.
5:13 Yet these things do not please me whenever I see Mordecai the Jew in the court.”
5:14 His wife Zosara and his friends said to him, “Let a tree fifty cubits long be cut for you. At dawn speak to the king, and let Mordecai be hung on the tree. Then enter the reception with the king and rejoice.” The proposal pleased Haman, and the tree was prepared.

ESTHER 6

6:1 But the Lord removed sleep from the king that night, and he told his teacher to bring in the written remembrances of the days and read them to him.
6:2 He found the writings recorded about Mordecai: how he had reported to the king about the king’s two eunuchs while they were on guard and sought to lay hands on Artaxerxes.
6:3 The king said, “What honor or favor have we shown Mordecai?” The king’s attendants said, “You have done nothing for him.”
6:4 While the king was inquiring about Mordecai’s loyalty, behold, Haman was in the court. The king said, “Who is in the court?” Haman had come in to ask the king to hang Mordecai on the tree he had prepared.
6:5 The king’s attendants said, “Behold, Haman is standing in the court.” The king said, “Call him.”
6:6 The king said to Haman, “What shall I do for the man whom I wish to honor?” Haman said to himself, “Whom does the king wish to honor except me?”
6:7 He said to the king, “As for a man whom the king wishes to honor,
6:8 let the king’s servants bring a fine linen robe that the king wears and a horse on which the king rides.
6:9 Let him give them to one of the king’s distinguished friends, and let that man clothe the man whom the king loves, mount him on the horse, and proclaim through the city square, ‘So shall it be for every man whom the king honors!’”
6:10 The king said to Haman, “Do just as you have said for Mordecai the Jew, who serves in the court. Do not let a word of what you have said fall away from you.”
6:11 Haman took the robe and the horse, clothed Mordecai, mounted him on the horse, went through the city square, and proclaimed, “So shall it be for every man whom the king wishes to honor!”
6:12 Mordecai returned to the court, but Haman returned to his own home, grieving and with his head covered.
6:13 Haman recounted what had happened to him to Zosara his wife and to his friends. His friends and his wife said to him, “If Mordecai is of the race of the Jews, and you have begun to be humbled before him, you will surely fall. You will certainly not be able to ward him off, because a living God is with him.”
6:14 While they were still speaking, the eunuchs arrived, hurrying Haman to the drinking feast Esther had prepared.

ESTHER 7

7:1 The king and Haman went in to drink with the queen.
7:2 On the second day, at the drinking feast, the king said to Esther, “What is it, Queen Esther? What is your petition, and what is your request? Let it be yours, up to half my kingdom.”
7:3 She answered, “If I have found favor before the king, let my life be granted at my petition, and my people at my request.
7:4 For I and my people have been sold for destruction, plunder, and slavery—we and our children as male and female slaves—and I let it pass. For the slanderer is not worthy of the king’s court.”
7:5 The king said, “Who is this who dared to do this deed?”
7:6 Esther said, “An enemy—this wicked Haman!” Haman was disturbed before the king and the queen.
7:7 The king rose from the banquet and went into the garden. Haman pleaded with the queen, for he saw that he himself was in trouble.
7:8 The king returned from the garden, and Haman had fallen upon the couch, entreating the queen. The king said, “Are you even forcing yourself on the woman in my house?” When Haman heard this, his face changed.
7:9 Bougathan, one of the eunuchs, said to the king, “Behold, Haman also prepared a tree for Mordecai, who spoke concerning the king. A tree of fifty cubits has been erected at Haman’s place.” The king said, “Let him be fastened to it.”
7:10 Haman was hung on the tree he had prepared for Mordecai. Then the king ceased from his anger.

ESTHER 8

8:1 On that very day King Artaxerxes gave Esther everything that belonged to Haman the slanderer. Mordecai was summoned by the king, for Esther had explained that he was related to her.
8:2 The king took the signet ring he had removed from Haman and gave it to Mordecai. Esther appointed Mordecai over all Haman’s possessions.
8:3 She spoke to the king again, fell at his feet, and asked him to remove Haman’s wickedness and everything he had done to the Jews.
8:4 The king extended the golden scepter to Esther, and Esther rose to stand before the king.
8:5 Esther said, “If it seems good to you, and I have found favor, let an order be sent to reverse the letters sent out by Haman, which were written for the destruction of the Jews in your kingdom.
8:6 For how shall I be able to see the affliction of my people? How shall I be able to survive the destruction of my homeland?”
8:7 The king said to Esther, “If I have given and granted you all Haman’s possessions, and have hung him on a tree because he raised his hands against the Jews, what more do you seek?
8:8 You yourselves may write in my name as seems good to you and seal it with my signet ring. For whatever is written at the king’s command and sealed with my signet ring cannot be contradicted.”
8:9 The scribes were summoned in the first month, which is Nisa, on the twenty-third day of that same year. What he commanded was written to the Jews, to the administrators, and to the rulers of the satraps from India to Ethiopia, to one hundred twenty-seven satrapies, region by region, in their own language.
8:10 It was written through the king and sealed with his signet ring, and they sent out the letters by letter carriers:
8:11 he commanded them to observe their laws in every city, to help themselves, and to deal with their adversaries and opponents as they wished,
8:12 on one day throughout Artaxerxes’s kingdom, on the thirteenth day of the twelfth month, which is Adar.
8:12a The following is a copy of the letter:
8:12b “The great King Artaxerxes, to the rulers of the regions in the one hundred twenty-seven satrapies from India to Ethiopia, and to those who are loyal to us: greetings.
8:12c “Many, repeatedly honored by the abundant kindness of their benefactors, have become arrogant. They not only seek to harm those subject to us, but, unable to bear such abundance, they also attempt to plot against their own benefactors.
8:12d They not only abolish gratitude among people, but, lifted up by the boasts of those unacquainted with goodness, they suppose they will escape the evil-hating justice of God, Who always watches over all things.
8:12e Often the persuasion of friends entrusted with managing affairs has made many appointed to authority complicit in innocent bloodshed and has surrounded them with irreparable calamities,
8:12f as these men have deceived the rulers’ innocent good intentions with their malicious false reasoning.
8:12g You can examine this, not so much from the older accounts we have handed down, as by investigating what has been impiously carried out close at hand through the pestilence of those who wield power unworthily.
8:12h You can also attend to what comes afterward, so that we may provide an undisturbed kingdom for all people in peace,
8:12i responding to changes, yet always judging the matters that come before our eyes with a more equitable response.
8:12k For Haman son of Hammedatha, a Macedonian, in truth foreign to the blood of the Persians and far removed from our kindness, was received among us as a guest.
8:12l He received the humanity we show toward every nation to such a degree that he was proclaimed our father and continued as the second person beside the royal throne, receiving homage from everyone.
8:12m But, unable to contain his arrogance, he set about depriving us of our rule and our breath of life.
8:12n With the intricate false reasoning of his schemes, he asked for the destruction of our savior and constant benefactor Mordecai, and Esther, the blameless partner in our kingship, together with their whole nation.
8:12o For he thought that by these means he could find us isolated and transfer the rule of the Persians to the Macedonians.
8:12p But we find that the Jews handed over to annihilation by this thrice-accursed man are not evildoers, but conduct their lives by most righteous laws.
8:12q They are sons of the Most High, greatest, living God, Who has guided the kingdom for us and our ancestors in the finest condition.
8:12r Therefore you will do well not to act upon the letters sent by Haman son of Hammedatha. For he himself, who accomplished these things, has been fastened to a stake at the gates of Susa with his whole household, for God, Who rules over all things, quickly repaid him with the judgment he deserved.
8:12s Display a copy of this letter in every place openly. Allow the Jews to observe their own ordinances, and strengthen them so that they may ward off those who attack them in a time of affliction, on the same day, the thirteenth of the twelfth month, Adar.
8:12t For God, Who has dominion over all things, has made this day a joy for them instead of a destruction of the chosen race.
8:12u You also, therefore, shall observe it as a notable day among your named festivals, with every festivity, so that both now and hereafter there may be deliverance for us and for the Persians loyal to us, but a remembrance of destruction for those who plot against us.
8:12x Every city or region, without exception, that does not act according to these instructions will be consumed in wrath by spear and fire. It will be made not only impassable to human beings but also most hateful to wild beasts and birds for all time.
8:13 Let the copies be displayed plainly throughout the whole kingdom, and let all the Jews be ready for this day to fight their opponents.”
8:14 The horsemen went out, hurrying to carry out what the king had said. The decree was also displayed in Susa.
8:15 Mordecai went out clothed in the royal robe, wearing a golden crown and a fine linen, purple diadem. When the people in Susa saw him, they rejoiced.
8:16 For the Jews there was light and joy.
8:17 In every city and region, wherever the decree was displayed, wherever the proclamation was displayed, there was gladness and joy for the Jews, drinking and rejoicing. Many of the nations were circumcised and lived as Jews because of fear of the Jews.

ESTHER 9

9:1 For in the twelfth month, on the thirteenth day of the month, which is Adar, the letters written by the king were at hand.
9:2 On that very day those opposed to the Jews perished. No one resisted, for they feared them.
9:3 For the rulers of the satraps, the potentates, and the royal scribes honored the Jews, for fear of Mordecai lay upon them.
9:4 For the king’s decree had arrived, to be proclaimed throughout the whole kingdom.
9:6 In the city of Susa the Jews killed five hundred men,
9:7 and Pharsannestain, Delphon, Phasga,
9:8 Phardatha, Barea, Sarbacha,
9:9 Marmasima, Arouphaion, Arsaion, and Zabouthaithan,
9:10 the ten sons of Haman son of Hammedatha the Bougaean, the enemy of the Jews; and they plundered.
9:11 On that very day the number of those who had perished in Susa was delivered to the king.
9:12 The king said to Esther, “The Jews have destroyed five hundred men in the city of Susa. How do you think they have acted in the surrounding country? What further request do you have? It will be yours.”
9:13 Esther said to the king, “Let the Jews be granted to act in the same way tomorrow, so as also to hang Haman’s ten sons.”
9:14 He permitted this to be done and published an order for the Jews of the city to hang the bodies of Haman’s sons.
9:15 The Jews in Susa gathered on the fourteenth of Adar and killed three hundred men, and they plundered nothing.
9:16 The rest of the Jews in the kingdom gathered, helped themselves, and gained rest from their enemies. For they destroyed fifteen thousand of them on the thirteenth of Adar, and they plundered nothing.
9:17 They rested on the fourteenth of that same month and observed it as a day of rest with gladness and joy.
9:18 But the Jews in the city of Susa gathered also on the fourteenth and did not rest. They also observed the fifteenth with gladness and joy.
9:19 Therefore the Jews scattered throughout all the country outside observe the fourteenth of Adar as a festive day with joy, each sending portions to a neighbor. But those living in the chief cities also observe the fifteenth of Adar as a good day of joy, sending portions to their neighbors.
9:20 Mordecai wrote these words in a book and sent it to the Jews throughout Artaxerxes’s kingdom, those near and those far away,
9:21 to establish these days as festive days and to observe the fourteenth and fifteenth of Adar—
9:22 for on these days the Jews gained rest from their enemies—and to observe the whole month in which their circumstances were changed, which was Adar, from mourning to gladness and from distress to a festive day, as festive days of wedding feasts and joy, sending portions to their friends and to the poor.
9:23 The Jews accepted it, as Mordecai had written to them:
9:24 how Haman son of Hammedatha the Macedonian had warred against them, how he had established a decree and a lot to annihilate them,
9:25 and how he had gone in to the king asking for Mordecai to be hung. But all the evils he attempted to bring upon the Jews came upon him, and he and his children were hung.
9:26 Therefore these days were called Phrourai because of the lots, since in their language they are called Phrourai, because of the words of this letter, what they suffered through these things, and what happened to them.
9:27 He established it, and the Jews accepted it for themselves, for their offspring, and for those who had joined them; nor would they act otherwise. These days were a remembrance to be kept in every generation, city, family, and region.
9:28 These days of Phrourai will be observed for all time, and their remembrance will certainly not fail from the generations.
9:29 Queen Esther daughter of Aminadab and Mordecai the Jew wrote what they had done, and the confirmation of the letter about Phrourai.
9:31 Mordecai and Queen Esther established these things for themselves, against themselves, and then also set their plan against their own health.
9:32 Esther established it by a word for ever, and it was written for a remembrance.

ESTHER 10

10:1 The king set down taxes upon the kingdom, both on land and on sea.
10:2 His strength and courage, and the wealth and glory of his kingdom—behold, they are written in the book of the kings of the Persians and Medes for a remembrance.
10:3 Mordecai acted as deputy to King Artaxerxes. He was great in the kingdom and honored by the Jews. Being beloved, he recounted the course of affairs to his whole nation.
10:3a Mordecai said, “These things came from God.
10:3b For I remembered the dream I saw concerning these matters, and not a word of them passed away.
10:3c The small spring that became a river, and there was light and sun and much water—Esther is the river, whom the king married and made queen.
10:3d The two dragons are Haman and I.
10:3e The nations are those gathered together to destroy the name of the Jews.
10:3f My nation is Israel, those who cried out to God and were saved. The Lord saved His people, and the Lord rescued us from all these evils. God performed the signs and the great wonders that had not happened among the nations.
10:3g Therefore He made two lots, one for the people of God and one for all the nations.
10:3h These two lots came to their hour and appointed time, to a day of judgment before God and among all the nations.
10:3i God remembered His people and vindicated His inheritance.
10:3k These days will be theirs in the month of Adar, on the fourteenth and fifteenth of the same month, with assembly, gladness, and joy before God, through the generations for ever among His people Israel.”
10:3l In the fourth year of the reign of Ptolemy and Cleopatra, Dositheus, who said he was a priest and a Levite, and Ptolemy his son brought in the preceding letter about Phrourai. They said it was authentic and had been translated by Lysimachus son of Ptolemy, one of those in Jerusalem.
