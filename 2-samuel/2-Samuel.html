2 Samuel 1

Read this chapter on staging

The Amalekite Reports Saul's Death

1:1After Saul died, David returned from striking Amalek. David stayed in Ziklag for two days.

1:2On the third day, behold, a man came from the camp, from Saul’s people, with his garments torn and earth on his head. When he came in to David, he fell to the ground and bowed down to him.

1:3David said to him, “Where have you come from?” He said to him, “I have come safely away from Israel’s camp.”

1:4David said to him, “What is this report? Tell me.” He said, “The people fled from the war, and many of the people have fallen and died. Saul also died, and Jonathan his son died.”

1:5David said to the young man reporting to him, “How do you know that Saul and Jonathan his son have died?”

1:6The young man reporting to him said, “By chance I happened to be on Mount Gilboa. Behold, Saul was leaning on his spear, and behold, the chariots and cavalry commanders had closed in on him.

1:7He looked behind him and saw me. He called me, and I said, ‘Here I am.’

1:8He said to me, ‘Who are you?’ I said, ‘I am an Amalekite.’

1:9He said to me, ‘Stand over me now and put me to death, for terrible darkness has seized me, for my whole soul is in me.’

1:10I stood over him and put him to death, for I knew he would not live after he had fallen. I took the royal crown on his head and the armlet on his arm, and have brought them here to my lord.”

David Mourns for Saul and Jonathan

1:11David took hold of his garments and tore them, and all the men with him tore their garments.

1:12They mourned, wept, and fasted until evening for Saul, for Jonathan his son, for Judah’s people, and for the house of Israel, because they had been struck by the sword.

The Amalekite Put to Death

1:13David said to the young man reporting to him, “Where are you from?” He said, “I am the son of an Amalekite resident alien.”

1:14David said to him, “How were you not afraid to bring your hand against the Lord’s anointed to destroy him?”

1:15David called one of his young men and said, “Come near and strike him.” He struck him, and he died.

1:16David said to him, “Your blood is on your own head, for your mouth answered against you, saying, ‘I put the Lord’s anointed to death.’”

The Lament of the Bow

1:17David lamented this lament over Saul and over Jonathan his son,

1:18and he gave orders to teach the sons of Judah—behold, it is written in the Book of the Upright—

1:19“Set up a memorial, Israel, for the dead, slain upon your heights! How the mighty have fallen!

1:20Do not report it in Gath, and do not proclaim good news in Ashkelon’s exits, lest the daughters of the foreigners rejoice, lest the daughters of the uncircumcised exult.

1:21Mountains in Gilboa, let no dew descend and no rain upon you, nor fields of firstfruits! For there the shield of the mighty was treated with contempt;
Saul’s shield was not anointed with oil.

1:22From the blood of the slain, from the fat of the mighty, Jonathan’s bow did not turn back empty, and Saul’s sword did not return empty.

1:23Saul and Jonathan, loved and beautiful, not separated, splendid in their life and not separated in their death: swifter than eagles, they proved stronger than lions.

1:24Daughters of Israel, weep for Saul, who clothed you in scarlet with your adornment, who put gold adornment upon your clothing.

1:25How the mighty have fallen in the midst of war! Jonathan lies slain upon your heights.

1:26I grieve for you, my brother Jonathan.
You were very beautiful to me;
your love for me was wonderful, beyond the love of women.

1:27How the mighty have fallen, and the equipment of war has perished!”

2 Samuel 2

Read this chapter on staging

David Anointed King over Judah

2:1After this, David inquired of the Lord, saying, “Shall I go up into one of Judah’s cities?” The Lord said to him, “Go up.” David said, “Where shall I go up?” He said, “To Hebron.”

2:2David went up there to Hebron, and both his wives, Ahinoam the Jezreelite and Abigail, the wife of Nabal the Carmelite,

2:3and the men with him, each man and his household; and they settled in Hebron’s cities.

2:4Men of Judea came and anointed David there to reign over the house of Judah. They reported to David, “The men of Jabesh in Gilead buried Saul.”

David Blesses the Men of Jabesh

2:5David sent messengers to the leaders of Jabesh in Gilead and said to them, “Blessed are you by the Lord, because you have shown this mercy to your lord, to Saul, the Lord’s anointed, and buried him and Jonathan his son.

2:6Now may the Lord show you mercy and truth. Indeed, I too will do these good things for you, because you have done this thing.

2:7Now let your hands be strong, and become men of valour, for your lord Saul has died. Indeed, the house of Judah has anointed me king over themselves.”

Ish-bosheth Made King over Israel

2:8Abner son of Ner, Saul’s commander in chief, took Ish-bosheth son of Saul and brought him up from the camp to Mahanaim.

2:9He made him king over the land of Gilead, over Thasiri, over Jezreel, over Ephraim, over Benjamin, and over all Israel.

2:10Ish-bosheth son of Saul was forty years old when he became king over Israel, and he reigned two years, except over the house of Judah, who followed David.

2:11The time David reigned in Hebron over the house of Judah was seven years and six months.

The Contest at the Pool of Gibeon

2:12Abner son of Ner and the servants of Ish-bosheth son of Saul went out from Mahanaim to Gibeon.

2:13Joab son of Zeruiah and David’s servants went out from Hebron and met them together at Gibeon’s spring. They sat down, one group on this side of Gibeon’s spring and the other on that side of the spring.

2:14Abner said to Joab, “Let the young men rise now and play before us.” Joab said, “Let them rise.”

2:15They rose and passed over by number: twelve of Benjamin’s young men belonging to Ish-bosheth son of Saul, and twelve of David’s servants.

2:16Each seized his opponent’s head with his hand and thrust his sword into his opponent’s side, and they fell together. That place was named Portion of the Plotters, which is in Gibeon.

2:17The battle was very severe that day, and Abner and Israel’s men were defeated before David’s servants.

Asahel Pursues Abner

2:18The three sons of Zeruiah were there: Joab, Abishai, and Asahel. Asahel was light on his feet, like a gazelle in the field.

2:19Asahel pursued Abner and did not turn to the right or to the left from following Abner.

2:20Abner looked behind him and said, “Are you Asahel?” He said, “It is I.”

2:21Abner said to him, “Turn to the right or to the left. Seize one of the young men and take his full armour for yourself.” But Asahel was unwilling to turn aside from following him.

2:22Abner again said to Asahel, “Withdraw from me, lest I strike you to the ground. How could I lift my face to Joab? And where do these things lead? Return to your brother Joab.”

2:23He was unwilling to withdraw. Abner struck him in the loins with the butt of the spear, and the spear passed out through his back. He fell there and died beneath him. Everyone who came to the place where Asahel had fallen and died stopped.

2:24Joab and Abishai pursued Abner. The sun was setting when they reached the hill of Amman, facing Gai, on Gibeon’s desert road.

2:25The sons of Benjamin gathered behind Abner and formed one company. They stood on the top of a hill.

Joab Sounds the Trumpet

2:26Abner called to Joab and said, “Will the sword devour for ever? Do you not know it will be bitter in the end? How long will you fail to tell the people to turn back from pursuing our brothers?”

2:27Joab said, “As the Lord lives, if you had not spoken, then from early morning the people would have gone up, each after his brother.”

2:28Joab sounded the trumpet, and all the people withdrew. They did not pursue Israel and did not continue fighting.

2:29Abner and his men went westward all that night. They crossed the Jordan, traveled the entire stretch, and came to the camp.

2:30Joab returned from following Abner and gathered all the people. Nineteen men of David’s servants, and Asahel, were counted.

2:31David’s servants struck down three hundred sixty men from the sons of Benjamin, Abner’s men, belonging to him.

2:32They carried Asahel away and buried him in his father’s tomb at Bethlehem. Joab and the men with him traveled all night, and day dawned for them in Hebron.

2 Samuel 3

Read this chapter on staging

The Sons Born to David in Hebron

3:1The war between Saul’s house and David’s house lasted a long time. David’s house grew stronger and stronger, while Saul’s house grew weaker and weaker.

3:2Sons were born to David in Hebron. His firstborn was Amnon, by Ahinoam the Jezreelite;

3:3his second was Daluia, by Abigail the Carmelite; and the third was Absalom son of Maacah, daughter of Talmai king of Geshur;

3:4the fourth was Ornia son of Phengith; and the fifth was Sabatia, by Abital;

3:5and the sixth was Ithream, by Eglah, David’s wife. These were born to David in Hebron.

Abner Turns Against the House of Saul

3:6While there was war between Saul’s house and David’s house, Abner was holding power over Saul’s house.

3:7Saul had a concubine, Rizpah daughter of Ial. Mephibosheth son of Saul said to Abner, “Why have you gone in to my father’s concubine?”

3:8Abner became very angry over Mephibosheth’s statement. Abner said to him, “Am I a dog’s head? Today I have shown mercy to the house of Saul your father, and toward brothers and acquaintances, and I have not deserted to David’s house. Yet today you call me to account for wrongdoing concerning a woman!

3:9May God do these things to Abner, and add these things to him, for just as the Lord swore to David, so I will do for him this very day:

3:10to remove the kingdom from Saul’s house and raise up David’s throne over Israel and over Judah, from Dan to Beersheba.”

3:11Mephibosheth could no longer answer Abner a word, because he feared him.

Abner Makes a Covenant with David

3:12Abner sent messengers to David at Thailam, where he was at the time, saying, “Make your covenant with me, and behold, my hand will be with you to turn all the house of Israel to you.”

3:13David said, “I will gladly make a covenant with you. But I ask one thing of you: you will not see my face unless you bring Michal daughter of Saul when you come to see my face.”

3:14David sent messengers to Mephibosheth son of Saul, saying, “Give me back my wife Michal, whom I obtained for a hundred foreskins of foreigners.”

3:15Mephibosheth sent and took her from her husband, from Paltiel son of Selles.

3:16Her husband went with her, weeping behind her as far as Barakim. Abner said to him, “Return.” He went back.

3:17Abner said to Israel’s elders, “In days past you were seeking David to reign over you.

3:18Now act, for the Lord spoke concerning David, saying, ‘By the hand of My servant David I will save Israel from the hand of foreigners and from the hand of all their enemies.’”

3:19Abner spoke in Benjamin’s hearing. Abner went to speak in David’s hearing at Hebron about everything that pleased Israel and the whole house of Benjamin.

3:20Abner came to David at Hebron with twenty men. David made a drinking feast for Abner and the men with him.

3:21Abner said to David, “I will rise now and go and gather all Israel to my lord the king. I will make a covenant with you, and you will reign over all that your soul desires.” David sent Abner away, and he went in peace.

Joab Kills Abner at the Gate

3:22Behold, David’s servants and Joab were arriving from an expedition, bringing much spoil with them. Abner was not with David in Hebron, for David had sent him away and he had departed in peace.

3:23Joab and all his army were brought in. Joab was told, “Abner son of Ner has come to David, and he has sent him away, and he departed in peace.”

3:24Joab came in to the king and said, “What is this you have done? Behold, Abner came to you! Why have you sent him away, and he has departed in peace?

3:25Do you not know the wickedness of Abner son of Ner? He came to deceive you, to learn your going out and your coming in, and to learn everything you are doing.”

3:26Joab returned from David and sent messengers after Abner. They brought him back from the well of Seiram, but David did not know.

3:27Abner returned to Hebron. Joab took him aside beside the gate to speak with him while lying in wait, and struck him there in the loins. He died for the blood of Asahel, Joab’s brother.

David Curses the House of Joab

3:28Afterward David heard and said, “I and my kingdom are for ever guiltless before the Lord of the blood of Abner son of Ner.

3:29Let it fall upon Joab’s head and upon his father’s whole house. May Joab’s house never lack someone with a seminal discharge, someone who is leprous, someone holding a staff, someone falling by the sword, or someone lacking bread.”

3:30Joab and Abishai his brother kept watch for Abner because he had put their brother Asahel to death in the battle at Gibeon.

David's Lament over Abner

3:31David said to Joab and all the people with him, “Tear your garments, put on sackcloth, and beat yourselves in mourning before Abner.” King David walked behind the bier.

3:32They buried Abner in Hebron. The king lifted his voice and wept at his grave, and all the people wept over Abner.

3:33The king lamented over Abner and said, “Should Abner die according to Nabal’s death?

3:34Your hands were not bound; your feet were not in fetters. He did not bring near, as Nabal; before men of injustice you fell.” All the people gathered to weep over him.

3:35All the people came to have David eat a funeral meal with bread while it was still day. David swore, “May God do these things to me, and add these things: unless the sun sets, I will certainly not taste bread or anything at all.”

3:36All the people understood, and everything the king did before the people pleased them.

3:37All the people and all Israel knew that day that putting Abner son of Ner to death had not come from the king.

3:38The king said to his servants, “Do you not know that a great leader has fallen this day in Israel,

3:39and that today I am a kinsman and one appointed by a king? But these men, the sons of Zeruiah, are harder than I. May the Lord repay the evildoer according to his wickedness.”

2 Samuel 4

Read this chapter on staging

Rechab and Baanah, Leaders of Bands

4:1When Mephibosheth son of Saul heard that Abner had died in Hebron, his hands grew weak, and all Israel’s men lost heart.

4:2Mephibosheth son of Saul had two men who led bands. One was named Baanah, and the second Rechab, sons of Rimmon the Beerothite, from the sons of Benjamin, for Beeroth was counted as belonging to Benjamin’s sons.

4:3The Beerothites had escaped to Gittaim and remained there as resident aliens to this day.

4:4Jonathan son of Saul had a son whose feet had been injured. He was five years old when news of Saul and Jonathan his son came from Jezreel. His nurse picked him up and fled. As she hurried and withdrew, he fell and became lame. His name was Mephibosheth.

The Murder of Mephibosheth

4:5The sons of Rimmon the Beerothite, Rechab and Baanah, went and entered Mephibosheth’s house in the heat of the day. He was sleeping in bed at midday.

4:6Behold, the woman keeping the house’s door was cleaning wheat. She became drowsy and fell asleep, and the brothers Rechab and Baanah slipped past unnoticed.

4:7They entered the house while Mephibosheth was sleeping on his bed in his bedchamber. They struck him, killed him, and removed his head. They took his head and went by the westward road all night.

4:8They brought Mephibosheth’s head to David at Hebron and said to the king, “Behold, the head of Mephibosheth son of Saul your enemy, who sought your life! The Lord has given the lord king vengeance upon his enemies, as on this day, upon Saul your enemy and his offspring.”

David Avenges the Blood

4:9David answered Rechab and his brother Baanah, the sons of Rimmon the Beerothite, and said to them, “As the Lord lives, who redeemed my life from every distress,

4:10the one who reported to me that Saul had died was like a bearer of good news before me, and I seized him and killed him in Ziklag—the one to whom I was supposed to give a reward for good news!

4:11And now wicked men have killed a righteous man in his own house, on his bed! Now I will require his blood from your hands and destroy you from the earth.”

4:12David commanded his young men, and they killed them, cut off their hands and feet, and hung them beside the spring in Hebron. They buried Mephibosheth’s head in the tomb of Abner son of Ner.

2 Samuel 5

Read this chapter on staging

David Anointed King over All Israel

5:1All Israel’s tribes came to David at Hebron and said to him, “Behold, we are your bones and your flesh.

5:2In days past, when Saul was king over us, you were the one who led Israel out and brought them in. The Lord said to you, ‘You will shepherd My people Israel, and you will be ruler over Israel.’”

5:3All Israel’s elders came to the king at Hebron. King David made a covenant with them in Hebron before the Lord, and they anointed David king over all Israel.

5:4David was thirty years old when he became king, and he reigned forty years,

5:5seven years and six months he reigned in Hebron over Judah, and thirty-three years he reigned over all Israel and Judah in Jerusalem.

The Taking of Zion

5:6David and his men went to Jerusalem against the Jebusites inhabiting the land. David was told, “You will not come in here,” for the blind and the lame resisted, saying, “David will not come in here.”

5:7David captured the stronghold of Zion, which is the city of David.

5:8David said that day, “Everyone striking a Jebusite, let him attack with a dagger both the lame and the blind and those who hate David’s soul.” Therefore they will say, “The blind and the lame shall not enter the Lord’s house.”

5:9David settled in the stronghold, and it was called the city of David. He built the city around from the citadel, and his own house.

5:10David grew greater and greater, and the Lord Almighty was with him.

Hiram's Messengers and David's House

5:11Hiram king of Tyre sent messengers to David, cedar wood, woodworkers, and stoneworkers. They built a house for David.

5:12David knew that the Lord had prepared him as king over Israel and that his kingdom had been exalted for the sake of his people Israel.

David's Children Born in Jerusalem

5:13David took more wives and concubines from Jerusalem after coming from Hebron, and David had more sons and daughters.

5:14These are the names of those born to him in Jerusalem: Shammua, Shobab, Nathan, and Solomon;

5:15Ibhar, Elishua, Nepheg, and Japhia;

5:16Elishama, Eliada, and Eliphelet;

5:16aSamae, Iessibath, Nathan, Galamaan, Iebaar, Theesous, Elphalat, Naged, Naphek, Ianatha, Leasamys, Baalimath, Eliphalath.

The Foreigners in the Valley of the Titans

5:17The foreigners heard that David had been anointed king over Israel, and all the foreigners went up to seek David. David heard and went down to the stronghold.

5:18The foreigners arrived and gathered in the Valley of the Titans.

5:19David inquired through the Lord, saying, “Shall I go up against the foreigners? Wilt Thou deliver them into my hands?” The Lord said to David, “Go up, for I will certainly deliver the foreigners into your hands.”

5:20David came from the Upper Breaches and struck the foreigners there. David said, “The Lord has broken through my enemies, the foreigners, before me as waters break through.” Therefore that place was named Upper Breaches.

5:21They abandoned their gods there, and David and the men with him took them.

The Sound in the Tops of the Grove

5:22The foreigners came up again and gathered in the Valley of the Titans.

5:23David inquired through the Lord, and the Lord said, “Do not go up to meet them. Turn away from them, and you will come upon them near the place of weeping.

5:24When you hear the sound of the closing together of the grove of weeping, then go down against them, for then the Lord will go out before you to strike in the battle with the foreigners.”

5:25David did as the Lord commanded him and struck the foreigners from Gibeon as far as the land of Gezer.

2 Samuel 6

Read this chapter on staging

The Ark Brought Up from Baal of Judah

6:1David again gathered all the young men from Israel, about seventy thousand.

6:2David and all the people with him rose and went from Judah’s rulers on an ascent to bring up from there the ark of God, over which is called the name of the Lord of hosts, enthroned upon the cherubim above it.

6:3He placed the ark of the Lord on a new cart and brought it from Abinadab’s house on the hill. Uzzah and his brothers, Abinadab’s sons, were driving the cart

6:4with the ark, and his brothers were walking before the ark.

6:5David and the sons of Israel were celebrating before the Lord on tuned instruments, with strength and songs, with lyres, harps, tambourines, cymbals, and pipes.

The Striking of Uzzah

6:6They came to Nodab’s threshing floor. Uzzah stretched out his hand toward the ark of God to hold it fast, and gripped it because the bull calf had pulled it aside, so as to hold it fast.

6:7The Lord became angry with Uzzah, and God struck him there. He died there beside the ark of the Lord, before God.

6:8David became disheartened because the Lord had made a breach against Uzzah. That place was called Breach of Uzzah to this day.

6:9David feared the Lord that day, saying, “How can the ark of the Lord come to me?”

The Ark in the House of Obed-edom

6:10David was unwilling to turn the ark of the Lord’s covenant aside to himself in the city of David. David turned it aside to the house of Obed-edom the Gittite.

6:11The ark of the Lord remained in the house of Obed-edom the Gittite for three months. The Lord blessed Obed-edom’s whole household and all that was his.

David Dances Before the Lord

6:12King David was told, “The Lord has blessed Obed-edom’s household and all that is his because of the ark of God.” David went and brought the ark of the Lord up from Obed-edom’s house to the city of David with gladness.

6:13With them were seven dancing companies carrying the ark, and a calf and a lamb as a sacrifice.

6:14David was playing on tuned instruments before the Lord, and David was clothed in a distinctive robe.

6:15David and the whole house of Israel brought up the ark of the Lord with shouting and the sound of a trumpet.

6:16As the ark reached the city of David, Michal daughter of Saul looked through the window. She saw King David dancing and playing before the Lord, and despised him in her heart.

6:17They brought the ark of the Lord in and set it in its place, inside the tent David had pitched for it. David offered whole burnt offerings and peace offerings before the Lord.

6:18When David finished offering the whole burnt offerings and peace offerings together, he blessed the people in the name of the Lord of hosts.

6:19He distributed to all the people, the whole force of Israel, from Dan to Beersheba, both men and women, to each a loaf of bread, a hearth cake, and a flat cake from a frying pan. Then all the people went away, each to his own house.

Michal Despises David

6:20David returned to bless his household. Michal daughter of Saul came out to meet David, blessed him, and said, “How glorious Israel’s king has been today, uncovering himself today before the eyes of his servants’ servant women, as one of the dancers openly uncovers himself!”

6:21David said to Michal, “I will dance before the Lord. Blessed is the Lord, who chose me above your father and above his whole house to appoint me ruler over His people, over Israel. I will celebrate and dance before the Lord,

6:22and I will uncover myself still in this way and be worthless in your eyes, and with the servant women among whom you said I was to be honoured.”

6:23Michal daughter of Saul had no child until the day of her death.

18:2David sent out the people: one third under Joab, one third under Abishai son of Zeruiah, Joab’s brother, and one third under Ittai the Gittite. David said to the people, “I too will certainly go out with you.”

18:3They said, “You must not go out. If we flee, they will pay no attention to us; if half of us die, they will pay no attention to us. You are worth ten thousand of us. It is better now for you to be a help to us in the city.”

18:4The king said to them, “I will do whatever pleases you.” The king stood beside the gate, and all the people went out by hundreds and thousands.

18:5The king commanded Joab, Abishai, and Ittai, “For my sake, spare the young man Absalom.” All the people heard the king command all the officers concerning Absalom.

The Battle in the Forest of Ephraim

18:6All the people went out into the forest to face Israel, and the battle took place in the forest of Ephraim.

18:7The people of Israel fell there before David’s servants. The crushing defeat that day was great: twenty thousand men.

18:8The battle spread there across the whole land, and the forest devoured more of the people that day than the sword devoured among them.

Absalom Caught in the Oak

18:9Absalom encountered David’s servants. Absalom was riding his mule, and the mule went under the thick growth of the great oak. His head hung in the oak, and he hung between heaven and earth, while the mule beneath him went on.

18:10A man saw it and told Joab, “I have just seen Absalom hanging in the oak.”

18:11Joab said to the man reporting to him, “You saw him! Why did you not strike him to the ground? I would have given you ten pieces of silver and a belt.”

18:12The man said to Joab, “Even if I weigh a thousand silver shekels in my hands, I will certainly not put out my hand against the king’s son. In our hearing the king commanded you, Abishai, and Ittai, ‘Guard the young man Absalom for me,

18:13so as to do no wrong against his life.’ Nothing will escape the king’s notice, and you yourself would stand opposite me.”

18:14Joab said, “I will begin this myself; I will not wait before you like this.” Joab took three darts in his hand and drove them into Absalom’s heart. While he was still alive in the heart of the oak,

18:15ten young men who carried Joab’s armour surrounded Absalom, struck him, and killed him.

18:16Joab sounded the horn, and the people turned back from pursuing Israel, for Joab spared the people.

18:17He took Absalom and threw him into a great chasm in the forest, into the great pit, and raised a very large heap of stones over him. All Israel fled, each to his tent.

18:18While still alive, Absalom set up for himself the pillar in which he was taken, and erected it to take—the pillar in the King’s Valley—for he said that he had no son to keep his name in remembrance. He called the pillar Absalom’s Hand, to this day.

Ahimaaz and Cushi Run

18:19Ahimaaz son of Zadok said, “Let me run and bring the king good news: the Lord has vindicated him against the hand of his enemies.”

18:20Joab said to him, “You are not the man to bring good news today. You may bring good news another day, but today you must not bring good news, because the king’s son is dead.”

18:21Joab said to Cushi, “Go, report to the king everything you have seen.” Cushi bowed to Joab and went out.

18:22Ahimaaz son of Zadok spoke again to Joab: “Let me too run after Cushi.” Joab said, “Why are you running, my son? Come here. There is no news that will benefit you by going.”

18:23He said, “What if I do run?” Joab told him, “Run.” Ahimaaz ran by the road of Kechar and overtook Cushi.

18:24David was sitting between the two gates. The watchman went up to the gate roof by the wall, raised his eyes, and saw a man running alone before him.

18:25The watchman called out and told the king. The king said, “If he is alone, there is good news in his mouth.” The man kept coming nearer.

18:26The watchman saw another man running. He called out by the gate, “There is another man running alone.” The king said, “He too brings good news.”

18:27The watchman said, “I recognize the first man’s running: it is like Ahimaaz son of Zadok’s.” The king said, “He is a good man; he comes with good news.”

18:28Ahimaaz called to the king, “Peace!” He bowed to the king with his face to the ground and said, “Blessed is the Lord your God, who has shut out the men who hated their hand against my lord the king.”

18:29The king asked, “Is the young man Absalom well?” Ahimaaz said, “I saw a great crowd when Joab sent the king’s servant and your servant, but I did not know what was there.”

18:30The king said, “Turn aside and stand here.” He turned aside and stood.

Is the Young Man Absalom Well?

18:31Then Cushi arrived and said to the king, “Let my lord the king receive good news: the Lord has vindicated you today against the hand of all who rose against you.”

18:32The king asked Cushi, “Is the young man Absalom well?” Cushi said, “May the enemies of my lord the king, and all who rose against him to do harm, be like that young man.”

My Son Absalom!

19:1The king was shaken and went up to the upper chamber over the gate and wept. As he went, he said, “My son Absalom! My son, my son Absalom! If only my death had been granted instead of yours—I instead of you! Absalom, my son, my son!”

19:2Joab was told, “The king is weeping and mourning over Absalom.”

19:3That day’s deliverance turned to mourning for all the people, because the people heard that day, “The king is grieving over his son.”

19:4That day the people slipped into the city as people slip away in shame when they flee in battle.

19:5The king hid his face and cried aloud, “My son Absalom! Absalom, my son!”

Joab Rebukes the King

19:6Joab came to the king in the house and said, “Today you have shamed all your servants, who rescued you today and the lives of your sons, daughters, wives, and concubines,

19:7by loving those who hate you and hating those who love you. Today you have declared that your officers and servants do not exist. I know today that if Absalom were alive and all of us dead today, that would be right in your eyes.

19:8Now rise, go out, and speak to your servants’ hearts. I swear by the Lord: if you do not go out today, not one man will stay with you tonight. Understand this for yourself: this will be worse for you than all the calamity that has come upon you from your youth until now.”

19:9The king rose and sat in the gate. All the people announced, “The king is sitting in the gate,” and all the people came before the king. Israel had fled, each to his tents.

Judah Called to Bring the King Back

19:10All the people were disputing throughout all the tribes of Israel: “King David delivered us from all our enemies and rescued us from the hand of the foreigners. Now he has fled from the land and from his kingdom because of Absalom.

19:11Absalom, whom we anointed over us, has died in battle. Why are you silent now about bringing the king back?” The word of all Israel reached the king.

19:12King David sent word to Zadok and Abiathar the priests: “Speak to the elders of Judah: ‘Why should you be the last to bring the king back to his house? The word of all Israel has reached the king.

19:13You are my brothers, my bones and my flesh. Why should you be the last to bring the king back to his house?’

19:14Say to Amasa, ‘Are you not my bone and my flesh? Now may God do so to me, and more besides, if you are not commander of the army before me always in Joab’s place.’”

19:15He turned the heart of every man of Judah as one man, and they sent word to the king: “Return, you and all your servants.”

19:16The king returned and came as far as the Jordan. The men of Judah came to Gilgal to meet the king and bring the king across the Jordan.

Shimei Meets the King

19:17Shimei son of Gera, son of the Benjaminite, from Bahurim, hurried down with the men of Judah to meet King David,

19:18with a thousand men from Benjamin beside him. Ziba, the servant of Saul’s house, was there, with his fifteen sons beside him and his twenty servants beside him. They made the Jordan ready before the king,

19:19and performed the service of bringing the king across. The ferry crossed to rouse the king’s household and do what was right in his eyes. Shimei son of Gera fell on his face before the king as he was crossing the Jordan.

19:20He said to the king, “Let my lord not reckon lawlessness. Do not remember your servant’s wrongs on the day my lord the king was leaving Jerusalem, so that the king takes them to heart.

19:21Your servant knows that I have sinned. See, I have come today ahead of all the house of Joseph, to come down and meet my lord the king.”

19:22Abishai son of Zeruiah answered, “Should Shimei not be put to death for this, since he cursed the Lord’s anointed?”

19:23David said, “What have I to do with you, sons of Zeruiah, that you should become a plotter against me today? No man of Israel will be put to death today, for I do not know whether I reign over Israel today.”

19:24The king said to Shimei, “You will certainly not die.” The king swore to him.

Mephibosheth Meets the King

19:25Mephibosheth son of Jonathan son of Saul came down to meet the king. He had not tended his feet, cut his nails, trimmed his mustache, or washed his clothes from the day the king departed until the day he returned in peace.

19:26When he entered Jerusalem to meet the king, the king said to him, “Why did you not go with me, Mephibosheth?”

19:27Mephibosheth said to him, “My lord the king, my servant deceived me. Your servant had said to him, ‘Saddle the donkey for me, so that I may ride it and go with the king,’ for your servant is lame.

19:28He schemed against your servant before my lord the king. But my lord the king is like an angel of God; do what is good in your eyes.

19:29All my father’s house were nothing but men liable to death before my lord the king, yet you placed your servant among those who eat at your table. What further right have I to cry out again to the king?”

19:30The king said to him, “Why speak any further about your affairs? I have said: you and Ziba shall divide the land.”

19:31Mephibosheth said to the king, “Let him take it all, now that my lord the king has returned to his house in peace.”

Barzillai the Gileadite

19:32Barzillai the Gileadite came down from Rogelim and crossed the Jordan with the king to escort him over the Jordan.

19:33Barzillai was a very old man, eighty years of age. He had supported the king during his stay at Mahanaim, for he was a very wealthy man.

19:34The king said to Barzillai, “Cross over with me, and I will support your old age beside me in Jerusalem.”

19:35Barzillai said to the king, “How many days remain in the years of my life, that I should go up with the king to Jerusalem?

19:36I am eighty years old today. Can I discern good from bad? Can your servant still taste what I eat or drink? Can I still hear the voices of male and female singers? Why should your servant be a further burden to my lord the king?

19:37Your servant will cross the Jordan with the king only a little way. Why should the king repay me with this repayment?

19:38Please let your servant remain, so that I may die in my own city beside my father’s and mother’s grave. Here is your servant Chimham; he will cross with my lord the king. Do for him what is good in your eyes.”

19:39The king said, “Let Chimham cross with me, and I will do for him what is good in your eyes. Whatever you choose to ask of me, I will do for you.”

19:40All the people crossed the Jordan, and the king crossed. The king warmly kissed Barzillai and blessed him, and Barzillai returned to his own place.

19:41The king crossed on to Gilgal, and Chimham crossed with him. All the people of Judah crossed with the king, and also half the people of Israel.

The Quarrel Between Israel and Judah

19:42All the men of Israel came to the king and said, “Why have our brothers, the men of Judah, stolen you away and brought the king and his household across the Jordan, with all David’s men beside him?”

19:43All the men of Judah answered the men of Israel, “The king is close to us. Why are you so angry about this? Have we eaten at the king’s expense? Has he given us a gift, or carried a contribution to us?”

19:44The men of Israel answered the men of Judah, “We have ten shares in the king, and we are firstborn compared with you. We also have a greater claim on David than you. Why have you insulted us? Was our proposal not considered first for bringing the king back to us?” But the words of the men of Judah were harsher than the words of the men of Israel.

The Revolt of Sheba Son of Bichri

20:1There was one called a lawless son, named Sheba son of Bichri, a Benjaminite. He sounded the horn and said, “We have no share in David, no inheritance in Jesse’s son. Each of you to your tents, Israel!”

20:2All the men of Israel left off following David and followed Sheba son of Bichri. But the men of Judah held fast to their king from the Jordan to Jerusalem.

20:3David came to his house in Jerusalem. The king took his ten concubines, whom he had left to guard the house, and placed them in a guarded house. He supported them but did not go in to them. They remained confined until the day of their death, living as widows.

Joab Kills Amasa

20:4The king said to Amasa, “Summon the men of Judah for me within three days, and be there yourself.”

20:5Amasa went to summon Judah, but delayed beyond the time David appointed for him.

20:6David said to Abishai, “Now Sheba son of Bichri will do us more harm than Absalom. Now take your lord’s servants with you and pursue him, lest he find fortified cities and cast a shadow over our eyes.”

20:7Joab’s men went out after him, together with the Cherethites, the Pelethites, and all the powerful men. They left Jerusalem to pursue Sheba son of Bichri.

20:8They were beside the great stone at Gibeon when Amasa came before them. Joab was wearing his belted cloak, with a sword strapped at his waist in its sheath. The sword came out and fell.

20:9Joab said to Amasa, “Are you in good health, brother?” Joab’s right hand took hold of Amasa’s beard to kiss him warmly.

20:10Amasa did not guard against the sword in Joab’s hand. Joab struck him with it in the loin, and his entrails spilled to the ground. He did not strike him a second time, and Amasa died. Joab and his brother Abishai pursued Sheba son of Bichri.

20:11One of Joab’s young men stood over Amasa and said, “Whoever favors Joab, whoever is for David—follow Joab!”

20:12Amasa lay covered in blood in the middle of the path. The man saw that all the people were stopping, so he moved Amasa off the path into a field and threw a garment over him, because he saw everyone who came upon him stop.

20:13When he was clear of the path, every man of Israel passed on after Joab to pursue Sheba son of Bichri.

The Pursuit to Abel Beth-maacah

20:14He passed through all the tribes of Israel to Abel and Beth-maacah, and all those in Charri gathered and came after him.

20:15They came and besieged him at Abel and Beth-maacah. They heaped up a siege mound against the city, and it stood at the outer defence. All the people with Joab were planning to bring down the wall.

The Wise Woman of Abel

20:16A wise woman called from the wall, “Listen, listen! Please tell Joab to come near here, and I will speak to him.”

20:17He came near her. The woman asked, “Are you Joab?” He said, “I am.” She said to him, “Hear your handmaid’s words.” Joab said, “I am listening.”

20:18She said, “Formerly they spoke a saying: ‘Inquiry was made at Abel and Dan whether the things Israel’s faithful had established had come to an end. They will certainly inquire at Abel, and thus whether they had come to an end.’

20:19I am the peaceable part of Israel’s strongholds. You seek to kill a city and a mother city in Israel. Why drown the Lord’s inheritance?”

20:20Joab answered, “Far be it from me, far be it from me to drown or destroy!

20:21That is not the matter. A man from the hill country of Ephraim, named Sheba son of Bichri, has raised his hand against King David. Give him alone to me, and I will withdraw from the city.” The woman said to Joab, “His head will be thrown to you through the wall.”

20:22The woman went to all the people and spoke to the whole city in her wisdom. She cut off Sheba son of Bichri’s head and threw it to Joab. He sounded the horn, and they dispersed from the city, each to his tents. Joab returned to the king in Jerusalem.

David’s Officers After the Revolt

20:23Joab was over all Israel’s army, and Benaiah son of Jehoiada was over the Cherethites and Pelethites,

20:24Adoniram was over tribute, and Jehoshaphat son of Ahilud was recorder,

20:25Sousa was scribe, and Zadok and Abiathar were priests.

20:26Ira the Jairite was also priest to David.

The Famine and the Gibeonites

21:1There was a famine in David’s days for three years, year after year. David sought the Lord’s face. The Lord said, “Injustice rests upon Saul and his house because of his bloody killing, in that he put the Gibeonites to death.”

21:2King David called the Gibeonites and spoke to them. The Gibeonites were not Israelites but a remnant of the Amorites. The Israelites had sworn an oath to them, yet Saul sought to strike them down in his zeal for Israel and Judah.

21:3David said to the Gibeonites, “What shall I do for you? With what shall I make atonement, so that you bless the Lord’s inheritance?”

21:4The Gibeonites said to him, “We have no claim for silver or gold against Saul or his house, nor have we anyone in Israel to put to death.” He said, “What do you say I should do for you?”

21:5They said to the king, “The man brought destruction upon us and pursued us, scheming to destroy us. Let us wipe him out, so that he does not remain anywhere within Israel’s borders.

21:6Let him give us seven men from his descendants. We will expose them to the sun before the Lord at Gibeon of Saul, chosen ones of the Lord.” The king said, “I will give them.”

21:7The king spared Mephibosheth son of Jonathan son of Saul because of the oath by the Lord between them, between David and Jonathan son of Saul.

21:8The king took Rizpah daughter of Aiah’s two sons whom she bore to Saul, Ermoni and Mephibosheth, and Michal daughter of Saul’s five sons whom she bore to Esriel son of Barzillai the Meholathite.

21:9He gave them into the Gibeonites’ hand. They exposed them to the sun on the hill before the Lord, and all seven fell together. They were put to death in the first days of harvest, at the beginning of the barley harvest.

Rizpah Keeps Watch over the Slain

21:10Rizpah daughter of Aiah took sackcloth and fixed it for herself against the rock at the beginning of the barley harvest, until water dripped on them from heaven. She did not let the birds of heaven settle on them by day or the beasts of the field by night.

21:11David was told what Rizpah daughter of Aiah, Saul’s concubine, had done. They grew weak, and Dan son of Ioa, one of the descendants of the giants, overtook them.

The Bones of Saul and Jonathan Buried

21:12David went and took Saul’s bones and the bones of his son Jonathan from the men of Jabesh-gilead. They had stolen them from the square of Beth-shan, where the foreigners had set them up on the day the foreigners struck Saul down at Gilboa.

21:13He brought Saul’s bones and the bones of his son Jonathan up from there. He gathered the bones of those who had been exposed to the sun.

21:14They buried Saul’s bones, the bones of his son Jonathan, and the bones of those exposed to the sun in Benjamin’s land, on the slope, in the tomb of Saul’s father Kish. They did everything the king commanded. Afterward God listened to the land.

The Wars with the Descendants of Rapha

21:15War arose again between the foreigners and Israel. David went down with his servants, and they fought the foreigners. David grew weak.

21:16Jesbi, one of Rapha’s descendants, had a spear weighing three hundred shekels of bronze and was belted with a club. He intended to strike David down.

21:17Abishai son of Zeruiah came to his aid, struck the foreigner, and killed him. Then David’s men swore, “You must no longer go out with us to battle, and you must certainly not extinguish Israel’s lamp.”

21:18Afterward war arose again at Gath with the foreigners. Then Sebocha the Astatothite struck down Seph, one of Rapha’s descendants.

21:19War arose at Gob with the foreigners. Elhanan son of Ariorgim the Bethlehemite struck down Goliath the Gittite, whose spear-shaft was like a weaver’s beam.

21:20War arose again at Gath. There was a man named Madon with six fingers on each hand and six toes on each foot, twenty-four in number. He too was born to Rapha.

21:21He taunted Israel, and Jonathan son of Shimei, David’s brother, struck him down.

21:22These four were born as descendants of the giants at Gath, a house belonging to Rapha, and they fell by David’s hand and by his servants’ hand.

The Song of David

22:1David spoke the words of this song to the Lord on the day the Lord rescued him from the hand of all his enemies and from Saul’s hand,

22:2and said: “Lord, my rock, my fortress, the One who delivers me for my sake!

22:3My God will be my Guard;
in Him I will trust.
My Defender, the horn of my salvation, my Helper, my refuge of salvation—Thou wilt save me from injustice.

22:4I will call upon the Lord, who is worthy of praise, and I will be saved from my enemies.

22:5The crushings of death surrounded me;
torrents of lawlessness stunned me.

22:6The birth pangs of death encircled me;
the harsh assaults of death confronted me.

22:7In my distress I will call upon the Lord;
I will cry to my God.
From His temple He will hear my voice;
my cry will be in His ears.

22:8The earth was shaken and quaked;
the foundations of heaven were shaken together and torn apart because the Lord was angry with them.

22:9Smoke rose in His anger;
fire from His mouth will devour.
Coals were kindled from Him.

22:10He bent the heavens and came down, with thick darkness beneath His feet.

22:11He sat upon cherubim and flew;
He appeared upon the wind’s wings.

22:12He made darkness His hiding place around Him;
His tent was darkness of waters.
He made it thick with clouds of air.

22:13From the brightness before Him, coals of fire were kindled.

22:14The Lord thundered from heaven;
the Most High gave His voice,

22:15and sent arrows and scattered them, lightning and threw them into confusion.

22:16The outlets of the sea appeared;
the foundations of the inhabited world were uncovered at the Lord’s rebuke, at the blast of the breath of His anger.

22:17He reached from on high and took me;
He drew me out of many waters.

22:18He rescued me from my powerful enemies, from those who hated me, for they had grown stronger than I.

22:19They confronted me on the day of my distress, but the Lord became my support,

22:20and brought me into a spacious place;
He rescued me because He delighted in me.

22:21The Lord repaid me according to my righteousness;
according to the cleanness of my hands He repaid me,

22:22for I kept the Lord’s ways and did not act impiously by departing from my God,

22:23for all His judgments were before me, and I did not turn away from His statutes.

22:24I will be blameless toward Him and guard myself beforehand against my lawlessness.

22:25The Lord will repay me according to my righteousness and according to the cleanness of my hands before His eyes.

22:26With the holy Thou wilt show Thyself holy;
with the complete man Thou wilt show Thyself complete,

22:27and with the chosen Thou wilt be chosen;
with the crooked Thou wilt show Thyself crooked.

22:28Thou wilt save the poor people, and Thou wilt bring low the eyes of the haughty.

22:29Thou art my lamp, Lord;
the Lord will light my darkness for me.

22:30By Thee I will run lightly armed;
by my God I will pass over a wall.

22:31The Mighty One—His way is blameless.
The Lord’s word is strong, refined by fire;
He is a Defender to all who trust in Him.

22:32Who is mighty apart from the Lord? Who will be a Creator apart from our God?

22:33The Mighty One strengthens me with power;
He shook my way free, blameless.

22:34He makes my feet like deer’s feet and sets me upon the heights.

22:35He trains my hands for war and broke a bronze bow with my arm.

22:36Thou gavest me the defence of my salvation;
Thy heeding made me great,

22:37making room for my steps beneath me, and my legs did not shake.

22:38I will pursue my enemies and wipe them out;
I will not turn back until I finish them.

22:39I will crush them, and they will not rise;
they will fall beneath my feet.

22:40Thou wilt strengthen me with power for war;
Thou wilt bend my attackers beneath me.

22:41Thou gavest me my enemies’ backs, those who hated me, and Thou didst put them to death.

22:42They will cry out, but there is no helper;
they will cry to the Lord, but He did not listen to them.

22:43I ground them like the dust of the earth;
like the mud of the streets I crushed them fine.

22:44Thou wilt rescue me from the battle of peoples;
Thou wilt preserve me as head of nations.
A people I did not know served me.

22:45Foreigners lied to me;

David Numbers Israel

24:1The Lord’s anger again blazed against Israel, and He incited David against them, saying, “Go, count Israel and Judah.”

24:2The king said to Joab, the commander of the force who was with him, “Please go through all Israel’s tribes, from Dan to Beersheba, and muster the people, so that I may know the people’s number.”

24:3Joab said to the king, “May the Lord your God add to the people as many as they are, and as many again, a hundredfold, while my lord the king’s eyes see it! But why does my lord the king desire this thing?”

24:4The king’s word prevailed over Joab and the army officers. Joab and the commanders of the force went out from the king’s presence to muster the people Israel.

24:5They crossed the Jordan and camped at Aroer, to the right of the city in the middle of the ravine of Gad and Eliezer,

24:6and came to Gilead and the land of Thabason, which is Adasai; they arrived at Danidan and Oudan and went around to Sidon,

24:7and came to Mapsar of Tyre and all the Hivite and Canaanite cities, and came southward through Judah to Beersheba.

24:8They traveled throughout the whole land and arrived at Jerusalem at the end of nine months and twenty days.

24:9Joab gave the king the number of the people’s muster: Israel had eight hundred thousand men of strength who drew the sword, and Judah had five hundred thousand fighting men.

The Three Choices Set Before David

24:10After David had counted the people, his heart struck him. David said to the Lord, “I have sinned greatly in what I have done. Now, Lord, please take away Thy servant’s lawlessness, for I have acted very foolishly.”

24:11David rose in the morning. The Lord’s word came to Gad the prophet, David’s seer, saying,

24:12“Go and speak to David, saying, ‘Thus says the Lord: Three things—I Myself lay them upon you. Choose one of them for yourself, and I will do it to you.’”

24:13Gad entered David’s presence and told him, saying, “Choose for yourself what shall happen: whether three years of famine come upon you in your land, or you flee three months before your enemies and they pursue you, or there are three days of death in your land. Now therefore consider and see what answer I should give the One who sent me.”

24:14David said to Gad, “I am greatly hemmed in on every side. I will fall into the Lord’s hand, for His compassions are very many; I certainly will not fall into human hands.” David chose death for himself.

The Angel at Jerusalem

24:15It was the time of the wheat harvest. The Lord sent death upon Israel from morning until the time of the morning meal; the devastation began among the people, and seventy thousand men of the people died from Dan to Beersheba.

24:16God’s angel stretched his hand toward Jerusalem to destroy it, but the Lord relented concerning the calamity and said to the angel destroying the people, “Enough! Now withdraw your hand.” The Lord’s angel was beside the threshing floor of Orna the Jebusite.

24:17When David saw the angel striking the people, he spoke to the Lord and said, “Behold, I myself have done wrong; I, the shepherd, have done evil. But these sheep—what have they done? Please let Thy hand be against me and my father’s house.”

David Buys the Threshing Floor of Orna

24:18Gad came to David that day and said to him, “Go up and establish an altar to the Lord on the threshing floor of Orna the Jebusite.”

24:19David went up according to Gad’s word, as the Lord had commanded him.

24:20Orna bent forward and saw the king and his servants passing toward him. Orna went out and bowed to the king with his face to the ground.

24:21Orna said, “Why has my lord the king come to his servant?” David said, “To acquire the threshing floor from you, to build an altar to the Lord, so that the devastation may be restrained from the people.”

24:22Orna said to David, “Let my lord the king take and offer to the Lord what is good in his eyes. Look: the oxen for the whole burnt offering, and the wheels and oxen’s equipment for wood.”

24:23Orna gave everything to the king. Orna said to the king, “May the Lord your God bless you.”

24:24The king said to Orna, “No! I will certainly acquire it from you in exchange; I will not offer the Lord my God a whole burnt offering that costs me nothing.” David acquired the threshing floor and the oxen for fifty shekels of silver.

24:25David built an altar to the Lord there and offered whole burnt offerings and peace offerings. Solomon enlarged the altar afterward, because it had been small at first. The Lord listened to the land, and the devastation was restrained from Israel.
