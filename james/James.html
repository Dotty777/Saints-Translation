James 1

Read this chapter on staging

Greeting

1:1James, a servant of God and of the Lord Jesus Christ, to the twelve tribes in the dispersion: greetings.

Trials and Wisdom

1:2Count it all joy, my brothers, whenever you encounter trials of various kinds,

1:3knowing that the testing of your faith produces endurance.

1:4And let endurance have its complete work, so that you may be complete and whole, lacking in nothing.

1:5But if any of you lacks wisdom, let him ask God, who gives to all generously and without reproach, and it will be given to him.

1:6But let him ask in faith, doubting nothing, for the one who doubts is like a wave of the sea, driven and tossed by the wind.

1:7For that person must not suppose that he will receive anything from the Lord,

1:8a man of divided mind, unstable in all his ways.

1:9Let the lowly brother boast in his exaltation,

1:10and the rich person in his humiliation, because he will pass away like a flower of grass.

1:11For the sun rose with its scorching heat and dried the grass, and its flower fell, and the beauty of its appearance perished. So also the rich person will wither in his pursuits.

Temptation and Every Good Gift

1:12Blessed is the man who endures a trial, because, having been approved, he will receive the crown of life that He promised to those who love Him.

1:13Let no one who is tempted say, “I am being tempted by God.” For God cannot be tempted by evil, and He Himself tempts no one.

1:14But each person is tempted when drawn away and enticed by his own desire.

1:15Then desire, after conceiving, gives birth to sin; and sin, when fully grown, gives birth to death.

1:16Do not be led astray, my beloved brothers.

1:17Every good act of giving and every complete gift is from above, coming down from the Father of lights, with whom there is no variation or shadow from turning.

1:18By His will He gave us birth through the word of truth, so that we might be a kind of firstfruits of His creatures.

Hearers and Doers of the Word

1:19Know this, my beloved brothers: let every person be quick to hear, slow to speak, slow to anger,

1:20for a man’s anger does not accomplish the righteousness of God.

1:21Therefore, putting away every impurity and the abundance of evil, receive with gentleness the implanted word, which is able to save your souls.

1:22But become doers of the word, and not hearers only, deceiving yourselves.

1:23For if anyone is a hearer of the word and not a doer, he is like a man looking at his natural face in a mirror,

1:24for he looked at himself and went away and immediately forgot what he was like.

1:25But the one who looks closely into the complete law, the law of freedom, and continues in it—not a forgetful hearer but a doer of the work—will be blessed in what he does.

Pure Religion

1:26If anyone thinks he is religious while not bridling his tongue but deceiving his heart, that person’s religion is futile.

1:27Religion that is pure and undefiled before God the Father is this: to care for orphans and widows in their affliction, and to keep oneself unstained by the world.

James 2

Read this chapter on staging

Do Not Hold the Faith with Favoritism

2:1My brothers, do not hold the faith of our Lord Jesus Christ, the Lord of glory, with acts of favoritism.

2:2For if a man with gold rings and splendid clothing enters your gathering, and a poor person in filthy clothing also enters,

2:3and you pay attention to the one wearing splendid clothing and say, “You sit here in a good place,” but say to the poor person, “You stand, or sit there beneath my footstool,”

2:4have you not made distinctions among yourselves and become judges with evil thoughts?

2:5Listen, my beloved brothers: has not God chosen those poor in the world to be rich in faith and heirs of the kingdom He promised to those who love Him?

2:6But you have dishonored the poor person. Is it not the rich who oppress you and drag you into courts?

2:7Do they not blaspheme the good name called over you?

The Royal Law

2:8If, however, you fulfill the royal law according to the Scripture, “You shall love your neighbor as yourself,” you do well.

2:9But if you show favoritism, you commit sin and are convicted by the law as transgressors.

2:10For whoever keeps the whole law but stumbles in one point has become accountable for all of it.

2:11For He who said, “Do not commit adultery,” also said, “Do not murder.” Now if you do not commit adultery but do murder, you have become a transgressor of the law.

2:12So speak and so act as those who are going to be judged through a law of freedom.

2:13For judgment is without mercy to the one who has shown no mercy; mercy triumphs over judgment.

What Good Is It If Someone Says He Has Faith?

2:14What good is it, my brothers, if someone says he has faith but does not have works? Can that faith save him?

2:15If a brother or sister is without clothing and lacks daily food,

2:16and one of you says to them, “Go in peace; be warmed and filled,” but you do not give them what the body needs, what good is it?

2:17So also faith, if it does not have works, is dead by itself.

2:18But someone will say, “You have faith, and I have works.” Show me your faith apart from works, and I will show you faith by my works.

2:19You believe that God is one. You do well. Even the demons believe—and shudder.

2:20But do you want to know, empty man, that faith apart from works is idle?

2:21Was not Abraham our father justified by works when he offered Isaac his son upon the altar?

2:22You see that faith was working together with his works, and by the works faith was brought to completion,

2:23and the Scripture was fulfilled that says, “Abraham believed God, and it was reckoned to him as righteousness,” and he was called a friend of God.

2:24You see that a person is justified by works and not by faith alone.

2:25And likewise was not Rahab the prostitute also justified by works, when she welcomed the messengers and sent them out by another way?

2:26For just as the body without spirit is dead, so also faith without works is dead.

James 3

Read this chapter on staging

Taming the Tongue

3:1Not many of you should become teachers, my brothers, knowing that we will receive a greater judgment.

3:2For we all stumble in many things. If anyone does not stumble in speech, that person is a complete man, able to bridle his whole body also.

3:3Now if we put bits into horses’ mouths so that they obey us, we guide their whole body also.

3:4Look also at ships: although so large and driven by harsh winds, they are guided by a very small rudder wherever the pilot intends.

3:5So also the tongue is a small member and boasts great things. Look how small a fire sets so great a forest ablaze!

3:6And the tongue is a fire. The tongue is established among our members as a world of unrighteousness, staining the whole body, setting the wheel of life ablaze, and itself set ablaze by Gehenna.

3:7For every kind of wild animal and bird, of creeping creature and sea creature, is tamed and has been tamed by humankind;

3:8but no human being can tame the tongue. It is a restless evil, full of deadly poison.

3:9With it we bless the Lord and Father, and with it we curse human beings who have been made in God’s likeness.

3:10From the same mouth come blessing and cursing. My brothers, these things ought not to happen this way.

3:11Does a spring pour out sweet and bitter water from the same opening?

3:12My brothers, can a fig tree produce olives, or a vine figs? Neither can salt water produce sweet water.

Wisdom from Above

3:13Who is wise and understanding among you? Let him show his works by good conduct in the gentleness of wisdom.

3:14But if you have bitter jealousy and selfish ambition in your heart, do not boast against the truth and lie against it.

3:15This is not the wisdom coming down from above; it is earthly, merely natural, demonic.

3:16For where there are jealousy and selfish ambition, there are disorder and every foul practice.

3:17But the wisdom from above is first pure, then peaceable, considerate, open to persuasion, full of mercy and good fruits, impartial, without hypocrisy.

3:18And a fruit of righteousness is sown in peace for those who make peace.

James 4

Read this chapter on staging

From Where Come Wars and Fights?

4:1Where do wars come from, and where do fights among you come from? Is it not from here—from your pleasures that wage war in your members?

4:2You desire and do not have; you murder and envy and cannot obtain. You fight and wage war. You do not have because you do not ask.

4:3You ask and do not receive, because you ask badly, so that you may spend it on your pleasures.

4:4Adulteresses! Do you not know that friendship with the world is hostility toward God? Whoever therefore wishes to be a friend of the world makes himself an enemy of God.

4:5Or do you think Scripture says in vain, “He yearns jealously for the spirit that He made to dwell in us”?

4:6But He gives greater grace. Therefore it says, “God opposes the proud, but gives grace to the humble.”

Submit to God

4:7Submit yourselves therefore to God; but resist the devil, and he will flee from you.

4:8Draw near to God, and He will draw near to you. Cleanse your hands, sinners, and purify your hearts, you of divided mind.

4:9Be wretched and mourn and weep. Let your laughter be turned into mourning and your joy into dejection.

4:10Humble yourselves before the Lord, and He will exalt you.

Do Not Speak Against a Brother

4:11Do not speak against one another, brothers. The one who speaks against a brother or judges his brother speaks against the law and judges the law. But if you judge the law, you are not a doer of the law but a judge.

4:12There is one Lawgiver and Judge, the One able to save and destroy. But who are you to judge your neighbor?

Boasting About Tomorrow

4:13Come now, you who say, “Today or tomorrow we will go to this city, spend a year there, trade, and make a profit”—

4:14you who do not know what tomorrow brings. What is your life? For you are a vapor, appearing for a little while and then disappearing.

4:15Instead, you ought to say, “If the Lord wills, we shall both live and do this or that.”

4:16But now you boast in your pretensions. All such boasting is evil.

4:17Therefore, if someone knows the good he ought to do and does not do it, it is sin for him.

James 5

Read this chapter on staging

A Warning to the Rich

5:1Come now, you rich people: weep and howl over the miseries coming upon you.

5:2Your riches have rotted, and your garments have become moth-eaten.

5:3Your gold and silver have corroded, and their corrosion will be a testimony against you and will devour your flesh like fire. You have stored up treasure in the last days.

5:4Look, the wages of the laborers who reaped your fields, withheld by you, cry out; and the cries of the harvesters have entered the ears of the Lord Sabaoth.

5:5You lived luxuriously on the earth and indulged yourselves; you fattened your hearts on a day of slaughter.

5:6You condemned; you murdered the righteous one. He does not oppose you.

Patience Until the Lord’s Coming

5:7Be patient, therefore, brothers, until the coming of the Lord. Look, the farmer waits for the precious fruit of the earth, being patient over it until he receives the early and late rain.

5:8You too be patient; strengthen your hearts, because the coming of the Lord has drawn near.

5:9Do not groan against one another, brothers, so that you may not be judged. Look, the Judge stands before the doors.

5:10Brothers, take as an example of suffering and patience the prophets who spoke in the Lord’s name.

5:11Look, we call those who endured blessed. You heard of Job’s endurance and saw the outcome from the Lord, because the Lord is full of compassion and merciful.

Let Your Yes Be Yes

5:12But above all, my brothers, do not swear, either by heaven or by earth or by any other oath. Let your yes be yes, and your no be no, so that you may not fall under judgment.

The Prayer of Faith

5:13Is anyone among you suffering? Let him pray. Is anyone cheerful? Let him sing praise.

5:14Is anyone among you sick? Let him summon the elders of the Church, and let them pray over him, anointing him with oil in the Lord’s name.

5:15And the prayer of faith will save the weary sick person, and the Lord will raise him up; and if he has committed sins, he will be forgiven.

5:16Therefore confess your sins to one another and pray for one another, so that you may be healed. A righteous person’s petition has great strength when it is at work.

5:17Elijah was a human being with experiences like ours, and he prayed earnestly that it might not rain; and it did not rain upon the earth for three years and six months.

5:18And he prayed again, and heaven gave rain, and the earth brought forth its fruit.

My Brothers, If Anyone Among You Wanders

5:19My brothers, if anyone among you is led astray from the truth and someone turns him back,

5:20let him know that the one who turns a sinner back from the error of his way will save his soul from death and cover a multitude of sins.

Current Bespoke editorial notes

The newer Bespoke Saints English (1).pdf governs, with accepted Genesis 1–11 as the English benchmark. It supersedes the older Founding Style Guide where their pronoun rules conflict: Thou/Thee/Thy/Thine address God directly; ordinary address uses you/your.

The fire simile in 5:3 now modifies devouring flesh, matching displayed NA28 punctuation, rather than treasure storage. In 5:6 He does not oppose you replaces the inherited question, matching displayed NA28 declarative punctuation. Modern verbs and ordinary you address restored. Lord Sabaoth, the day of slaughter, early/late rain, doors, Job, the sick person, oil, confession and prayer remain. The prayer participle in 5:16 stays when it is at work without adding emotional fervor; Elijah’s emphatic prayer and three years six months remain.
