JONAH 1

1:1 The word of the Lord came to Jonah, the son of Amittai, saying:
1:2 “Rise and go to Nineveh, the great city, and proclaim in it, for the outcry of its wickedness has come up to Me.”
1:3 But Jonah rose to flee to Tarshish from the presence of the Lord. He went down to Joppa, found a ship traveling to Tarshish, paid its fare and boarded it, to sail with them to Tarshish from the presence of the Lord.
1:4 The Lord stirred up a wind upon the sea, and there was a great surge on the sea; the ship was in danger of being shattered.
1:5 The sailors were afraid, and each was crying out to their god. They threw the equipment in the ship into the sea, to lighten their load. But Jonah had gone down into the hollow of the ship, and was sleeping and snoring.
1:6 The ship’s officer approached him and said, “Why are you snoring? Rise and call upon your god, so that the god may bring us safely through and we may not perish.”
1:7 Each said to his neighbor, “Come, let us cast lots and learn on whose account this calamity is among us.” They cast lots, and the lot fell on Jonah.
1:8 They said to him, “Tell us on whose account this calamity is among us. What is your occupation? Where do you come from? What country and what people are you from?”
1:9 He said to them, “I am a servant of the Lord, and I worship the Lord, the God of heaven, who made the sea and the dry land.”
1:10 The men were greatly afraid and said to him, “What is this you have done?” For the men knew that he was fleeing from the presence of the Lord, because he had told them.
1:11 They said to him, “What shall we do to you, so that the sea may subside for us?” For the sea kept raising a greater surge.
1:12 Jonah said to them, “Lift me up and throw me into the sea; then the sea will subside for you. For I know that this great surge is upon you because of me.”
1:13 But the men strained to return to land and could not, for the sea kept rising more fiercely against them.
1:14 They cried out to the Lord and said, “By no means, Lord! May we not perish for this man’s life, and do not lay innocent blood upon us, for Thou, Lord, hast done as Thou didst wish.”
1:15 They took Jonah and threw him out into the sea, and the sea stopped its tossing.
1:16 The men feared the Lord with great fear, offered a sacrifice to the Lord and made vows.

JONAH 2

2:1 The Lord commanded a great sea creature to swallow Jonah, and Jonah was in the sea creature’s belly three days and three nights.
2:2 Jonah prayed to the Lord his God from the sea creature’s belly,
2:3 and said: “In my affliction I cried to the Lord my God, and He listened to me. From the belly of Hades Thou didst hear the voice of my cry.
2:4 Thou didst cast me into the depths of the heart of the sea, and rivers encircled me. All Thy billows and Thy waves passed over me.
2:5 I said, ‘I have been thrust away from Thine eyes. Shall I again look toward Thy holy sanctuary?’
2:6 Water poured around me up to my life; the uttermost abyss encircled me; my head sank into clefts of mountains.
2:7 I descended into a land whose bars are everlasting restraints; and let the ruin of my life rise up, Lord my God.
2:8 As my life was failing me, I remembered the Lord; may my prayer come to Thee, into Thy holy sanctuary.
2:9 Those who guard vain and false things abandoned their mercy.
2:10 But I, with a voice of praise and thanksgiving, will sacrifice to Thee. Whatever I vowed I will repay to Thee, for deliverance to the Lord!”
2:11 The sea creature was commanded, and it cast Jonah out onto dry land.

JONAH 3

3:1 The word of the Lord came to Jonah a second time, saying:
3:2 “Rise and go to Nineveh, the great city, and proclaim in it according to the former proclamation that I spoke to you.”
3:3 Jonah rose and went to Nineveh as the Lord had spoken. Now Nineveh was a city great before God, about a three-day journey.
3:4 And Jonah began to enter the city, about a day’s journey, and he proclaimed and said, “Three more days, and Nineveh shall be overthrown.”
3:5 The people of Nineveh believed God, proclaimed a fast and put on sackcloth, from their greatest to their least.
3:6 The word reached the king of Nineveh. He rose from his throne, took his robe off himself, wrapped himself in sackcloth and sat in ashes.
3:7 It was proclaimed and declared in Nineveh from the king and his nobles: “Let people and livestock, cattle and sheep, taste nothing; let them not graze or drink water.”
3:8 People and livestock clothed themselves in sackcloth, and they cried earnestly to God. Each turned away from his evil way and from the injustice in their hands, saying:
3:9 “Who knows whether God will relent and turn from His fierce anger, so that we certainly do not perish?”
3:10 God saw their deeds, that they had turned from their evil ways. God relented concerning the calamity He had said He would bring upon them, and He did not do it.

JONAH 4

4:1 Jonah was grieved with great grief and was disturbed.
4:2 He prayed to the Lord and said, “O Lord, were these not my words while I was still in my own land? That is why I was quick to flee to Tarshish, for I knew that Thou art merciful and compassionate, patient, abundant in mercy, and relenting concerning calamities.
4:3 And now, Master Lord, take my life from me, for it is better for me to die than to live.”
4:4 The Lord said to Jonah, “Are you deeply grieved?”
4:5 Jonah went out of the city and sat opposite the city. He made himself a shelter there and sat beneath it in the shade, until he could see what would happen to the city.
4:6 The Lord God commanded a gourd, and it grew up above Jonah’s head, to provide shade over his head and shelter him from his troubles. Jonah rejoiced over the gourd with great joy.
4:7 God commanded a worm at dawn the next day; it struck the gourd, and it dried up.
4:8 As the sun rose, God commanded a burning, scorching wind. The sun struck Jonah’s head; he lost heart, renounced his life and said, “It is better for me to die than to live.”
4:9 God said to Jonah, “Are you deeply grieved about the gourd?” He said, “I am deeply grieved, even to death.”
4:10 The Lord said, “You spared the gourd, over which you did not toil and which you did not nurture, which came to be in a night and perished in a night.
4:11 And shall I not spare Nineveh, the great city, in which dwell more than one hundred and twenty thousand people who do not know their right from their left, and much livestock?”
