LUKE 1

1:1 Since many have undertaken to compile an account of the events fulfilled among us,
1:2 just as those who from the beginning were eyewitnesses and servants of the word handed them down to us,
1:3 it seemed good to me also, having followed everything carefully from the beginning, to write an orderly account for you, most excellent Theophilus,
1:4 so that you may know the certainty of the matters in which you have been instructed.
1:5 In the days of Herod, king of Judea, there was a priest named Zechariah, of the division of Abijah. He had a wife from the daughters of Aaron, and her name was Elizabeth.
1:6 Both were righteous before God, walking blamelessly in all the Lord’s commandments and ordinances.
1:7 They had no child, because Elizabeth was barren, and both were advanced in years.
1:8 While he was serving as priest before God in the order of his division,
1:9 according to the priesthood’s custom, he was chosen by lot to enter the Lord’s sanctuary and burn incense.
1:10 The whole multitude of the people was praying outside at the hour of incense.
1:11 An angel of the Lord appeared to him, standing at the right of the altar of incense.
1:12 Zechariah was troubled when he saw him, and fear fell upon him.
1:13 But the angel said to him, “Do not fear, Zechariah, for your petition has been heard. Your wife Elizabeth will bear you a son, and you shall name him John.
1:14 You will have joy and gladness, and many will rejoice at his birth,
1:15 for he will be great before the Lord. He will certainly drink neither wine nor strong drink, and he will be filled with the Holy Spirit even from his mother’s womb.
1:16 He will turn many of the sons of Israel to the Lord their God.
1:17 He himself will go before Him in Elijah’s spirit and power, to turn fathers’ hearts toward children and the disobedient to the understanding of the righteous, to prepare for the Lord a people made ready.”
1:18 Zechariah said to the angel, “How will I know this? For I am an old man, and my wife is advanced in years.”
1:19 The angel answered him, “I am Gabriel, who stands before God, and I was sent to speak to you and announce this good news to you.
1:20 And behold, you will be silent and unable to speak until the day these things happen, because you did not believe my words, which will be fulfilled at their appointed time.”
1:21 The people were waiting for Zechariah and wondering at his delay in the sanctuary.
1:22 When he came out, he could not speak to them, and they realized that he had seen a vision in the sanctuary. He kept making signs to them and remained mute.
1:23 When the days of his service were completed, he went home.
1:24 After those days his wife Elizabeth conceived, and she kept herself secluded for five months, saying,
1:25 “This is what the Lord has done for me in these days, when He looked upon me to take away my disgrace among people.”
1:26 In the sixth month, the angel Gabriel was sent from God to a city of Galilee named Nazareth,
1:27 to a virgin betrothed to a man named Joseph, of David’s house. The virgin’s name was Mary.
1:28 And he entered and said to her, “Rejoice, you who have been favoured! The Lord is with you.”
1:29 She was greatly troubled at the saying and considered what sort of greeting this might be.
1:30 The angel said to her, “Do not fear, Mary, for you have found grace with God.
1:31 And behold, you will conceive in your womb and bear a Son, and you shall name Him Jesus.
1:32 He will be great and will be called Son of the Most High. The Lord God will give Him the throne of His father David,
1:33 and He will reign over Jacob’s house forever, and His kingdom will have no end.”
1:34 Mary said to the angel, “How will this be, since I do not know a man?”
1:35 The angel answered her, “The Holy Spirit will come upon you, and the power of the Most High will overshadow you; and so the holy One to be born will be called Son of God.
1:36 And behold, your relative Elizabeth has also conceived a son in her old age, and this is the sixth month for her who was called barren,
1:37 for no word from God will be powerless.”
1:38 Mary said, “Behold, I am the Lord’s servant. May it happen to me according to your word.” Then the angel departed from her.
1:39 In those days Mary arose and went in haste into the hill country, to a city of Judah.
1:40 She entered Zechariah’s house and greeted Elizabeth.
1:41 When Elizabeth heard Mary’s greeting, the infant leaped in her womb, and Elizabeth was filled with the Holy Spirit.
1:42 She cried out with a loud shout and said, “Blessed are you among women, and blessed is the fruit of your womb!
1:43 And how is it that the mother of my Lord should come to me?
1:44 For behold, when the sound of your greeting entered my ears, the infant in my womb leaped with gladness.
1:45 And blessed is she who believed that there would be a fulfillment of the things spoken to her from the Lord.”
1:46 Mary said, “My soul magnifies the Lord,
1:47 and my spirit rejoiced in God my Savior,
1:48 because He looked upon the lowly condition of His servant. For behold, from now on all generations will call me blessed,
1:49 because the Mighty One has done great things for me, and holy is His name.
1:50 His mercy is for generations and generations upon those who fear Him.
1:51 He has performed a mighty deed with His arm; He scattered the proud in the thought of their heart.
1:52 He brought rulers down from thrones and raised up the lowly.
1:53 He filled the hungry with good things and sent the rich away empty.
1:54 He helped Israel His servant, remembering mercy,
1:55 as He spoke to our fathers, to Abraham and his offspring forever.”
1:56 Mary stayed with her about three months and returned to her home.
1:57 When Elizabeth’s time to give birth had come, she bore a son.
1:58 Her neighbors and relatives heard that the Lord had magnified His mercy toward her, and they rejoiced with her.
1:59 On the eighth day they came to circumcise the child and were calling him Zechariah after his father’s name.
1:60 But his mother answered, “No; he shall be called John.”
1:61 They said to her, “No one among your relatives is called by this name.”
1:62 They made signs to his father to ask what he wanted the child to be called.
1:63 He asked for a writing tablet and wrote, “His name is John.” And they all wondered.
1:64 Immediately his mouth and tongue were opened, and he spoke, blessing God.
1:65 Fear came upon all who lived around them, and all these matters were discussed throughout the whole hill country of Judea.
1:66 Everyone who heard took these matters to heart, saying, “What then will this child become?” For the Lord’s hand was indeed with him.
1:67 His father Zechariah was filled with the Holy Spirit and prophesied, saying,
1:68 “Blessed is the Lord, the God of Israel, because He has visited and accomplished redemption for His people,
1:69 and has raised up a horn of salvation for us in the house of David His servant,
1:70 as He spoke through the mouth of His holy prophets from long ago:
1:71 salvation from our enemies and from the hand of all who hate us,
1:72 to show mercy to our fathers and remember His holy covenant,
1:73 the oath He swore to Abraham our father, to grant us
1:74 that, rescued from our enemies’ hand, we might serve Him without fear,
1:75 in holiness and righteousness before Him all our days.
1:76 And you also, child, will be called prophet of the Most High, for you will go before the Lord to prepare His ways,
1:77 to give His people knowledge of salvation in the forgiveness of their sins,
1:78 through the tender mercy of our God, by which the dawn from on high will visit us,
1:79 to shine upon those who sit in darkness and death’s shadow, to direct our feet into a way of peace.”
1:80 The child grew and became strong in spirit. He remained in the wilderness until the day of his public appearance to Israel.

LUKE 2

2:1 In those days a decree went out from Caesar Augustus for all the inhabited world to be registered.
2:2 This first registration took place while Quirinius was governing Syria.
2:3 Everyone went to be registered, each to his own city.
2:4 Joseph also went up from Galilee, from the city of Nazareth, into Judea, to David’s city, which is called Bethlehem, because he belonged to David’s house and family,
2:5 to be registered with Mary, who was betrothed to him and was pregnant.
2:6 While they were there, the time came for her to give birth.
2:7 She bore her firstborn Son, wrapped Him in swaddling cloths, and laid Him in a manger, because there was no place for them in the lodging place.
2:8 In that same region there were shepherds living in the fields and keeping watch over their flock through the watches of the night.
2:9 An angel of the Lord stood beside them, and the Lord’s glory shone around them; they feared with a great fear.
2:10 The angel said to them, “Do not fear, for behold, I announce to you good news of great joy, which will be for all the people:
2:11 today in David’s city a Savior has been born for you, who is Christ the Lord.
2:12 This is the sign for you: you will find an infant wrapped in swaddling cloths and lying in a manger.”
2:13 Suddenly there was with the angel a multitude of the heavenly army, praising God and saying,
2:14 “Glory to God in the highest, and on earth peace among people of His favour.”
2:15 When the angels had departed from them into heaven, the shepherds said to one another, “Let us go over to Bethlehem and see this event that has happened, which the Lord has made known to us.”
2:16 They came in haste and found Mary and Joseph, and the infant lying in the manger.
2:17 When they saw Him, they made known the saying spoken to them about this Child.
2:18 Everyone who heard wondered at what the shepherds told them.
2:19 But Mary kept all these matters, pondering them in her heart.
2:20 The shepherds returned, glorifying and praising God for everything they had heard and seen, just as it had been spoken to them.
2:21 When eight days were completed for His circumcision, He was named Jesus, the name given by the angel before He was conceived in the womb.
2:22 When the days of their purification according to Moses’ law were completed, they brought Him up to Jerusalem to present Him to the Lord,
2:23 as it is written in the Lord’s law: “Every male opening the womb shall be called holy to the Lord,”
2:24 and to give a sacrifice according to what is said in the Lord’s law: “A pair of turtledoves or two young pigeons.”
2:25 And behold, there was a man in Jerusalem named Simeon. This man was righteous and devout, awaiting Israel’s consolation, and the Holy Spirit was upon him.
2:26 It had been revealed to him by the Holy Spirit that he would not see death before he saw the Lord’s Christ.
2:27 He came in the Spirit into the temple. When the parents brought in the Child Jesus to do for Him what was customary under the law,
2:28 he received Him into his arms, blessed God, and said,
2:29 “Now, Master, Thou art releasing Thy servant in peace according to Thy word,
2:30 because my eyes have seen Thy salvation,
2:31 which Thou didst prepare in the sight of all peoples:
2:32 a light for revelation to the nations and glory for Thy people Israel.”
2:33 His father and mother were wondering at what was being said about Him.
2:34 Simeon blessed them and said to His mother Mary, “Behold, this One is appointed for the fall and rising of many in Israel, and for a sign spoken against—
2:35 and a sword will pass through your own soul also—so that thoughts from many hearts may be revealed.”
2:36 There was also a prophetess, Anna, daughter of Phanuel, from the tribe of Asher. She was very old. She had lived with her husband for seven years after marrying as a virgin,
2:37 and had been a widow until the age of eighty-four. She did not leave the temple, serving with fasts and petitions night and day.
2:38 Coming up at that very hour, she gave thanks to God and spoke about Him to everyone awaiting Jerusalem’s redemption.
2:39 When they had completed everything according to the Lord’s law, they returned to Galilee, to their own city of Nazareth.
2:40 The Child grew and became strong, being filled with wisdom, and God’s grace was upon Him.
2:41 His parents went each year to Jerusalem for the Festival of Passover.
2:42 When He became twelve years old, they went up according to the festival’s custom.
2:43 When the festival days were over and they were returning, the boy Jesus remained in Jerusalem, and His parents did not know.
2:44 Supposing Him to be in the traveling company, they went a day’s journey and searched for Him among their relatives and acquaintances.
2:45 When they did not find Him, they returned to Jerusalem searching for Him.
2:46 After three days they found Him in the temple, sitting among the teachers, listening to them and questioning them.
2:47 All who heard Him were astonished at His understanding and answers.
2:48 When His parents saw Him, they were overwhelmed. His mother said to Him, “Child, why hast Thou treated us this way? Behold, Thy father and I have been searching for Thee in anguish.”
2:49 He said to them, “Why were you searching for Me? Did you not know that I must be in what belongs to My Father?”
2:50 But they did not understand the saying He spoke to them.
2:51 He went down with them and came to Nazareth, and He remained subject to them. His mother kept all these matters in her heart.
2:52 Jesus advanced in wisdom and age, and in favor with God and people.

LUKE 3

3:1 In the fifteenth year of Tiberius Caesar’s rule, when Pontius Pilate governed Judea, Herod was tetrarch of Galilee, his brother Philip tetrarch of the region of Iturea and Trachonitis, and Lysanias tetrarch of Abilene,
3:2 during the high priesthood of Annas and Caiaphas, God’s word came to John, Zechariah’s son, in the wilderness.
3:3 He went into all the region around the Jordan, proclaiming a baptism of repentance for the forgiveness of sins,
3:4 as it is written in the book of the words of Isaiah the prophet: “A voice of one crying in the wilderness: Prepare the Lord’s way; make His paths straight.
3:5 Every ravine will be filled, and every mountain and hill brought low. The crooked will become straight, and the rough ways smooth roads,
3:6 and all flesh will see God’s salvation.”
3:7 So he said to the crowds coming out to be baptized by him, “Brood of vipers! Who warned you to flee the coming wrath?
3:8 Produce fruits worthy of repentance, and do not begin saying among yourselves, ‘We have Abraham as father.’ For I tell you that God can raise up children for Abraham from these stones.
3:9 Already the axe also lies at the root of the trees. Therefore every tree that does not produce good fruit is cut down and thrown into fire.”
3:10 The crowds asked him, “What then should we do?”
3:11 He answered them, “Let the one who has two tunics share with the one who has none, and let the one who has food do likewise.”
3:12 Tax collectors also came to be baptized and said to him, “Teacher, what should we do?”
3:13 He said to them, “Collect nothing beyond what has been appointed for you.”
3:14 Soldiers also asked him, “And we, what should we do?” He said to them, “Extort from no one, do not make false accusations, and be content with your pay.”
3:15 As the people were waiting expectantly and everyone was considering in their hearts whether John might be the Christ,
3:16 John answered them all, “I baptize you with water, but One stronger than I is coming, whose sandal strap I am not worthy to untie. He will baptize you in the Holy Spirit and fire.
3:17 His winnowing fork is in His hand, to cleanse His threshing floor thoroughly and gather the wheat into His storehouse; but the chaff He will burn with unquenchable fire.”
3:18 With many other exhortations also, he announced good news to the people.
3:19 But Herod the tetrarch, rebuked by him concerning Herodias, his brother’s wife, and all the evil things Herod had done,
3:20 added this also to them all: he shut John up in prison.
3:21 When all the people were baptized, and Jesus also had been baptized and was praying, heaven was opened,
3:22 and the Holy Spirit descended upon Him in bodily form like a dove. A voice came from heaven: “Thou art My beloved Son; in Thee I have taken delight.”
3:23 Jesus Himself was about thirty years old when He began His ministry. He was the son, as was supposed, of Joseph, of Heli,
3:24 of Matthat, of Levi, of Melchi, of Jannai, of Joseph,
3:25 of Mattathias, of Amos, of Nahum, of Esli, of Naggai,
3:26 of Maath, of Mattathias, of Semein, of Josech, of Joda,
3:27 of Joanan, of Rhesa, of Zerubbabel, of Shealtiel, of Neri,
3:28 of Melchi, of Addi, of Cosam, of Elmadam, of Er,
3:29 of Jesus, of Eliezer, of Jorim, of Matthat, of Levi,
3:30 of Simeon, of Judah, of Joseph, of Jonam, of Eliakim,
3:31 of Melea, of Menna, of Mattatha, of Nathan, of David,
3:32 of Jesse, of Obed, of Boaz, of Sala, of Nahshon,
3:33 of Amminadab, of Admin, of Arni, of Hezron, of Perez, of Judah,
3:34 of Jacob, of Isaac, of Abraham, of Terah, of Nahor,
3:35 of Serug, of Reu, of Peleg, of Eber, of Shelah,
3:36 of Cainan, of Arphaxad, of Shem, of Noah, of Lamech,
3:37 of Methuselah, of Enoch, of Jared, of Mahalalel, of Cainan,
3:38 of Enosh, of Seth, of Adam, of God.

LUKE 4

4:1 Jesus, full of the Holy Spirit, returned from the Jordan and was led in the Spirit in the wilderness
4:2 for forty days, being tested by the devil. He ate nothing during those days, and when they were completed He was hungry.
4:3 The devil said to Him, “If Thou art God’s Son, tell this stone to become bread.”
4:4 Jesus answered him, “It is written: ‘A human being shall not live on bread alone.’”
4:5 Taking Him up, the devil showed Him all the kingdoms of the inhabited world in a moment of time.
4:6 The devil said to Him, “I will give Thee all this authority and their glory, because it has been handed over to me, and I give it to whomever I wish.
4:7 Therefore, if Thou dost bow down before me, it will all be Thine.”
4:8 Jesus answered him, “It is written: ‘You shall worship the Lord your God, and Him alone shall you serve.’”
4:9 He brought Him to Jerusalem, placed Him on the temple’s pinnacle, and said to Him, “If Thou art God’s Son, throw Thyself down from here,
4:10 for it is written: ‘He will command His angels concerning Thee, to guard Thee,’
4:11 and, ‘They will lift Thee upon their hands, lest Thou strike Thy foot against a stone.’”
4:12 Jesus answered him, “It has been said: ‘You shall not put the Lord your God to the test.’”
4:13 Having completed every test, the devil departed from Him until an opportune time.
4:14 Jesus returned in the Spirit’s power into Galilee, and a report about Him spread throughout the whole surrounding region.
4:15 He taught in their synagogues, being glorified by everyone.
4:16 He came to Nazareth, where He had been brought up, and according to His custom entered the synagogue on the Sabbath day. He stood up to read.
4:17 The scroll of the prophet Isaiah was given to Him. Unrolling it, He found the place where it was written:
4:18 “The Spirit of the Lord is upon Me, because He anointed Me to proclaim good news to the poor. He has sent Me to proclaim release to captives and recovery of sight to the blind, to send the oppressed away in release,
4:19 to proclaim the Lord’s acceptable year.”
4:20 He rolled up the scroll, gave it back to the attendant, and sat down. The eyes of everyone in the synagogue were fixed on Him.
4:21 He began to say to them, “Today this Scripture has been fulfilled in your ears.”
4:22 All bore witness to Him and wondered at the words of grace coming from His mouth. They said, “Is this not Joseph’s son?”
4:23 He said to them, “Surely you will tell Me this proverb: ‘Physician, heal yourself.’ ‘Do here in Thy homeland also everything we heard happened in Capernaum.’”
4:24 He said, “Amen, I tell you, no prophet is acceptable in his homeland.
4:25 But in truth I tell you, there were many widows in Israel in Elijah’s days, when heaven was shut for three years and six months and a great famine came over the whole land.
4:26 Yet Elijah was sent to none of them except to a widow at Zarephath in Sidon’s territory.
4:27 And there were many leprous people in Israel in the time of Elisha the prophet, but none of them was cleansed except Naaman the Syrian.”
4:28 Everyone in the synagogue was filled with anger when they heard these things.
4:29 They rose, threw Him out of the city, and led Him to the brow of the hill on which their city was built, intending to hurl Him down.
4:30 But He passed through their midst and went on His way.
4:31 He went down to Capernaum, a city of Galilee, and was teaching them on the Sabbaths.
4:32 They were overwhelmed at His teaching, because His word was with authority.
4:33 In the synagogue was a man who had the spirit of an unclean demon. He cried out with a loud voice,
4:34 “Ah! What have we to do with Thee, Jesus of Nazareth? Hast Thou come to destroy us? I know who Thou art: God’s Holy One.”
4:35 Jesus rebuked him, saying, “Be silent and come out of him!” The demon threw him down in their midst and came out of him without harming him.
4:36 Amazement came upon everyone, and they said to one another, “What is this word? With authority and power He commands the unclean spirits, and they come out!”
4:37 A report about Him went out into every place in the surrounding region.
4:38 Leaving the synagogue, He entered Simon’s house. Simon’s mother-in-law was gripped by a high fever, and they asked Him about her.
4:39 Standing over her, He rebuked the fever, and it left her. Immediately she rose and served them.
4:40 As the sun was setting, everyone who had people sick with various diseases brought them to Him. He laid His hands on each one and healed them.
4:41 Demons also came out of many, crying, “Thou art God’s Son!” He rebuked them and would not let them speak, because they knew He was the Christ.
4:42 When day came, He went out to a deserted place. The crowds searched for Him, came to Him, and tried to keep Him from departing from them.
4:43 But He said to them, “I must announce the good news of God’s kingdom to the other cities also, because for this I was sent.”
4:44 And He continued proclaiming in the synagogues of Judea.

LUKE 5

5:1 As the crowd pressed upon Him to hear God’s word, He was standing beside the lake of Gennesaret.
5:2 He saw two boats beside the lake; the fishermen had left them and were washing their nets.
5:3 He entered one of the boats, which belonged to Simon, and asked him to put out a little from the land. Sitting down, He taught the crowds from the boat.
5:4 When He finished speaking, He said to Simon, “Put out into the deep water, and let down your nets for a catch.”
5:5 Simon answered, “Master, we labored through the whole night and caught nothing, but at Thy word I will let down the nets.”
5:6 When they did this, they enclosed a great multitude of fish, and their nets began to tear.
5:7 They signaled to their partners in the other boat to come and help them. They came and filled both boats, so that they began to sink.
5:8 When Simon Peter saw it, he fell at Jesus’ knees, saying, “Depart from me, Lord, because I am a sinful man.”
5:9 For amazement had seized him and everyone with him at the catch of fish they had taken,
5:10 and likewise James and John, Zebedee’s sons, who were Simon’s partners. Jesus said to Simon, “Do not fear. From now on you will be catching people alive.”
5:11 They brought the boats to land, left everything, and followed Him.
5:12 While He was in one of the cities, behold, there was a man full of leprosy. Seeing Jesus, he fell on his face and petitioned Him, saying, “Lord, if Thou art willing, Thou canst cleanse me.”
5:13 Stretching out His hand, He touched him and said, “I am willing; be cleansed.” Immediately the leprosy departed from him.
5:14 He commanded him to tell no one: “But go, show yourself to the priest, and make the offering for your cleansing as Moses commanded, for a testimony to them.”
5:15 Yet the report about Him spread all the more, and large crowds gathered to hear and to be healed of their illnesses.
5:16 But He kept withdrawing to deserted places and praying.
5:17 On one of those days He was teaching, and Pharisees and teachers of the law were sitting there. They had come from every village of Galilee and Judea and from Jerusalem; and the Lord’s power was present for Him to heal.
5:18 And behold, men were carrying on a bed a man who was paralyzed. They sought to bring him in and place him before Him.
5:19 Finding no way to bring him in because of the crowd, they went up onto the roof and lowered him through the tiles with his little bed into their midst, before Jesus.
5:20 Seeing their faith, He said, “Man, your sins are forgiven you.”
5:21 The scribes and Pharisees began to reason, saying, “Who is this who speaks blasphemies? Who can forgive sins except God alone?”
5:22 Jesus recognized their thoughts and answered them, “What are you reasoning in your hearts?
5:23 Which is easier: to say, ‘Your sins are forgiven you,’ or to say, ‘Rise and walk’?
5:24 But so that you may know that the Son of Man has authority on earth to forgive sins”—He said to the paralyzed man—“I tell you, rise, take up your little bed, and go home.”
5:25 Immediately he rose before them, took up what he had been lying on, and went home glorifying God.
5:26 Astonishment seized everyone, and they glorified God and were filled with fear, saying, “We have seen extraordinary things today.”
5:27 After this He went out and saw a tax collector named Levi sitting at the tax booth, and said to him, “Follow Me.”
5:28 Leaving everything, he rose and followed Him.
5:29 Levi gave a great reception for Him in his house. A large crowd of tax collectors and others was reclining with them.
5:30 The Pharisees and their scribes grumbled to His disciples, saying, “Why do you eat and drink with tax collectors and sinners?”
5:31 Jesus answered them, “Those who are healthy have no need of a physician, but those who are ill do.
5:32 I have not come to call the righteous, but sinners to repentance.”
5:33 They said to Him, “John’s disciples fast often and offer petitions, and likewise those of the Pharisees, but Thine eat and drink.”
5:34 Jesus said to them, “Can you make the bridegroom’s companions fast while the bridegroom is with them?
5:35 But days will come, and when the bridegroom is taken from them, then they will fast in those days.”
5:36 He also told them a parable: “No one tears a patch from a new garment and puts it on an old garment. Otherwise he will tear the new one, and the patch from the new will not match the old.
5:37 And no one puts new wine into old wineskins. Otherwise the new wine will burst the skins, and it will be poured out and the skins destroyed.
5:38 But new wine must be put into fresh wineskins.
5:39 And no one who has drunk old wine desires new, for he says, ‘The old is good.’”

LUKE 6

6:1 On a Sabbath He was passing through grainfields. His disciples plucked the ears, rubbed them in their hands, and ate.
6:2 Some of the Pharisees said, “Why are you doing what is not lawful on the Sabbaths?”
6:3 Jesus answered them, “Have you not even read what David did when he and those with him were hungry,
6:4 how he entered God’s house, took and ate the loaves of the presentation, and gave them to those with him, although no one except the priests alone may lawfully eat them?”
6:5 He said to them, “The Son of Man is Lord of the Sabbath.”
6:6 On another Sabbath He entered the synagogue and taught. A man was there whose right hand was withered.
6:7 The scribes and Pharisees watched closely to see whether He would heal on the Sabbath, so that they might find a way to accuse Him.
6:8 But He knew their thoughts and said to the man with the withered hand, “Rise and stand in the middle.” He rose and stood.
6:9 Jesus said to them, “I ask you: Is it lawful on the Sabbath to do good or do evil, to save a life or destroy it?”
6:10 Looking around at all of them, He said to him, “Stretch out your hand.” He did so, and his hand was restored.
6:11 They were filled with mindless anger and discussed with one another what they might do to Jesus.
6:12 In those days He went out to the mountain to pray, and spent the night in prayer to God.
6:13 When day came, He called His disciples and chose twelve from them, whom He also named apostles:
6:14 Simon, whom He also named Peter, and his brother Andrew, and James and John, and Philip and Bartholomew,
6:15 and Matthew and Thomas, and James son of Alphaeus, and Simon called the Zealot,
6:16 and Judas son of James, and Judas Iscariot, who became a traitor.
6:17 He came down with them and stood on a level place. There was a large crowd of His disciples and a great multitude of the people from all Judea, Jerusalem, and the coastal region of Tyre and Sidon.
6:18 They had come to hear Him and be healed of their diseases; those troubled by unclean spirits were also healed.
6:19 The whole crowd sought to touch Him, because power went out from Him and healed everyone.
6:20 Lifting His eyes toward His disciples, He said, “Blessed are you who are poor, because yours is God’s kingdom.
6:21 Blessed are you who hunger now, because you will be filled. Blessed are you who weep now, because you will laugh.
6:22 Blessed are you when people hate you, when they exclude you, reproach you, and cast out your name as evil because of the Son of Man.
6:23 Rejoice on that day and leap, for behold, your reward is great in heaven; their fathers treated the prophets in the same way.
6:24 But woe to you who are rich, because you have received your consolation.
6:25 Woe to you who are filled now, because you will hunger. Woe to you who laugh now, because you will mourn and weep.
6:26 Woe when all people speak well of you, for their fathers treated the false prophets in the same way.
6:27 But I say to you who hear: Love your enemies; do good to those who hate you.
6:28 Bless those who curse you; pray for those who mistreat you.
6:29 Offer the other cheek also to the one striking you on the cheek; and from the one taking your cloak, do not withhold your tunic also.
6:30 Give to everyone who asks you, and do not demand back your belongings from the one who takes them.
6:31 As you wish people to do to you, do likewise to them.
6:32 If you love those who love you, what credit is that to you? For even sinners love those who love them.
6:33 If you do good to those who do good to you, what credit is that to you? Even sinners do the same.
6:34 If you lend to those from whom you hope to receive, what credit is that to you? Even sinners lend to sinners to receive the equivalent back.
6:35 But love your enemies, do good, and lend, expecting nothing back. Your reward will be great, and you will be sons of the Most High, because He is kind to the ungrateful and evil.
6:36 Be merciful, just as your Father is merciful.
6:37 Do not judge, and you will certainly not be judged. Do not condemn, and you will certainly not be condemned. Release, and you will be released.
6:38 Give, and it will be given to you. They will put into your lap a good measure, pressed down, shaken together, overflowing; for by the measure you measure, it will be measured back to you.”
6:39 He also told them a parable: “Can a blind person guide a blind person? Will they not both fall into a pit?
6:40 A disciple is not above the teacher, but everyone fully trained will be like his teacher.
6:41 Why do you look at the speck in your brother’s eye but fail to notice the beam in your own eye?
6:42 How can you say to your brother, ‘Brother, let me take out the speck in your eye,’ when you yourself do not see the beam in your own eye? Hypocrite! First take the beam out of your eye, and then you will see clearly to take the speck out of your brother’s eye.
6:43 For no good tree produces rotten fruit, nor again does a rotten tree produce good fruit.
6:44 Each tree is known by its own fruit. People do not gather figs from thorns, nor harvest grapes from a bramble.
6:45 The good person brings out good from the good treasure of his heart, and the evil person brings out evil from the evil treasure; for his mouth speaks from the heart’s abundance.
6:46 Why do you call Me ‘Lord, Lord,’ and not do what I say?
6:47 Everyone who comes to Me, hears My words and does them—I will show you whom he resembles.
6:48 He resembles a man building a house, who dug and went deep and laid a foundation on the rock. When a flood came, the river burst against that house but could not shake it, because it had been well built.
6:49 But the one who heard and did not act resembles a man who built a house on the ground without a foundation. The river burst against it, and immediately it collapsed; the ruin of that house was great.”

LUKE 7

7:1 When He had finished all His sayings in the people’s hearing, He entered Capernaum.
7:2 A certain centurion had a slave who was ill and about to die, and who was precious to him.
7:3 Hearing about Jesus, he sent elders of the Jews to Him, asking Him to come and save his slave.
7:4 When they reached Jesus, they urged Him earnestly, saying, “He is worthy for Thee to grant him this,
7:5 for he loves our nation, and he himself built our synagogue for us.”
7:6 Jesus went with them. When He was already not far from the house, the centurion sent friends, saying to Him, “Lord, do not trouble Thyself, for I am not worthy for Thee to enter under my roof.
7:7 Therefore I did not even consider myself worthy to come to Thee. But speak a word, and let my servant be healed.
7:8 For I too am a man placed under authority, with soldiers under me. I tell this one, ‘Go,’ and he goes; another, ‘Come,’ and he comes; and my slave, ‘Do this,’ and he does it.”
7:9 When Jesus heard this, He marveled at him. Turning to the crowd following Him, He said, “I tell you, not even in Israel have I found such faith.”
7:10 Those who had been sent returned to the house and found the slave in good health.
7:11 Afterward He went to a city called Nain, and His disciples and a large crowd traveled with Him.
7:12 As He approached the city’s gate, behold, a dead man was being carried out, his mother’s only son. She was a widow, and a considerable crowd from the city was with her.
7:13 When the Lord saw her, He was moved with compassion for her and said, “Do not weep.”
7:14 He approached and touched the bier; the bearers stood still. He said, “Young man, I tell you, rise.”
7:15 The dead man sat up and began speaking, and He gave him to his mother.
7:16 Fear seized everyone, and they glorified God, saying, “A great prophet has arisen among us,” and, “God has visited His people.”
7:17 This report about Him spread throughout all Judea and all the surrounding region.
7:18 John’s disciples reported all these things to him. John called two of his disciples
7:19 and sent them to the Lord, saying, “Art Thou the One who is coming, or should we expect another?”
7:20 When the men came to Him, they said, “John the Baptist sent us to Thee, saying, ‘Art Thou the One who is coming, or should we expect another?’”
7:21 At that hour He healed many of diseases, afflictions and evil spirits, and graciously gave sight to many blind people.
7:22 He answered them, “Go and report to John what you have seen and heard: the blind recover sight, the lame walk, leprous people are cleansed, the deaf hear, the dead are raised, and the poor receive good news.
7:23 And blessed is whoever does not stumble because of Me.”
7:24 When John’s messengers had departed, He began to speak to the crowds about John: “What did you go out into the wilderness to behold? A reed shaken by the wind?
7:25 But what did you go out to see? A man clothed in soft garments? Behold, those who live in splendid clothing and luxury are in royal palaces.
7:26 But what did you go out to see? A prophet? Yes, I tell you, and more than a prophet.
7:27 This is the one about whom it is written: ‘Behold, I send My messenger before Thy face, who will prepare Thy way ahead of Thee.’
7:28 I tell you, among those born of women no one is greater than John; yet the lesser one in God’s kingdom is greater than he.”
7:29 When all the people and the tax collectors heard, they acknowledged God’s righteousness, having been baptized with John’s baptism.
7:30 But the Pharisees and experts in the law rejected God’s purpose for themselves, not having been baptized by him.
7:31 “To what, then, shall I compare the people of this generation, and what are they like?
7:32 They are like children sitting in the marketplace and calling to one another, saying, ‘We played the flute for you, and you did not dance; we lamented, and you did not weep.’
7:33 For John the Baptist has come neither eating bread nor drinking wine, and you say, ‘He has a demon.’
7:34 The Son of Man has come eating and drinking, and you say, ‘Behold, a glutton and wine-drinker, a friend of tax collectors and sinners.’
7:35 Yet wisdom was justified by all her children.”
7:36 One of the Pharisees asked Him to eat with him. He entered the Pharisee’s house and reclined at table.
7:37 And behold, a woman in the city who was a sinner learned that He was reclining in the Pharisee’s house. She brought an alabaster vessel of perfume
7:38 and stood behind Him at His feet, weeping. She began to wet His feet with her tears and wiped them with the hair of her head. She kept kissing His feet and anointing them with the perfume.
7:39 When the Pharisee who had invited Him saw it, he said within himself, “If this man were a prophet, He would know who and what sort of woman is touching Him, that she is a sinner.”
7:40 Jesus answered him, “Simon, I have something to say to you.” He said, “Teacher, say it.”
7:41 “A certain lender had two debtors. One owed five hundred denarii and the other fifty.
7:42 Since they had nothing with which to repay, he graciously forgave them both. Which of them, then, will love him more?”
7:43 Simon answered, “I suppose the one to whom he forgave more.” He said to him, “You judged correctly.”
7:44 Turning toward the woman, He said to Simon, “Do you see this woman? I entered your house; you gave Me no water for My feet, but she wet My feet with her tears and wiped them with her hair.
7:45 You gave Me no kiss, but from the time I entered she has not stopped kissing My feet.
7:46 You did not anoint My head with oil, but she anointed My feet with perfume.
7:47 For this reason I tell you: her many sins are forgiven, because she loved much. But the one who is forgiven little loves little.”
7:48 He said to her, “Your sins are forgiven.”
7:49 Those reclining with Him began to say within themselves, “Who is this who even forgives sins?”
7:50 And He said to the woman, “Your faith has saved you; go in peace.”

LUKE 8

8:1 After this He traveled through cities and villages, proclaiming and announcing the good news of God’s kingdom. The Twelve were with Him,
8:2 and certain women who had been healed of evil spirits and illnesses: Mary called Magdalene, from whom seven demons had gone out,
8:3 Joanna, wife of Chuza, Herod’s steward, Susanna, and many others, who served them out of their own belongings.
8:4 When a large crowd was gathering and people from each city were coming to Him, He spoke through a parable:
8:5 “The sower went out to sow his seed. As he sowed, some fell beside the road, was trampled, and the birds of the sky ate it.
8:6 Other seed fell on the rock, and when it sprouted it withered because it had no moisture.
8:7 Other seed fell among thorns, and the thorns grew up with it and choked it.
8:8 Other seed fell into good soil, sprouted and produced fruit a hundredfold.” As He said these things, He called out, “Let the one who has ears to hear listen.”
8:9 His disciples asked Him what this parable meant.
8:10 He said, “To you it has been given to know the mysteries of God’s kingdom, but to the rest in parables, so that seeing they may not see, and hearing they may not understand.
8:11 This is the parable: the seed is God’s word.
8:12 Those beside the road are the ones who have heard; then the devil comes and takes the word from their heart, so that they may not believe and be saved.
8:13 Those on the rock are the ones who, when they hear, receive the word with joy. They have no root; they believe for a time and fall away in a time of testing.
8:14 What fell among thorns represents those who have heard, but as they go along they are choked by life’s cares, wealth and pleasures, and do not bring fruit to maturity.
8:15 What is in the good soil represents those who hear the word in a noble and good heart, hold it fast, and bear fruit with endurance.
8:16 No one lights a lamp and covers it with a vessel or puts it under a bed, but places it on a lampstand, so that those entering may see the light.
8:17 For nothing is hidden that will not become manifest, nor concealed that will not be known and come into the open.
8:18 Therefore watch how you hear. For whoever has will be given more, and whoever does not have will have even what he thinks he has taken from him.”
8:19 His mother and brothers came to Him but could not reach Him because of the crowd.
8:20 It was reported to Him, “Thy mother and Thy brothers are standing outside, wanting to see Thee.”
8:21 He answered them, “My mother and My brothers are these who hear God’s word and do it.”
8:22 On one of those days He entered a boat with His disciples and said to them, “Let us cross to the other side of the lake.” They put out.
8:23 As they sailed, He fell asleep. A windstorm came down upon the lake, and they were being swamped and were in danger.
8:24 They came and woke Him, saying, “Master, Master, we are perishing!” He awoke and rebuked the wind and the surging water. They ceased, and there was calm.
8:25 He said to them, “Where is your faith?” Afraid, they marveled and said to one another, “Who then is this, that He commands even the winds and the water, and they obey Him?”
8:26 They sailed into the country of the Gerasenes, which lies opposite Galilee.
8:27 When He stepped onto the land, a man from the city who had demons met Him. For a considerable time he had worn no garment and had not lived in a house, but among the tombs.
8:28 Seeing Jesus, he cried out, fell before Him, and said with a loud voice, “What have I to do with Thee, Jesus, Son of the Most High God? I beg Thee, do not torment me!”
8:29 For He was commanding the unclean spirit to leave the man. It had seized him many times; he was bound with chains and shackles and kept under guard, but he broke the bonds and was driven by the demon into deserted places.
8:30 Jesus asked him, “What is your name?” He said, “Legion,” because many demons had entered him.
8:31 They begged Him not to command them to go away into the abyss.
8:32 A herd of many pigs was feeding there on the hillside. They begged Him to permit them to enter those pigs, and He permitted them.
8:33 The demons came out of the man and entered the pigs. The herd rushed down the steep bank into the lake and was drowned.
8:34 When the herdsmen saw what had happened, they fled and reported it in the city and in the fields.
8:35 People came out to see what had happened. They came to Jesus and found the man from whom the demons had gone out sitting at Jesus’ feet, clothed and in his right mind; and they were afraid.
8:36 Those who had seen reported to them how the demon-possessed man had been saved.
8:37 The whole multitude of the surrounding country of the Gerasenes asked Him to depart from them, because they were gripped by great fear. He entered a boat and returned.
8:38 The man from whom the demons had gone out begged to be with Him, but He sent him away, saying,
8:39 “Return to your home and recount everything God has done for you.” He went away proclaiming throughout the whole city everything Jesus had done for him.
8:40 When Jesus returned, the crowd welcomed Him, for they were all awaiting Him.
8:41 And behold, a man named Jairus came, who was a ruler of the synagogue. Falling at Jesus’ feet, he begged Him to enter his house,
8:42 because he had an only daughter, about twelve years old, and she was dying. As He went, the crowds pressed closely around Him.
8:43 A woman had suffered a flow of blood for twelve years. She had spent her whole livelihood on physicians, but could not be healed by anyone.
8:44 Coming up behind Him, she touched the fringe of His garment, and immediately her flow of blood stopped.
8:45 Jesus said, “Who touched Me?” When everyone denied it, Peter said, “Master, the crowds surround Thee and press against Thee.”
8:46 Jesus said, “Someone touched Me, for I knew that power had gone out from Me.”
8:47 When the woman saw that she had not escaped notice, she came trembling and fell before Him. Before all the people she reported why she had touched Him and how she had been healed immediately.
8:48 He said to her, “Daughter, your faith has saved you; go in peace.”
8:49 While He was still speaking, someone came from the synagogue ruler’s house, saying, “Your daughter has died. Do not trouble the Teacher any longer.”
8:50 Jesus heard and answered him, “Do not fear; only believe, and she will be saved.”
8:51 When He came to the house, He allowed no one to enter with Him except Peter, John and James, and the child’s father and mother.
8:52 Everyone was weeping and beating themselves in mourning for her. He said, “Do not weep, for she has not died, but is sleeping.”
8:53 They laughed at Him, knowing that she had died.
8:54 But He took her hand and called, “Child, rise.”
8:55 Her spirit returned, and she rose immediately. He ordered that she be given something to eat.
8:56 Her parents were astonished, but He commanded them to tell no one what had happened.

LUKE 9

9:1 Calling the Twelve together, He gave them power and authority over all demons and to heal diseases.
9:2 He sent them to proclaim God’s kingdom and heal the sick.
9:3 He said to them, “Take nothing for the journey: neither a staff nor a bag nor bread nor silver; neither are you to have two tunics each.
9:4 Whatever house you enter, remain there and depart from there.
9:5 Wherever people do not receive you, as you leave that city shake the dust from your feet as a testimony against them.”
9:6 They went out and passed through the villages, announcing good news and healing everywhere.
9:7 Herod the tetrarch heard of everything that was happening and was perplexed, because some were saying that John had been raised from the dead,
9:8 others that Elijah had appeared, and others that one of the ancient prophets had arisen.
9:9 Herod said, “John I beheaded. But who is this about whom I hear such things?” And he sought to see Him.
9:10 When the apostles returned, they recounted to Him everything they had done. Taking them with Him, He withdrew privately to a city called Bethsaida.
9:11 The crowds learned of it and followed Him. He welcomed them, spoke to them about God’s kingdom, and healed those needing a cure.
9:12 The day was beginning to draw to a close. The Twelve approached and said to Him, “Dismiss the crowd, so that they may go into the surrounding villages and countryside, find lodging and obtain provisions, because we are here in a deserted place.”
9:13 He said to them, “You give them something to eat.” They said, “We have no more than five loaves and two fish, unless we ourselves go and buy food for all these people.”
9:14 For there were about five thousand men. He said to His disciples, “Have them recline in groups of about fifty each.”
9:15 They did so and had everyone recline.
9:16 Taking the five loaves and the two fish, He looked up to heaven, blessed them, broke them, and kept giving them to the disciples to set before the crowd.
9:17 They all ate and were filled, and the leftover fragments were gathered up: twelve baskets.
9:18 While He was praying apart, His disciples were with Him, and He asked them, “Who do the crowds say I am?”
9:19 They answered, “John the Baptist; others say Elijah; and others that one of the ancient prophets has arisen.”
9:20 He said to them, “But you, who do you say I am?” Peter answered, “God’s Christ.”
9:21 He sternly commanded them to tell this to no one,
9:22 saying, “The Son of Man must suffer many things, be rejected by the elders, chief priests and scribes, be killed, and on the third day be raised.”
9:23 He said to everyone, “If anyone wishes to come after Me, let him deny himself, take up his cross daily, and follow Me.
9:24 For whoever wishes to save his life will lose it, but whoever loses his life for My sake will save it.
9:25 For what benefit is there for a person to gain the whole world but destroy or forfeit himself?
9:26 Whoever is ashamed of Me and My words, of him the Son of Man will be ashamed when He comes in His glory and that of the Father and the holy angels.
9:27 But truly I tell you, some standing here will certainly not taste death until they see God’s kingdom.”
9:28 About eight days after these sayings, He took Peter, John and James and went up the mountain to pray.
9:29 As He prayed, the appearance of His face became different, and His clothing became white and flashing like lightning.
9:30 And behold, two men were speaking with Him, who were Moses and Elijah.
9:31 Appearing in glory, they spoke of His departure, which He was about to fulfill in Jerusalem.
9:32 Peter and those with him were weighed down with sleep, but when fully awake they saw His glory and the two men standing with Him.
9:33 As they were parting from Him, Peter said to Jesus, “Master, it is good for us to be here. Let us make three tents, one for Thee, one for Moses and one for Elijah,” not knowing what he was saying.
9:34 While he said this, a cloud came and overshadowed them, and they were afraid as they entered the cloud.
9:35 A voice came from the cloud, saying, “This is My Son, the chosen One; listen to Him.”
9:36 When the voice came, Jesus was found alone. They kept silent and in those days reported to no one anything they had seen.
9:37 On the following day, when they came down from the mountain, a large crowd met Him.
9:38 And behold, a man from the crowd cried out, “Teacher, I beg Thee to look upon my son, because he is my only child.
9:39 Behold, a spirit seizes him, and suddenly he cries out. It convulses him with foaming and scarcely leaves him, bruising him.
9:40 I begged Thy disciples to cast it out, but they could not.”
9:41 Jesus answered, “O faithless and distorted generation, how long shall I be with you and bear with you? Bring your son here.”
9:42 While he was still approaching, the demon dashed him down and convulsed him. Jesus rebuked the unclean spirit, healed the boy, and gave him back to his father.
9:43 Everyone was overwhelmed at God’s majesty. While they all marveled at everything He was doing, He said to His disciples,
9:44 “You, place these words in your ears: the Son of Man is about to be handed over into human hands.”
9:45 But they did not understand this saying; it was veiled from them so that they might not perceive it, and they were afraid to ask Him about it.
9:46 A discussion arose among them about which of them might be greatest.
9:47 Jesus knew the thought of their heart. He took a child and placed him beside Himself,
9:48 and said to them, “Whoever receives this child in My name receives Me; whoever receives Me receives the One who sent Me. For the one who is least among you all is great.”
9:49 John answered, “Master, we saw someone casting out demons in Thy name and tried to stop him, because he does not follow with us.”
9:50 Jesus said to him, “Do not stop him, for whoever is not against you is for you.”
9:51 As the days for His being taken up approached their fulfillment, He firmly set His face to go to Jerusalem.
9:52 He sent messengers before His face. They went and entered a Samaritan village to prepare for Him.
9:53 But they did not receive Him, because His face was set toward Jerusalem.
9:54 Seeing this, the disciples James and John said, “Lord, dost Thou wish us to command fire to come down from heaven and consume them?”
9:55 But He turned and rebuked them.
9:56 And they went to another village.
9:57 As they traveled on the road, someone said to Him, “I will follow Thee wherever Thou goest.”
9:58 Jesus said to him, “Foxes have dens and the birds of the sky have dwelling places, but the Son of Man has nowhere to lay His head.”
9:59 He said to another, “Follow Me.” But he said, “Lord, permit me first to go and bury my father.”
9:60 He said to him, “Let the dead bury their own dead; but you go and proclaim God’s kingdom.”
9:61 Another also said, “I will follow Thee, Lord; but first permit me to say farewell to those in my house.”
9:62 Jesus said to him, “No one who puts his hand to a plow and looks backward is fit for God’s kingdom.”

LUKE 10

10:1 After this the Lord appointed seventy-two others also, and sent them two by two before His face into every city and place where He Himself was about to go.
10:2 He said to them, “The harvest is plentiful, but the workers are few. Therefore petition the Lord of the harvest to send workers out into His harvest.
10:3 Go; behold, I send you as lambs among wolves.
10:4 Carry no purse, no bag, no sandals, and greet no one along the road.
10:5 Whatever house you enter, first say, ‘Peace to this house.’
10:6 If a son of peace is there, your peace will rest upon him; otherwise it will return to you.
10:7 Remain in that same house, eating and drinking what they provide, for the worker is worthy of his pay. Do not move from house to house.
10:8 Whatever city you enter and they receive you, eat what is set before you.
10:9 Heal the sick in it and say to them, ‘God’s kingdom has drawn near to you.’
10:10 But whatever city you enter and they do not receive you, go out into its streets and say,
10:11 ‘Even the dust from your city that clings to our feet we wipe off against you. Nevertheless know this: God’s kingdom has drawn near.’
10:12 I tell you, it will be more bearable for Sodom on that day than for that city.
10:13 Woe to you, Chorazin! Woe to you, Bethsaida! For if the mighty deeds performed among you had happened in Tyre and Sidon, they would long ago have repented, sitting in sackcloth and ashes.
10:14 Nevertheless, it will be more bearable for Tyre and Sidon at the judgment than for you.
10:15 And you, Capernaum, will you be exalted as far as heaven? You will go down as far as Hades.
10:16 The one who hears you hears Me, and the one who rejects you rejects Me; the one who rejects Me rejects the One who sent Me.”
10:17 The seventy-two returned with joy, saying, “Lord, even the demons submit to us in Thy name.”
10:18 He said to them, “I was watching Satan fall from heaven like lightning.
10:19 Behold, I have given you authority to tread upon snakes and scorpions, and over all the enemy’s power, and nothing will ever harm you.
10:20 Nevertheless, do not rejoice in this, that the spirits submit to you, but rejoice that your names have been written in the heavens.”
10:21 At that very hour He rejoiced in the Holy Spirit and said, “I acknowledge Thee, Father, Lord of heaven and earth, because Thou didst hide these things from the wise and understanding and reveal them to infants. Yes, Father, for such was Thy good pleasure.
10:22 Everything has been handed over to Me by My Father. No one knows who the Son is except the Father, or who the Father is except the Son and anyone to whom the Son wishes to reveal Him.”
10:23 Turning privately to the disciples, He said, “Blessed are the eyes that see what you see.
10:24 For I tell you that many prophets and kings wished to see what you see, and did not see it, and to hear what you hear, and did not hear it.”
10:25 And behold, an expert in the law stood up to test Him, saying, “Teacher, what shall I do to inherit eternal life?”
10:26 He said to him, “What is written in the law? How do you read it?”
10:27 He answered, “You shall love the Lord your God from your whole heart, with your whole soul, with your whole strength and with your whole mind, and your neighbor as yourself.”
10:28 He said to him, “You answered correctly. Do this and you will live.”
10:29 But wishing to justify himself, he said to Jesus, “And who is my neighbor?”
10:30 Jesus replied, “A certain man was going down from Jerusalem to Jericho and fell among robbers. They stripped him, inflicted blows, and went away, leaving him half dead.
10:31 By coincidence a certain priest was going down that road. He saw him and passed by on the opposite side.
10:32 Likewise a Levite also came to the place, saw him, and passed by on the opposite side.
10:33 But a certain Samaritan, traveling, came upon him. When he saw him, he was moved with compassion.
10:34 He approached and bound his wounds, pouring on oil and wine. He placed him on his own animal, brought him to an inn, and cared for him.
10:35 On the next day he took out two denarii, gave them to the innkeeper, and said, ‘Care for him, and whatever more you spend I will repay you when I return.’
10:36 Which of these three seems to you to have become a neighbor to the man who fell among the robbers?”
10:37 He said, “The one who showed him mercy.” Jesus said to him, “Go, and you do likewise.”
10:38 As they traveled, He entered a certain village. A woman named Martha welcomed Him.
10:39 She had a sister called Mary, who also sat at the Lord’s feet and listened to His word.
10:40 But Martha was distracted by much serving. She came up and said, “Lord, dost Thou not care that my sister has left me to serve alone? Tell her, then, to help me.”
10:41 The Lord answered her, “Martha, Martha, you are anxious and troubled about many things,
10:42 but one thing is needed; for Mary has chosen the good portion, which shall not be taken from her.”

LUKE 11

11:1 And it happened that He was praying in a certain place. When He stopped, one of His disciples said to Him, “Lord, teach us to pray, just as John also taught his disciples.”
11:2 He said to them, “When you pray, say: ‘Father, let Thy name be hallowed. Let Thy kingdom come.
11:3 Give us day by day our daily bread.
11:4 And forgive us our sins, for we ourselves also forgive everyone who owes us. And do not bring us into testing.’”
11:5 And He said to them, “Which of you will have a friend and go to him at midnight and say to him, ‘Friend, lend me three loaves,
11:6 since a friend of mine has arrived at my home from a journey, and I have nothing to set before him,’
11:7 and that man will answer from inside, ‘Do not trouble me. The door is already shut, and my children are in bed with me. I cannot get up and give you anything’?
11:8 I tell you, even if he will not get up and give to him because he is his friend, yet because of his shameless insistence he will rise and give him as much as he needs.
11:9 And I tell you: ask, and it will be given to you; seek, and you will find; knock, and it will be opened to you.
11:10 For everyone who asks receives, and the one who seeks finds, and to the one who knocks it will be opened.
11:11 What father among you, if his son asks for a fish, will give him a snake instead of a fish?
11:12 Or again, if he asks for an egg, will he give him a scorpion?
11:13 So if you, being evil, know how to give good gifts to your children, how much more will the Father from heaven give the Holy Spirit to those who ask Him!”
11:14 And He was driving out a mute demon. When the demon came out, the mute man spoke, and the crowds marveled.
11:15 But some of them said, “He drives out the demons by Beelzebul, the ruler of the demons.”
11:16 Others, testing Him, were seeking from Him a sign from heaven.
11:17 But He, knowing their thoughts, said to them, “Every kingdom divided against itself is laid waste, and house falls upon house.
11:18 And if Satan also is divided against himself, how will his kingdom stand? For you say that I drive out the demons by Beelzebul.
11:19 But if I drive out the demons by Beelzebul, by whom do your sons drive them out? Therefore they will be your judges.
11:20 But if I drive out the demons by the finger of God, then the kingdom of God has come upon you.
11:21 When the strong man, fully armed, guards his own courtyard, his possessions are at peace.
11:22 But when someone stronger than he attacks and defeats him, he takes away the full armor in which he trusted and distributes his spoils.
11:23 Whoever is not with Me is against Me, and whoever does not gather with Me scatters.
11:24 When the unclean spirit comes out of a person, it passes through waterless places seeking rest. Finding none, it then says, ‘I will return to my house from which I came.’
11:25 And when it comes, it finds it swept and put in order.
11:26 Then it goes and takes seven other spirits more evil than itself, and they enter and dwell there. And the last state of that person becomes worse than the first.”
11:27 While He was saying these things, a woman from the crowd raised her voice and said to Him, “Blessed is the womb that bore Thee and the breasts at which Thou didst nurse!”
11:28 But He said, “Rather, blessed are those who hear the word of God and keep it!”
11:29 As the crowds were gathering in greater numbers, He began to say, “This generation is an evil generation. It seeks a sign, and no sign will be given to it except the sign of Jonah.
11:30 For just as Jonah became a sign to the Ninevites, so the Son of Man will also be to this generation.
11:31 The queen of the south will be raised at the judgment with the men of this generation and will condemn them, because she came from the ends of the earth to hear the wisdom of Solomon, and behold, something greater than Solomon is here.
11:32 The men of Nineveh will rise at the judgment with this generation and condemn it, because they repented at the proclamation of Jonah, and behold, something greater than Jonah is here.
11:33 No one who lights a lamp puts it in a hidden place or under the modius basket, but on the lampstand, so that those who enter may see its light.
11:34 Your eye is the lamp of the body. When your eye is single, your whole body is also full of light; but when it is evil, your body is also dark.
11:35 Watch, therefore, that the light within you is not darkness.
11:36 So if your whole body is full of light, with no part of it dark, it will be wholly full of light, as when a lamp illumines you with its radiance.”
11:37 As He was speaking, a Pharisee invited Him to dine with him. He went in and reclined at the table.
11:38 But the Pharisee, seeing this, marveled that He had not first washed before the meal.
11:39 And the Lord said to him, “Now you Pharisees cleanse the outside of the cup and the dish, but your inside is full of plunder and wickedness.
11:40 Fools! Did not the One who made the outside also make the inside?
11:41 But give what is within as alms, and behold, everything is clean for you.
11:42 But woe to you Pharisees, because you tithe mint and rue and every garden herb, and pass by justice and the love of God! You ought to have done these things without leaving those undone.
11:43 Woe to you Pharisees, because you love the foremost seat in the synagogues and greetings in the marketplaces!
11:44 Woe to you, because you are like unmarked tombs, and the people who walk over them do not know it!”
11:45 One of the experts in the Law answered Him, “Teacher, by saying these things Thou dost insult us also.”
11:46 But He said, “Woe also to you experts in the Law, because you load people with burdens hard to carry, and you yourselves do not touch the burdens with one of your fingers!
11:47 Woe to you, because you build the tombs of the prophets, but your fathers killed them!
11:48 So you are witnesses and approve the deeds of your fathers: they killed them, and you build.
11:49 For this reason the wisdom of God also said, ‘I will send them prophets and apostles, and some of these they will kill and persecute,’
11:50 so that the blood of all the prophets poured out since the foundation of the world may be required of this generation,
11:51 from the blood of Abel to the blood of Zechariah, who perished between the altar and the house. Yes, I tell you, it will be required of this generation.
11:52 Woe to you experts in the Law, because you have taken away the key of knowledge! You did not enter yourselves, and you hindered those who were entering.”
11:53 When He went out from there, the scribes and Pharisees began to press Him fiercely and question Him closely about many things,
11:54 lying in wait for Him to catch something from His mouth.

LUKE 12

12:1 Meanwhile, as the crowd gathered in its tens of thousands, so that they were trampling one another, He began to speak first to His disciples: “Guard yourselves against the leaven of the Pharisees, which is hypocrisy.
12:2 Nothing is covered up that will not be uncovered, or hidden that will not become known.
12:3 Therefore whatever you have said in the darkness will be heard in the light, and what you have spoken into the ear in the inner rooms will be proclaimed on the housetops.
12:4 I tell you, My friends, do not fear those who kill the body and after that have nothing more they can do.
12:5 But I will show you whom to fear: fear the One who, after killing, has authority to cast into Gehenna. Yes, I tell you, fear Him.
12:6 Are not five sparrows sold for two assaria? Yet not one of them is forgotten before God.
12:7 Indeed, even the hairs of your head are all numbered. Do not fear; you are worth more than many sparrows.
12:8 And I tell you, everyone who acknowledges Me before people, the Son of Man will also acknowledge before the angels of God.
12:9 But whoever denies Me before people will be denied before the angels of God.
12:10 And everyone who speaks a word against the Son of Man will be forgiven, but whoever blasphemes against the Holy Spirit will not be forgiven.
12:11 When they bring you before the synagogues and the rulers and authorities, do not be anxious about how or what you should say in your defense, or what you should say,
12:12 for the Holy Spirit will teach you in that very hour what must be said.”
12:13 Someone from the crowd said to Him, “Teacher, tell my brother to divide the inheritance with me.”
12:14 But He said to him, “Man, who appointed Me a judge or divider over you?”
12:15 And He said to them, “See to it that you guard against every kind of greed, because a person’s life does not consist in the abundance of his possessions.”
12:16 Then He told them a parable: “The land of a certain rich man yielded abundantly.
12:17 And he reasoned within himself, saying, ‘What shall I do, since I have nowhere to gather my crops?’
12:18 And he said, ‘This is what I will do: I will pull down my storehouses and build larger ones, and gather all the grain and my goods there.
12:19 And I will say to my soul, “Soul, you have many goods laid up for many years. Rest, eat, drink, be glad!”’
12:20 But God said to him, ‘Fool, this very night they demand your soul from you; and the things you have prepared—whose will they be?’
12:21 So it is with the one who stores up treasure for himself and is not rich toward God.”
12:22 And He said to His disciples, “For this reason I tell you: do not be anxious about your life, what you will eat, or about your body, what you will wear.
12:23 For life is more than food, and the body more than clothing.
12:24 Consider the ravens: they neither sow nor reap, and they have neither storeroom nor storehouse, yet God feeds them. How much more valuable you are than the birds!
12:25 And which of you by being anxious can add a cubit to his span of life?
12:26 So if you cannot do even the smallest thing, why are you anxious about the rest?
12:27 Consider the lilies, how they grow. They neither toil nor spin. Yet I tell you, not even Solomon in all his glory was clothed like one of these.
12:28 But if God so clothes the grass that is in the field today and tomorrow is thrown into the oven, how much more will He clothe you, you of little faith!
12:29 And do not seek what you will eat and what you will drink, and do not be unsettled,
12:30 for the nations of the world seek after all these things, and your Father knows that you need them.
12:31 But seek His kingdom, and these things will be added to you.
12:32 Do not fear, little flock, because your Father was pleased to give you the kingdom.
12:33 Sell your possessions and give alms. Make for yourselves purses that do not wear out, an unfailing treasure in the heavens, where no thief draws near and no moth destroys.
12:34 For where your treasure is, there your heart will be also.
12:35 Let your loins be girded and your lamps burning,
12:36 and be like people waiting for their own master to return from the wedding feast, so that when he comes and knocks, they may open to him at once.
12:37 Blessed are those slaves whom the master finds keeping watch when he comes. Amen, I tell you: he will gird himself, have them recline at table, and come and serve them.
12:38 Whether he comes in the second watch or in the third and finds them so, blessed are they.
12:39 But know this: if the master of the house had known at what hour the thief was coming, he would not have allowed his house to be broken into.
12:40 You also must be ready, because the Son of Man comes at an hour you do not expect.”
12:41 Peter said, “Lord, art Thou telling this parable to us, or also to everyone?”
12:42 And the Lord said, “Who, then, is the faithful steward, the prudent one, whom his master will appoint over his household staff to give out their ration of grain at the proper time?
12:43 Blessed is that slave whom his master finds doing so when he comes.
12:44 Truly, I tell you, he will appoint him over all his possessions.
12:45 But if that slave says in his heart, ‘My master is taking a long time to come,’ and begins to beat the male and female servants, and to eat and drink and become drunk,
12:46 the master of that slave will come on a day he does not expect and at an hour he does not know, and will cut him in two and assign his portion with the unfaithful.
12:47 That slave who knew his master’s will and did not prepare or act according to his will shall receive many blows.
12:48 But the one who did not know and did things deserving blows shall receive few. From everyone to whom much was given, much will be required; and from the one to whom they entrusted much, they will ask all the more.
12:49 I came to cast fire upon the earth, and how I wish it were already kindled!
12:50 But I have a baptism to be baptized with, and how I am constrained until it is accomplished!
12:51 Do you think I have come to give peace on earth? No, I tell you, but division.
12:52 For from now on five in one house will be divided, three against two and two against three.
12:53 They will be divided: father against son and son against father, mother against daughter and daughter against mother, mother-in-law against her daughter-in-law and daughter-in-law against her mother-in-law.”
12:54 He also said to the crowds, “When you see a cloud rising in the west, you say at once, ‘A rainstorm is coming,’ and so it happens.
12:55 And when you see a south wind blowing, you say, ‘There will be scorching heat,’ and it happens.
12:56 Hypocrites! You know how to assess the appearance of the earth and the sky, but how is it that you do not know how to assess this time?
12:57 And why do you not also judge for yourselves what is right?
12:58 For as you go with your adversary before a ruler, make an effort on the way to be released from him, lest he drag you before the judge, and the judge hand you over to the officer, and the officer throw you into prison.
12:59 I tell you, you will certainly not come out from there until you have paid even the last lepton.”

LUKE 13

13:1 At that very time some people were present who told Him about the Galileans whose blood Pilate had mixed with their sacrifices.
13:2 And He answered them, “Do you think these Galileans were greater sinners than all the other Galileans because they suffered these things?
13:3 No, I tell you; but unless you repent, you will all likewise perish.
13:4 Or those eighteen on whom the tower in Siloam fell and killed them—do you think they were greater debtors than all the people living in Jerusalem?
13:5 No, I tell you; but unless you repent, you will all perish in the same way.”
13:6 And He told this parable: “A certain man had a fig tree planted in his vineyard. He came looking for fruit on it and found none.
13:7 So he said to the vineyard worker, ‘Behold, for three years I have been coming to look for fruit on this fig tree and have found none. Cut it down, then! Why should it also make the ground unproductive?’
13:8 But he answered him, ‘Master, leave it this year also, until I dig around it and put on manure.
13:9 And if it produces fruit in the coming year—but if not, you shall cut it down.’”
13:10 Now He was teaching in one of the synagogues on the Sabbath.
13:11 And behold, there was a woman who had had a spirit of weakness for eighteen years. She was bent over and could not straighten up completely.
13:12 When Jesus saw her, He called to her and said, “Woman, you have been released from your weakness.”
13:13 And He laid His hands on her, and at once she was made straight, and she glorified God.
13:14 But the synagogue ruler, indignant because Jesus had healed on the Sabbath, said to the crowd, “There are six days on which work ought to be done. So come and be healed on those days, and not on the Sabbath day.”
13:15 But the Lord answered him, “Hypocrites! Does not each of you on the Sabbath untie his ox or donkey from the manger and lead it away to give it water?
13:16 And this woman, who is a daughter of Abraham, whom Satan bound for, behold, eighteen years—ought she not to have been released from this bond on the Sabbath day?”
13:17 As He said these things, all who opposed Him were put to shame, and the whole crowd rejoiced at all the glorious things being done by Him.
13:18 So He said, “What is the kingdom of God like, and to what shall I compare it?
13:19 It is like a mustard seed that a man took and cast into his own garden. It grew and became a tree, and the birds of the sky nested in its branches.”
13:20 And again He said, “To what shall I compare the kingdom of God?
13:21 It is like leaven that a woman took and hid in three sata of flour, until the whole was leavened.”
13:22 And He traveled through cities and villages, teaching and making His journey toward Jerusalem.
13:23 Someone said to Him, “Lord, are those who are being saved few?” And He said to them,
13:24 “Strive to enter through the narrow door, because many, I tell you, will seek to enter and will not be able,
13:25 once the master of the house rises and shuts the door. Then you will begin to stand outside and knock at the door, saying, ‘Lord, open to us!’ And he will answer you, ‘I do not know you or where you come from.’
13:26 Then you will begin to say, ‘We ate and drank in your presence, and you taught in our streets.’
13:27 And he will say to you, ‘I do not know you or where you come from. Depart from me, all you workers of unrighteousness!’
13:28 There will be weeping and grinding of teeth there, when you see Abraham and Isaac and Jacob and all the prophets in the kingdom of God, but yourselves being thrown outside.
13:29 And people will come from east and west and from north and south and recline at table in the kingdom of God.
13:30 And behold, there are last who will be first, and there are first who will be last.”
13:31 At that very hour some Pharisees approached and said to Him, “Leave and go away from here, because Herod wants to kill Thee.”
13:32 And He said to them, “Go and tell that fox, ‘Behold, I drive out demons and accomplish healings today and tomorrow, and on the third day I am brought to completion.’
13:33 Nevertheless, I must travel today and tomorrow and the day following, because it is not possible for a prophet to perish outside Jerusalem.
13:34 Jerusalem, Jerusalem, who kills the prophets and stones those sent to her! How often I wanted to gather your children as a bird gathers her own brood under her wings, and you were not willing!
13:35 Behold, your house is left to you. And I tell you, you will certainly not see Me until the time comes when you say, ‘Blessed is He who comes in the name of the Lord!’”

LUKE 14

14:1 And it happened that He went into the house of one of the leaders of the Pharisees on a Sabbath to eat bread, and they were watching Him closely.
14:2 And behold, a certain man suffering from dropsy was before Him.
14:3 Jesus spoke to the experts in the Law and the Pharisees, saying, “Is it lawful to heal on the Sabbath, or not?”
14:4 But they were silent. And He took hold of the man, healed him, and sent him away.
14:5 And He said to them, “Which of you, if his son or ox falls into a well, will not pull him out at once on the Sabbath day?”
14:6 And they were unable to answer these things.
14:7 He told a parable to those who had been invited, noticing how they chose the foremost places at table. He said to them,
14:8 “When someone invites you to a wedding feast, do not recline in the foremost place. Someone more honored than you may have been invited by him,
14:9 and the one who invited you both will come and say to you, ‘Give this man your place.’ Then you will begin, with shame, to occupy the last place.
14:10 But when you are invited, go and recline in the last place, so that when the one who invited you comes, he will say to you, ‘Friend, move up higher.’ Then you will have honor before all who recline at table with you.
14:11 For everyone who exalts himself will be humbled, and the one who humbles himself will be exalted.”
14:12 He also said to the one who had invited Him, “When you give a midday meal or a supper, do not invite your friends or your brothers or your relatives or rich neighbors, lest they also invite you in return and you receive repayment.
14:13 But when you give a reception, invite the poor, the disabled, the lame, and the blind.
14:14 And you will be blessed, because they have nothing with which to repay you; for you will be repaid at the resurrection of the righteous.”
14:15 When one of those reclining at table heard these things, he said to Him, “Blessed is whoever shall eat bread in the kingdom of God!”
14:16 But He said to him, “A certain man was giving a great supper, and he invited many.
14:17 At the hour of the supper he sent his slave to tell those who had been invited, ‘Come, because things are now ready.’
14:18 And they all alike began to excuse themselves. The first said to him, ‘I bought a field, and I need to go out and see it. I ask you to excuse me.’
14:19 And another said, ‘I bought five pairs of oxen, and I am going to test them. I ask you to excuse me.’
14:20 And another said, ‘I married a wife, and for this reason I cannot come.’
14:21 The slave returned and reported these things to his master. Then the master of the house became angry and said to his slave, ‘Go out quickly into the streets and lanes of the city, and bring the poor, the disabled, the blind, and the lame in here.’
14:22 And the slave said, ‘Master, what you commanded has been done, and there is still room.’
14:23 And the master said to the slave, ‘Go out into the roads and along the fences, and compel people to come in, so that my house may be filled.
14:24 For I tell you, none of those men who were invited will taste my supper.’”
14:25 Now great crowds were traveling with Him. He turned and said to them,
14:26 “If anyone comes to Me and does not hate his own father and mother and wife and children and brothers and sisters, and even his own life, he cannot be My disciple.
14:27 Whoever does not carry his own cross and come after Me cannot be My disciple.
14:28 For which of you, wanting to build a tower, does not first sit down and calculate the expense, whether he has enough to finish it?
14:29 Otherwise, when he has laid a foundation and is unable to complete it, all who see it may begin to mock him,
14:30 saying, ‘This man began to build and was unable to finish.’
14:31 Or what king, going to meet another king in war, will not first sit down and consider whether he is able with ten thousand to meet the one coming against him with twenty thousand?
14:32 And if not, while the other is still far away, he sends a delegation and asks for terms of peace.
14:33 So, therefore, every one of you who does not renounce all his own possessions cannot be My disciple.
14:34 Salt, then, is good. But if even the salt becomes tasteless, with what will it be seasoned?
14:35 It is fit neither for the soil nor for the manure heap; they throw it outside. Whoever has ears to hear, let him hear.”

LUKE 15

15:1 Now all the tax collectors and sinners were drawing near to hear Him.
15:2 And both the Pharisees and the scribes grumbled, saying, “This man welcomes sinners and eats with them.”
15:3 So He told them this parable:
15:4 “What man among you, having a hundred sheep and losing one of them, does not leave the ninety-nine in the wilderness and go after the lost one until he finds it?
15:5 And when he finds it, he lays it on his shoulders, rejoicing.
15:6 When he comes home, he calls his friends and neighbors together and says to them, ‘Rejoice with me, because I found my sheep that was lost!’
15:7 I tell you, in the same way there will be more joy in heaven over one sinner who repents than over ninety-nine righteous people who have no need of repentance.
15:8 Or what woman, having ten drachmas, if she loses one drachma, does not light a lamp and sweep the house and search carefully until she finds it?
15:9 And when she finds it, she calls her women friends and neighbors together, saying, ‘Rejoice with me, because I found the drachma that I lost!’
15:10 In the same way, I tell you, there is joy before the angels of God over one sinner who repents.”
15:11 And He said, “A certain man had two sons.
15:12 The younger of them said to his father, ‘Father, give me the share of the property that falls to me.’ And he divided his livelihood between them.
15:13 Not many days later, the younger son gathered everything together and traveled to a distant country. There he scattered his property by living recklessly.
15:14 When he had spent everything, a severe famine arose throughout that country, and he began to be in need.
15:15 So he went and attached himself to one of the citizens of that country, who sent him into his fields to feed pigs.
15:16 And he longed to be filled with the carob pods the pigs were eating, and no one gave him anything.
15:17 But when he came to himself, he said, ‘How many of my father’s hired workers have bread to spare, while I am perishing here from hunger!
15:18 I will rise and go to my father and say to him, “Father, I have sinned against heaven and before you.
15:19 I am no longer worthy to be called your son. Make me like one of your hired workers.”’
15:20 And he rose and came to his own father. But while he was still far away, his father saw him and was moved with compassion. He ran, fell upon his neck, and kissed him tenderly.
15:21 And the son said to him, ‘Father, I have sinned against heaven and before you; I am no longer worthy to be called your son.’
15:22 But the father said to his slaves, ‘Quickly bring out the best robe, and clothe him; and put a ring on his hand, and sandals on his feet.
15:23 And bring the fattened calf, and slaughter it; and let us eat and rejoice,
15:24 because this son of mine was dead and came to life again; he was lost and was found.’ And they began to be glad.
15:25 Now his older son was in the field. As he came and drew near the house, he heard music and dancing.
15:26 And he called one of the servants and asked what these things might mean.
15:27 He said to him, ‘Your brother has come, and your father slaughtered the fattened calf because he received him back in good health.’
15:28 But he became angry and was unwilling to enter. His father came out and pleaded with him.
15:29 But he answered his father, ‘Behold, all these years I have served you as a slave, and I never disobeyed your command; yet you never gave me a young goat so that I might be glad with my friends.
15:30 But when this son of yours came, who devoured your livelihood with prostitutes, you slaughtered the fattened calf for him!’
15:31 And he said to him, ‘Child, you are always with me, and everything of mine is yours.
15:32 But we had to be glad and rejoice, because this brother of yours was dead and came to life; he was lost and was found.’”

LUKE 16

16:1 He also said to the disciples, “There was a certain rich man who had a steward, and this man was accused before him of scattering his possessions.
16:2 He called him and said to him, ‘What is this I hear about you? Give an account of your stewardship, for you can no longer be steward.’
16:3 And the steward said within himself, ‘What shall I do, since my master is taking the stewardship away from me? I am not strong enough to dig; I am ashamed to beg.
16:4 I know what I will do, so that when I am removed from the stewardship, they may welcome me into their own houses.’
16:5 And he called each one of his own master’s debtors and said to the first, ‘How much do you owe my master?’
16:6 He said, ‘A hundred baths of oil.’ And he said to him, ‘Take your document, sit down quickly, and write fifty.’
16:7 Then he said to another, ‘And how much do you owe?’ He said, ‘A hundred kors of wheat.’ He said to him, ‘Take your document and write eighty.’
16:8 And the master praised the unrighteous steward because he had acted prudently. For the sons of this age are more prudent toward their own generation than the sons of light.
16:9 And I tell you, make friends for yourselves from the mammon of unrighteousness, so that when it fails, they may welcome you into the eternal tents.
16:10 The one who is faithful in the least is faithful also in much, and the one who is unrighteous in the least is unrighteous also in much.
16:11 So if you have not been faithful with unrighteous mammon, who will entrust the true riches to you?
16:12 And if you have not been faithful with what belongs to another, who will give you what is your own?
16:13 No household servant can serve two masters as a slave. For either he will hate the one and love the other, or he will hold to the one and despise the other. You cannot serve God and mammon.”
16:14 Now the Pharisees, who were lovers of money, heard all these things and sneered at Him.
16:15 And He said to them, “You are those who justify yourselves before people, but God knows your hearts, because what is exalted among people is an abomination before God.
16:16 The Law and the Prophets were until John. From that time the kingdom of God is proclaimed as good news, and everyone presses into it.
16:17 But it is easier for heaven and earth to pass away than for one small stroke of the Law to fall.
16:18 Everyone who divorces his wife and marries another commits adultery, and the one who marries a woman divorced from a husband commits adultery.
16:19 Now there was a certain rich man, and he clothed himself in purple and fine linen, enjoying himself in splendor every day.
16:20 And a certain poor man named Lazarus had been laid at his gateway, covered with sores
16:21 and longing to be filled with what fell from the rich man’s table. Even the dogs came and licked his sores.
16:22 And it happened that the poor man died and was carried away by the angels to Abraham’s bosom. The rich man also died and was buried.
16:23 And in Hades, being in torment, he lifted his eyes and saw Abraham far away and Lazarus in his bosom.
16:24 And he called out, ‘Father Abraham, have mercy on me and send Lazarus to dip the tip of his finger in water and cool my tongue, because I am in pain in this flame.’
16:25 But Abraham said, ‘Child, remember that you received your good things during your life, and Lazarus likewise bad things. But now he is comforted here, and you are in pain.
16:26 And besides all this, a great chasm has been fixed between us and you, so that those who want to cross from here to you cannot, nor may they cross over from there to us.’
16:27 And he said, ‘Then I ask you, father, to send him to my father’s house,
16:28 for I have five brothers, so that he may solemnly warn them, lest they also come to this place of torment.’
16:29 But Abraham said, ‘They have Moses and the Prophets; let them hear them.’
16:30 But he said, ‘No, father Abraham; but if someone goes to them from the dead, they will repent.’
16:31 And he said to him, ‘If they do not hear Moses and the Prophets, neither will they be persuaded if someone rises from the dead.’”

LUKE 17

17:1 And He said to His disciples, “It is impossible for stumbling blocks not to come, but woe to the one through whom they come!
17:2 It would be better for him to have a millstone placed around his neck and be thrown into the sea than to cause one of these little ones to stumble.
17:3 Watch yourselves. If your brother sins, rebuke him; and if he repents, forgive him.
17:4 And if he sins against you seven times in the day and turns back to you seven times, saying, ‘I repent,’ you shall forgive him.”
17:5 And the apostles said to the Lord, “Increase our faith.”
17:6 And the Lord said, “If you have faith like a mustard seed, you would say to this mulberry tree, ‘Be uprooted and planted in the sea,’ and it would obey you.
17:7 Which of you, having a slave plowing or tending sheep, will say to him when he comes in from the field, ‘Come at once and recline at table’?
17:8 Will he not instead say to him, ‘Prepare something for my supper, gird yourself, and serve me while I eat and drink; and after that you shall eat and drink’?
17:9 Does he thank the slave because he did what was commanded?
17:10 So you also, when you have done everything commanded you, say, ‘We are unprofitable slaves; we have done what we ought to have done.’”
17:11 And it happened that, as He was traveling to Jerusalem, He passed through the midst of Samaria and Galilee.

23:4 And Pilate said to the chief priests and the crowds, “I find no ground for a charge against this man.”
23:5 But they insisted, saying, “He stirs up the people, teaching throughout all Judea, beginning from Galilee even to this place.”
23:6 When Pilate heard this, he asked whether the man was a Galilean.
23:7 And learning that He belonged to Herod’s jurisdiction, he sent Him to Herod, who was himself also in Jerusalem in those days.
23:8 When Herod saw Jesus, he rejoiced greatly, for he had long wanted to see Him because he had heard about Him, and he hoped to see some sign performed by Him.
23:9 He questioned Him at length, but He gave him no answer.
23:10 And the chief priests and scribes stood there, accusing Him vehemently.
23:11 Herod also, with his troops, treated Him with contempt and mocked Him. He clothed Him in splendid clothing and sent Him back to Pilate.
23:12 Herod and Pilate became friends with one another on that very day, for previously they had been at enmity with each other.
23:13 Pilate called together the chief priests, the rulers, and the people
23:14 and said to them, “You brought this man to me as one turning the people astray. And behold, I examined Him before you and found no ground in this man for the accusations you bring against Him.
23:15 Nor did Herod, for he sent Him back to us. And behold, nothing deserving death has been done by Him.
23:16 Therefore I will punish Him and release Him.”
23:18 But they cried out all together, saying, “Take this man away, and release Barabbas to us!”
23:19 He had been thrown into prison for a certain uprising that took place in the city and for murder.
23:20 Pilate addressed them again, wanting to release Jesus.
23:21 But they kept shouting, saying, “Crucify, crucify Him!”
23:22 And for the third time he said to them, “Why, what evil has this man done? I found no ground for death in Him. Therefore I will punish Him and release Him.”
23:23 But they pressed him with loud voices, demanding that He be crucified, and their voices prevailed.
23:24 And Pilate decided that their demand should be granted.
23:25 He released the man who had been thrown into prison for uprising and murder, whom they were demanding; but he handed Jesus over to their will.
23:26 As they led Him away, they seized a certain Simon of Cyrene coming from the countryside, and laid the cross on him to carry behind Jesus.
23:27 And a great multitude of the people followed Him, and women who beat themselves in mourning and lamented Him.
23:28 But Jesus turned toward them and said, “Daughters of Jerusalem, do not weep over Me, but weep over yourselves and over your children.
23:29 For behold, days are coming in which they will say, ‘Blessed are the barren, and the wombs that did not bear, and the breasts that did not nourish.’
23:30 Then they will begin to say to the mountains, ‘Fall upon us,’ and to the hills, ‘Cover us.’
23:31 For if they do these things with the green wood, what will happen with the dry?”
23:32 Two others also, criminals, were being led with Him to be put to death.
23:33 When they came to the place called Skull, there they crucified Him and the criminals, one on the right and the other on the left.
23:34 But Jesus said, “Father, forgive them, for they do not know what they are doing.” And dividing His garments, they cast lots.
23:35 The people stood watching. The rulers also sneered, saying, “He saved others; let Him save Himself, if this is the Christ of God, the chosen One.”
23:36 The soldiers also mocked Him, coming up and offering Him sour wine
23:37 and saying, “If Thou art the King of the Jews, save Thyself.”
23:38 There was also an inscription above Him: “This is the King of the Jews.”
23:39 One of the criminals who were hanging there blasphemed Him, saying, “Art Thou not the Christ? Save Thyself and us!”
23:40 But the other answered, rebuking him, “Do you not even fear God, since you are under the same judgment?
23:41 And we justly so, for we receive what our deeds deserve. But this man did nothing out of place.”
23:42 And he said, “Jesus, remember me when Thou comest into Thy kingdom.”
23:43 And He said to him, “Amen, I tell you: today you will be with Me in Paradise.”
23:44 And it was already about the sixth hour, and darkness came over the whole land until the ninth hour,
23:45 as the sun’s light failed. And the curtain of the sanctuary was split down the middle.
23:46 And Jesus cried out with a loud voice and said, “Father, into Thy hands I entrust My spirit.” Having said this, He breathed His last.
23:47 When the centurion saw what had happened, he glorified God, saying, “Truly, this man was righteous.”
23:48 And all the crowds who had gathered for this spectacle, seeing what had happened, returned beating their breasts.
23:49 All His acquaintances stood at a distance, and the women who had followed Him from Galilee, watching these things.
23:50 And behold, there was a man named Joseph, a council member, a good and righteous man.
23:51 He had not consented to their decision and action. He was from Arimathea, a city of the Jews, and was awaiting the kingdom of God.
23:52 This man approached Pilate and asked for the body of Jesus.
23:53 Taking it down, he wrapped it in linen and laid Him in a rock-cut tomb where no one had yet been laid.
23:54 It was the day of Preparation, and the Sabbath was beginning.
23:55 The women who had come with Him from Galilee followed and saw the tomb and how His body was laid.
23:56 Then they returned and prepared spices and perfumed ointments. And on the Sabbath they rested according to the commandment.

LUKE 24

24:1 But on the first day of the week, at early dawn, they came to the tomb, carrying the spices they had prepared.
24:2 They found the stone rolled away from the tomb,
24:3 but when they entered, they did not find the body of the Lord Jesus.
24:4 And it happened that, while they were perplexed about this, behold, two men stood beside them in flashing clothing.
24:5 They became afraid and bowed their faces to the ground, and the men said to them, “Why do you seek the living One among the dead?
24:6 He is not here, but has been raised. Remember how He spoke to you while He was still in Galilee,
24:7 saying that the Son of Man must be handed over into the hands of sinful people and be crucified and rise on the third day.”
24:8 And they remembered His words,
24:9 and returned from the tomb and reported all these things to the Eleven and to all the rest.
24:10 It was Mary Magdalene and Joanna and Mary of James and the other women with them who told these things to the apostles.
24:11 But these words seemed to them like nonsense, and they did not believe the women.
24:12 Yet Peter rose and ran to the tomb. Stooping to look in, he saw the linen cloths alone, and went away, wondering to himself at what had happened.
24:13 And behold, two of them were traveling that same day to a village named Emmaus, sixty stadia away from Jerusalem.
24:14 They were talking with one another about all these things that had happened.
24:15 And it happened that, as they talked and discussed, Jesus Himself drew near and traveled with them.
24:16 But their eyes were held back from recognizing Him.
24:17 And He said to them, “What are these words you are exchanging with one another as you walk?” And they stood still, downcast.
24:18 One named Cleopas answered Him, “Art Thou the only visitor in Jerusalem who does not know the things that have happened there in these days?”
24:19 And He said to them, “What things?” They said to Him, “The things concerning Jesus the Nazarene, who was a prophet mighty in deed and word before God and all the people,
24:20 and how our chief priests and rulers handed Him over to a judgment of death and crucified Him.
24:21 But we were hoping that He was the One about to redeem Israel. Yet indeed, besides all these things, this is the third day since these things happened.
24:22 Moreover, some women from among us astonished us. They went to the tomb early
24:23 and did not find His body, and came saying that they had also seen a vision of angels who say that He is alive.
24:24 And some of those with us went to the tomb and found it just as the women had said, but Him they did not see.”
24:25 And He said to them, “O foolish ones, and slow in heart to believe all that the prophets spoke!
24:26 Was it not necessary for the Christ to suffer these things and enter into His glory?”
24:27 And beginning from Moses and from all the prophets, He interpreted to them in all the Scriptures the things concerning Himself.
24:28 They drew near the village to which they were traveling, and He acted as though He would travel farther.
24:29 But they urged Him strongly, saying, “Stay with us, because it is toward evening and the day has already waned.” And He went in to stay with them.
24:30 And it happened that as He reclined at table with them, He took the bread, blessed it, broke it, and gave it to them.
24:31 Then their eyes were opened and they recognized Him, and He vanished from their sight.
24:32 And they said to one another, “Was not our heart burning within us as He spoke to us on the road, as He opened the Scriptures to us?”
24:33 They rose that very hour and returned to Jerusalem, and found the Eleven and those with them gathered together,
24:34 saying, “The Lord has truly been raised and has appeared to Simon!”
24:35 And they related the things that happened on the road, and how He was made known to them in the breaking of the bread.
24:36 While they were speaking these things, He Himself stood among them and said to them, “Peace be with you.”
24:37 But they were startled and became afraid, thinking they were seeing a spirit.
24:38 And He said to them, “Why are you troubled, and why do doubts arise in your heart?
24:39 See My hands and My feet, that it is I Myself. Feel Me and see, because a spirit does not have flesh and bones as you see that I have.”
24:40 And having said this, He showed them His hands and feet.
24:41 While they still did not believe for joy and were marveling, He said to them, “Do you have anything to eat here?”
24:42 They gave Him a portion of broiled fish.
24:43 And He took it and ate before them.
24:44 And He said to them, “These are My words that I spoke to you while I was still with you: everything written about Me in the Law of Moses and the Prophets and Psalms must be fulfilled.”
24:45 Then He opened their mind to understand the Scriptures
24:46 and said to them, “Thus it is written that the Christ should suffer and rise from the dead on the third day,
24:47 and that repentance for the forgiveness of sins should be proclaimed in His name to all the nations—beginning from Jerusalem.
24:48 You are witnesses of these things.
24:49 And behold, I am sending out the promise of My Father upon you; but remain in the city until you are clothed with power from on high.”
24:50 And He led them out as far as toward Bethany, and lifted His hands and blessed them.
24:51 And it happened that while He blessed them, He parted from them and was carried up into heaven.
24:52 And they, having worshiped Him, returned to Jerusalem with great joy,
24:53 and were continually in the temple, blessing God.
