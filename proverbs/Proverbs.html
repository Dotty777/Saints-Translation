Proverbs 1

Read this chapter on staging

1:1The proverbs of Solomon son of David, who reigned in Israel,

1:2for knowing wisdom and discipline and understanding words of good sense,

1:3for receiving turns of speech, understanding true righteousness, and directing judgment rightly,

1:4so that he may give shrewdness to the innocent, perception and thought to a young child.

1:5For a wise person who hears these things will become wiser, and the discerning will acquire guidance,

1:6and understand a comparison and an obscure saying, the utterances of the wise and riddles.

1:7The fear of God is the beginning of wisdom, and good understanding belongs to all who practice it. Reverence toward God is the beginning of perception, but the ungodly will despise wisdom and discipline.

1:8Listen, son, to your father’s discipline, and do not reject your mother’s ordinances,

1:9for you will receive a crown of graces for your head and a golden collar around your neck.

1:10Son, do not let ungodly men lead you astray; do not agree if they invite you, saying,

1:11Come with us; share in bloodshed. Let us hide a righteous man in the earth unjustly;

1:12let us swallow him alive like Hades and remove his memory from the earth.

1:13Let us seize his costly property and fill our houses with spoils.

1:14Cast your lot among us; let us all obtain a common purse, and let there be one moneybag for us.

1:15Do not walk in the way with them; turn your foot away from their paths,

1:16for their feet run toward evil, and they are quick to shed blood.

1:17For nets are not unjustly stretched out for winged creatures.

1:18For those who share in murder store up evils for themselves, and the overthrow of lawless men is evil.

1:19These are the ways of all who carry out lawless deeds, for through ungodliness they take away their own life.

1:20Wisdom is celebrated at the exits, and in the squares she speaks openly.

1:21At the tops of walls she is proclaimed; at rulers’ gates she sits nearby, and at city gates she speaks boldly:

1:22As long as the innocent hold to righteousness, they will not be ashamed. But the senseless, desiring insolence, became ungodly and hated perception,

1:23and became liable to reproofs. Behold, I will send forth to you an utterance of my breath, and I will teach you my word.

1:24Since I kept calling and you did not obey, and kept extending words and you paid no attention,

1:25but you were making my counsels ineffective and disobeyed my reproofs,

1:26therefore I too will laugh at your ruin and exult when destruction comes to you,

1:27and when sudden tumult reaches you and overthrow arrives like a storm, when affliction and siege come upon you, or when destruction comes to you.

1:28For when you call upon me, I will not listen to you; evil people will seek me and will not find me,

1:29for they hated wisdom and did not choose the fear of the Lord,

1:30nor did they want to attend to my counsels, but they were sneering at my reproofs.

1:31Therefore they will eat the fruits of their own way and be filled with their own ungodliness.

1:32For because they wronged the innocent, they will be murdered, and examination will destroy the ungodly.

1:33But whoever listens to me will dwell in hope and rest fearlessly from every evil.

Saints English wording notes

Proverbs 1:2 · discipline

Sense. Instruction or formative training in the book's stated purpose.

Keep wisdom and discipline distinct. Discipline can include correction, but this opening does not restrict it to punishment; the following understanding clause explains another part of learning.

Historical comparison. KJV and Brenton use instruction. DRC divides the corresponding material between 1:2 and 1:3, so both numbered verses are shown.

Proverbs 1:7 · perception

Sense. Discernment associated with reverence toward God.

Retain the Greek's extended sequence of fear, understanding, reverence, and perception. Explain each relationship without collapsing the whole verse into the shorter familiar maxim.

Historical comparison. KJV speaks of the beginning of knowledge; DRC has wisdom. Saints and Brenton include the additional reverence and discernment clause found in the checked Greek.

Proverbs 2

Read this chapter on staging

2:1Son, if you receive an utterance of my commandment and hide it within yourself,

2:2your ear will obey wisdom, and you will apply your heart to understanding; you will also apply it to admonishing your son.

2:3for if you call upon wisdom and give your voice to understanding, and seek perception with a loud voice,

2:4and if you seek it like silver and search it out like treasures,

2:5then you will understand the fear of the Lord and find the knowledge of God.

2:6For the Lord gives wisdom, and from His presence come knowledge and understanding.

2:7He stores up salvation for those acting rightly; He will shield their journey,

2:8to guard the ways of righteous ordinances, and He will protect the way of those who revere Him.

2:9Then you will understand righteousness and judgment, and you will make all good courses straight.

2:10For if wisdom enters your mind and perception seems good to your soul,

2:11good counsel will guard you, and holy thought will keep you,

2:12so that it may rescue you from an evil way and from a man who says nothing trustworthy.

2:13O you who abandon straight ways to walk in ways of darkness,

2:14who delight in evils and rejoice in evil distortion,

2:15whose paths are crooked and whose tracks are bent,

2:16to put you far from a straight way and make you alien to a righteous judgment.

2:17Son, do not let evil counsel seize you, counsel that abandons the teaching of youth and has forgotten a divine covenant.

2:18For she has set her house beside death and her courses beside Hades with the earthborn.

2:19All who travel in her will not return, nor will they reach straight paths, for they are not reached by years of life.

2:20For if they had walked good paths, they would have found smooth paths of righteousness.

2:21Good people will inhabit the land, and the innocent will remain in it, for the upright will dwell in the land and the holy will remain in it.

2:22The ways of the ungodly will perish from the land, and the lawless will be driven out of it.

Proverbs 3

Read this chapter on staging

3:1Son, do not forget my ordinances; let your heart keep my words,

3:2for they will add length of life, years of living, and peace to you.

3:3Do not let acts of mercy and faithfulness fail you. Fasten them around your neck, and you will find favor.

3:4And take thought for good things before the Lord and people.

3:5Trust God with your whole heart; do not exalt yourself in your own wisdom.

3:6In all your ways acknowledge her, that she may cut your ways straight; and your foot will surely not stumble.

3:7Do not be prudent in your own estimation; fear God and turn away from every evil.

3:8Then there will be healing for your body and care for your bones.

3:9Honor the Lord from your righteous labors, and offer Him firstfruits from your fruits of righteousness,

3:10so that your storerooms may be filled with an abundance of grain and your vats gush with wine.

3:11Son, do not make light of the Lord’s discipline or lose heart when corrected by Him,

3:12for the Lord disciplines the one He loves and scourges every son He receives.

3:13Blessed is the person who found wisdom, the mortal who saw good sense,

3:14for trading in her is better than treasures of gold and silver.

3:15She is more valuable than costly stones; nothing evil will stand against her. She is readily known to all who approach her, and nothing precious is worthy of her.

3:16For length of life and years of living are in her right hand, and wealth and glory in her left.

3:16aRighteousness proceeds from her mouth, and she carries law and mercy on her tongue.

3:17Her ways are good ways, and all her paths are in peace.

3:18She is a tree of life to all who hold her fast, and secure for those who lean on her as on the Lord.

3:19God founded the earth by wisdom and prepared the heavens by good sense.

3:20By perception the deeps were split open, and clouds flowed with dew.

3:21Son, do not drift away; keep my counsel and thought,

3:22so that your soul may live and grace be around your neck.

3:22aThere will be healing for your flesh and care for your bones,

3:23so that you may walk all your ways confidently in peace, and your foot will certainly not stumble.

3:24For if you sit, you will be fearless, and if you sleep, you will sleep pleasantly.

3:25You will not fear an approaching fright or the oncoming assaults of the ungodly,

3:26for the Lord will be upon all your ways and support your foot so that you are not shaken.

3:27Do not refrain from doing good to someone in need whenever your hand has the means to help.

3:28Do not say, ‘Go away and come back; tomorrow I will give,’ when you are able to do good, for you do not know what the coming day will bring forth.

3:29Do not devise evils against your friend who lives nearby and trusts in you.

3:30Do not seek enmity with anyone in vain, lest he do you some harm.

3:31Do not acquire the reproaches of evil men or imitate their ways,

3:32for every lawless person is unclean before the Lord, and he does not sit in council among the righteous.

3:33God’s curse is in the houses of the ungodly, but the dwellings of the righteous are blessed.

3:34The Lord opposes the proud, but gives grace to the humble.

3:35The wise will inherit glory, but the ungodly raised up dishonor.

Proverbs 3:3 · acts of mercy

Sense. Merciful deeds and expressions of reliability or fidelity.

Preserve the plural nouns through an explanatory English phrase and retain the neck image. Almsgiving is a related sense of the first noun elsewhere, not its compulsory meaning in this paired ethical instruction.

Historical comparison. KJV, Brenton, and DRC have mercy and truth. The Greek's finding-favour clause appears in this verse; Brenton continues it in 3:4. KJV and DRC include a heart-tablet clause absent here.

Proverbs 4

Read this chapter on staging

4:1Listen, children, to a father’s discipline; pay attention to gain discernment,

4:2for I give you a good gift; do not abandon my law.

4:3For I too became a son obedient to a father and loved in a mother’s presence,

4:4who used to speak to me and teach me: Let our word take hold in your heart;

4:5keep commandments; do not forget or disregard an utterance of my mouth,

4:6and do not abandon her, and she will hold fast to you; love her, and she will keep you.

4:8Fortify her, and she will exalt you; honor her so that she may embrace you,

4:9so that she may give your head a crown of graces and shield you with a crown of delight.

4:10Listen, son, and receive my words, and your years of life will multiply, so that many ways of living may be yours,

4:11for I teach you ways of wisdom and lead you onto straight tracks.

4:12For if you walk, your steps will not be shut in, and if you run, you will not grow weary.

4:13Take hold of my discipline; do not release it, but keep it for yourself for your life.

4:14Do not enter the ways of the ungodly or imitate the ways of the lawless.

4:15Wherever they encamp, do not go there; turn away from them and pass by.

4:16For they will not sleep unless they do harm; their sleep has been taken away, and they do not lie down.

4:17For these people eat food of ungodliness and get drunk on lawless wine.

4:18But the ways of the righteous shine like light; they go forward and illuminate until the day comes right.

4:19But the ways of the ungodly are dark; they do not know how they stumble.

4:20Son, attend to my utterance; apply your ear to my words,

4:21so that your springs do not fail you; keep them in your heart,

4:22for there is life for those who find them and healing for all flesh.

4:23With every guard keep your heart, for from these come life’s outlets.

4:24Remove a crooked mouth from yourself and thrust unjust lips far away from you.

4:25Let your eyes look straight, and let your eyelids incline toward righteous things.

4:26Make straight tracks for your feet and direct your ways rightly.

4:27Do not turn to the right or the left; turn your foot away from an evil way.

4:27aFor God knows the ways on the right, but those on the left are perverted.

4:27bHe Himself will make your tracks straight and advance your journeys in peace.

Proverbs 5

Read this chapter on staging

5:1Son, attend to my wisdom, and incline your ear to my words,

5:2so that you may guard good understanding. I command you to heed the discernment of my lips.

5:3Do not attend to a base woman, for honey drips from a prostitute’s lips; for a time she oils your throat.

5:4Afterward, however, you will find it more bitter than gall and sharper than a two-edged blade.

5:5For folly’s feet lead those who engage with her down with death into Hades; her tracks are not firmly set.

5:6For she does not travel life’s ways; her tracks are slippery and not easy to recognize.

5:7Now therefore, son, listen to me, and do not make my words ineffective.

5:8Put your way far from her; do not approach the doors of her houses,

5:9so that you do not surrender your life to others and your livelihood to the merciless,

5:10so that outsiders are not filled with your strength, and your labors enter outsiders’ houses,

5:11and you will come to regret it at the end, when the flesh of your body is worn away,

5:12and you will say, “How I hated discipline! My heart turned aside from rebukes.

5:13I did not listen to the voice of the one disciplining and teaching me, nor incline my ear.

5:14I was nearly in every kind of evil in the midst of the assembly and congregation.”

5:15Drink waters from your vessels and from your spring wells.

5:16Do not let waters overflow for you from your spring; but let your waters travel into your own squares.

5:17Let them belong to you alone, and let no outsider share with you.

5:18Let your fountain of water be your own, and share joy with the wife of your youth.

5:19Let a deer of affection and a colt of your graces keep company with you. Let your own woman lead you and be with you at every time, for as you move in the company of her affection, you will be one among many.

5:20Do not be much involved with another woman, nor be held in the arms of one who is not your own.

5:21For a man’s ways are before God’s eyes, and He watches all his tracks.

5:22Lawless acts hunt a man, and each is bound tight by the cords of his own sins.

5:23He dies among the undisciplined; from the abundance of his own life he was cast out and perished through folly.

Proverbs 6

Read this chapter on staging

6:1Son, if you act as guarantor for your friend, you will give your hand over to an enemy.

6:2For a man’s own lips are a strong trap, and he is caught by the lips of his own mouth.

6:3Son, do what I command you and save yourself—for through your friend you have come into evil men’s hands. Go without growing slack, and press the friend for whom you acted as guarantor.

6:4Give no sleep to your eyes, nor doze with your eyelids,

6:5so that you may save yourself like a gazelle from snares and like a bird from a trap.

6:6Go to the ant, sluggard; see its ways and emulate them, and become wiser than it.

6:7For although it has no cultivated field, and has no one forcing it, and is under no master,

6:8it prepares its food in summer and makes abundant provision at harvest.

6:8aOr go to the bee and learn how industrious she is, and how nobly she performs her work,

6:8bwhose labors kings and private people use for health; she is desired by everyone and held in esteem.

6:8cAlthough weak in bodily strength, she was advanced because she honored wisdom.

6:9How long will you remain lying down, sluggard? When will you rise from sleep?

6:10You sleep a little, sit a little, doze a little, and clasp your chest with your hands a little;

6:11then poverty arrives beside you like an evil traveler, and want like a good runner.

6:11aBut if you are diligent, your harvest will come like a spring, and want will desert you like a bad runner.

6:12A foolish and lawless man travels ways that are not good;

6:13the same man signals with his eye and gives signs with his foot, and instructs by gestures of his fingers;

6:14with a twisted heart he devises evils at every time. Such a person stirs up disturbances for a city.

6:15Therefore his destruction comes suddenly: a cutting down and an incurable crushing,

6:16because he rejoices in everything the Lord hates, and is crushed because of his soul’s uncleanness:

6:17an insolent person’s eye, an unjust tongue, hands shedding a righteous person’s blood,

6:18and a heart devising evil thoughts, and feet hastening to do harm;

6:19an unjust witness kindles falsehoods and sends disputes among brothers.

6:20Son, guard your father’s laws, and do not reject your mother’s ordinances.

6:21Fasten them always upon your soul, and collar them around your neck.

6:22Whenever you walk, take her along and let her be with you; when you sleep, let her guard you, so that when you rise she may converse with you,

6:23because the commandment of law is a lamp and a light, and correction and discipline are life’s way,

6:24to preserve you from a married woman and from an alien tongue’s slander.

6:25Do not let desire for beauty overcome you; do not be hunted by your own eyes, nor seized by her eyelids.

6:26For a prostitute’s price is as much as one loaf, but a woman hunts men’s precious lives.

6:27Will someone bind fire in his bosom without burning his garments?

6:28Or will someone walk on fiery coals without burning his feet?

6:29So is the one who goes in to a married woman: he will not be acquitted, nor will anyone touching her.

6:30It is no wonder if someone is caught stealing, for he steals to fill his appetite when hungry.

6:31But if caught, he will repay sevenfold, and by giving all his possessions he will rescue himself.

6:32But the adulterer, through lack of sense, brings destruction upon his own life;

6:33he bears pains and dishonors, and his reproach will never be wiped away.

6:34For her husband’s anger is full of jealousy; he will not spare on the day of judgment,

6:35he will not trade his enmity for any ransom, nor be reconciled by many gifts.

Proverbs 7

Read this chapter on staging

7:1Son, guard my words, and hide my commandments with yourself.

7:1aSon, honor the Lord and you will be strong; apart from Him, fear no other.

7:2Guard my commandments and you will live, and my words like the pupils of eyes.

7:3Put them around your fingers; inscribe them on the broad surface of your heart.

7:4Say that wisdom is your sister, and acquire understanding as your familiar companion,

7:5so that she may keep you from an alien and wicked woman, if she catches you with pleasing words.

7:6For she leans out from a window of her house toward the squares,

7:7and whenever she sees among the foolish children a young man lacking sense,

7:8passing by a corner in the passages of her houses

7:9and talking in evening darkness, when night is still and murky,

7:10the woman meets him, with a prostitute’s appearance, making young men’s hearts fly away.

7:11She is fluttering with excitement and dissolute; her feet do not rest in the house.

7:12For at one time she roams outside, and at another she lies in wait in the squares, at every corner.

7:13Then she took hold of him and kissed him; with a shameless face she addressed him:

7:14“I have a peace sacrifice; today I am paying my vows.

7:15For this reason I came out to meet you; longing for your face, I have found you.

7:16I have stretched my bed with bands and spread it with double-sided rugs from Egypt.

7:17I have sprinkled my bed with saffron and my house with cinnamon.

7:18Come, let us enjoy affection until dawn; come here, let us roll in love.

7:19For my husband is not at home; he has gone on a long journey,

7:20taking a pouch of silver in his hand; after many days he will return to his house.”

Proverbs 26

Read this chapter on staging

26:1Like dew at harvest and rain in summer, so honor does not belong to a fool.

26:2As birds and sparrows fly, so an empty curse will come upon no one.

26:3As a whip for a horse and a goad for a donkey, so a rod for a lawless nation.

26:4Do not answer a fool in relation to his foolishness, so that you do not become like him;

26:5but answer a fool according to his foolishness, so that he does not appear wise to himself.

26:6One who sends a word through a foolish messenger will drink reproach from his own feet.

26:7Remove walking from legs, and a proverb from the mouth of fools.

26:8One who binds a stone in a sling is like one who gives glory to a fool.

26:9Thorns grow in the hand of the drunkard, but slavery is in the hand of fools.

26:10All the flesh of fools is greatly storm-tossed, for their distraction is shattered.

26:11Like a dog that comes upon its own vomit and becomes hateful is a fool who has returned in his own wickedness to his own sin.

26:11aThere is shame that brings on sin, and there is shame that is glory and favor.

26:12I saw a man who thought himself wise, yet a fool had more hope than he.

26:13A lazy person sent on a journey says, “A lion is in the roads!”

26:14As a door turns on its hinge, so a lazy person on his bed.

26:15A lazy person, having hidden his hand in his lap, will not be able to bring it to his mouth.

26:16The lazy person appears wiser to himself than one who brings back a message in full.

26:17As one who grasps a dog’s tail, so is one who presides over another’s dispute.

26:18As healers put forward words to people, and the first to meet the word will be tripped up,

26:19so are all who ambush their own friends, and when detected say, “I did it in play.”

26:20With much wood a fire flourishes, but where there is no double-minded person, battle is quiet.

26:21A hearth for coals and wood for fire, and an abusive man for the turmoil of battle.

26:22The words of tricksters are soft, but they strike into the store-chambers of the inward parts.

26:23Silver given with deceit must be regarded like a potsherd; smooth lips cover a painful heart.

26:24A weeping enemy agrees to everything with his lips, but in his heart devises deceits;

26:25if the enemy entreats you with a loud voice, do not be persuaded, for seven evils are in his life.

26:26One who hides enmity establishes deceit, but one well known in councils discloses his own sins.

26:27One who digs a pit for his neighbor will fall into it, and one who rolls a stone rolls it upon himself.

26:28A false tongue hates truth, but an unrestrained mouth makes disorders.

Proverbs 27

Read this chapter on staging

27:1Do not boast of things for tomorrow, for you do not know what the coming day will bear.

27:2Let your neighbor praise you, and not your mouth, another, and not your lips.

27:3A stone is heavy and sand hard to carry, but a fool’s anger is heavier than both.

27:4Wrath is merciless and anger sharp, but jealousy bears with no one.

27:5Open rebukes are better than concealed friendship.

27:6 A friend’s wounds are more trustworthy than an enemy’s willing kisses.

27:7A life in fullness mocks honeycombs, but to a needy life even bitter things seem sweet.

27:8As when a bird flies down from its own nest, so a person is enslaved when estranged from his own places.

27:9The heart delights in perfumes and wines and incense, but the life is torn apart by misfortunes.

27:10Do not abandon your friend or an ancestral friend, and do not enter your brother’s house when misfortune strikes you; a nearby friend is better than a brother dwelling far away.

27:11Become wise, son, so that my heart may rejoice, and turn reproachful words away from yourself.

27:12A resourceful person hid when evils approached, but fools who went on will pay a penalty.

27:13Take away his garment, for he has transgressed; one who ruins another’s property is insolent.

27:14One who blesses a friend early with a loud voice will seem no different from one cursing.

27:15Drops drive a man from his house on a wintry day, and likewise an abusive wife from his own house.

27:16The north wind is a harsh wind, but by name it is called favorable.

27:17Iron sharpens iron, but a man provokes the face of a companion.

27:18One who plants a fig tree will eat its fruits, and one who guards his own master will be honored.

27:19As faces are not like faces, so neither are human hearts.

27:20Hades and destruction are not filled, and likewise human eyes are insatiable.

27:20aOne who fixes an eye is an abomination to the Lord, and the uneducated are unrestrained in tongue.

27:21A test for silver and gold is burning, but a man is tested through the mouth of those who praise him.

27:21aA lawless person’s heart searches out evils, but an upright heart searches out knowledge.

27:22If you whip a fool in the midst of a council, dishonoring him, you will certainly not strip away his foolishness.

27:23Know thoroughly the lives of your flock, and set your heart upon your herds;

27:24for a man’s might and strength do not last for ever, nor does he hand them on from generation to generation.

27:25Care for the green growth in the plain, and you will cut grass, and gather mountain fodder,

27:26so that you may have sheep for clothing; value a plain, so that you may have lambs.

27:27Son, from me you have strong sayings for your life, and for the life of your attendants.

Saints English wording notes

Proverbs 27:17 · provoke

Sense. Sharpens or stirs another person's response in the iron comparison.

Keep the verbal play with sharpening while explaining provoke as stir or incite. Its evaluative force needs context; it must not be reduced automatically to either friendly improvement or malicious anger.

Historical comparison. KJV, Brenton, and DRC repeat sharpens in the second clause. Saints distinguishes the related Greek verb and retains face. Both approaches need the metaphor explained.

Proverbs 28

Read this chapter on staging

28:1An impious person flees when no one pursues, but a righteous person is confident like a lion.

28:2Through the sins of the impious, disputes arise, but a prudent man will extinguish them.

28:3A man vigorous in impieties extorts the poor. Just like a violent and useless rain,

28:4so those who abandon the law praise impiety, but those who love the law surround themselves with a wall.

28:5Evil men will not understand judgment, but those who seek the Lord will understand in everything.

28:6Better a poor person walking in truth than a lying rich person.

28:7An understanding son keeps the law, but one who tends dissipation dishonors a father.

28:8One who increases his wealth with interest and gains gathers it for one who shows mercy to the poor.

28:9One who turns his ear aside so as not to hear the law has himself also abhorred his own prayer.

28:10One who leads the upright astray on an evil way will himself fall into destruction; but the lawless will pass by good things and will not enter into them.

28:11A rich man is wise in his own estimation, but an intelligent poor person will judge him.

28:12Through the help of the righteous much glory comes about, but in the places of the impious people are captured.

28:13One who covers his own impiety will not prosper, but one who explains reproofs will be loved.

28:14Happy is a man who shrinks from everything out of reverence, but one hard of heart will fall into evils.

28:15One who tyrannizes over a needy nation while himself poor is a hungry lion and a thirsty wolf.

28:16A king lacking revenues is a great extortioner, but one who hates injustice will live a long time.

28:17One who acts as guarantor for a man charged with murder will be a fugitive and will not be secure.

28:17aDiscipline a son, and he will love you and give adornment to your life; you must not heed a lawless nation.

28:18One who walks righteously has been helped, but one who walks crooked ways will become entangled.

28:19One who works his own land will be filled with bread, but one who pursues leisure will be filled with poverty.

28:20A trustworthy man will be greatly blessed, but the evil person will not be unpunished.

28:21One who has no respect for the faces of the righteous is not good; such a person will sell a man for a morsel of bread.

28:22A grudging man hastens to become rich and does not know that a merciful person will rule over him.

28:23One who reproves a person’s ways will have more favor than one who flatters with the tongue.

28:24One who casts away a father or mother and thinks he is not sinning is a partner of an impious man.

28:25An insatiable man judges rashly, but one who trusts in the Lord will be cared for.

28:26One who trusts in a rash heart is a fool; but one who walks in wisdom will be saved.

28:27One who gives to the poor will not be in need; but one who turns away his eye will be in great difficulty.

28:28In the places of the impious the righteous groan, but at their destruction the righteous will multiply.

Proverbs 29

Read this chapter on staging

29:1Better a man who reproves than a stiff-necked man; for when the latter suddenly burns, there is no healing.

29:2When the righteous are praised, peoples will rejoice; but when the impious rule, men groan.

29:3When a man loves wisdom, his father rejoices; but one who tends prostitutes will destroy wealth.

29:4A righteous king raises up a country, but a lawless man tears it down.

29:5One who prepares a net before his own friend’s face wraps it around his own feet.

29:6For a sinning man there is a great snare, but a righteous person will be in joy and gladness.

29:7A righteous person knows how to judge for the needy, but the impious person will not understand knowledge, and a poor person has no discerning mind.

29:8Pestilent men set a city ablaze, but wise people turned away wrath.

29:9A wise man judges nations, but a worthless man who becomes angry is laughed at and does not shrink back.

29:10Men sharing in bloodshed will hate a holy person, but the upright will seek his life.

29:11A fool brings out all his anger, but a wise person stores it up portion by portion.

29:12When a king listens to an unjust word, all those under him are lawless.

29:13When lender and debtor come together, the Lord inspects both.

29:14When a king judges the poor in truth, his throne will be established as a testimony.

29:15Blows and reproofs give wisdom, but a wandering child shames his parents.

29:16When the impious are many, sins become many, but the righteous become terrified when those people fall.

29:17Discipline your son, and he will give you rest and give adornment to your life.

29:18There will certainly be no interpreter for a lawless nation, but one who keeps the law is blessed.

29:19A harsh household servant will not be disciplined by words; for even if he understands, he will not obey.

29:20If you see a man swift in words, know that a fool has more hope than he.

29:21One who lives extravagantly from childhood will become a household servant, and in the end he will grieve over himself.

29:22A wrathful man digs up strife, and an angry man dug out sins.

29:23Insolence brings a man low, but the Lord supports the humble-minded with glory.

29:24One who shares with a thief hates his own life; but if, when an oath is set before them, those who heard do not report,

29:25having feared and felt shame before people, they were tripped up; but one who trusts in the Lord will rejoice. Impiety gives a man a fall; but one who trusts in the Master will be saved.

29:26Many court the faces of leaders, but what is just for a man comes from the Lord.

29:27An unjust man is an abomination to the righteous, but a straightening way is an abomination to a lawless person.

Proverbs 31:10–31

Read this chapter on staging

31:10A courageous woman—who will find her? Such a woman is more precious than costly stones.

31:11Her husband’s heart is confident in her; such a woman will not lack fine spoils.

31:12For she works good things for her husband throughout her life.

31:13Spinning wool and flax, she made useful work with her hands.

31:14She became like a ship trading from afar, and she gathers her livelihood.

31:15And she rises in the night and gave food to the household and tasks to the maidservants.

31:16After inspecting a farm she bought it, and from the fruits of her hands she planted an estate.

31:17Having girded her waist strongly, she braced her arms for work.

31:18She tasted that working is good, and her lamp is not extinguished throughout the night.

31:19She stretches her forearms toward profitable things, and braces her hands on a spindle.

31:20She opened her hands to a needy person and stretched out her wrist to a poor person.

31:21Her husband is not anxious about those at home when he stays away somewhere, for all those with her are clothed.

31:22She made double cloaks for her husband, and garments of fine linen and purple for herself.

31:23Her husband becomes distinguished in the gates when he sits in council with the elder inhabitants of the land.

31:24She made linen cloths and sold them, and girdles for the Canaanites.

31:25She opened her mouth attentively and lawfully and prescribed order for her tongue.

31:26She clothed herself in strength and dignity and rejoiced in the last days.

31:27The occupations of her houses are secure, and she did not eat idle food.

31:28She opens her mouth wisely and according to law, and her mercy raised up her children, and they became wealthy, and her husband praised her:

31:29“Many daughters acquired wealth, many did mighty things, but you stand above and have surpassed them all.”

31:30A woman’s pleasing qualities are false and beauty is vain; for an understanding woman is blessed, and let her praise the fear of the Lord.

31:31Give her from the fruits of her hands, and let her husband be praised in the gates.

Proverbs 31:10 · courageous
