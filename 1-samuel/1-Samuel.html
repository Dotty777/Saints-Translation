1 Samuel 1

Read this chapter on staging

Elkanah and His Two Wives

1:1There was a man from Armathaim Sipha in the hill country of Ephraim. His name was Elkanah, son of Jerahmeel, son of Elihu, son of Thoke, in Nasib Ephraim.

1:2He had two wives. One was named Hannah, and the second was named Peninnah. Peninnah had children, but Hannah had no child.

1:3The man went up year by year from his city, from Armathaim, to bow down and sacrifice to the Lord God Sabaoth at Shiloh. Eli and his two sons, Hophni and Phinehas, were there as priests of the Lord.

1:4When the day came and Elkanah sacrificed, he gave portions to Peninnah his wife and to her sons and daughters.

1:5To Hannah he gave one portion, because she had no child. Yet Elkanah loved Hannah more than this other woman, and the Lord had closed her womb.

1:6For the Lord had not given her a child, in her affliction and in the despondency of her affliction. She was despondent because of this: the Lord had shut her womb so as not to give her a child.

1:7He would do this year after year when she went up to the Lord’s house, and she would be despondent and weep and not eat.

1:8Elkanah her husband said to her, “Hannah.” She said to him, “Here I am, my lord.” He said to her, “What is the matter, that you are weeping? Why do you not eat? Why does your heart strike you? Am I not better to you than ten children?”

The Vow of Hannah at Shiloh

1:9Hannah arose after they had eaten at Shiloh and stood before the Lord. Eli the priest was sitting on the seat by the doorposts of the Lord’s sanctuary.

1:10She was in anguish of soul and prayed to the Lord, weeping abundantly.

1:11She vowed a vow to the Lord, saying, “Adonai, Lord, Eloai Sabaoth, if Thou wilt indeed look upon Thy handmaid’s humiliation and remember me, and give Thy handmaid male offspring, then I will give him before Thee as a gift until the day of his death. He shall drink neither wine nor intoxicating drink, and no iron shall come upon his head.”

1:12As she continued praying before the Lord, Eli the priest watched her mouth.

1:13She was speaking in her heart, and her lips moved, but her voice was not heard. Eli considered her drunk.

1:14Eli’s servant said to her, “How long will you be drunk? Put away your wine and leave the Lord’s presence.”

1:15Hannah answered and said, “No, my lord. I am a woman whose day is hard. I have drunk neither wine nor intoxicating drink, and I am pouring out my soul before the Lord.

1:16Do not regard your handmaid as a pestilent daughter, for I have continued until now out of the abundance of my murmuring.”

1:17Eli answered her, “Go in peace. May the God of Israel grant the whole request you have made of Him.”

1:18She said, “Your handmaid has found favour in your eyes.” The woman went her way, entered her lodging, ate with her husband, and drank. Her face no longer fell.

The Birth of Samuel

1:19They rose early in the morning, bowed down to the Lord, and went on their way. Elkanah entered his house at Armathaim and knew Hannah his wife, and the Lord remembered her,

1:20and she conceived. When the time had come, she bore a son. She called his name Samuel and said, “For I asked him of the Lord God Sabaoth.”

Samuel Lent to the Lord

1:21The man Elkanah went up with all his household to sacrifice at Shiloh the yearly sacrifice, his vow offerings, and all the tithes of his land.

1:22Hannah did not go up with him, for she said to her husband, “Not until the child goes up, when I have weaned him. He will appear before the Lord and remain there for ever.”

1:23Elkanah her husband said to her, “Do what is good in your eyes. Stay until you have weaned him. But may the Lord establish what has come from your mouth.” The woman stayed and nursed her son until she weaned him.

1:24She went up with him to Shiloh, with a three-year-old calf, loaves, an oiphi of fine flour, and a nebel of wine. She entered the Lord’s house at Shiloh, and the child was with them.

1:25They brought him before the Lord, and his father slaughtered the sacrifice that he made year by year to the Lord. He brought the child forward and slaughtered the calf. Hannah, the child’s mother, brought him to Eli

1:26and said, “Please, my lord! As your soul lives, I am the woman who stood before you when praying to the Lord.

1:27For this child I prayed, and the Lord gave me my request that I asked of Him.

1:28I too lend him to the Lord for all the days that he lives, as a loan to the Lord.”

1 Samuel 2

Read this chapter on staging

The Prayer of Hannah

2:1She said, “My heart is established in the Lord;
my horn is exalted in my God.
My mouth is opened wide against enemies;
I rejoice in Thy salvation.

2:2For there is none holy like the Lord, and there is none righteous like our God;
there is none holy besides Thee.

2:3Do not boast, and do not speak loftily;
let no grandiose speech come from your mouth.
For the Lord is a God of knowledge, and a God who prepares His undertakings.

2:4The bow of the mighty has grown weak, but those who were weak have girded themselves with strength.

2:5Those who were full of bread have been diminished, but the hungry have left the land.
For the barren woman has borne seven, while she who had many children has grown weak.

2:6The Lord puts to death and gives life;
He brings down to Hades and brings up.

2:7The Lord makes poor and makes rich;
He humbles and raises high.

2:8He raises the needy from the earth and lifts the poor from the dung heap, to seat them with rulers of peoples and grant them a throne of glory as their inheritance,

2:9granting a prayer to the one who prays, and He has blessed the years of the righteous one;
for a man is not mighty by strength.

2:10The Lord will weaken His opponent;
the Lord is holy.
Let the wise person not boast in his wisdom, nor the mighty person in his might, nor the rich person in his riches.
Rather, let the one who boasts boast in this: understanding and knowing the Lord, and doing judgment and righteousness in the midst of the earth.
The Lord ascended into the heavens and thundered.
He will judge the earth’s extremities, and He gives strength to our kings and will raise high the horn of His anointed.”

The Sons of Eli

2:11They left him there before the Lord and departed to Armathaim, while the child ministered in the Lord’s presence before Eli the priest.

2:12The sons of Eli the priest were pestilent sons who did not know the Lord.

2:13This was the priest’s claimed right from the people, from anyone sacrificing: the priest’s servant would come when the meat was boiling, with a three-pronged meat hook in his hand,

2:14and thrust it into the large cauldron, bronze kettle, or pot. Whatever came up on the meat hook, the priest took for himself. This is what they did to all Israel who came to sacrifice to the Lord at Shiloh.

2:15Before the fat was burned, the priest’s servant would come and say to the man sacrificing, “Give meat to roast for the priest. I will certainly not take from you meat boiled in the cauldron.”

2:16The man sacrificing would say, “Let the fat first be burned, as is proper, and take for yourself from whatever your soul desires.” But he said, “No! You must give it now; otherwise I will take it by force.”

2:17The servants’ sin was very great before the Lord, for they rejected the Lord’s sacrifice.

Samuel Ministers Before the Lord

2:18Samuel ministered before the Lord, a child girded with an ephoud bar.

2:19His mother made him a little double cloak and would bring it up to him year by year, when she went up with her husband to sacrifice the yearly sacrifice.

2:20Eli blessed Elkanah and his wife, saying, “May the Lord repay you with offspring from this woman in return for the loan you made to the Lord.” The man departed to his place,

2:21and the Lord visited Hannah, and she bore three more sons and two daughters. The child Samuel grew up before the Lord.

Eli Rebukes His Sons

2:22Eli was very old, and he heard what his sons were doing to the sons of Israel.

2:23He said to them, “Why do you act according to this report that I hear from the mouth of all the Lord’s people?

2:24No, children! The report I hear is not good. Do not act this way, for the reports I hear are not good, so that the people do not serve God.

2:25If a man indeed sins against a man, people will pray for him to the Lord; but if he sins against the Lord, who will pray for him?” They would not listen to their father’s voice, for the Lord was firmly resolved to destroy them.

2:26The child Samuel continued to grow and was in favour both with the Lord and with people.

The Word Against the House of Eli

2:27A man of God came to Eli and said, “Thus says the Lord: ‘I clearly revealed Myself to your father’s house when they were in the land of Egypt as slaves to Pharaoh’s house,

2:28and I chose your father’s house from all Israel’s tribes to serve Me as priests, ascend My altar, burn incense, and bear an ephoud. I gave your father’s house all the Israelites’ fire offerings for food.

2:29Why did you look upon My incense and My sacrifice with a shameless eye, and honour your sons more than Me, so that they bless themselves with the firstfruits of every sacrifice of Israel before Me?

2:30Therefore thus said the Lord, the God of Israel: I said that your house and your father’s house would pass before Me for ever. But now the Lord says: By no means! I will honour only those who honour Me, and the one who despises Me will be dishonoured.

2:31Behold, days are coming when I will destroy your offspring and the offspring of your father’s house,

2:32and no old man of yours will be in My house all the days.

2:33Yet I will not destroy every man of yours from My altar, so that his eyes fail and his soul ebbs away. Everyone remaining in your house will fall by the sword of men.

2:34This will be the sign for you that will come upon these two sons of yours, Hophni and Phinehas: both will die in one day.

2:35I will raise up for Myself a faithful priest who will do everything in My heart and in My soul. I will build him a faithful house, and he will pass before My anointed all the days.

2:36The one remaining in your house will come to bow down to him for a silver obol, saying, “Place me in one of your priestly offices, so I may eat bread.”’”

1 Samuel 3

Read this chapter on staging

The Lord Calls Samuel

3:1The child Samuel ministered to the Lord before Eli the priest. The Lord’s word was precious in those days; there was no distinct vision.

3:2At that time Eli was sleeping in his place. His eyes had begun to grow heavy, and he could not see,

3:3and it was before God’s lamp was put in order. Samuel was sleeping in the sanctuary where God’s ark was,

3:4and the Lord called, “Samuel, Samuel!” He said, “Here I am.”

3:5He ran to Eli and said, “Here I am, for you have called me.” He said, “I have not called you. Return and sleep.” He went back and slept.

3:6The Lord called again, “Samuel, Samuel!” He went to Eli a second time and said, “Here I am, for you have called me.” He said, “I have not called you. Return and sleep.”

3:7This was before Samuel knew God and before the Lord’s word had been revealed to him.

3:8The Lord called Samuel a third time. He arose, went to Eli, and said, “Here I am, for you have called me.” Eli realized that the Lord had called the child.

3:9He said, “Return and sleep, child. If He calls you, say, ‘Speak, Lord, for Thy servant hears.’” Samuel went and lay down in his place.

3:10The Lord came and stood there. He called him as before, and Samuel said, “Speak, for Thy servant hears.”

The Word Against Eli's House

3:11The Lord said to Samuel, “Behold, I am carrying out My words in Israel, so that both ears of everyone who hears them will resound.

3:12On that day I will raise against Eli everything I have spoken concerning his house. I will begin and bring it to completion.

3:13I have told him that I am taking vengeance on his house for ever for his sons’ wrongdoings, because his sons were reviling God, and even so he did not admonish them.

3:14I swore concerning Eli’s house: the wrongdoing of Eli’s house shall never be expiated by incense or sacrifices.”

Samuel Tells Eli Everything

3:15Samuel lay until morning, rose early in the morning, and opened the doors of the Lord’s house. Samuel was afraid to report the vision to Eli.

3:16Eli said to Samuel, “Samuel, child.” He said, “Here I am.”

3:17He said, “What was the word spoken to you? Do not hide it from me. May God do these things to you, and add these things, if you hide from me a word of all the words spoken to you in your ears.”

3:18Samuel reported all the words and hid nothing from him. Eli said, “He is the Lord. He will do what is good before Him.”

Samuel Established as Prophet

3:19Samuel grew, and the Lord was with him, and none of his words fell to the ground.

3:20All Israel, from Dan to Beersheba, knew that Samuel was faithful as a prophet to the Lord.

3:21The Lord continued to make Himself known at Shiloh, for the Lord revealed Himself to Samuel. Samuel was recognized as a prophet of the Lord for all Israel, from the ends of the land to the ends. Eli was very old, and his sons continued on their way, and their way was evil before the Lord.

1 Samuel 4

Read this chapter on staging

Israel Defeated by the Foreigners

4:1In those days the foreigners gathered for war against Israel. Israel went out to meet them in battle and camped beside Ebenezer, and the foreigners camped at Aphek.

4:2The foreigners drew up for war against Israel. The battle turned, and the men of Israel stumbled before the foreigners. Four thousand men were struck down in the battle line in the field.

The Ark Brought into the Camp

4:3The people came into the camp, and Israel’s elders said, “Why has the Lord made us stumble today before the foreigners? Let us take our God’s ark from Shiloh. Let it go out among us, and it will save us from our enemies’ hand.”

4:4The people sent to Shiloh and carried from there the ark of the Lord, who is seated upon the cherubim. Both Eli’s sons, Hophni and Phinehas, were with the ark.

4:5When the Lord’s ark came into the camp, all Israel cried out with a loud voice, and the earth resounded.

4:6The foreigners heard the cry, and the foreigners said, “What is this great cry in the Hebrews’ camp?” They learned that the Lord’s ark had come into the camp.

4:7The foreigners were afraid and said, “These gods have come to them in the camp. Woe to us! Rescue us today, Lord, for nothing like this has happened before.

4:8Woe to us! Who will rescue us from the hand of these powerful gods? These are the gods who struck Egypt with every plague and in the wilderness.

4:9Be strong and be men, foreigners, so that you do not serve the Hebrews as they served us. Be men and fight them!”

4:10They fought them, and the men of Israel stumbled. Each fled to his dwelling. The slaughter was very great, and thirty thousand troops of Israel fell.

4:11God’s ark was captured, and both Eli’s sons, Hophni and Phinehas, died.

The Death of Eli

4:12A Benjaminite man ran from the battle line and came to Shiloh that day, with his garments torn and earth on his head.

4:13He came, and behold, Eli was sitting on the seat by the gate, watching the road, for his heart was distraught over God’s ark. The man entered the city to report, and the city cried out.

4:14Eli heard the sound of the cry and said, “What is this crying sound?” The man hurried in and reported to Eli.

4:15Eli was ninety years old. His eyes were fixed, and he could not see. Eli said to the men standing around him, “What is the sound of this uproar?”

4:16The man hurried up to Eli and said to him, “I am the one who has come from the camp. I fled from the battle line today.” He said, “What happened, child?”

4:17The young man answered and said, “The men of Israel have fled before the foreigners. There has been a great slaughter among the people. Both your sons are dead, and God’s ark has been captured.”

4:18When the man mentioned God’s ark, Eli fell backward from the seat beside the gate. His back was crushed, and he died, for the man was old and heavy. He had judged Israel twenty years.

Woe-barchaboth: The Glory Carried Away

4:19His daughter-in-law, Phinehas’s wife, was pregnant and about to give birth. When she heard the report that God’s ark had been captured and that her father-in-law and her husband were dead, she crouched and gave birth, for her labor pains turned upon her.

4:20At that time she was dying. The women standing beside her said, “Do not fear, for you have borne a son.” She did not answer, and her heart did not understand.

4:21She called the child Woe-barchaboth, because of God’s ark, her father-in-law, and her husband.

4:22They said, “Israel’s glory has been carried away with the capture of the Lord’s ark.”

1 Samuel 5

Read this chapter on staging

The Ark in the House of Dagon

5:1The foreigners took the ark of God and brought it from Ebenezer to Ashdod.

5:2The foreigners took the ark of the Lord, brought it into Dagon’s house, and placed it beside Dagon.

5:3The Ashdodites rose early, entered Dagon’s house, and looked: there was Dagon, fallen on his face before the ark of God. They raised Dagon and restored him to his place. The Lord’s hand became heavy upon the Ashdodites. He tormented them and struck them on their buttocks—Ashdod and its territory.

5:4When they rose early in the morning, there was Dagon, fallen on his face before the ark of the Lord’s covenant. Dagon’s head and both soles of his hands were severed, each upon the front parts of amapheth; both wrists of his hands had fallen at the doorway. Only Dagon’s back remained.

5:5That is why Dagon’s priests and everyone entering Dagon’s house do not step on the step of Dagon’s house in Ashdod to this day: they always step over it.

The Hand of the Lord upon Ashdod

5:6The Lord’s hand became heavy upon Ashdod. He brought affliction upon them, and it broke out upon them into the ships. Mice sprang up in the middle of its territory, and there was great deadly turmoil in the city.

5:7When Ashdod’s men saw that it was so, they said, “The ark of Israel’s God must not stay with us, for His hand is harsh upon us and upon Dagon our god.”

5:8They sent and gathered the foreigners’ satraps to them. They said, “What shall we do with the ark of Israel’s God?” The Gathites said, “Let the ark of God come over to us.” So the ark of God came over to Gath.

5:9After it came over, the Lord’s hand was in the city, with exceedingly great turmoil. He struck the city’s men, small and great, and struck them on their buttocks. The Gathites made seats for themselves.

The Ark Sent to Ashkelon

5:10They sent the ark of God away to Ashkelon. When the ark of God entered Ashkelon, the Ashkelonites cried out, “Why have you sent the ark of Israel’s God around to us to kill us and our people?”

5:11They sent and gathered the foreigners’ satraps. They said, “Send away the ark of Israel’s God. Let it settle in its place, and it will certainly not kill us and our people!” For exceedingly heavy deadly turmoil was throughout the city when the ark of Israel’s God entered there.

5:12Those who were alive and had not died were struck on their buttocks, and the city’s cry went up to heaven.

1 Samuel 6

Read this chapter on staging

The Foreigners Take Counsel

6:1The ark was in the foreigners’ country for seven months, and their land teemed with mice.

6:2The foreigners called for the priests, diviners, and their enchanters, saying, “What shall we do with the ark of the Lord? Tell us in what we should send it to its place.”

6:3They said, “If you send away the ark of the covenant of the Lord, Israel’s God, do not send it empty. You must return to it something for the torment. Then you will be healed, and propitiation will be made for you, lest His hand fail to withdraw from you.”

6:4They said, “What shall we return to it for the torment?” They said, “Five gold seats, matching the number of the foreigners’ satraps, for one affliction is upon you, your rulers, and the people;

6:5and gold mice, in the likeness of your mice that are destroying the land. Give glory to the Lord, so that He may lighten His hand upon you, upon your gods, and upon your land.

6:6Why make your hearts heavy, as Egypt and Pharaoh made their heart heavy? After He mocked them, did they not send them away, and they departed?

The Cart and the Two Cows

6:7Now take and prepare a new cart and two cows that have calved for the first time, without their calves. Yoke the cows to the cart, and take their calves home away from them.

6:8Take the ark and set it on the cart. Return the gold objects to it for the torment, and put them in a deposit, bersechthan, at its side. Send it away and drive it off, and it will go.

6:9Watch: if it goes by the road to its own territory toward Beth-shemesh, He has done this great harm to us. If not, we will know that His hand has not touched us; this happened to us by chance.”

6:10The foreigners did so. They took two cows that had calved for the first time, yoked them to the cart, and kept their calves in the house.

6:11They set the ark on the cart, together with the deposit, ergab, and the gold mice.

6:12The cows went straight along the road toward Beth-shemesh. They followed one path, laboring as they went, and did not turn right or left. The foreigners’ satraps followed it as far as the territory of Beth-shemesh.

The Ark at Beth-shemesh

6:13Those in Beth-shemesh were reaping the wheat harvest in the valley. They lifted their eyes, saw the ark of the Lord, and rejoiced to meet it.

6:14The cart came into Hosea’s field in Beth-shemesh. They set up a large stone there beside it, split the cart’s wood, and offered the cows as a whole burnt offering to the Lord.

6:15The Levites brought up the ark of the Lord and the deposit, ergab, with it, and the gold objects upon it, and set them on the large stone. The men of Beth-shemesh offered whole burnt offerings and sacrifices to the Lord that day.

6:16The foreigners’ five satraps watched and returned to Ashkelon that day.

6:17These are the gold seats that the foreigners returned to the Lord for the torment: one for Ashdod, one for Gaza, one for Ashkelon, one for Gath, and one for Ekron.

6:18The gold mice matched the number of all the foreigners’ cities belonging to the five satraps, from fortified city to the Perizzite’s village and as far as the large stone on which they set the ark of the Lord’s covenant, in the field of Hosea the Beth-shemite.

The Men of Beth-shemesh Struck

6:19The sons of Jeconiah were not pleased among the men of Beth-shemesh because they saw the ark of the Lord. He struck among them seventy men and fifty thousand men. The people mourned because the Lord had struck the people with an exceedingly great blow.

6:20The men from Beth-shemesh said, “Who can pass before this holy Lord? To whom shall the ark of the Lord go up away from us?”

6:21They sent messengers to the inhabitants of Kiriath-jearim, saying, “The foreigners have returned the ark of the Lord. Come down and bring it up to yourselves.”

1 Samuel 7

Read this chapter on staging

The Ark at Kiriath-jearim

7:1The men of Kiriath-jearim came and brought up the ark of the Lord’s covenant. They brought it into Aminadab’s house on the hill and consecrated his son Eleazar to guard the ark of the Lord’s covenant.

7:2From the day the ark was in Kiriath-jearim, the days multiplied until twenty years had passed. All Israel’s house looked after the Lord.

Samuel Gathers Israel at Mizpah

7:3Samuel said to all Israel’s house, “If you are returning to the Lord with all your heart, remove the foreign gods and the groves from among you. Prepare your hearts toward the Lord and serve Him alone, and He will rescue you from the foreigners’ hand.”

14:3Ahijah son of Ahitub, Jochabed’s brother, son of Phinehas, son of Eli, was God’s priest in Shiloh, bearing an ephoud. The people did not know Jonathan had gone.

14:4Within the crossing where Jonathan sought to cross to the foreigners’ station was a rocky promontory on one side and a rocky promontory on the other. One was named Bazes and the other Senna.

14:5One road approached Michmash from the north, and the other road approached Geba from the south.

14:6Jonathan said to the young servant carrying his equipment, “Come, let us cross to these uncircumcised men’s messab. Perhaps the Lord will do something for us, for the Lord is not constrained from saving by many or by few.”

14:7The one carrying his equipment said to him, “Do everything to which your heart inclines. Here I am with you: my heart is as your heart.”

14:8Jonathan said, “We will cross to the men and roll down toward them.

14:9If they say to us, ‘Stay back there until we tell you,’ we will stand on our own and certainly not go up against them.

14:10But if they say to us, ‘Come up to us,’ we will go up, for the Lord has handed them into our hands. This will be our sign.”

14:11The two of them entered the foreigners’ messab. The foreigners said, “Behold, the Hebrews are coming out of their holes where they were hiding.”

14:12The men of the messab called to Jonathan and the one carrying his equipment, “Come up to us, and we will let you know a word.” Jonathan said to the one carrying his equipment, “Come up after me, for the Lord has handed them into Israel’s hands.”

14:13Jonathan climbed up on his hands and feet, with the one carrying his equipment beside him. They looked Jonathan in the face, and he struck them; the one carrying his equipment kept following behind him.

14:14The first blow Jonathan and the one carrying his equipment struck was about twenty men, with darts, stone-throwers, and pebbles of the plain.

Terror in the Camp

14:15There was terror in the camp and in the field. All the people in the messab and the destroyers were terrified, and they were unwilling to act. The earth was astonished, and there was terror from the Lord.

14:16Saul’s lookouts in Benjamin’s Geba saw the camp in turmoil this way and that.

14:17Saul said to the people with him, “Please muster and see who has gone from you.” They mustered, and Jonathan and the one carrying his equipment could not be found.

14:18Saul said to Ahijah, “Bring the ephoud near.” For he was bearing the ephoud before Israel that day.

14:19While Saul spoke to the priest, the sound in the foreigners’ camp kept growing louder. Saul said to the priest, “Withdraw your hands.”

14:20Saul and all the people with him cried out and came to the battle. Each man’s sword was against his neighbour, with exceedingly great confusion.

14:21The slaves who had previously been with the foreigners and had gone up into the camp also turned to join Israel with Saul and Jonathan.

14:22All Israel hiding on Ephraim’s hill heard that the foreigners had fled. They too joined in behind them for battle.

14:23The Lord saved Israel that day. The battle passed beyond Baithon, and all the people with Saul numbered about ten thousand men. The battle spread through the whole city in Ephraim’s hill country.

Saul's Oath and the Honeycomb

14:24Saul acted with great ignorance that day and laid a curse upon the people: “Cursed is the man who eats bread before evening, until I take vengeance on my enemy.” None of the people tasted bread.

14:25All the land was eating a meal. There was an iaar, a wooded bee-place, facing the field.

14:26The people entered the bee-place. There he was, going along and speaking; no one was bringing his hand back to his mouth, for the people feared the Lord’s oath.

14:27Jonathan had not heard when his father put the people under oath. He stretched out the tip of the staff in his hand, dipped it into the honeycomb, and brought his hand back to his mouth. His eyes looked up.

14:28One of the people said, “Your father solemnly put the people under oath, saying, ‘Cursed is the man who eats bread today.’” The people were exhausted.

14:29Jonathan understood and said, “My father has dismissed the land. See how my eyes have seen because I tasted a little of this honey.

14:30If the people had eaten freely today from their enemies’ spoils that they found, the blow against the foreigners would now have been greater.”

The People Eat with the Blood

14:31He struck some of the foreigners at Michmash that day, and the people were exceedingly weary.

14:32The people turned to the spoils. They took flocks, cattle herds, and calves, slaughtered them on the ground, and ate with the blood.

14:33Saul was told, “The people have sinned against the Lord by eating with the blood.” Saul said at Geththem, “Roll a large stone here to me.”

14:34Saul said, “Scatter among the people and tell them to bring here each man his calf and each man his sheep. Let him slaughter it upon this, and you will certainly not sin against the Lord by eating with the blood.” All the people brought what was in their hands and slaughtered there.

14:35Saul built an altar there to the Lord. This was the altar Saul began to build to the Lord.

The Lot Falls on Jonathan

14:36Saul said, “Let us go down after the foreigners by night and plunder them until daybreak. Let us leave none of their men.” They said, “Do whatever is good before you.” The priest said, “Let us draw near here to God.”

14:37Saul inquired of God, “Shall I go down after the foreigners? Wilt Thou hand them into Israel’s hands?” But He did not answer him that day.

14:38Saul said, “Bring here all Israel’s chiefs. Find out and see in whom this sin has occurred today.

14:39As the Lord lives, who saved Israel, if an answer is given against my son Jonathan, he must certainly die.” But none of all the people answered.

14:40He said to all Israel, “You will be in servitude, and my son Jonathan and I will be in servitude.” The people said to Saul, “Do what is good before you.”

14:41Saul said, “Lord, Israel’s God, why hast Thou not answered Thy servant today? If the wrongdoing is in me or my son Jonathan, Lord, Israel’s God, give clear signs. But if Thou sayest it is in Thy people Israel, give holiness.” Jonathan and Saul were selected by lot, and the people went free.

14:42Saul said, “Cast between me and my son Jonathan. Let the one whom the Lord selects by lot die.” The people said to Saul, “This must not be!” Saul prevailed over the people. They cast between him and his son Jonathan, and Jonathan was selected by lot.

14:43Saul said to Jonathan, “Tell me what you have done.” Jonathan told him, “I did taste a little honey with the tip of the staff in my hand. Here I am; I am dying.”

14:44Saul said to him, “May God do so to me and add still more, for you must certainly die today.”

14:45The people said to Saul, “Must the one who worked this great deliverance in Israel be put to death today? As the Lord lives, not one hair of his head shall fall to the ground, for God’s people have worked this day.” The people prayed for Jonathan that day, and he did not die.

14:46Saul went up from pursuing the foreigners, and the foreigners went to their own place.

The Wars and the House of Saul

14:47Saul was allotted a task over Israel and fought all his enemies around him: Moab, Ammon’s sons, Edom’s sons, Baitheor, Zobah’s king, and the foreigners. Wherever he turned, he was delivered.

14:48He acted mightily, struck Amalek, and rescued Israel from the hand of those trampling it.

14:49Saul’s sons were Jonathan, Jessiu, and Melchisa. His two daughters’ names were Merab, the firstborn, and Michal, the second.

14:50His wife’s name was Ahinoam daughter of Ahimaaz. His commander-in-chief’s name was Abner son of Ner, son of Saul’s relative.

14:51Kish was Saul’s father, and Ner, Abner’s father, was Jamin’s son, Abiel’s grandson.

14:52War against the foreigners was fierce throughout Saul’s days. Whenever Saul saw a mighty man or a son of might, he gathered them to himself.

The Command to Strike Amalek

15:1Samuel said to Saul, “The Lord sent me to anoint you king over Israel. Now listen to the Lord’s voice.

15:2Thus says the Lord Sabaoth: ‘Now I will avenge what Amalek did to Israel, how he met him on the way when he came up from Egypt.

15:3Now go and strike Amalek and Jerim and everything he has. Preserve nothing of his. Destroy him and place him and everything he has under the ban. Do not spare him. Kill man and woman, child and nursing infant, calf and sheep, camel and donkey.’”

15:4Saul summoned the people and mustered them at Gilgal: four hundred thousand troops and thirty thousand troops of Judah.

15:5Saul came to Amalek’s cities and lay in ambush in the wadi.

15:6Saul said to the Kenite, “Go away and turn aside from among the Amalekite, lest I add you with him. You showed mercy to Israel’s sons when they came up from Egypt.” The Kenite withdrew from among Amalek.

15:7Saul struck Amalek from Havilah to Shur, facing Egypt.

15:8He captured Agag, Amalek’s king, alive and killed all the people of Jerim by the sword’s edge.

15:9Saul and all the people preserved Agag alive, the good things of the flocks, cattle herds, foods, vineyards, and everything good. He was unwilling to destroy them. But every despised and disregarded thing they destroyed.

The Lord Is Consoled of Making Saul King

15:12Samuel rose early and went to meet Israel in the morning. 15:13Samuel arrived at Saul, and Saul said to him, “Blessed are you by the Lord! I have established everything the Lord spoke.”

15:14Samuel said, “What is this sound of the flock in my ears, and the sound of oxen that I hear?”

15:18The Lord sent you on a mission and said to you, ‘Go and destroy those sinning against Me, Amalek. Fight them until you finish them.’

15:19Why did you not listen to the Lord’s voice, but rush to lay hold of the spoils and do evil before the Lord?”

15:20Saul said to Samuel, “Because I listened to the people’s voice. I went on the mission the Lord sent me on, brought Agag, Amalek’s king, and destroyed Amalek.

15:21The people took flocks and cattle herds from the spoils, the first of what was marked for destruction, to sacrifice before the Lord our God at Gilgal.”

To Listen Is Better Than Sacrifice

15:22Samuel said, “Are whole burnt offerings and sacrifices as desirable to the Lord as listening to the Lord’s voice? Behold, obedience is better than sacrifice, and attentiveness than the fat of rams.

15:23Divination is sin; teraphim bring sorrow and troubles. Because you treated the Lord’s word as nothing, the Lord will treat you as nothing, so that you will not be king over Israel.”

Saul Seizes the Edge of Samuel's Cloak

15:24Saul said to Samuel, “I have sinned. I transgressed the Lord’s word and your word, for I feared the people and listened to their voice.

15:25Now please take away my sin and return with me so that I may bow down to the Lord your God.”

15:26Samuel said to Saul, “I will not return with you, for you treated the Lord’s word as nothing, and the Lord will treat you as nothing, so that you will not be king over Israel.”

15:27Samuel turned his face to leave. Saul seized the edge of his double cloak and tore it.

15:28Samuel said to him, “The Lord has torn Israel’s kingdom from your hand today and will give it to your neighbour, who is better than you.

15:29Israel will be divided in two. He will not turn back or change His mind, for He is not like a human being who changes his mind.”

15:30Saul said, “I have sinned. But please honour me before Israel’s elders and before my people. Return with me so that I may bow down to the Lord your God.”

15:31Samuel returned after Saul, and he bowed down to the Lord.

Agag Put to Death at Gilgal

15:32Samuel said, “Bring Agag, Amalek’s king, to me.” Agag approached him trembling. Agag said, “Is death so bitter?”

15:33Samuel said to Agag, “As your sword has made women childless, so your mother shall be made childless among women.” Samuel slaughtered Agag before the Lord at Gilgal.

15:34Samuel went to Armathaim, and Saul went up to his house in Gibeah.

15:35Samuel did not see Saul again until the day of his death, for Samuel mourned over Saul. The Lord regretted that He had made Saul king over Israel.

Samuel Sent to Jesse of Bethlehem

16:1The Lord said to Samuel, “How long will you mourn over Saul, when I have treated him as nothing so that he should not reign over Israel? Fill your horn with oil and come. I will send you to Jesse, to Bethlehem, for I have seen one among his sons to reign for Me.”

David Anointed

16:19Saul sent messengers to Jesse, saying, “Send me your son David, who is with your flock.”

16:20Jesse took a gomor of loaves, a skin of wine, and one young goat, and sent them by the hand of his son David to Saul.

16:21David came to Saul and stood before him. He loved him greatly, and he became the one carrying his equipment.

16:22Saul sent to Jesse, saying, “Please let David stand before me, for he has found favour in my eyes.”

16:23Whenever an evil spirit came upon Saul, David took the kinyra and played with his hand. Saul was refreshed, and it was well with him, and the evil spirit departed from him.

The Foreigners Draw Up for War

17:1The foreigners gathered their camps for war and assembled at Soccoth in Judea. They camped between Soccoth and Azekah at Epher-mem.

17:2Saul and Israel’s men gathered and camped in the valley. They drew up for war opposite the foreigners.

17:3The foreigners stood on the mountain on one side, and Israel stood on the mountain on the other, with the ravine between them.

Goliath Defies the Battle Line

17:4A mighty man came out from the foreigners’ battle line, named Goliath, from Gath. His height was four cubits and a span.

17:5A helmet was on his head, and he wore a coat of chain armour. His armour’s weight was five thousand shekels of bronze and iron.

17:6Bronze greaves were on his legs, and a bronze shield was between his shoulders.

17:7His spear’s shaft was like a weavers’ beam, and its iron head weighed six hundred shekels. The one carrying his weapons walked before him.

17:8He stood and shouted to Israel’s battle line, “Why have you come out to draw up for war opposite us? Am I not a foreigner, and are you not Saul’s Hebrews? Choose a man for yourselves and let him come down to me.

17:9If he can fight me and strike me down, we will become your slaves. But if I prevail and strike him down, you will become our slaves and serve us.”

17:10The foreigner said, “Behold, I have defied Israel’s battle line today, on this day. Give me a man, and let the two of us fight in single combat.”

17:11Saul and all Israel heard these words of the foreigner and were terrified and exceedingly afraid.

David Offers to Fight

17:32David said to Saul, “Let my lord’s heart not collapse because of him. Your servant will go and fight this foreigner.”

17:33Saul said to David, “You certainly cannot go against the foreigner to fight him. You are a boy, and he has been a warrior from his youth.”

17:34David said to Saul, “Your servant tended his father’s flock. Whenever a lion or a bear came and took a sheep from the herd,

17:35I went after it, struck it, and pulled the sheep from its mouth. If it rose against me, I seized its throat, struck it, and killed it.

17:36Your servant struck both bear and lion. The uncircumcised foreigner will be like one of them. Shall I not go and strike him and remove disgrace from Israel today? For who is this uncircumcised man who has defied the battle line of the living God?

17:37The Lord, who rescued me from the lion’s paw and the bear’s paw, will rescue me from this uncircumcised foreigner’s hand.” Saul said to David, “Go, and the Lord will be with you.”

David Refuses Saul's Armor

17:38Saul clothed David in a cloak and set a bronze helmet around his head.

17:39He strapped his sword on David over his cloak. David became weary after walking once or twice. David said to Saul, “I certainly cannot walk in these, for I am not practiced.” They took them off him.

17:40He took his staff in his hand, chose five smooth stones from the wadi, and put them in the shepherd’s little vessel he had for collecting. His sling was in his hand, and he approached the foreign man.

17:42Goliath saw David and dishonoured him, for he was a boy, ruddy, with beautiful eyes.

17:43The foreigner said to David, “Am I like a dog, that you come against me with a staff and stones?” David said, “No, you are worse than a dog.” The foreigner cursed David by his gods.

17:44The foreigner said to David, “Come to me, and I will give your flesh to the birds of heaven and the animals of the land.”

I Come to You in the Name of the Lord

17:45David said to the foreigner, “You come to me with sword, spear, and shield. I come to you in the name of the Lord Sabaoth, God of Israel’s battle line, which you have defied today.

17:46This day the Lord will shut you into my hand. I will kill you and remove your head from you. This day I will give your limbs and the limbs of the foreigners’ camp to the birds of heaven and the beasts of the earth, and all the earth will know that there is a God in Israel.

17:47All this assembly will know that the Lord does not save by sword and spear. The battle belongs to the Lord, and the Lord will hand you all into our hands.”

17:48The foreigner rose and went to meet David.

17:49David reached his hand into the little vessel, took one stone from it, slung it, and struck the foreigner on his forehead. The stone passed through the helmet into his forehead, and he fell face down to the ground.

The Foreigners Flee

17:51David ran and stood over him, took his sword, killed him, and removed his head. When the foreigners saw that their mighty man was dead, they fled.

17:52Israel’s and Judah’s men rose with a shout and pursued them as far as Gath’s entrance and Ashkelon’s gate. Wounded foreigners fell along the road of the gates, as far as Gath and Ekron.

17:53Israel’s men turned back from following the foreigners and trampled their camps.

17:54David took the foreigner’s head and brought it to Jerusalem. He placed his equipment in his tent.
