2 CHRONICLES 1

1:1 Solomon son of David grew strong over his kingdom. The Lord his God was with him and made him exceedingly great.
1:2 Solomon spoke to all Israel: the commanders of thousands and hundreds, the judges, and all the rulers before Israel, the ancestral rulers.
1:3 Solomon and the whole assembly with him went to the high place in Gabaon, where God’s Tent of Testimony was, which Moses, the Lord’s servant, had made in the wilderness.
1:4 But David had brought God’s ark up from the city of Kariathiarim, because he had prepared a tent for it in Jerusalem.
1:5 The bronze altar that Beseleel son of Ouriou son of Or had made was there before the Lord’s tent, and Solomon and the assembly sought it out.
1:6 Solomon offered there upon the bronze altar before the Lord, the one in the tent; he offered a thousandfold whole burnt offering upon it.
1:7 That night God appeared to Solomon and said to him, “Ask what I should give you.”
1:8 Solomon said to God, “Thou didst show great mercy to David my father, and Thou didst make me king in his place.
1:9 Now, Lord God, let Thy name be confirmed upon David my father, for Thou hast made me king over a people as numerous as the dust of the earth.
1:10 Now give me wisdom and understanding, and I shall go out before this people and come in; for who can judge this great people of Thine?”
1:11 God said to Solomon, “Because this came into your heart, and you did not ask for wealth of possessions, glory, or the life of opponents, and did not ask for many days, but did ask for wisdom and understanding to judge My people over whom I made you king,
1:12 I give you wisdom and understanding. I will also give you wealth, possessions, and glory, so that none like you has existed among the kings before you, nor will it be so after you.”
1:13 Solomon came from the bama in Gabaon to Jerusalem, from before the Tent of Testimony, and he reigned over Israel.
1:14 Solomon gathered chariots and horsemen. He had a thousand four hundred chariots and twelve thousand horsemen. He left them in the chariot cities, and the people were with the king in Jerusalem.
1:15 The king made gold and silver in Jerusalem like stones, and cedars in Judea as abundant as the sycamores in the plain.
1:16 Solomon’s horses came out of Egypt, and there was the price of the king’s merchants; they bought them to trade,
1:17 and they went up and exported from Egypt one chariot for six hundred in silver and a horse for a hundred fifty. Likewise they brought them through their hands for all the kings of the Chettites and the kings of Syria.
1:18 Solomon resolved to build a house for the Lord’s name and a house for his kingdom.

2 CHRONICLES 2

2:1 Solomon gathered seventy thousand burden-bearing men and eighty thousand quarrymen in the mountain, with three thousand six hundred overseers over them.
2:2 Solomon sent to Chiram king of Tyre, saying, “As you dealt with my father David and sent him cedars to build himself a house to live in,
2:3 behold, I his son am building a house for the name of the Lord my God, to consecrate it to Him, to burn incense before Him, with continual presentation and the continual offering of whole burnt offerings morning and evening, on sabbaths, new moons, and the feasts of the Lord our God. This is an everlasting obligation upon Israel.
2:4 The house I am building is great, for our God is greater than all the gods.
2:5 Who will be able to build Him a house, since heaven and the heaven of heaven cannot bear His glory? Who am I to build Him a house, except to burn incense before Him?
2:6 Now send me a wise and knowledgeable man to work in gold, silver, bronze, and iron, in purple, scarlet, and hyacinth blue, skilled at engraving, with the skilled men with me in Judah and Jerusalem whom David my father prepared.
2:7 Send me cedar, juniper, and pine timbers from Lebanon, for I know your slaves know how to cut timber from Lebanon. Behold, your servants will be with my servants
2:8 and will go to prepare abundant timber for me, for the house I am building is great and glorious.
2:9 Behold, I give the workers cutting timber grain for food, as gifts to your servants: twenty thousand kors, and twenty thousand kors of barley, twenty thousand measures of wine, and twenty thousand measures of oil.”
2:10 Chiram king of Tyre replied in writing and sent it to Solomon: “Because the Lord loves His people, He has appointed you king over them.”
2:11 Chiram said, “Blessed is the Lord, the God of Israel, who made heaven and earth, who gave King David a wise son, skilled in understanding and knowledge, who will build a house for the Lord and a house for his kingdom.
2:12 Now I have sent you a wise man, possessing understanding: Chiram, my father,
2:13 his mother is from Dan’s daughters, and his father is a Tyrian man. He knows how to work in gold, silver, bronze, iron, stone, and timber; to weave in purple, hyacinth blue, fine linen, and scarlet; to engrave engravings and devise every design, whatever you give him, with your skilled men and the skilled men of David my lord, your father.
2:14 Now let my lord send his servants the grain, barley, oil, and wine of which he spoke.
2:15 We will cut timber from Lebanon according to all your need and bring it in rafts to Joppa’s sea. You shall bring it to Jerusalem.”
2:16 Solomon gathered all the resident foreign men in Israel’s land after the numbering by which David his father had numbered them. They were found to be a hundred fifty-three thousand six hundred.
2:17 He assigned seventy thousand of them as burden bearers, eighty thousand as quarrymen, and three thousand six hundred as taskmasters over the people.

2 CHRONICLES 3

3:1 Solomon began building the Lord’s house in Jerusalem on Mount Amoria, where the Lord appeared to David his father, at the place David had prepared, at the threshing floor of Orna the Jebusite.
3:2 He began building in the second month, in the fourth year of his reign.
3:3 These were the measurements when Solomon began building God’s house: the length in cubits, by the first measurement, was sixty cubits, and the width twenty cubits.
3:4 The ailam before the house extended twenty cubits across the house’s width, and its height was a hundred twenty cubits. He gilded it inside with pure gold.
3:5 He paneled the great house with cedar wood, gilded it with pure gold, and carved palm trees and hanging ornaments upon it.
3:6 He adorned the house with precious stones for glory, and with gold from Pharouaim,
3:7 and he gilded the house, the walls, gateways, ceilings, and doorways with gold, and carved cherubim upon the walls.
3:8 He made the House of the Holy of Holies. Its length across the width was twenty cubits, and its width twenty cubits. He gilded it with pure gold for cherubim, amounting to six hundred talents.
3:9 As for the weight of the nails, the weight of one was fifty shekels of gold. He gilded the upper room with gold.
3:10 He made two cherubim in the House of the Holy of Holies, work made from wood, and he gilded them with gold.
3:11 The cherubim’s wings extended twenty cubits in length. One wing, five cubits, touched the house’s wall, and the other wing, five cubits, touched the other cherub’s wing.
3:12 One wing of the other cherub, five cubits, touched the house’s wall, and the other wing, five cubits, touched the wing of the other cherub.
3:13 The cherubim’s wings were spread twenty cubits. They stood upon their feet, with their faces toward the house.
3:14 He made the curtain of hyacinth blue, purple, scarlet, and fine linen, and wove cherubim into it.
3:15 He made two pillars before the house, thirty-five cubits in height, and their capitals five cubits.
3:16 He made serseroth in the dabir and placed them upon the pillars’ capitals. He made a hundred pomegranates and placed them upon the hanging ornaments.
3:17 He erected the pillars before the temple, one on the right and one on the left. He named the one on the right Establishment and the one on the left Strength.

2 CHRONICLES 4

4:1 He made the bronze altar, twenty cubits long, twenty cubits wide, and ten cubits high.
4:2 He made the cast sea, ten cubits in diameter, round all around, and five cubits high, with a circumference of thirty cubits.
4:3 Likenesses of calves were beneath it, circling it all around; ten cubits surrounded the basin. They cast the calves in two groups when they were cast,
4:4 in which they made them, twelve calves: three looking north, three looking west, three looking south, and three looking east. The sea was above them, with their hindquarters inward.
4:5 Its thickness was a handbreadth, and its rim was like a cup’s rim, carved with lily shoots, holding three thousand metretes. He completed it.
4:6 He made ten basins and placed five on the right and five on the left, to wash and rinse in them the preparations for the whole burnt offerings. The sea was for the priests to wash in.
4:7 He made ten golden lampstands according to their ordinance and placed them in the temple, five on the right and five on the left.
4:8 He made ten tables and placed them in the temple, five on the right and five on the left. He made a hundred golden bowls.
4:9 He made the priests’ court, the great court, and doors for the court, with their doorways covered in bronze.
4:10 He placed the sea at the house’s right-hand corner, facing toward the east.
4:11 Chiram made the meat hooks, fire pans, the altar’s grate, and all its vessels. Chiram finished doing all the work he performed for Solomon the king in God’s house:
4:12 two pillars, with golath for the chothareth upon the tops of the two pillars, and two networks covering the heads of the chothareth that are upon the pillars’ tops;
4:13 four hundred golden bells for the two networks, and two groups of pomegranates on each network, covering the two golath of the chothareth above the pillars.
4:14 He made ten mechonoth and made the basins upon the mechonoth,
4:15 and the one sea and the twelve calves beneath it,
4:16 and the foot pieces, lifting tools, cauldrons, meat hooks, and all their vessels, which Chiram made and brought up to King Solomon in the Lord’s house, of pure bronze.
4:17 The king cast them in the Jordan region, in the thick earth, in the house of Sokchoth and between Sirdatha.
4:18 Solomon made all these vessels in very great abundance, because the supply of bronze by weight did not run out.
4:19 Solomon made all the vessels of the Lord’s house, the golden altar, and the tables with the bread of presentation upon them;
4:20 the lampstands and the lamps for light according to the ordinance before the dabir, of pure gold,
4:21 and their tongs, their lamps, the bowls, the incense cups, and the fire pans of pure gold;
4:22 and the inner door of the house into the Holy of Holies, for the golden doors of the temple house.

2 CHRONICLES 5

5:1 All the work that Solomon made in the Lord’s house was finished. Solomon brought in the holy things of David his father—the silver, the gold, and the vessels—and put them in the treasury of the Lord’s house.
5:2 Then Solomon assembled Israel’s elders and all the rulers of the tribes, the leaders of the ancestral houses of Israel’s sons, at Jerusalem, to bring up the ark of the Lord’s covenant from David’s city, which is Zion.
5:3 All the men of Israel assembled before the king at the feast; this was the seventh month.
5:4 All Israel’s elders came, and all the Levites took up the ark.
5:5 They brought up the ark, the Tent of Testimony, and all the holy vessels that were in the tent; the priests and the Levites brought it up.
5:6 King Solomon and all Israel’s congregation, and those who feared, and their assembled ones, were before the ark, sacrificing calves and sheep that could neither be numbered nor reckoned because of their multitude.
5:7 The priests brought the ark of the Lord’s covenant to its place, into the dabir of the house, into the Holy of Holies, beneath the cherubim’s wings.
5:8 The cherubim spread out their wings over the ark’s place, and the cherubim covered the ark and its carrying poles from above.
5:9 The carrying poles projected, and their ends could be seen from the holy places in front of the dabir, but they could not be seen outside. They have been there to this day.
5:10 There was nothing in the ark except the two tablets that Moses had placed at Choreb, which the Lord covenanted with Israel’s sons when they came out of the land of Egypt.
5:11 When the priests came out of the holy places—for all the priests who were found there were sanctified, without being arranged by their courses—
5:12 and all the Levitical singers belonging to the sons of Asaph, to Heman, and to Idithoun, and their sons and their brethren, clothed in fine-linen robes, with cymbals, nablai, and kinyrai, stood opposite the altar, and with them were a hundred and twenty priests sounding trumpets,
5:13 there was one voice in trumpeting, singing psalms, and shouting, to give thanks and praise to the Lord. When they raised their voice with trumpets, cymbals, and musical instruments, they said, “Give thanks to the Lord, for it is good, for His mercy endures for ever.” The house was filled with a cloud of the Lord’s glory.
5:14 The priests could not stand to minister because of the cloud, for the Lord’s glory filled God’s house.

2 CHRONICLES 6

6:1 Then Solomon said, “The Lord said that He would dwell in thick darkness.
6:2 I have built a house for Thy name, holy to Thee and ready for dwelling throughout the ages.”
6:3 The king turned his face and blessed all Israel’s assembly, while all Israel’s assembly stood by.
6:4 He said, “Blessed be the Lord, Israel’s God, who spoke with His mouth to David my father and fulfilled it with His hands, saying,
6:5 ‘Since the day I brought My people up from Egypt’s land, I chose no city from all Israel’s tribes in which to build a house for My name to be there, and I chose no man to be leader over My people Israel.
6:6 But I chose Jerusalem for My name to be there, and I chose David to be over My people Israel.’
6:7 It came upon the heart of David my father to build a house for the name of the Lord, Israel’s God.
6:8 The Lord said to David my father, ‘Because it came upon your heart to build a house for My name, you did well that it came upon your heart.
6:9 Nevertheless, you will not build the house. Your son who will come from your loins—he will build the house for My name.’
6:10 The Lord has established the word He spoke. I have come to be in place of David my father and sat on Israel’s throne, as the Lord spoke, and I have built the house for the name of the Lord, Israel’s God.
6:11 I placed there the ark, in which is the Lord’s covenant that He made with Israel.”
6:12 He stood before the Lord’s altar, opposite all Israel’s assembly, and spread out his hands.
6:13 For Solomon had made a bronze base and put it in the middle of the temple court, five cubits long, five cubits wide, and three cubits high. He stood on it, fell on his knees opposite all Israel’s assembly, and spread out his hands toward heaven.
6:14 He said, “Lord, Israel’s God, there is no god like Thee in heaven or on earth, keeping covenant and mercy for Thy servants who walk before Thee with their whole heart.
6:15 Thou hast kept for Thy servant David my father the things Thou didst speak to him, saying them. Thou didst speak with Thy mouth and didst fulfill them with Thy hands, as on this day.
6:16 Now, O Lord, the God of Israel, keep for Thy servant David my father that which Thou didst speak to him: ‘You shall never lack a man before Me sitting upon the throne of Israel, provided your sons guard their way, walking in My law as you walked before Me.’
6:17 Now, Lord, Israel’s God, let Thine utterance that Thou didst speak to Thy servant David indeed be confirmed.
6:18 But will God indeed dwell with men upon earth? If heaven and the heaven of heaven will not suffice for Thee, what is this house that I have built?
6:19 Yet look upon Thy servant’s prayer and my supplication, Lord God, to hear the supplication and prayer that Thy servant prays before Thee today.
6:20 Let Thine eyes be open toward this house day and night, toward this place where Thou didst say Thy name would be invoked, to hear the prayer that Thy servant prays toward this place.
6:21 Hear the supplication of Thy servant and Thy people Israel, whatever they pray toward this place. Hear in Thy dwelling place, from heaven; hear and be propitious.
6:22 If a man sins against his neighbor and takes upon himself an oath-curse to bind himself by it, and comes and swears before the altar in this house,
6:23 then hear from heaven, act, and judge Thy servants, repaying the lawless and returning his ways upon his own head, and justifying the righteous, giving him according to his righteousness.
6:24 If Thy people Israel are crushed before the enemy, if they sin against Thee, and they return, confess Thy name, and pray and supplicate before Thee in this house,
6:25 then hear from heaven and be propitious toward the sins of Thy people Israel. Return them to the land Thou didst give to them and their fathers.
6:26 When heaven is shut and there is no rain because they will sin against Thee, and they pray toward this place, praise Thy name, and turn from their sins because Thou wilt humble them,
6:27 then hear from heaven and be propitious toward the sins of Thy servants and Thy people Israel, for Thou wilt make known to them the good way in which they should walk. Give rain upon Thy land that Thou didst give Thy people as an inheritance.
6:28 If there is famine upon the land, if there is death, damage from wind or yellowing, locust or consuming locust; if the enemy oppresses them before their cities; in every plague and every suffering,
6:29 and every prayer and every supplication that comes to any person or to all Thy people Israel, if a man knows his own affliction and weakness and spreads out his hands toward this house,
6:30 then hear from heaven, from Thy prepared dwelling place, and be propitious. Give to each man according to his ways, as Thou knowest his heart; for Thou alone knowest the heart of men,
6:31 so that they may fear Thy ways all the days they live upon the land Thou didst give our fathers.
6:32 And every foreigner who is not of Thy people Israel, who comes from a distant land because of Thy great name, Thy mighty hand, and Thine uplifted arm—when they come and pray toward this place,
6:33 then hear from heaven, from Thy prepared dwelling place, and do everything for which the foreigner invokes Thee, so that all earth’s peoples may know Thy name and fear Thee as Thy people Israel do, and know that Thy name has been invoked over this house that I have built.
6:34 If Thy people go out to war against their enemies by the way Thou sendest them, and pray to Thee toward this city Thou hast chosen and the house I have built for Thy name,
6:35 then hear from heaven their supplication and prayer and uphold their right.
6:36 For they will sin against Thee—for there will be no human being who will not sin—and Thou wilt strike them and hand them over before enemies, and their captors will carry them captive to an enemy land, a land far away or near,
6:37 and they turn their heart in their land where they were deported, and indeed return and supplicate Thee in their captivity, saying, ‘We have sinned, done wrong, and acted lawlessly,’
6:38 and return to Thee with all their heart and all their soul in the land of those who took them captive, and pray toward their land that Thou didst give their fathers, the city Thou didst choose, and the house I built for Thy name,
6:39 then hear from heaven, from Thy prepared dwelling place, their prayer and supplication, execute judgments, and be propitious toward the people who sinned against Thee.
6:40 Now, O Lord, I beseech Thee, let Thine eyes be open, and Thine ears attentive to the supplication of this place.
6:41 Now arise, O Lord God, to Thy rest, Thou and the ark of Thy strength. Let Thy priests, O Lord God, clothe themselves with salvation, and let Thy sons rejoice in good things.
6:42 Lord God, do not turn away the face of Thine anointed one. Remember the mercies of David Thy servant.”

2 CHRONICLES 7

7:1 When Solomon finished praying, fire came down from heaven and consumed the whole burnt offerings and sacrifices, and the Lord’s glory filled the house.
7:2 The priests could not enter the Lord’s house at that time, because the Lord’s glory had filled the house.
7:3 All Israel’s sons saw the fire descending and the Lord’s glory upon the house. They fell face down to the earth upon the stone pavement, worshiped, and praised the Lord: “For it is good, for His mercy endures for ever.”
7:4 The king and all the people were sacrificing offerings before the Lord.
7:5 Solomon offered the sacrifice: twenty-two thousand calves and a hundred and twenty thousand flock animals. The king and all the people dedicated God’s house.
7:6 The priests stood at their watches, and the Levites had instruments for the Lord’s songs belonging to King David, to give thanks before the Lord, “For His mercy endures for ever,” with David’s hymns through their hands. The priests sounded trumpets on the trumpets opposite them, while all Israel stood.
7:7 Solomon sanctified the middle of the court in the Lord’s house, for he offered the whole burnt offerings and the fat of the salvation offerings there, because the bronze altar Solomon had made was not sufficient to receive the whole burnt offerings, the manaa, and the fat portions.
7:8 Solomon kept the feast at that time for seven days, with all Israel, a very great assembly, from the entrance of Aimath to Egypt’s torrent.
7:9 On the eighth day he held a closing celebration, for he had celebrated the altar’s dedication as a feast for seven days.
7:10 On the twenty-third day of the seventh month he sent the people to their dwellings, rejoicing and with a good heart over the good things the Lord had done for David, Solomon, and His people Israel.
7:11 Solomon completed the Lord’s house and the king’s house. Everything Solomon desired in his soul to do in the Lord’s house and in his own house prospered.
7:12 God appeared to Solomon at night and said to him, “I have heard your prayer and chosen this place for Myself as a house of sacrifice.
7:13 If I shut heaven so that there is no rain, if I command the locust to devour the tree, or if I send death among My people,
7:14 and if My people, upon whom My name has been invoked, are ashamed, and pray, and seek My face, and turn from their wicked ways, then I will hear from heaven, and be propitious toward their sins, and heal their land.
7:15 Now My eyes will be open and My ears attentive to the prayer of this place.
7:16 Now I have chosen and sanctified this house for My name to be there for ever. My eyes and My heart will be there all the days.
7:17 And you, if you walk before Me as David your father did, doing everything I commanded you and keeping My ordinances and judgments,
7:18 then I will establish the throne of your kingdom, as I covenanted with David your father, saying, ‘A man leading in Israel will not be removed from you.’
7:19 But if you turn away and abandon My ordinances and commandments that I placed before you, and go and render service to other gods and worship them,
7:20 then I will remove you from the land that I gave them, and this house that I sanctified for My name I will turn away from My presence. I will make it a parable and a tale among all nations.
7:21 This lofty house—everyone passing through it will be astonished and say, ‘For what reason has the Lord done this to this land and this house?’
7:22 They will say, ‘Because they abandoned the Lord, their fathers’ God, who brought them out of Egypt’s land, and attached themselves to other gods, worshiped them, and served them. Therefore He brought all this calamity upon them.’”

2 CHRONICLES 8

8:1 After twenty years, during which Solomon built the Lord’s house and his own house,
8:2 Solomon rebuilt the cities Chiram had given him and settled Israel’s sons there.
8:3 Solomon came to Aimath Soba and prevailed over it.
8:4 He built Thedmor in the wilderness and all the fortified cities that he built in Emath.
8:5 He built Upper Baithoron and Lower Baithoron, fortified cities with walls, gates, and bars,
8:6 also Baalath, all the fortified cities belonging to Solomon, all the chariot cities, the cavalry cities, and everything Solomon desired, according to his desire to build in Jerusalem, Lebanon, and all his kingdom.
8:7 All the people left from the Chettites, Amorrites, Pherezites, Hevites, and Jebusites, who were not of Israel—
8:8 they were from their descendants left after them in the land, whom Israel’s sons had not exterminated—and Solomon brought them under tribute, continuing to this day.
8:9 But Solomon did not assign Israel’s sons as servants to his kingdom, for they were warriors, rulers, mighty men, and commanders of chariots and cavalry.
8:10 These were the chiefs of King Solomon’s supervisors: two hundred and fifty, driving the people’s work.
8:11 Solomon brought Pharaoh’s daughter up from David’s city to the house he had built for her, for he said, “My wife shall not live in the city of David, Israel’s king, because the place where the Lord’s ark entered is holy.”
8:12 Then Solomon offered whole burnt offerings to the Lord on the altar that he had built opposite the sanctuary,
8:13 according to each day’s requirement, offering as Moses commanded on the Sabbaths, at the months, and at the feasts, three seasons of the year: at the Feast of Unleavened Bread, the Feast of Weeks, and the Feast of Tents.
8:14 Following David’s ordinance, he stationed the priestly divisions according to their services; the Levites were at their watches to praise and minister before the priests according to each day’s requirement, and the gatekeepers in their divisions at each gate. For such were the commands of David, the man of God.
8:15 They did not transgress the king’s commands concerning the priests and Levites in any matter or concerning the treasuries.
8:16 All the work was prepared from the day it was founded until Solomon completed the Lord’s house.
8:17 Then Solomon went to Gasion-gaber and to coastal Ailath in Idumean land.
8:18 Chiram sent ships and servants who knew the sea through his servants’ hands. They went with Solomon’s servants to Sophira, took four hundred and fifty talents of gold from there, and came to King Solomon.

2 CHRONICLES 9

9:1 The queen of Saba heard Solomon’s name and came to Jerusalem to test Solomon with riddles, with a very great force and camels carrying spices, plentiful gold, and precious stones. She came to Solomon and spoke to him about everything in her soul.
9:2 Solomon explained all her questions to her. No matter escaped Solomon that he did not explain to her.
9:3 When the queen of Saba saw Solomon’s wisdom and the house he had built,
9:4 the food of the tables, the seating of his servants, the station of his ministers and their clothing, his wine-pourers and their attire, and the whole burnt offerings he offered in the Lord’s house, she was beside herself.
9:5 She said to the king, “The report I heard in my land about your words and wisdom was true.
9:6 I did not believe the words until I came and my eyes saw. Behold, not half the abundance of your wisdom was reported to me! You surpass the report I heard.
9:7 Happy are the men, happy are these servants of yours who stand by you continually and hear your wisdom!
9:8 Blessed be the Lord your God, who favored you to put you on His throne as king for the Lord your God! Because the Lord your God loved Israel, to establish it for ever, He made you king over them to enact judgment and righteousness.”
9:9 She gave the king a hundred and twenty talents of gold, a very great quantity of spices, and precious stones. There was nothing like those spices the queen of Saba gave King Solomon.
9:10 Solomon’s servants and Chiram’s servants brought gold to Solomon from Souphir, pinewood, and precious stones.
9:11 The king made the pinewood into stairways for the Lord’s house and the king’s house, and kitharas and nablas for the singers. Such things had not previously been seen in Judah’s land.
9:12 King Solomon gave the queen of Saba everything she desired and requested, apart from all that she had brought to King Solomon. Then she returned to her land.
9:13 The weight of gold brought to Solomon in one year was six hundred and sixty-six talents of gold,
9:14 apart from what the subjected men and merchants brought; all Arabia’s kings and the land’s satraps brought gold and silver to King Solomon.
9:15 King Solomon made two hundred large shields of hammered gold: six hundred pure gold pieces for each large shield; six hundred gold pieces went onto each shield.
9:16 He made three hundred shields of hammered gold; three hundred gold pieces were applied to each shield. The king placed them in the House of the Forest of Lebanon.
9:17 The king made a great ivory throne of tusks and gilded it with tested gold.
9:18 The throne had six steps fastened with gold, with armrests on either side of the throne’s seat and two lions standing beside the armrests.
9:19 Twelve lions stood there on the six steps, on either side. Nothing like it had existed in any kingdom.
9:20 All King Solomon’s vessels were of gold, and all the vessels of the House of the Forest of Lebanon were covered with gold. Silver was considered of no account in Solomon’s days.
9:21 For a ship belonging to the king traveled to Tharsis with Chiram’s servants. Once every three years ships came from Tharsis to the king, laden with gold, silver, elephant tusks, and apes.
9:22 Solomon became greater than all the kings in both wealth and wisdom.
9:23 All earth’s kings sought Solomon’s presence to hear the wisdom God had put in his heart.
9:24 Each brought his gifts: silver vessels, gold vessels, clothing, stacte, aromatic spices, horses, and mules, year after year.
9:25 Solomon had four thousand mares for chariots and twelve thousand horsemen. He stationed them in the chariot cities and with the king in Jerusalem.
9:26 He was leader over all the kings from the River to the foreigners’ land and to Egypt’s border.
9:27 The king made gold and silver in Jerusalem like stones, and cedars as plentiful as sycamores in the plain.
9:28 The supply of horses for Solomon came out of Egypt and all the land.
9:29 The remaining deeds of Solomon, first and last—behold, they are written in the records of Nathan the prophet, the records of Achia the Selonite, and the visions of Joel the seer concerning Jeroboam son of Nabat.
9:30 King Solomon reigned over all Israel for forty years.
9:31 Solomon fell asleep, and they buried him in the city of David his father. Rehoboam his son became king in his place.

2 CHRONICLES 10

10:1 Rehoboam came to Sychem, for all Israel was coming to Sychem to make him king.
10:2 When Jeroboam son of Nabat heard—he was in Egypt, having fled from King Solomon’s presence, and Jeroboam had settled in Egypt—Jeroboam returned from Egypt.
10:3 They sent and called him, and Jeroboam and all Israel’s assembly came to Rehoboam, saying,
10:4 “Your father made our yoke harsh. Now release us from your father’s harsh bondage and the heavy yoke he placed upon us, and we will serve you.”
10:5 He said to them, “Go away for three days and come to me.” The people went away.
10:6 King Rehoboam gathered the elders who had stood before Solomon his father while he was alive: “How do you advise answering this people?”
10:7 They told him, “If today you are good to this people, show favor, and speak good words to them, they will be your servants all the days.”
10:8 He abandoned the elders’ counsel that they had given him and consulted the young men brought up with him who stood before him.
10:9 He said to them, “What do you advise, that I may answer this people who said to me, ‘Relax the yoke your father placed upon us’?”
10:10 The young men brought up with him told him, “Speak this way to the people who said to you, ‘Your father made our yoke heavy; you release us from it.’ Say this: ‘My little finger is thicker than my father’s loins.
10:11 My father disciplined you with a heavy yoke; I will add to your yoke. My father disciplined you with whips; I will discipline you with scorpions.’”
10:12 Jeroboam and all the people came to Rehoboam on the third day, as the king had said, “Return to me on the third day.”
10:13 The king answered harshly. King Rehoboam abandoned the elders’ counsel,
10:14 and spoke to them according to the younger men’s counsel: “My father made your yoke heavy, and I will add to it. My father disciplined you with whips; I will discipline you with scorpions.”
10:15 The king did not listen to the people, for there was a reversal from God, saying, “The Lord established His word that He spoke through Achia the Selonite concerning Jeroboam son of Nabat
10:16 and all Israel,” because the king did not listen to them. The people answered the king: “What share have we in David, and what inheritance in Jesse’s son? To your dwellings, Israel! Now see to your house, David!” All Israel went to their dwellings.
10:17 As for Israel’s men living in Judah’s cities, Rehoboam ruled over them.
10:18 King Rehoboam sent Adoniram, who was over the tribute, and Israel’s sons stoned him with stones, and he died. King Rehoboam hurried to mount the chariot and flee to Jerusalem.
10:19 Israel rejected allegiance to David’s house, continuing to this day.

2 CHRONICLES 11

11:1 Rehoboam came to Jerusalem and assembled Judah and Benjamin, a hundred and eighty thousand young warriors, and was waging war against Israel to restore the kingdom to Rehoboam.
11:2 The Lord’s word came to Samaia, the man of God, saying,
11:3 “Speak to Rehoboam, Solomon’s son, and to all Judah and Benjamin, saying,
11:4 ‘Thus says the Lord: You shall not go up or fight your brethren. Return, each to his house, for this matter has come from Me.’” They obeyed the Lord’s word and turned back, so as not to go against Jeroboam.
11:5 Rehoboam dwelt in Jerusalem and built walled cities in Judea.
11:6 He built Baithleem, Aitam, and Thekoe,
11:7 Baithsoura, Sokchoth, and Odollam,
11:8 Geth, Marisa, and Ziph,
11:9 Adoraim, Lachis, and Azeka,
11:10 Saraa, Aialon, and Chebron, which belongs to Judah and Benjamin—walled cities.
11:11 He fortified them with walls and placed leaders in them, with supplies of food, oil, and wine.
11:12 In every city there were large shields and spears, and he strengthened them exceedingly. Judah and Benjamin belonged to him.
11:13 The priests and Levites throughout Israel gathered to him from all the territories.
11:14 For the Levites abandoned the dwellings of their possession and went to Judah, to Jerusalem, because Jeroboam and his sons had expelled them so that they could not minister to the Lord,
11:15 and he appointed priests for himself for the high places, the idols, the vain things, and the calves Jeroboam had made,
11:16 and he expelled them from Israel’s tribes, those who gave their heart to seek the Lord, Israel’s God; and they came to Jerusalem to sacrifice to the Lord, their fathers’ God.
11:17 They strengthened Judah’s kingdom and strengthened Rehoboam, Solomon’s son, for three years, for he walked in the ways of David and Solomon for three years.
11:18 Rehoboam took as wife Mollath, daughter of Jerimouth son of David, and Abaia daughter of Eliab, Jesse’s son.
11:19 She bore him sons: Jaous, Samaria, and Roollam.
11:20 After these things he took Maacah daughter of Absalom, and she bore him Abijah, Jetthi, Ziza, and Emmoth.
11:21 Rehoboam loved Maacah daughter of Absalom more than all his wives and concubines, for he had eighteen wives and thirty concubines. He begot twenty-eight sons and sixty daughters.
11:22 Rehoboam appointed Abijah, Maacah’s son, as ruler, a leader among his brethren, because he intended to make him king.
11:23 He grew greater than all his sons throughout Judah’s and Benjamin’s territories and in the fortified cities. He gave those cities very abundant provisions and requested many wives.

2 CHRONICLES 12

12:1 When Rehoboam’s kingdom was established and secured, he abandoned the Lord’s commandments, and all Israel with him.
12:2 In the fifth year of Rehoboam’s reign, Sousakim king of Egypt came up against Jerusalem, because they had sinned before the Lord,
12:3 with a thousand and two hundred chariots and sixty thousand horses. The multitude that came with him from Egypt was beyond numbering: Libyans, Troglodytes, and Ethiopians.
12:4 They seized the fortified cities in Judah, and he came to Jerusalem.
12:5 Samaia the prophet came to Rehoboam and Judah’s rulers who had gathered in Jerusalem because of Sousakim, and said to them, “Thus said the Lord: You forsook Me, and I will forsake you into Sousakim’s hand.”
12:6 Israel’s rulers and the king felt shame and said, “The Lord is righteous.”
12:7 When the Lord saw that they felt shame, the Lord’s word came to Samaia: “They have felt shame. I will not destroy them; I will give them over in a little while to salvation, and My wrath will certainly not drip upon Jerusalem,
12:8 for they will become servants, and they will know My service and the service of the kingdom of the earth.”
12:9 Sousakim king of Egypt came up and took the treasures in the Lord’s house and the king’s house; he took everything. He also took the large gold shields Solomon had made,
12:10 and Rehoboam made large bronze shields in their place. Sousakim appointed over him commanders of runners who guarded the king’s gateway.
12:11 Whenever the king entered the Lord’s house, the guards entered, and the runners, and those returning to meet the runners.
12:12 When he felt shame, the Lord’s wrath turned away from him, and not toward utter destruction. For there were also good things in Judah.
12:13 Rehoboam became strong in Jerusalem and reigned. Rehoboam was forty-one years old when he became king, and he reigned seventeen years in Jerusalem, the city the Lord chose from all the tribes of Israel’s sons to name His name there. His mother’s name was Noomma the Ammanite.
12:14 He did evil, because he did not direct his heart to seek the Lord.
12:15 Rehoboam’s deeds, first and last—behold, are they not written in the records of Samaia the prophet and Addo the seer, and his acts? Rehoboam fought Jeroboam all the days.
12:16 Rehoboam died and was buried with his fathers, and he was buried in David’s city. Abijah his son became king in his place.

2 CHRONICLES 13

13:1 In the eighteenth year of Jeroboam’s reign, Abijah became king over Judah.
13:2 He reigned three years in Jerusalem. His mother’s name was Maacah daughter of Ouriel from Gabaon. There was war between Abijah and Jeroboam.
13:3 Abijah prepared for battle with a force of powerful warriors, four hundred thousand mighty men. Jeroboam prepared for battle against him with eight hundred thousand, mighty warriors of strength.
13:4 Abijah stood on Mount Somoron in Ephraim’s hill country and said, “Listen, Jeroboam and all Israel!
13:5 Should you not know that the Lord, Israel’s God, gave kingship over Israel for ever to David and his sons, a covenant of salt?
13:6 Yet Jeroboam, Nabat’s son, servant of Solomon, David’s son, arose and withdrew from his lord.
13:7 Pestilent men, lawless sons, gathered to him, and he opposed Rehoboam, Solomon’s son. Rehoboam was younger and cowardly in heart and did not withstand him.
13:8 Now you say you will stand against the Lord’s kingdom through the hands of David’s sons. You are a great multitude, and with you are the gold calves Jeroboam made for you as gods.
13:9 Did you not expel the Lord’s priests, Aaron’s sons, and the Levites, and make priests for yourselves from the people of the land? Anyone approaching to fill his hands with a calf from the cattle and seven rams became a priest to the god who does not exist.
13:10 But we have not abandoned the Lord our God. His priests minister to the Lord, Aaron’s sons, and the Levites in their appointed courses.
13:11 They burn whole burnt offerings to the Lord morning and evening, and compounded incense; bread is presented upon the pure table, and the gold lampstand and its burning lamps are kindled in the evening. For we keep the charges of the Lord, our fathers’ God, but you have abandoned Him.
13:12 Behold, the Lord is with us in command, with His priests and the signal trumpets to signal against you. Israel’s sons, you will fight against the Lord, our fathers’ God, for it will not prosper for you!”
13:13 Jeroboam turned the ambush around to come behind them. He was in front of Judah, and the ambush was behind.
13:14 Judah turned, and behold, the battle was before and behind them! They cried to the Lord, and the priests sounded the trumpets.
13:15 Judah’s men cried out. As Judah’s men cried out, the Lord struck Jeroboam and Israel before Abijah and Judah.
13:16 Israel’s sons fled before Judah, and the Lord handed them over into their hands.
13:17 Abijah and his people struck them a great blow, and five hundred thousand mighty men of Israel fell wounded.
13:18 Israel’s sons were humbled on that day, while Judah’s sons prevailed, because they hoped in the Lord, their fathers’ God.
13:19 Abijah pursued Jeroboam and seized cities from him: Baithel and its villages, Isana and its villages, and Ephron and its villages.
13:20 Jeroboam had no strength again throughout all Abijah’s days. The Lord struck him, and he died.
13:21 Abijah grew strong and took fourteen wives. He begot twenty-two sons and sixteen daughters.
13:22 The remaining deeds of Abijah, his acts, and his words are written in the book of Addo the prophet.
13:23 Abijah died with his fathers, and they buried him in David’s city. Asa his son became king in his place. In Asa’s days Judah’s land was quiet for ten years.

2 CHRONICLES 14

14:1 He did what was good and right before the Lord his God.
14:2 He removed the foreigners’ altars and the high places, shattered the pillars, and cut down the sacred groves.
14:3 He told Judah to seek the Lord, their fathers’ God, and carry out the law and commandments.
14:4 He removed the altars and idols from all Judah’s cities, and he made peace;
14:5 walled cities in Judah’s land, for the land was at peace. There was no war with him during those years, because the Lord had given him rest.
14:6 He said to Judah, “Let us build these cities and make walls, towers, gates, and bars while we hold the land. For just as we sought the Lord our God, He sought us, gave us rest all around, and prospered us.”
14:7 Asa had a force of armed men carrying large shields and spears in Judah’s land, three hundred thousand; and in Benjamin’s land, light-shield bearers and archers, two hundred and fifty thousand. All these were powerful warriors.
14:8 Zare the Ethiopian came out against them with a force of a thousand thousands and three hundred chariots. He came as far as Marisa.
14:9 Asa went out to meet him and arrayed the battle in the ravine north of Marisa.
14:10 Asa cried to the Lord his God: “Lord, it is not beyond Thy power to save with many or with few. Strengthen us, Lord our God, for we trust in Thee, and in Thy name we have come against this great multitude. Lord our God, let no human being prevail against Thee.”
14:11 The Lord struck the Ethiopians before Judah, and the Ethiopians fled.
14:12 Asa and his people pursued as far as Gedor. Ethiopians fell so that there was no preservation among them, for they were shattered before the Lord and His force. They plundered much spoil.
14:13 They cut down their villages around Gedor, for terror from the Lord had come upon them. They plundered all their cities, for there was abundant spoil for them.
14:14 They also cut down tents of possessions—the Amazonians—and took many sheep and camels, then returned to Jerusalem.

2 CHRONICLES 15

15:1 The Spirit of the Lord came upon Azarias son of Oded,
15:2 and he went out to meet Asa and all Judah and Benjamin, and said, “Hear me, Asa and all Judah and Benjamin! The Lord is with you while you are with Him. If you seek Him, He shall be found by you; but if you forsake Him, He will forsake you.
15:3 For many days Israel is without a true God, without a priest giving guidance, and without law.
15:4 They will return to the Lord, Israel’s God, and He will be found by them.
15:5 At that time there is no peace for anyone going out or coming in, for terror from the Lord is upon all who inhabit the regions.
15:6 Nation will fight against nation and city against city, for God terrified them with every affliction.
15:7 But you, be strong, and do not let your hands grow feeble; for there is a reward for your work.”
15:8 When he heard these words and the prophecy of Adad the prophet, he grew strong. He expelled the abominations from all Judah’s and Benjamin’s land and from the cities he had seized in Ephraim’s hill country, and renewed the Lord’s altar before the Lord’s sanctuary.
15:9 He assembled Judah and Benjamin and the resident foreigners living with him from Ephraim, Manasse, and Symeon, for many from Israel joined him when they saw that the Lord his God was with him.
15:10 They gathered in Jerusalem in the third month of the fifteenth year of Asa’s reign.
15:11 On that day he sacrificed to the Lord from the spoil they had brought: seven hundred calves and seven thousand sheep.
15:12 He passed into a covenant to seek the Lord, their fathers’ God, with all the heart and all the soul.
15:13 Everyone who does not seek the Lord, Israel’s God, will die, from younger to older, man or woman.
15:14 They swore by the Lord with a loud voice, trumpets, and horn trumpets.
15:15 All Judah rejoiced over the oath, for they had sworn with all their soul and sought Him with every desire. He was found by them, and the Lord gave them rest all around.
15:16 He also removed Maacah his mother from ministering to Astarte, cut down the idol, and burned it in the Kedron torrent.
15:17 But the high places did not depart; they still existed in Israel. Yet Asa’s heart was complete all his days.
15:18 He brought in the holy things of David his father and the holy things of the house of the Lord God: silver, gold, and vessels.
15:19 There was no war with him until the thirty-fifth year of Asa’s reign.

2 CHRONICLES 16

16:1 In the thirty-eighth year of Asa’s reign, Baasha king of Israel came up against Judah and fortified Rama, to allow no exit or entrance for Asa king of Judah.
16:2 Asa took gold and silver from the treasuries of the Lord’s house and the king’s house and sent it to the son of Ader, Syria’s king, who dwelt in Damascus, saying,
16:3 “Make a covenant between me and you, and between my father and your father. Behold, I have sent you gold and silver. Come, drive Baasha king of Israel away from me, and let him depart from me.”
16:4 The son of Ader listened to King Asa and sent his army commanders against Israel’s cities. He struck Ion, Dan, Abelmain, and all Naphtali’s surrounding districts.
16:5 When Baasha heard, he gave up building Rama any further and stopped his work.
16:6 King Asa took all Judah and took Rama’s stones and timber with which Baasha had built. With them he built Gabae and Maspha.
16:7 At that time Anani the prophet came to Asa king of Judah and said, “Because you trusted in Syria’s king and did not trust in the Lord your God, Syria’s force has been saved from your hand.
16:8 Were not the Ethiopians and Libyans a great force, with confidence and exceedingly many horsemen? Yet because you trusted in the Lord, He handed them into your hands.
16:9 For the Lord’s eyes look throughout all the earth, to show strength in every heart that is whole toward Him. You have acted ignorantly in this; from now on there will be war with you.”
16:10 Asa became enraged with the prophet and put him in prison, because he was angry over this. Asa also maltreated the people at that time.
16:11 Behold, Asa’s deeds, first and last, are written in the Book of the Kings of Judah and Israel.
16:12 In the thirty-ninth year of his reign Asa became ill in his feet, until he was very ill. In his illness he did not seek the Lord, but the physicians.
16:13 Asa fell asleep with his fathers and died in the thirty-ninth year of his reign,
16:14 and they buried him in the tomb he had dug for himself in David’s city. They laid him on the bed and filled it with spices and kinds of perfumers’ ointments, and made an exceedingly great funeral procession for him.

2 CHRONICLES 17

17:1 Jehoshaphat his son became king in his place, and Jehoshaphat grew strong against Israel.
17:2 He stationed a force in all Judah’s fortified cities and appointed leaders in all Judah’s cities and in Ephraim’s cities that Asa his father had seized.
17:3 The Lord was with Jehoshaphat because he walked in his father’s earlier ways and did not seek the idols,
17:4 but sought the Lord, his father’s God, and walked in his father’s commandments, unlike Israel’s works.
17:5 The Lord established the kingdom in his hand, and all Judah gave gifts to Jehoshaphat. He had abundant wealth and glory.
17:6 His heart was lifted up in the Lord’s way, and he further removed the high places and sacred groves from Judah’s land.
17:7 In the third year of his reign he sent his leaders and the sons of the mighty men—Abdia, Zacharia, Nathanael, and Michaia—to teach in Judah’s cities,
17:8 and with them the Levites Samouia, Nathania, Zabdia, Asiel, Semiramoth, Jonathan, Adonia, and Tobia, the Levites; and with them Elisama and Joram, the priests.
17:9 They taught in Judah, having with them a book of the Lord’s law. They went through Judah’s cities and taught the people.
17:10 Terror from the Lord fell upon all the kingdoms of the earth around Judah, and they did not fight Jehoshaphat.
17:11 Some of the foreigners brought Jehoshaphat gifts, silver, and offerings. The Arabs brought him seven thousand and seven hundred rams of the flock.
17:12 Jehoshaphat continued to grow greater, rising to a height. He built dwellings in Judea and fortified cities.
17:13 He had many works in Judea, and powerful, mighty warriors in Jerusalem.
17:14 This was their number by ancestral houses. For Judah, commanders of thousands: Ednas the ruler, with three hundred thousand mighty sons of strength;
17:15 after him, Joanan the leader, with two hundred and eighty thousand;
17:16 after him, Amasia, Zachri’s son, who was eager for the Lord, with two hundred thousand mighty men of strength.
17:17 From Benjamin, Eliada, a mighty man of strength, with two hundred thousand archers and light-shield bearers;
17:18 after him, Jozabad, with a hundred and eighty thousand mighty in war.
17:19 These ministered to the king, apart from those the king had stationed in the fortified cities throughout Judea.

2 CHRONICLES 18

18:1 Jehoshaphat still had abundant wealth and glory, and he formed a marriage alliance with Ahab’s house.
18:2 At the end of some years he went down to Ahab in Samaria. Ahab sacrificed many sheep and calves for him and the people with him, and was enticing him to go up with him to Ramoth in Galaaditis.
18:3 Ahab king of Israel said to Jehoshaphat king of Judah, “Will you go with me to Ramoth in Galaaditis?” He answered him, “As I am, so are you; as your people, so my people will be with you for war.”
18:4 Jehoshaphat said to the king of Israel, “Please seek the Lord today.”
18:5 The king of Israel gathered the prophets, four hundred men, and said to them, “Shall I go to Ramoth-galaad for war, or shall I refrain?” They said, “Go up, and God will give it into the king’s hands.”
18:6 Jehoshaphat said, “Is there not another prophet of the Lord here, so that we may inquire from him?”
18:7 The king of Israel said to Jehoshaphat, “There is still one man through whom to seek the Lord, but I have hated him, because he does not prophesy good concerning me; all his days are for evil. He is Michaias son of Iemla.” Jehoshaphat said, “Let the king not speak so.”
18:8 The king of Israel called one eunuch and said, “Quickly, Michaias son of Iemla!”
18:9 The king of Israel and Jehoshaphat king of Judah were seated, each on his throne and dressed in robes, sitting in the broad space at the doorway of Samaria’s gate. All the prophets were prophesying before them.
18:10 Sedekias son of Chanana made himself iron horns and said, “Thus says the Lord: With these you will gore Syria until it is finished.”
18:11 All the prophets were prophesying in this way, saying, “Go up to Ramoth-galaad, and you will prosper; the Lord will give it into the king’s hands.”
18:12 The messenger who went to summon Michaias spoke to him: “Behold, the prophets have spoken with one mouth favorably about the king. Please let your words be like those of one of them, and you shall speak favorably.”
18:13 Michaias said, “As the Lord lives, whatever God says to me, that I will speak.”
18:14 He came to the king, and the king said to him, “Michaias, shall I go to Ramoth-galaad for war, or shall I refrain?” He said, “Go up and prosper, and they will be given into your hands.”
18:15 The king said to him, “How many times do I put you under oath that you speak to me nothing but truth in the Lord’s name?”
18:16 He said, “I saw Israel scattered on the mountains like sheep that have no shepherd. The Lord said, ‘They have no leader; let each return to his house in peace.’”
18:17 The king of Israel said to Jehoshaphat, “Did I not tell you that he does not prophesy good concerning me, but only evil?”
18:18 He said, “Not so! Hear the word of the Lord. I saw the Lord seated on His throne, and all the host of heaven stood at His right and His left.
18:19 The Lord said, ‘Who will deceive Ahab king of Israel, so that he will go up and fall at Ramoth-galaad?’ One said this, and another said that.
18:20 The spirit came out and stood before the Lord and said, ‘I will deceive him.’ The Lord said, ‘By what means?’
18:21 He said, ‘I will go out and become a lying spirit in the mouth of all his prophets.’ He said, ‘You will deceive him, and you will prevail. Go out and do so.’
18:22 Now behold: the Lord has put a lying spirit in the mouth of all these prophets of yours, and the Lord has spoken evil against you.”
18:23 Sedekias son of Chanana approached and struck Michaias on the jaw. He said to him, “By which road did the Spirit of the Lord pass from me to speak to you?”
18:24 Michaias said, “Behold, you will see on that day when you enter a chamber from a chamber to hide yourself.”
18:25 The king of Israel said, “Take Michaias and return him to Emer the city’s ruler and to Joash, a ruler and the king’s son.
18:26 You shall say, ‘Thus said the king: Put this man in the prison house, and let him eat bread of affliction and water of affliction until I return in peace.’”
18:27 Michaias said, “If you return indeed in peace, the Lord has not spoken in me. Hear, all you peoples!”
18:28 The king of Israel and Jehoshaphat king of Judah went up to Ramoth-galaad.
18:29 The king of Israel said to Jehoshaphat, “I will cover myself and enter the battle, but you put on my clothing.” The king of Israel covered himself and entered the battle.
18:30 The king of Syria had commanded the chariot commanders who were with him, “Do not fight the small or the great, but only the king of Israel.”
18:31 When the chariot commanders saw Jehoshaphat, they said, “He is the king of Israel!” They encircled him to fight, but Jehoshaphat cried out. The Lord saved him, and God turned them away from him.
18:32 When the chariot commanders saw that he was not the king of Israel, they turned away from him.
18:33 A man drew his bow with accurate aim and struck the king of Israel between the lung and the breastplate. He said to the charioteer, “Turn your hand and take me out of the battle, for I am hurt.”
18:34 The battle turned into a rout that day. The king of Israel stood on the chariot facing Syria until evening, and he died as the sun was setting.

2 CHRONICLES 19

19:1 Jehoshaphat king of Judah returned in peace to his house in Jerusalem.
19:2 Jehu son of Anani the prophet went out to meet him and said, “King Jehoshaphat, do you help a sinner, or befriend one hated by the Lord? For this cause wrath from the Lord has come upon you.
19:3 Nevertheless, good things have been found in you, because you removed the sacred groves from the land of Judah and directed your heart to seek the Lord.”
19:4 Jehoshaphat dwelt in Jerusalem. He went out again to the people, from Beersabee to the mountain of Ephraim, and turned them back to the Lord, the God of their fathers.
19:5 He appointed judges in all the fortified cities of Judah, city by city.
19:6 He said to the judges, “Consider what you are doing, because you judge for the Lord, not for a human being, and the matters of judgment are with you.
19:7 Now let the fear of the Lord be upon you. Keep watch and act, because with the Lord our God there is no injustice, admiration of a person’s status, or taking of gifts.”
19:8 In Jerusalem also, Jehoshaphat appointed some of the priests, Levites, and ancestral rulers of Israel for the Lord’s judgment and to judge those living in Jerusalem.
19:9 He commanded them, “This is how you shall act: in the fear of the Lord, in truth, and with a full heart.
19:10 Whatever judgment comes before you from your brethren dwelling in their cities, between blood and blood, between ordinance and commandment, and statutes and judgments, you shall give them clear instruction, so that they do not sin against the Lord, and wrath does not come upon you and your brethren. This is how you shall act, and you shall not sin.
19:11 Behold: Amarias the priest is the leader over you in every matter of the Lord, and Zabdias son of Ismael, the leader for Judah’s house, in every matter of the king. The scribes and the Levites are before you. Be strong and act, and the Lord will be with the good.”

2 CHRONICLES 20

20:1 After this, the sons of Moab and the sons of Ammon, with some of the Minaeans, came against Jehoshaphat for war.
20:2 People came and informed Jehoshaphat, “A great multitude has arrived against you from beyond the sea, from Syria. Behold, they are at Asasanthamar, that is, Engaddi.”
20:3 Jehoshaphat was afraid and set his face to seek the Lord. He proclaimed a fast throughout Judah.
20:4 Judah assembled to seek the Lord. They came from all the cities of Judah to seek the Lord.
20:5 Jehoshaphat stood in the assembly of Judah in Jerusalem, in the Lord’s house before the new courtyard.
20:6 He said, “Lord, God of our fathers, art Thou not God in heaven? Thou rulest over all the kingdoms of the nations. In Thy hand is mighty strength, and no one can resist Thee.
20:7 Was it not Thou, O Lord, who didst destroy this land’s inhabitants before Thy people Israel and didst give it for ever to the descendants of Abraham, Thy beloved?
20:8 They dwelt in it and built a sanctuary in it for Thy name, saying,
20:9 ‘If calamities come upon us—sword, judgment, death, famine—we will stand before this house and before Thee, for Thy name is upon this house. We will cry to Thee from affliction, and Thou wilt hear and save.’
20:10 Now behold the sons of Ammon and Moab and Mount Seir, through whom Thou didst not let Israel pass when they came out from Egypt’s land. They turned aside from them and did not destroy them.
20:11 Now behold: they are attempting to come out against us and expel us from our inheritance, which Thou didst give us.
20:12 O Lord our God, wilt Thou not judge among them? For we have no strength to withstand this great multitude that has come against us, and we do not know what we should do to them: but our eyes are upon Thee.”
20:13 All Judah stood before the Lord, with their children and the women.
20:14 The Spirit of the Lord came upon Oziel son of Zacharias, of the sons of Banaias, of the sons of Eleel, of Manthanias the Levite, from Asaph’s sons, in the assembly.
20:15 He said, “Listen, all Judah, inhabitants of Jerusalem, and King Jehoshaphat! Thus says the Lord to you yourselves: Do not fear or be terrified before this great crowd, because the battle array belongs to God, not to you.
20:16 Tomorrow go down against them. Behold, they are coming up by the ascent of Asas. You will find them at the edge of the river of the wilderness of Jeriel.
20:17 You will not have to wage war. Understand these things and see the Lord’s salvation with you, Judah and Jerusalem. Do not fear or be terrified about going out tomorrow to meet them, and the Lord will be with you.”
20:18 Jehoshaphat bowed on his face, and all Judah and Jerusalem’s inhabitants fell before the Lord to worship the Lord.
20:19 The Levites from the sons of Kaath and the sons of Kore stood up to praise the Lord, the God of Israel, with a loud voice raised high.
20:20 They rose early in the morning and went out to the wilderness of Thekoe. As they went out, Jehoshaphat stood, cried out, and said, “Hear me, Judah and Jerusalem’s inhabitants. Trust in the Lord your God, and you will be trusted. Trust in His prophet, and you will prosper.”
20:21 He consulted the people and appointed psalm singers and those giving praise, to give thanks and praise the holy things as they went out before the army. They said, “Give thanks to the Lord, for His mercy is for ever.”
20:22 When His praise of thanksgiving began, the Lord set the sons of Ammon to fight against Moab and Mount Seir, who had come out against Judah, and they were routed.
20:23 The sons of Ammon and Moab rose against Mount Seir’s inhabitants to destroy and wipe them out. When they had finished Seir’s inhabitants, they rose against one another to be destroyed.
20:24 Judah reached the wilderness lookout and looked out and saw the multitude. There they were: all dead, fallen on the ground, with no survivor.
20:25 Jehoshaphat and his people came to plunder their spoils. They found many herds, baggage, spoils, and precious articles, and plundered them for themselves. They spent three days plundering the spoils, because they were so many.
20:26 On the fourth day they assembled in the Vale of Blessing, for there they blessed the Lord. Therefore they called that place the Valley of Blessing, as it is to this day.
20:27 Every man of Judah returned to Jerusalem, with Jehoshaphat leading them, in great rejoicing, because the Lord had made them rejoice over their enemies.
20:28 They entered Jerusalem with nablas, kinyras, and trumpets, going to the Lord’s house.
20:29 Terror from the Lord came upon all the kingdoms of the earth when they heard that the Lord had fought against Israel’s adversaries.
20:30 Jehoshaphat’s kingdom was at peace, and his God gave him rest all around.
20:31 Jehoshaphat reigned over Judah. He was thirty-five years old when he became king, and he reigned twenty-five years in Jerusalem. His mother’s name was Azouba daughter of Sali.
20:32 He walked in the ways of his father Asa and did not turn aside from doing what was right before the Lord.
20:33 However, the high places still existed, and the people still had not directed their heart toward the Lord, the God of their fathers.
20:34 The rest of Jehoshaphat’s acts, first and last, are written in the accounts of Jehu son of Anani, who wrote down a book of the kings of Israel.
20:35 After this, Jehoshaphat king of Judah entered a partnership with Ahaziah king of Israel—this man acted lawlessly—
20:36 in undertaking the work and going to him to make ships to sail to Tharsis; and he made ships at Gasion-gaber.
20:37 Eliezer son of Dodia from Marisa prophesied against Jehoshaphat: “Because you befriended Ahaziah, the Lord has shattered your work, and your ships have been wrecked.” He was unable to go to Tharsis.

2 CHRONICLES 21

21:1 Jehoshaphat fell asleep with his fathers and was buried beside his fathers in David’s city. His son Joram reigned in his place.
21:2 He had six brethren, sons of Jehoshaphat: Azarias, Iiel, Zacharias, Azarias, Michael, and Saphatias. All these were sons of Jehoshaphat king of Judah.
21:3 Their father gave them many gifts: silver, gold, and weapons, along with walled cities in Judah. But he gave the kingdom to Joram, because he was the firstborn.
21:4 Joram rose over his kingdom and grew strong. He killed all his brethren with the sword, and some of Israel’s rulers.
21:5 When Joram was thirty-two years old, he was established over his kingdom, and he reigned eight years in Jerusalem.
21:6 He walked in the way of Israel’s kings, as Ahab’s house had done, because Ahab’s daughter was his wife. He did what was evil before the Lord.
21:7 Yet the Lord was unwilling to destroy David’s house because of the covenant He had made with David, and as He had told him, to give him and his sons a lamp always.
21:8 In those days Edom broke away from Judah, and they set a king over themselves.
21:9 Joram went out with the rulers, with all the cavalry accompanying him. He rose by night and struck Edom, which was surrounding him, and the chariot commanders. The people fled to their tents.
21:10 Edom has remained apart from Judah to this day. At that same time Lomna rebelled from his hand, because he had abandoned the Lord, the God of his fathers.
21:11 He also made high places in Judah’s cities, caused Jerusalem’s inhabitants to prostitute themselves, and led Judah astray.
21:12 A written message came to him from Elias the prophet, saying, “Thus says the Lord, the God of David your father: Because you have not walked in the way of Jehoshaphat your father or in the ways of Asa king of Judah,
21:13 but have walked in the ways of Israel’s kings and caused Judah and Jerusalem’s inhabitants to prostitute themselves as Ahab’s house caused prostitution, and because you have killed your brethren, your father’s sons, who were better than you,
21:14 behold: the Lord will strike you with a great blow in your people, your sons, your wives, and all your belongings.
21:15 You yourself will be in a grievous sickness, a disease of the belly, until your belly comes out with the sickness, from day to day.”
21:16 The Lord stirred up against Joram the foreigners, the Arabs, and the neighbors of the Ethiopians.
21:17 They came up against Judah and overpowered it. They carried away all the belongings they found in the king’s house, his sons, and his daughters. No son remained to him except Ahaziah, the youngest of his sons.
21:18 After all this, the Lord struck him in his belly with a sickness for which there was no cure.
21:19 As day followed day, when the time of the days came, two days, his belly came out with the disease, and he died in grievous sickness. His people made no funeral procession like the procession for his fathers.
21:20 He was thirty-two years old when he became king, and he reigned eight years in Jerusalem. He departed without praise and was buried in David’s city, but not in the tombs of the kings.

2 CHRONICLES 22

22:1 Jerusalem’s inhabitants made his younger son Ahaziah king in his place, because the raiding band that had come against them, the Arabs and the Alimazonians, had killed all the older ones. Ahaziah son of Joram king of Judah reigned.
22:2 Ahaziah was twenty years old when he became king, and he reigned one year in Jerusalem. His mother’s name was Athaliah daughter of Omri.
22:3 He too walked in the way of Ahab’s house, because his mother was his counselor in sinning.
22:4 He did what was evil before the Lord, like Ahab’s house, because after his father died they were counselors to him, to destroy him.
22:5 He followed their counsels and went with Joram son of Ahab to war against Hazael king of Syria at Rama-galaad. The archers struck Joram.
22:6 Joram returned to Jezrael to be healed of the wounds the Syrians had inflicted on him at Rama when he fought Hazael king of Syria. Ahaziah son of Joram king of Judah went down to see Joram son of Ahab in Jezrael, because he was ill.
22:7 Ahaziah’s downfall came from God through his going to Joram. When he arrived, Joram went out with him to Jehu son of Namessi, the Lord’s anointed—Ahab’s house.
22:8 When Jehu took vengeance on Ahab’s house, he found Judah’s rulers and Ahaziah’s brethren ministering to Ahaziah, and he killed them.
22:9 He ordered a search for Ahaziah, and they caught him while he was receiving treatment in Samaria. They brought him to Jehu, and he killed him. They buried him, for they said, “He is a son of Jehoshaphat, who sought the Lord with his whole heart.” There was no one in Ahaziah’s house able to hold power over the kingdom.
22:10 Athaliah, Ahaziah’s mother, saw that her son was dead. She rose and destroyed all the royal offspring in Judah’s house.
22:11 Jehoshabeath the king’s daughter took Joash son of Ahaziah and stole him away from among the king’s sons who were being killed. She put him and his nurse in the chamber of beds. Jehoshabeath, daughter of King Joram, sister of Ahaziah, and wife of Jehoiada the priest, hid him; she hid him from Athaliah, and Athaliah did not kill him.
22:12 He remained hidden with her in God’s house for six years, while Athaliah ruled over the land.

2 CHRONICLES 23

23:1 In the seventh year Jehoiada grew strong and took the commanders of hundreds with him into a house: Azarias son of Joram, Ismael son of Joanan, Azarias son of Obed, Maasaias son of Adaia, and Elisaphan son of Zacharias.
23:2 They went around Judah and gathered the Levites from all Judah’s cities and the ancestral rulers of Israel. They came to Jerusalem.
23:3 The whole assembly of Judah made a covenant with the king in God’s house. He showed them the king’s son and said to them, “Behold, let the king’s son reign, as the Lord spoke concerning David’s house.
23:4 Now this is what you shall do: let a third of you, from the priests and Levites, enter on the Sabbath, at the gates of the entrances;
23:5 a third shall be in the king’s house, and a third at the Middle Gate. All the people shall be in the courtyards of the Lord’s house.
23:6 Let no one enter the Lord’s house except the priests, the Levites, and the ministering Levites. They may enter, because they are holy. Let all the people keep the Lord’s charges.
23:7 The Levites shall surround the king, each man’s weapon in his hand. Anyone entering the house will die. They shall be with the king when he enters and leaves.”
23:8 The Levites and all Judah did everything Jehoiada the priest commanded. Each took his men from the beginning of the Sabbath until the Sabbath’s departure, because Jehoiada had not dismissed the divisions.
23:9 He gave the swords, large shields, and weapons that had belonged to King David, in God’s house.
23:10 He stationed all the people, each with his weapons, from the house’s right side to the left side of the altar and the house, surrounding the king.
23:11 He brought out the king’s son and placed the royal crown and the testimonies upon him. Jehoiada and his sons made him king and anointed him, and said, “Long live the king!”
23:12 Athaliah heard the sound of the people running, giving thanks, and praising the king, and she came to the king in the Lord’s house.
23:13 She looked: there was the king at his station, and at the entrance were the rulers and trumpets around the king. All the people rejoiced and blew trumpets, and the singers with instruments were singing and hymning praise. Athaliah tore her robe, cried out, and said, “You are attacking! You are attacking!”
23:14 Jehoiada the priest went out and commanded the commanders of hundreds and the leaders of the army: “Throw her outside the house and go in after her, and let her die by the sword.” For the priest had said, “Do not let her die in the Lord’s house.”
23:15 They gave her room, and she passed through the Gate of the Horsemen at the king’s house. They put her to death there.
23:16 Jehoiada made a covenant between himself, the people, and the king, that they should be the Lord’s people.
23:17 All the people of the land entered Baal’s house and pulled it down. They ground its altars and idols fine, and put Matthan the priest of Baal to death before its altars.
23:18 Jehoiada the priest entrusted the work of the Lord’s house to priests and Levites and established the divisions of priests and Levites that David had arranged over the Lord’s house, to offer burnt offerings to the Lord as written in Moses’ law, with rejoicing and songs according to David’s direction.
23:19 The gatekeepers stood at the gates of the Lord’s house, and no one unclean in any respect shall enter.
23:20 He took the ancestral rulers, the powerful men, the people’s rulers, and all the people of the land. They brought the king up into the Lord’s house, and he entered the king’s house through the Inner Gate. They seated the king on the royal throne.
23:21 All the people of the land rejoiced, and the city was quiet. They had put Athaliah to death with the sword.

2 CHRONICLES 24

24:1 Joash was seven years old when he became king, and he reigned forty years in Jerusalem. His mother’s name was Sabia from Beersabee.
24:2 Joash did what was right before the Lord all the days of Jehoiada the priest.
24:3 Jehoiada took two wives for him, and he begot sons and daughters.
24:4 After this, it came into Joash’s heart to repair the Lord’s house.
24:5 He gathered the priests and Levites and said to them, “Go out to Judah’s cities and collect silver from all Israel to strengthen the Lord’s house year by year. Hurry to speak.” But the Levites did not hurry.
24:6 King Joash called Jehoiada the ruler and said to him, “Why have you not overseen the Levites’ bringing from Judah and Jerusalem what Moses the man of God prescribed when he assembled Israel for the Tent of Testimony?”
24:7 For Athaliah was the lawless woman, and her sons had pulled down God’s house; they had also used the holy things of the Lord’s house for the Baalim.
24:8 The king said, “Let a collection box be made and placed outside at the gate of the Lord’s house.
24:9 Let them proclaim in Judah and Jerusalem that they should bring to the Lord as Moses, God’s servant, directed Israel in the wilderness.”
24:10 All the rulers and all the people gave. They brought their contributions and put them into the collection box until it was full.
24:11 Whenever the collection box was brought by the Levites to the king’s overseers, and they saw that the silver had increased, the king’s scribe and the high priest’s overseer came, emptied the collection box, and restored it to its place. They did this day after day and gathered much silver.
24:12 The king and Jehoiada the priest gave it to those doing the work for the service of the Lord’s house. They hired stonecutters and carpenters to repair the Lord’s house, and smiths of iron and bronze to repair the Lord’s house.
24:13 The workers did their work, and the extent of the work increased through their hands. They set the Lord’s house in its standing place and strengthened it.
24:14 When they finished, they brought the remaining silver to the king and Jehoiada. They made articles for the Lord’s house: articles for the service of burnt offerings and golden and silver incense vessels. They offered burnt offerings continually in the Lord’s house all Jehoiada’s days.
24:15 Jehoiada grew old, full of days, and died, being one hundred thirty years old at his death.
24:16 They buried him in David’s city with the kings, because he had done good toward Israel and toward God and His house.
24:17 After Jehoiada’s death, Judah’s rulers came in and bowed down to the king. Then the king listened to them.
24:18 They abandoned the Lord, the God of their fathers, and served the Astartes and idols. Wrath came upon Judah and Jerusalem on that day.
24:19 He sent prophets to them to turn them back to the Lord, but they did not listen. They testified to them, but they did not listen.
24:20 The Spirit of God clothed Azarias, the priest, son of Jehoiada. He stood above the people and said, “Thus says the Lord: Why do you transgress the Lord’s commandments? You shall not prosper. Because you have forsaken the Lord, He will forsake you.”
24:21 They attacked him and stoned him by the command of King Joash in the courtyard of the Lord’s house.
24:22 Joash did not remember the mercy that Jehoiada, the victim’s father, had shown him, but put his son to death. As he was dying, he said, “May the Lord see and judge!”
24:23 After the year’s completion, Syria’s army came up against him. It came against Judah and Jerusalem, and they destroyed all the people’s rulers among the people and sent all their spoils to the king of Damascus.
24:24 Although Syria’s army arrived with few men, God delivered a very great army into their hands, because they had abandoned the Lord, the God of their fathers. He executed judgments against Joash.
24:25 After they left him, leaving him with grave illnesses, his servants attacked him because of the blood of the son of Jehoiada the priest. They put him to death on his bed, and he died. They buried him in David’s city, but did not bury him in the tomb of the kings.
24:26 Those who attacked him were Zabed son of Samath, the Ammonite, and Jozabed son of Somaroth, the Moabite,
24:27 and all his sons; the five came against him. The remaining matters are written in the writing of the kings. His son Amaziah reigned in his place.

2 CHRONICLES 25

25:1 Amaziah became king at twenty-five years of age, and he reigned twenty-nine years in Jerusalem. His mother’s name was Joaden from Jerusalem.
25:2 He did what was right before the Lord, but not with a full heart.
25:3 When the kingdom was established in his hand, he put to death his servants who had murdered his father the king.
25:4 But he did not kill their sons, according to the covenant of the Lord’s law, as written, as the Lord commanded: “Fathers shall not die for children, and sons shall not die for fathers; each shall die for his own sin.”
25:5 Amaziah gathered Judah’s house and organized them by their ancestral houses under commanders of thousands and hundreds throughout Judah and Jerusalem. He counted those twenty years old and upward and found three hundred thousand capable men able to go out to war, bearing spear and large shield.
25:6 He hired one hundred thousand men mighty in strength from Israel for one hundred talents of silver.
25:7 A man of God came to him and said, “King, Israel’s army shall not go with you, because the Lord is not with Israel, all the sons of Ephraim.
25:8 For if you suppose you can be strong through these men, the Lord will rout you before your enemies, because both strength and rout come from the Lord.”
25:9 Amaziah said to the man of God, “What shall I do about the hundred talents I gave Israel’s army?” The man of God said, “The Lord can give you far more than these.”
25:10 Amaziah separated off the army that had come to him from Ephraim, to return to their place. They became very angry with Judah and returned to their place in furious wrath.
25:11 Amaziah grew strong, took his people with him, and went to the Valley of Salts. There he struck down ten thousand of Seir’s sons.
25:12 The sons of Judah captured ten thousand alive and brought them to the top of the cliff. They hurled them down from the cliff’s top, and all were torn apart.
25:13 The men of the army that Amaziah had sent back from accompanying him to war attacked Judah’s cities, from Samaria to Baithoron. They struck down three thousand in them and took many spoils.
25:14 After Amaziah returned from striking Idumea, he brought to them the gods of Seir’s sons and set them up as gods for himself. He bowed before them and himself sacrificed to them.
25:15 The Lord’s wrath came upon Amaziah, and He sent him prophets who said, “Why have you sought the gods of a people who could not rescue their own people from your hand?”
25:16 While he was speaking to him, the king said, “Have I appointed you the king’s counselor? Take care that you are not flogged.” The prophet fell silent, but said, “I know that He has purposed against you to destroy you, because you have done this and have not heeded my counsel.”
25:17 Amaziah took counsel and sent to Joash son of Joachaz, son of Jehu, king of Israel: “Come, let us appear face to face.”
25:18 Joash king of Israel sent to Amaziah king of Judah: “The achouch in Lebanon sent to the cedar in Lebanon, saying, ‘Give your daughter to my son as a wife.’ Behold, the wild animals of the field in Lebanon will come. The animals came and trampled the achouch.
25:19 You say, ‘Behold, you have struck Idumea,’ and your heavy heart lifts you up. Now remain in your house. Why engage in evil, so that you fall, you and Judah with you?”
25:20 Amaziah did not listen, for this came from the Lord, to deliver him into others’ hands, because he had sought the Idumeans’ gods.
25:21 Joash king of Israel went up, and he and Amaziah king of Judah appeared before one another at Baithsamys, which belongs to Judah.
25:22 Judah was routed before Israel, and each man fled to his tent.
25:23 Joash king of Israel captured Amaziah king of Judah, son of Joash, at Baithsamys. He brought him into Jerusalem and tore down four hundred cubits of Jerusalem’s wall, from the Ephraim Gate to the Corner Gate.
25:24 He took all the gold and silver, all the articles found in the Lord’s house and with Abdedom, the treasures of the king’s house, and the sons of the mixed company, and returned to Samaria.
25:25 Amaziah son of Joash king of Judah lived fifteen years after the death of Joash son of Joachaz king of Israel.
25:26 As for the rest of Amaziah’s acts, first and last, are they not written in the Book of the Kings of Judah and Israel?
25:27 At the time Amaziah departed from the Lord, they formed an attack against him. He fled from Jerusalem to Lachis, but they sent after him to Lachis and put him to death there.
25:28 They carried him on horses and buried him with his fathers in David’s city.

2 CHRONICLES 26

26:1 All the people of the land took Uzziah, who was sixteen years old, and made him king in place of his father Amaziah.
26:2 He built Ailath; he restored it to Judah after the king fell asleep with his fathers.
26:3 Uzziah became king at sixteen years of age, and he reigned fifty-two years in Jerusalem. His mother’s name was Chalia from Jerusalem.
26:4 He did what was right before the Lord, according to everything his father Amaziah had done.
26:5 He sought the Lord in the days of Zacharias, who understood the fear of the Lord. In his days he sought the Lord, and the Lord made him prosper.
26:6 He went out and fought the foreigners. He pulled down Geth’s walls, Jabne’s walls, and Azotus’s walls, and built cities of Azotus and among the foreigners.
26:7 The Lord strengthened him against the foreigners, against the Arabs living on the rock, and against the Minaeans.
26:8 The Minaeans gave gifts to Uzziah, and his name reached as far as Egypt’s entrance, because he grew exceedingly strong.
26:9 Uzziah built towers in Jerusalem, at the Corner Gate, the Ravine Gate, and at the corners, and grew strong.
26:10 He built towers in the wilderness and hewed many cisterns, because he had many herds in Sephela and the plain, and vinedressers in the hill country and Carmel, for he loved farming.
26:11 Uzziah had forces that waged war and went out to battle array by number. Their number was recorded by Iiel the scribe and Maasaias the judge under Ananias, the king’s deputy.
26:12 The total number of ancestral rulers mighty for war was two thousand six hundred.
26:13 With them was a fighting force of three hundred seven thousand five hundred, waging war with mighty strength to help the king against his adversaries.
26:14 Uzziah prepared for them, for the whole force, large shields, spears, helmets, breastplates, bows, and slings for stones.
26:15 In Jerusalem he made machines devised by a designer to stand on the towers and corners for shooting missiles and large stones. Their construction was heard of far away, for he was wonderfully helped until he grew strong.
26:16 But when he grew strong, his heart was lifted up to destruction. He acted unjustly against the Lord his God and entered the Lord’s sanctuary to burn incense on the incense altar.
26:17 Azarias the priest entered after him, with eighty priests of the Lord, mighty men.
26:18 They stood against King Uzziah and said to him, “Uzziah, burning incense to the Lord belongs to the priests, Aaron’s sons, who are sanctified to burn incense; it does not belong to you. Leave the sanctuary, for you have departed from the Lord. This will bring you no glory from the Lord God.”
26:19 Uzziah became angry, holding the censer in his hand to burn incense in the sanctuary. As he raged against the priests, leprosy rose on his forehead before the priests in the Lord’s house, above the incense altar.
26:20 The chief priest and the priests turned toward him: there he was, leprous on his forehead. They hurried him from there, and he himself also hurried to leave, because the Lord had rebuked him.
26:21 King Uzziah remained leprous until the day of his death. He lived leprous in a house of apphousoth, for he was separated from the Lord’s house. His son Jotham was over his kingdom, judging the people of the land.
26:22 The rest of Uzziah’s acts, first and last, were written by Jessias the prophet.
26:23 Uzziah fell asleep with his fathers, and they buried him with his fathers in the field of the kings’ burial, for they said, “He is leprous.” His son Jotham reigned in his place.

2 CHRONICLES 27

27:1 Jotham was twenty-five years old when he became king, and he reigned sixteen years in Jerusalem. His mother’s name was Jerousa daughter of Sadok.
27:2 He did what was right before the Lord, according to everything his father Uzziah had done, except that he did not enter the Lord’s sanctuary. But the people were still becoming corrupt.
27:3 He built the Upper Gate of the Lord’s house and did much building on the wall of Ophla.
27:4 He built cities in Judah’s hill country and in the wooded areas, and also dwellings and towers.
27:5 He fought the king of the sons of Ammon and prevailed over him. The sons of Ammon gave him yearly one hundred talents of silver, ten thousand kors of wheat, and ten thousand of barley. The king of Ammon brought him these each year, in the first, second, and third years.
27:6 Jotham grew strong because he prepared his ways before the Lord his God.
27:7 The rest of Jotham’s acts, his war, and his deeds are written in the Book of the Kings of Judah and Israel.
27:9 Jotham fell asleep with his fathers and was buried in David’s city. His son Ahaz reigned in his place.

2 CHRONICLES 28

28:1 Ahaz was twenty years old when he became king, and he reigned sixteen years in Jerusalem. He did not do what was right before the Lord as David his father had done.
28:2 He walked according to the ways of Israel’s kings and also made carved images for their idols.
28:3 He sacrificed in Gaibenenom and made his children pass through fire, according to the abominations of the nations the Lord had destroyed before the sons of Israel.
28:4 He burned incense at the high places, on the rooftops, and beneath every leafy tree.
28:5 The Lord his God delivered him through the hand of Syria’s king, who struck him, took a great number of captives from them, and brought them to Damascus. He also delivered him into Israel’s king’s hands, and the king struck him a great blow.
28:6 Phakee son of Romelia, king of Israel, killed one hundred twenty thousand men mighty in strength in Judah in one day, because they had abandoned the Lord, the God of their fathers.
28:7 Ezekri, the mighty man of Ephraim, killed Maasaias the king’s son, Esdrikam the ruler of his house, and Elkana, the king’s deputy.
28:8 The sons of Israel took captive three hundred thousand of their brethren: women, sons, and daughters. They took many spoils from them and brought the spoils to Samaria.
28:9 The Lord’s prophet was there, named Oded. He went out to meet the army coming to Samaria and said to them, “Behold: the wrath of the Lord, the God of your fathers, is upon Judah, and He delivered them into your hands. You killed among them in anger; it has reached the heavens.
28:10 Now you say you will possess Judah’s and Jerusalem’s sons as male and female slaves. Behold, am I not with you to bear witness to the Lord your God?
28:11 Now hear me: return the captivity you took from your brethren, for the Lord’s furious wrath is upon you.”
28:12 Rulers from Ephraim’s sons stood against those returning from the war: Oudia son of Joanos, Barachias son of Mosolamoth, Ezekias son of Sellem, and Amasias son of Chodli.
28:13 They said to them, “You must not bring the captivity here to us, for you intend to bring sin against the Lord upon us, adding to our sins and ignorance. Our sin is great, and the Lord’s furious wrath is upon Israel.”
28:14 The warriors left the captivity and the spoils before the rulers and the whole assembly.
28:15 The men called by name rose and took charge of the captivity. They covered all the naked from the spoils, dressed them, shod them, and provided for them to eat and anoint themselves. They helped every weak person on pack animals and brought them to their brethren at Jericho, the City of Palms. Then they returned to Samaria.
28:16 At that time Ahaz sent to the king of Assour to help him,
28:17 at this juncture, because Idumeans had attacked, struck Judah, and carried away captives,
28:18 and the foreigners had attacked the cities of Judah’s plain and southwest. They captured Baithsamys, Ailon, Gaderoth, Socho and its villages, Thamna and its villages, and Gamzo and its villages, and settled there.
28:19 For the Lord humbled Judah because of Ahaz king of Judah, because he had utterly departed from the Lord.
28:20 Thaglath-phellasar king of Assour came against him and struck him.
28:21 Ahaz took what was in the Lord’s house, in the king’s house, and among the rulers, and gave it to Assour’s king, but it brought him no help.
28:22 Instead, when he was afflicted, he departed still further from the Lord, and the king said,
28:23 “I will seek the gods of Damascus that are striking me.” He said, “The gods of Syria’s king will strengthen them; therefore I will sacrifice to them, and they will support me.” But they became a stumbling block to him and all Israel.
28:24 Ahaz removed the articles of the Lord’s house and cut them up. He shut the doors of the Lord’s house and made altars for himself at every corner in Jerusalem.
28:25 In every city of Judah he made high places for burning incense to foreign gods, and they provoked the Lord, the God of their fathers.
28:26 The rest of his acts and his deeds, first and last, are written in the Book of the Kings of Judah and Israel.
28:27 Ahaz fell asleep with his fathers and was buried in David’s city, for they did not bring him into the tombs of Israel’s kings. His son Hezekiah reigned in his place.

2 CHRONICLES 29

32:3 He consulted with his elders and mighty men about blocking the waters of the springs outside the city, and they joined in strengthening him.
32:4 He gathered a great crowd and blocked the waters of the springs and the river that divided the city, saying, “Let the king of Assyria not come and find abundant water and gain strength.”
32:5 Hezekiah grew strong and built the entire demolished wall and towers, and outside another defensive wall. He strengthened the retaining work of the City of David and prepared many weapons.
32:6 He appointed commanders of war over the people, and they gathered to him in the square at the gate of the ravine. He spoke to their hearts, saying,
32:7 “Be strong and act courageously. Do not be terrified before the king of Assyria or before the entire nation with him, because more are with us than with him.
32:8 With him are arms of flesh, but with us is the Lord our God, to save and to fight our war.” The people took courage from the words of Hezekiah king of Judah.
32:9 After these things, Sennacherib king of the Assyrians sent his servants against Jerusalem; he himself was at Lachish, with all the army with him. He sent to Hezekiah king of Judah and all Judah in Jerusalem, saying,
32:10 “Thus says Sennacherib king of the Assyrians: On what have you placed your trust, sitting within the siege in Jerusalem?
32:11 Is Hezekiah not deceiving you, to hand you over to death and hunger and thirst, saying, ‘The Lord our God will save us from the hand of the king of Assyria’?
32:12 Is this not Hezekiah who removed His altars and His high places and told Judah and the inhabitants of Jerusalem, ‘Before this altar you shall bow down, and upon it you shall burn incense’?
32:13 Will you not learn what I and my fathers did to all the peoples of the countries? Were the gods of the nations of the whole earth at all able to save their people from my hand?

32:20 King Hezekiah and Isaiah son of Amoz the prophet prayed about these things and cried out to heaven.
32:21 The Lord sent an angel, and he wiped out every mighty warrior, ruler, and commander in the camp of the king of Assyria. The king returned to his land with shame of face. He entered the house of his god, and some who had come from his own body struck him down with the sword.
32:22 The Lord saved Hezekiah and the inhabitants of Jerusalem from the hand of Sennacherib king of Assyria and from everyone’s hand, and He gave them rest all around.
32:23 Many brought gifts to the Lord at Jerusalem and presents to Hezekiah king of Judah, and afterward he was highly exalted in the eyes of all the nations.
32:24 In those days Hezekiah became ill to the point of death. He prayed to the Lord, and He listened to him and gave him a sign.
32:25 Hezekiah did not repay according to the recompense He had given him, but his heart was lifted up. Wrath came upon him and upon Judah and Jerusalem.
32:26 Hezekiah and the inhabitants of Jerusalem humbled themselves from the height of his heart, and the Lord’s wrath did not come upon them in Hezekiah’s days.
32:27 Hezekiah had exceedingly great wealth and glory. He made himself treasuries for silver, gold, precious stones, and spices, and armories, and places for desirable articles,
32:28 and cities for the produce of grain, oil, and wine, mangers for every animal, and folds for the flocks,
32:29 and cities that he built for himself, and a great stock of sheep and cattle, because the Lord gave him exceedingly many possessions.
32:30 Hezekiah himself blocked the upper outlet of the water of Gihon and directed the waters downward toward the southwest of the City of David. Hezekiah prospered in all his works.
32:31 And so, concerning the ambassadors of the rulers from Babylon sent to him to learn from him about the wonder that had happened upon the land, the Lord left him to test him, to know what was in his heart.
32:32 The rest of Hezekiah’s deeds and his mercy are written in the prophecy of Isaiah son of Amoz the prophet and in the Book of the Kings of Judah and Israel.
32:33 Hezekiah lay down with his fathers, and they buried him on the ascent to the tombs of David’s sons. All Judah and the inhabitants of Jerusalem gave him glory and honor at his death, and his son Manasseh reigned in his place.

35:16 All the Lord’s ministry was set in order and prepared that day, to observe the Passover and bring burnt offerings to the Lord’s altar according to King Josiah’s command.
35:17 The sons of Israel who were present observed the Passover at that time, and the Feast of Unleavened Bread for seven days.
35:18 No Passover like it had occurred in Israel since the days of Samuel the prophet. None of Israel’s kings had observed a Passover like the one Josiah observed with the priests, Levites, all Judah and Israel who were present, and the inhabitants of Jerusalem, to the Lord
35:19 in the eighteenth year of Josiah’s reign.
35:19a King Josiah burned the spirit-mediums, the spirit-knowers, the tharaphin, the idols, and the karasim that were in the land of Judah and in Jerusalem, to establish the words of the law written in the book Hilkiah the priest had found in the Lord’s house.
35:19b Before him there had been no one like him, who turned to the Lord with all his heart, all his soul, and all his strength according to all the law of Moses; after him no one like him arose.
35:19c Nevertheless the Lord did not turn from the wrath of His great anger, with which the Lord was angry against Judah over all the provocations with which Manasseh had provoked Him.
35:19d The Lord said, “I will indeed remove Judah from My presence, just as I removed Israel, and I have rejected the city I chose, Jerusalem, and the house of which I said, ‘My name shall be there.’”
35:20 Pharaoh Neco king of Egypt went up against the king of the Assyrians at the River Euphrates, and King Josiah went to meet him.
35:21 He sent messengers to him, saying, “What is there between me and you, king of Judah? I have not come against you today to make war, and God has told me to hurry. Beware of God, who is with me, lest He destroy you.”
35:22 Josiah did not turn his face away from him, but strengthened himself to fight him. He did not listen to Neco’s words through God’s mouth, and came to fight in the plain of Magedon.
35:23 The archers shot at King Josiah, and the king said to his servants, “Bring me out, for I am in great pain.”
35:24 His servants brought him out of the chariot, put him into his second chariot, and brought him to Jerusalem. He died and was buried with his fathers. All Judah and Jerusalem mourned for Josiah,
35:25 and Jeremiah lamented over Josiah. All the male and female rulers spoke a lament over Josiah to this day. They made it an ordinance for Israel, and it is written among the laments.
35:26 Josiah’s deeds and his hope were written in the Lord’s law;
35:27 and his deeds, first and last, are written in the Book of the Kings of Israel and Judah.

2 CHRONICLES 36

36:1 The people of the land took Jehoahaz son of Josiah, anointed him, and installed him as king in his father’s place in Jerusalem.
36:2 Joahaz was twenty-three years old when he began to reign, and he reigned three months in Jerusalem,
36:2a and his mother’s name was Amital, daughter of Jeremiah from Lobena.
36:2b He did what was evil before the Lord, according to everything his fathers had done.
36:2c Pharaoh Neco bound him at Deblatha in the land of Emath so that he would not reign in Jerusalem,
36:3 and the king transported him to Egypt and imposed tribute on the land of one hundred talents of silver and one talent of gold.
36:4 Pharaoh Neco installed Eliakim son of Josiah as king of Judah in place of his father Josiah and changed his name to Jehoiakim. Pharaoh Neco took his brother Joahaz and brought him into Egypt, and he died there.
36:4a They gave the silver and gold to Pharaoh. Then the land began to be taxed to provide the silver at Pharaoh’s command, and each, according to his capacity, exacted the silver and gold from the people of the land to give to Pharaoh Neco.
36:5 Jehoiakim was twenty-five years old when he began to reign, and he reigned eleven years in Jerusalem. His mother’s name was Zechora, daughter of Nerias from Rama. He did what was evil before the Lord, according to everything his fathers had done.
36:5a In his days Nebuchadnezzar king of Babylon came into the land, and he served him for three years and then revolted against him.
36:5b The Lord sent against them the Chaldeans, bands of Syrian raiders, bands of Moabite raiders, and the sons of Ammon and of Samaria. They withdrew after this event, according to the Lord’s word through His servants the prophets.
36:5c Nevertheless the Lord’s anger was against Judah, to remove him from His presence because of Manasseh’s sins in everything he had done,
36:5d and because of the innocent blood that Jehoiakim shed, filling Jerusalem with innocent blood; yet the Lord did not wish to destroy them.
36:6 Nebuchadnezzar king of Babylon came up against him, bound him with bronze fetters, and took him away to Babylon.
36:7 He carried away part of the vessels of the Lord’s house to Babylon and put them in his temple in Babylon.
36:8 The rest of Jehoiakim’s deeds and everything he did—are these not written in the book of the accounts of the days for the kings of Judah? Jehoiakim lay down with his fathers and was buried in Ganoza with his fathers, and his son Jechonias reigned in his place.
36:9 Jechonias was eighteen years old when he began to reign, and he reigned three months and ten days in Jerusalem. He did what was evil before the Lord.
36:10 At the turn of the year King Nebuchadnezzar sent and brought him into Babylon with the desirable vessels of the Lord’s house. He made Zedekiah, his father’s brother, king over Judah and Jerusalem.
36:11 Zedekiah was twenty-one years old when he began to reign, and he reigned eleven years in Jerusalem.
36:12 He did what was evil before the Lord his God. He felt no shame before Jeremiah the prophet, and at what came from the Lord’s mouth,
36:13 in breaking his obligations to King Nebuchadnezzar, who had made him swear by God. He hardened his neck and strengthened his heart so as not to return to the Lord God of Israel.
36:14 All the distinguished men of Judah, the priests, and the people of the land multiplied breaches of faith in the nations’ abominations and defiled the Lord’s house in Jerusalem.
36:15 The Lord, the God of their fathers, sent through prophets, rising early and sending His messengers, because He spared His people and His sanctuary.
36:16 They kept sneering at His messengers, treating His words as nothing, and mocking His prophets, until the Lord’s anger rose among His people, until there was no healing.
36:17 He brought against them a king of the Chaldeans, who killed their young men with the sword in the house of his sanctuary and did not spare Zedekiah. They had no mercy on their virgins and carried away their elders. He handed everything into their hands.
36:18 All the vessels of God’s house, great and small, the treasures, and all the treasures of the king and the magnates—he brought everything into Babylon.
36:19 He burned the Lord’s house, demolished Jerusalem’s wall, and burned its citadels with fire, consigning every beautiful article to destruction.
36:20 He deported the survivors to Babylon, and they became slaves to him and his sons until the kingdom of the Medes,
36:21 to fulfill the Lord’s word through Jeremiah’s mouth, until the land accepted its Sabbaths, to keep Sabbath. All the days of its desolation it kept Sabbath, to complete seventy years.
36:22 In the first year of Cyrus king of the Persians, after the Lord’s word through Jeremiah’s mouth had been fulfilled, the Lord stirred the spirit of Cyrus king of the Persians. He ordered a proclamation throughout his kingdom in writing, saying,
36:23 “Thus says Cyrus king of the Persians: The Lord, the God of heaven, has given me all the kingdoms of the earth, and He has commanded me to build Him a house in Jerusalem in Judea. Who among you is of all His people? His God shall be with him, and let him go up.”
