Wisdom of Solomon — Chapter 1

Read this chapter on staging

Seek righteousness and the Lord

1:1 Love righteousness, you who judge the earth;
think of the Lord in goodness,
and seek Him in simplicity of heart.

1:2 For He is found by those who do not put Him to the test,
and He makes Himself known to those who do not distrust Him.

1:3 For crooked reasonings separate from God,
and power, when put to the test, exposes the foolish.

1:4 For wisdom will not enter a soul that devises evil,
nor will she dwell in a body indebted to sin.

1:5 For a holy spirit of discipline will flee deceit
and depart from senseless reasonings,
and will be put to shame when injustice comes upon it.

1:6 For wisdom is a spirit that loves humanity,
and she will not acquit a blasphemer of what his lips have said;
for God is a witness of his kidneys,
a true overseer of his heart,
and a hearer of his tongue.

1:7 For the Spirit of the Lord has filled the inhabited world,
and that which holds all things together has knowledge of utterance.

1:8 Therefore no one who speaks unjust things will escape notice,
nor will convicting justice pass him by.

1:9 For there will be an examination of the ungodly person’s deliberations,
and the report of his words will come to the Lord
to expose his lawless deeds.

1:10 For an ear of jealousy listens to all things,
and the murmur of grumblings is not hidden.

Do not summon death

1:11 Therefore guard yourselves against useless grumbling,
and restrain your tongue from slander;
for a secret utterance will not pass away without effect,
and a lying mouth destroys a soul.

1:12 Do not pursue death through the wandering of your life,
nor draw destruction upon yourselves through the works of your hands;

1:13 For God did not make death,
neither does He delight in the destruction of the living.

1:14 For He created all things that they might exist,
and the created things of the world are life-preserving;
there is no poison of destruction in them,
nor is Hades’ dominion upon the earth.

1:15 For righteousness is immortal.

The ungodly make a covenant with death

1:16 But the ungodly summoned him by their hands and words;
considering him a friend, they wasted away,
and they made a covenant with him,
because they are worthy to belong to his portion.

Saints English wording notes

1:1 · simplicity

Meaning. Sincerity or undivided intention in seeking God.

Wording. Retain simplicity with singleness or sincerity as its explanation. Keep the three plural commands directed to rulers; simplicity must not suggest intellectual deficiency.

English comparison. All four witnesses retain simplicity of heart. DRC justice corresponds to the others' righteousness in the opening command. KJV and Brenton mark an added heart in the earlier goodness phrase.

Meaning. Knowledge of speech in the statement about what holds all things together.

Wording. Use utterance or voice without the inherited added every. The context may imply comprehensiveness, but no separate every modifies voice in this clause. Preserve the preceding inhabited-world description.

English comparison. KJV, Brenton, and DRC have knowledge of the voice. Saints has every utterance; the proposal removes that explanatory addition from the reading phrase.

Meaning. Take pleasure in an event or condition.

Wording. Keep the denial of pleasure beside the separate assertion that God did not make death. Do not collapse these clauses into a generic statement that suffering does not exist.

English comparison. All witnesses agree closely, using delight or pleasure. The verse's claims belong to its argument and should be carried at their stated strength.

Wisdom of Solomon — Chapter 2

Read this chapter on staging

Their reasoning about life and death

2:1 For they said among themselves, reasoning wrongly:
“Our life is short and sorrowful,
and there is no healing when a human being dies,
and no one has been known to return from Hades.

2:2 For we came into being by chance,
and afterward we will be as though we had not existed;
for the breath in our nostrils is smoke,
and reason is a spark in the movement of our heart;

2:3 when it is extinguished, the body will become ashes,
and the spirit will disperse like insubstantial air.

2:4 And our name will be forgotten in time,
and no one will remember our works;
our life will pass like the traces of a cloud,
and will disperse like mist,
driven away by the sun’s rays
and weighed down by its heat.

2:5 For our allotted time is the passing of a shadow,
and there is no turning back from our end,
because it has been sealed, and no one returns.

2:6 Come, then, and let us enjoy the good things that exist,
and eagerly make use of creation as in youth.

2:7 Let us fill ourselves with costly wine and perfumes,
and let no spring blossom pass us by.

2:8 Let us crown ourselves with rosebuds before they wither.

2:9 Let none of us be without a share in our revelry;
let us leave tokens of our enjoyment everywhere,
for this is our portion, and this our lot.

The plot against the righteous person

2:10 Let us oppress the poor righteous person;
let us not spare a widow,
nor show regard for an old man’s many years of gray hair.

2:11 Let our strength be the law of righteousness,
for what is weak is shown to be useless.

2:12 Let us lie in wait for the righteous person, because he is troublesome to us
and opposes our works;
he reproaches us for sins against law
and charges us with sins against our upbringing.

2:13 He professes to have knowledge of God
and calls himself a child of the Lord.

2:14 He has become a reproach to our thoughts;
even the sight of him weighs upon us,

2:15 because his life is unlike that of others,
and his paths are different.

2:16 He has reckoned us counterfeit,
and keeps away from our ways as from impurities;
he calls the final lot of the righteous blessed,
and boasts that God is his father.

2:17 Let us see whether his words are true,
and test what will happen at his departure.

2:18 For if the righteous person is a son of God, He will help him
and rescue him from the hand of his opponents.

2:19 Let us examine him with insult and torture,
so that we may know his gentleness
and test his endurance of wrong.

2:20 Let us condemn him to a shameful death,
for, according to his words, he will be visited with care.”

Their blindness and God’s purpose

2:21 They reasoned these things and went astray,
for their wickedness blinded them;

2:22 they did not know the mysteries of God,
nor hope for a reward of holiness,
nor recognize a prize for blameless souls.

2:23 For God created the human being for incorruption
and made him an image of His own eternity;

2:24 but through the devil’s envy death entered the world,
and those who belong to his portion experience it.

2:12 · upbringing

Meaning. Violations of the formation or instruction acknowledged by the speakers.

Wording. Use upbringing or instruction with education as an older broad connection. Preserve the hostile speakers' voice and the separate law clause; their complaint is reported speech.

English comparison. KJV and Brenton have transgressings of our education; DRC sins of our way of life. Education here is wider than modern formal schooling.

Meaning. Freedom from decay or destruction in the purpose of human creation.

Wording. Retain for incorruption and the following image of God's eternity. Explain immortality as a related concept while keeping it distinct from the different Greek noun in 3:4.

English comparison. KJV and Brenton render to be immortal; DRC incorruptible. DRC's image of His likeness differs from the declared Greek eternity clause.

Wording. Retain envy, its stated agent, and the following clause about those of his portion. Discussion of the adversary's identification belongs in a note; the verse must retain its own wording.

Wisdom of Solomon — Chapter 3

Read this chapter on staging

Their hope is full of immortality

3:1 But the souls of the righteous are in the hand of God, and torment will surely not touch them.

3:2 In the eyes of the foolish they seemed to have died,
and their departure was reckoned a calamity,

3:3 and their going away from us a destruction;
but they are in peace.

3:4 For even if they are punished in the sight of human beings,
their hope is full of immortality;

3:5 and after being disciplined a little, they will receive great benefits,
because God tested them
and found them worthy of Himself.

3:6 Like gold in a crucible He tested them,
and like a sacrificial whole burnt offering He accepted them.

3:7 And at the time of their visitation they will shine out,
and like sparks among stubble they will dart about.

3:8 They will judge nations and rule over peoples,
and the Lord will reign over them unto the ages.

3:9 Those who trust in Him will understand truth,
and the faithful will remain with Him in love,
for grace and mercy are for His chosen ones.

Ungodliness and the fruit of faithfulness

3:10 But the ungodly will have punishment in keeping with their reasoning—
those who neglected what is righteous and departed from the Lord.

3:11 For the one who despises wisdom and discipline is wretched;
their hope is empty, their labors unprofitable,
and their works useless.

3:12 Their wives are foolish,
and their children wicked;
their offspring is accursed.

3:13 For blessed is the undefiled barren woman,
who has not known a bed of transgression;
she will have fruit at the visitation of souls,

3:14 and the eunuch who has done no lawless deed with his hand
and has not conceived evil against the Lord;
for a choice favor will be given him for his faithfulness,
and a more delightful portion in the Lord’s temple.

3:15 For the fruit of good labors is glorious,
and the root of understanding does not fail.

3:16 But the children of adulterers will not reach fulfillment,
and offspring from an unlawful bed will disappear.

3:17 For even if they become long-lived, they will be reckoned as nothing,
and their old age will be without honor at the end;

3:18 and if they die quickly, they will have no hope,
nor consolation on the day of decision.

3:19 For the ends of an unrighteous generation are harsh.

Saints English wording notes

3:1 · torment

Meaning. Severe suffering denied of the righteous in God's hand.

Wording. Retain the emphatic denial and the souls-in-God's-hand image. The following verses develop the contrast with what appears to human observers; preserve that argument rather than adding qualifications inside this line.

English comparison. KJV and Brenton closely match Saints. DRC has torment of death, while death is not a separate qualifier of the Greek noun in this verse.

3:4 · immortality

Meaning. Deathlessness as the object or content of the righteous person's hope.

Wording. Keep immortality and distinguish it from incorruption at 2:23. Preserve the conditional suffering in human sight; the verse does not say that the righteous have never suffered bodily pain.

English comparison. All four witnesses name immortality. Their tense and condition wording differs, with Saints, KJV, and Brenton retaining a conditional or concessive relation.

Wisdom of Solomon — Chapter 4

Read this chapter on staging

Virtue outweighs a multitude of descendants

4:1 Childlessness with virtue is better,
for immortality is in its remembrance,
because it is recognized both by God and by human beings.

4:2 When it is present, people imitate it,
and when it has departed, they long for it;
throughout the age it marches crowned in triumph,
having won the contest for undefiled prizes.

4:3 But the prolific multitude of the ungodly will be of no use;
from illegitimate shoots it will not send a root deep down,
nor establish a secure foundation.

4:4 For even if it flourishes in branches for a time,
standing insecurely, it will be shaken by wind
and uprooted by the force of winds.

4:5 Unfulfilled branches will be broken off,
and their fruit will be useless, unripe for eating,
and fit for nothing.

4:6 For children born from unlawful unions
are witnesses of wickedness against their parents at their examination.

A righteous life is measured by understanding

4:7 But if a righteous person dies early, he will be at rest.

4:8 For honorable old age is not length of time,
nor is it measured by a number of years;

4:9 rather, understanding is gray hair for human beings,
and an unstained life is the maturity of old age.

4:10 Having become pleasing to God, he was loved,
and while living among sinners he was transferred.

4:11 He was snatched away, lest evil change his understanding
or deceit mislead his soul;

4:12 for the fascination of wickedness obscures what is good,
and the wandering of desire alters an innocent mind.

4:13 Having been brought to completion in a short time, he fulfilled long years;

4:14 for his soul was pleasing to the Lord;
therefore he hastened from the midst of wickedness.
Yet the peoples saw and did not understand,
nor set such a thing in their minds:

4:15 that grace and mercy are among His chosen ones,
and visitation among His holy ones.

The judgment of those who despised the righteous

4:16 But a righteous person who has died will condemn the living ungodly,
and youth brought swiftly to completion will condemn the many years of an unrighteous person’s old age.

4:17 For they will see a wise person’s end
and will not understand what the Lord planned concerning him
or for what purpose He kept him safe.

4:18 They will see and despise,
but the Lord will laugh them to scorn.

4:19 And afterward they will become a dishonored corpse
and an object of outrage among the dead for ever;
for He will dash them down headlong, speechless,
and shake them from their foundations;
they will be laid utterly waste,
and will be in anguish,
and their remembrance will perish.

4:20 They will come in dread at the reckoning of their sins,
and their lawless deeds will confront and convict them.

Saints English wording notes

Wording. Singular address uses thou, thee, thy, and thine; plural address uses ye, you, and your. Source-sensitive images, inherited numbering, and uncertainties are retained.

Wisdom of Solomon — Chapter 5

Read this chapter on staging

The oppressors acknowledge their error

5:1 Then the righteous person will stand with great boldness
before those who afflicted him
and those who disregarded his labors.

5:2 When they see him, they will be troubled with terrible fear
and astonished at his unexpected salvation.

5:3 They will speak among themselves in repentance,
and in anguish of spirit they will groan and say:

5:4 “This was the one whom we fools once made a laughingstock
and a byword of reproach.
We reckoned his life madness
and his end dishonorable.

5:5 How was he counted among the sons of God,
and how is his portion among the saints?

5:6 So we strayed from the way of truth,
and the light of righteousness did not shine upon us,
and the sun did not rise upon us.

5:7 We were filled with paths of lawlessness and destruction,
and traveled through trackless deserts,
but the way of the Lord we did not know.

5:8 What did arrogance profit us?
And what did wealth with boasting contribute to us?

5:9 All those things passed like a shadow
and like a report rushing by;

5:10 like a ship passing through surging water,
whose trace cannot be found after it has crossed,
nor the path of its keel among the waves;

5:11 or like a bird that has flown through the air:
no sign of its passage is found.
It crossed the light air,
lashed by the beat of its pinions
and split by the force of its rushing wings;
and afterward no sign of its passage was found in it;

5:12 or like an arrow shot at a target:
the air it cut immediately flowed back into itself,
so that its course could not be known:

5:13 so we also were born and vanished,
and had no sign of virtue to show,
but were consumed in our wickedness.”

5:14 For the hope of an ungodly person is like dust borne by wind,
and like fine frost driven by a storm;
like smoke it was dispersed by wind,
and like the memory of a guest who stayed one day it passed away.

The Lord arms creation for judgment

5:15 But the righteous live for ever,
and their reward is with the Lord,
and the Most High takes care of them.

5:16 Therefore they will receive the royal crown of splendor
and the diadem of beauty from the Lord’s hand,
because with His right hand He will cover them
and with His arm He will shield them.

5:17 He will take His zeal as full armor
and make creation into weapons to repel His enemies;

5:18 He will put on righteousness as a breastplate,
and put on unfeigned judgment as a helmet;

5:19 He will take holiness as an invincible shield,

5:20 and sharpen stern wrath into a sword;
the world will fight together with Him against the senseless.

5:21 Well-aimed bolts of lightning will go forth,
and from the clouds, as from a well-curved bow, they will leap at the target;

5:22 and hailstones full of fury will be hurled from a catapult;
the water of the sea will rage against them,
and rivers will flood over them relentlessly.

5:23 A mighty wind will oppose them
and winnow them away like a storm;
lawlessness will lay the whole earth waste,
and wrongdoing will overturn the thrones of rulers.

Wisdom of Solomon — Chapter 6

Read this chapter on staging

Authority brings a searching judgment

6:1 Listen, then, kings, and understand;
learn, judges of the ends of the earth.

6:2 Give ear, you who rule a multitude
and boast over crowds of nations;

6:3 for your rule was given you by the Lord,
and your authority by the Most High,
who will examine your works and search out your plans.

6:4 For though you were servants of His kingdom, you did not judge rightly,
nor keep law,
nor walk according to God’s purpose.

6:5 He will come upon you terribly and swiftly,
for a severe judgment falls upon those in high position.

6:6 For the least is pardonable through mercy,
but the powerful will be powerfully examined.

6:7 For the Master of all will not shrink before anyone’s face
or show deference to greatness,
because He Himself made both small and great
and provides for all alike;

6:8 but a forceful inquiry awaits the mighty.

6:9 To you, therefore, rulers, my words are directed,
so that you may learn wisdom and not fall away.

6:10 For those who keep holy things in holiness will be made holy,
and those instructed in them will find a defense.

6:11 Desire my words, then;
long for them, and you will be instructed.

Wisdom welcomes those who seek her

6:12 Wisdom is radiant and unfading;
she is readily seen by those who love her
and found by those who seek her.

6:13 She goes ahead of those who desire her, making herself known first.

6:14 The one who rises early for her will not grow weary,
for he will find her sitting beside his gates.

6:15 For to reflect upon her is the perfection of understanding,
and the one who keeps watch for her will soon be free from care;

6:16 for she herself goes about seeking those worthy of her,
appears graciously to them on their paths,
and meets them in every thought.

6:17 For her beginning is the truest desire for discipline,
and concern for discipline is love;

6:18 love is the keeping of her laws,
and attention to laws is assurance of incorruption;

6:19 and incorruption brings one near to God.

6:20 Therefore desire for wisdom leads up to kingship.

Wisdom will be openly taught

6:21 If, then, you delight in thrones and scepters, rulers of peoples,
honor wisdom, so that you may reign for ever.

6:22 What wisdom is and how she came to be, I will declare,
and I will not hide mysteries from you;
instead I will trace her from the beginning of her origin,
and bring the knowledge of her into the open,
and I will certainly not pass truth by.

6:23 Nor will I travel with wasting envy,
for it will have no share with wisdom.

6:24 A multitude of wise people is the world’s salvation,
and an understanding king is the stability of a people.

Creation points beyond itself

13:1 For all human beings in whom ignorance of God was present were futile by nature;
from the good things seen they could not know the One who is,
nor, attending to the works, did they recognize the Artisan.

13:2 Instead, they supposed fire or wind or swift-moving air,
or the circuit of stars or violent water,
or the lights of heaven to be gods, rulers of the world.

13:3 If, delighted by their beauty, they supposed these things to be gods,
let them know how much better than these their Master is;
for the Originator of beauty created them.

13:4 And if they were astonished at their power and activity,
let them understand from these how much more powerful the One who fashioned them is.

13:5 For from the greatness and beauty of created things
their Maker is seen by analogy.

13:6 Yet the blame upon these is small,
for perhaps they themselves go astray
while seeking God and wishing to find Him.

13:7 For living among His works, they search them out
and are persuaded by their appearance, because the things seen are beautiful.

13:8 But again, even they are not excusable;

13:9 for if they were able to know so much
that they could investigate the world,
how did they not more quickly find the Master of these things?

The woodworker’s helpless god

13:10 But wretched are those whose hopes rest in dead things,
who called the works of human hands gods:
gold and silver, exercises of craftsmanship,
likenesses of living creatures,
or a useless stone, the work of an ancient hand.

13:11 Suppose a woodcutting craftsman sawed down a manageable tree,
skillfully scraped off all its bark,
and, working it handsomely,
fashioned a useful vessel for the service of life,

13:12 and burned the scraps from the work
to prepare food and ate his fill,

13:13 then took the scrap left from these, useful for nothing,
a crooked piece of wood grown full of knots,
and carved it with the care of his leisure,
shaped it with practiced understanding,
and made it resemble a human image,

13:14 or made it like some worthless animal,
coating it with red ocher and reddening its surface with paint,
and covering every blemish in it,

13:15 and made for it a dwelling worthy of it,
set it in a wall, and secured it with iron.

13:16 He took care that it would not fall,
knowing that it cannot help itself;
for it is an image and needs help.

13:17 Yet when praying concerning his possessions, marriage, and children,
he is not ashamed to address what has no soul;
for health he calls upon what is weak,

13:18 for life he petitions what is dead,
for assistance he begs what is utterly inexperienced,
for a journey what cannot even use its feet,

13:19 and for gain, work, and success of his hands,
he asks vigor of what is most powerless with its hands.

Wisdom of Solomon — Chapter 14

Wood that serves life and wood called a god

14:1 Again, someone preparing for a voyage and about to traverse wild waves
calls upon a piece of wood more rotten than the ship carrying him.

14:2 For the desire for gain devised that ship,
and wisdom, the artisan, fashioned it;

14:3 but Thy providence, Father, steers it,
for Thou didst give a way even in the sea
and a safe path among the waves,

14:4 showing that Thou canst save from every danger,
so that even someone without skill may embark.

14:5 Thou wilt that the works of Thy wisdom should not be idle;

and, crossing the surge on a raft, they were brought safely through.

14:6 For even at the beginning, when arrogant giants were perishing,
the hope of the world took refuge on a raft
and left to the age a seed for generation, steered by Thy hand.

14:7 For blessed is wood through which righteousness comes about;

14:8 but the handmade object is accursed, both it and its maker:
the one because he made it,
and the other because, though corruptible, it was named a god.

14:9 For equally hateful to God are the ungodly person and his ungodliness;

14:10 for what was done will be punished together with the one who did it.

14:11 Therefore there will also be a visitation upon the nations’ idols,
because within God’s creation they became an abomination,
stumbling blocks to human souls,
and a snare for the feet of fools.

Grief, rulers, and artistic flattery

14:12 For the devising of idols is the beginning of sexual immorality,
and their invention the corruption of life.

14:13 For they did not exist from the beginning,
nor will they exist for ever;

14:14 through human vainglory they entered the world,
and therefore a swift end was planned for them.

14:15 For a father worn down by untimely grief,
having made an image of a child swiftly taken away,
now honored as a god the one who had once been a dead human being,
and handed on mysteries and rites to those under him.

14:16 Then, strengthened over time, the ungodly custom was kept as law.

14:17 And carved images were worshiped at rulers’ commands;
when people could not honor them face to face because they lived far away,
they reproduced their distant appearance
and made a visible image of the honored king,
so that by their zeal they might flatter the absent one as though present.

14:18 The artist’s ambition urged even those who did not know the king
toward still greater worship;

14:19 for, perhaps wishing to please the ruler,
he used his art to force the likeness toward greater beauty.

14:20 The multitude, drawn by the charm of the workmanship,
now reckoned as an object of worship the human being whom they had honored a little before.

14:21 And this became an ambush for life,
because human beings, enslaved either to misfortune or to royal power,
bestowed upon stones and pieces of wood the name that cannot be shared.

False worship and the corruption of life

14:22 Then it was not enough to go astray about the knowledge of God;
living in the great war of ignorance,
they also call such great evils peace.

14:23 For while celebrating rites that murder children, or secret mysteries,
or frenzied revelries with strange customs,

14:24 they no longer keep either lives or marriages pure;
one kills another by ambush or grieves him by adultery.

14:25 Bloodshed and murder, theft and deceit, hold everything in confusion,
corruption, unfaithfulness, disturbance, perjury,

14:26 confusion of good things, forgetfulness of favor,
defilement of souls, alteration of natural relations,
disorder in marriages, adultery, and licentiousness.

14:27 For worship of nameless idols
is the beginning, cause, and end of every evil.

14:28 For they either go mad in their merrymaking, or prophesy lies,
or live unjustly, or readily swear falsely.

14:29 For trusting in lifeless idols,
they do not expect to be harmed when they swear wrongly.

14:30 But just consequences will pursue them for both things:
because they thought wrongly about God by attending to idols,
and swore unjustly in deceit, despising holiness.

14:31 For it is not the power of those by whom they swear,
but the justice due to sinners,
that always pursues the transgression of the unrighteous.

15:1 But Thou, our God, art good and true,
patient, and governing all things in mercy.

15:4 For neither the evil-devising invention of human beings misled us,

a form stained with varied colors,

15:5 the sight of which stirs desire in fools,

15:6 Lovers of evil and worthy of such hopes

The potter who fashions a futile god

15:7 For a potter, laboriously kneading soft earth,
shapes each object for our service;
from the same clay he fashioned alike
vessels serving clean purposes
and those serving the opposite;
and the clay-worker decides the use of each kind.

15:8 With evil labor he also shapes a futile god from the same clay,
though he himself, having come from earth a little before,
will shortly go back to that from which he was taken,
when the debt of his soul is demanded.

15:9 But his concern is not that he is about to die,
nor that his life is short;
instead, he competes with goldsmiths and silver-workers,
imitates bronze-workers,
and considers it glory that he fashions counterfeits.

15:10 His heart is ashes, and his hope more worthless than earth,
and his life more dishonorable than clay,

15:11 because he did not know the One who formed him,
who breathed into him an active soul
and breathed in a life-giving spirit.

15:12 Instead, they reckoned our life a plaything
and our existence a profitable fair;
for he says that one must make gain from whatever source, even evil.

15:13 For this man knows better than everyone that he is sinning,
when he crafts fragile vessels and carved images from earthy material.

The worship of what cannot live or act

15:14 But all the enemies of Thy people who oppressed them
are most foolish and more wretched than an infant’s soul,

15:15 because they also reckoned all the nations’ idols gods,
though these have neither use of eyes for sight,
nor nostrils for drawing in air,
nor ears to hear,
nor fingers of their hands to feel,
and their feet are idle for walking.

15:16 For a human being made them,
and one who had borrowed his spirit fashioned them;
for no human being can shape a god like himself.

15:17 Being mortal, he makes a dead thing with lawless hands;
for he is better than the objects he worships:
he at least has lived, but they never have.

15:18 They also worship the most hateful animals,
for compared in senselessness with the others, these are worse;

15:19 Nor, in their appearance as animals, are they beautiful enough to be desired,
and they have escaped both God’s praise and His blessing.

16:15 But it is impossible to escape Thy hand.

Heavenly food and grateful dependence

16:20 In contrast, Thou didst feed Thy people with angels’ food

it was transformed into whatever that person wished.

strains itself for punishment against the unrighteous

according to the desire of those in need,

16:26 so that Thy sons whom Thou didst love, Lord, might learn
that it is not the production of fruits that nourishes a human being,
but Thine utterance that preserves those who trust in Thee.

16:27 For what was not destroyed by fire
melted when merely warmed by a brief ray of the sun,

16:28 so that it might be known that one must rise before the sun to give Thee thanks
and appeal to Thee at the rising of the light.

16:29 For the hope of an ungrateful person will melt like wintry frost
and flow away like useless water.

Conscience and the failure of reason

Every sound becomes a terror

17:17 Whether a whistling wind,
or the melodious sound of birds among spreading branches,
or the rhythm of water rushing violently,
or the harsh crash of falling rocks,

or the voice of the fiercest roaring beasts,
or an echo rebounding from the hollows of mountains,
these terrified and paralyzed them.

17:19 For the whole world was illuminated by brilliant light
and occupied with unhindered works;

17:20 but over those alone a heavy night was spread,
an image of the darkness that was to receive them;
yet they were heavier to themselves than the darkness.

Light for God’s holy ones

18:5 When they planned to kill the infants of the holy ones,
and one child had been exposed and saved,
Thou didst take away their multitude of children as a rebuke
and destroy them together in violent water.

18:10 But the discordant cry of enemies echoed back,
and a pitiful sound spread abroad as children were mourned.

18:11 Slave and master were punished together by the same judgment,
and the commoner suffered the same things as the king;

18:12 all together, under one name of death,
they had countless dead;
for the living were not even sufficient to bury them,
since in a single moment their most honored offspring had been destroyed.

18:13 For though they disbelieved everything because of their sorceries,
at the destruction of the firstborn they acknowledged that the people were God’s son.

The all-powerful word descends

18:14 For while quiet silence surrounded all things
and night was midway through its swift course,

18:15 Thine all-powerful word leaped from heaven, from royal thrones,
a stern warrior, into the midst of the doomed land,
carrying Thine unfeigned command as a sharp sword;

18:16 standing there, it filled all things with death;
it touched heaven while standing upon the earth.

18:17 Then at once apparitions of dreadful dreams disturbed them,
and unexpected fears came upon them;

18:18 one here and another there, hurled down half-dead,
made known the reason why they were dying.

18:19 For the dreams that troubled them had announced this beforehand,
so that they would not perish without knowing why they suffered.

Prayer and incense stand between death and life

18:20 But a trial of death touched the righteous too,
and a breaking of the multitude occurred in the wilderness;
yet the wrath did not remain long.

18:21 For a blameless man hastened to fight for them,
bringing the weapon of his own ministry:
prayer and propitiation by incense.
He opposed the wrath and put an end to the calamity,
showing that he was Thy servant.

18:22 He conquered the anger, not by bodily strength,
not by the activity of weapons,
but by a word he subdued the punisher,
reminding him of the fathers’ oaths and covenants.

18:23 For when the dead had already fallen in heaps upon one another,
he stood between them and checked the wrath,
and cut off the path to the living.

18:24 For upon his ankle-length garment was the whole world,
and the glories of the fathers upon the engraving of four rows of stones,
and Thy majesty upon the diadem of his head.

18:25 To these the destroyer yielded, and these he feared;
for the trial of wrath alone was enough.

Wisdom of Solomon — Chapter 19

Read this chapter on staging

The pursuit and its end

19:1 But merciless fury came upon the ungodly to the end,
for He knew beforehand even what they would do,

19:2 that after permitting the people to depart
and sending them on their way with haste,
they would change their minds and pursue them.

19:3 For while their griefs were still at hand
and they were lamenting beside the graves of the dead,
they drew upon themselves another senseless plan,
and pursued as fugitives those whom they had begged to leave.

19:4 For the compulsion they deserved drew them toward this end
and cast upon them forgetfulness of what had happened,
so that they might fill up the punishment still lacking in their torments,

19:5 and Thy people might experience an astonishing journey,
while those others found an extraordinary death.

Creation serves the deliverance of God’s children

19:6 For the whole creation, in its own kind, was being fashioned anew from the beginning,
serving Thy commands,
so that Thy children might be guarded unharmed.

19:7 The cloud overshadowing the camp was seen,
and dry land rising from water that had existed before,
an unobstructed way out of the Red Sea,
and a grass-bearing plain out of the violent surge;

19:8 through it those sheltered by Thy hand passed as an entire nation,
having seen marvelous wonders.

19:9 For they grazed like horses
and leaped like lambs, praising Thee, Lord, who rescued them.

19:10 For they still remembered the things from their sojourn:
how, instead of producing animals, the earth brought forth gnats,
and instead of aquatic creatures the river disgorged a multitude of frogs.

19:11 Later they also saw a new production of birds,
when, urged on by desire, they asked for luxurious foods;

19:12 for as a consolation quail came up to them from the sea.

Hostility to strangers receives judgment

19:13 Punishments also came upon the sinners,
not without earlier signs in the violence of thunderbolts;
for they suffered justly for their own wickedness,
since they had practiced a harsher hatred of strangers.

19:14 For the former did not receive unfamiliar visitors when they came,
but these enslaved strangers who had benefited them.

19:15 And not only that: there will be a visitation upon the former,
since they received foreigners with hostility;

19:16 but the latter, after receiving with celebrations
those who had already shared in the same rights,
afflicted them with terrible labors.

19:17 They were also struck with blindness,
like those at the righteous man’s doors,
when, surrounded by yawning darkness,
each sought the passage through his own doors.

The elements change their operation

19:18 For the elements were being rearranged among themselves,
as notes on a harp change the pattern of the rhythm,
while always retaining their sound;
this can be inferred precisely from observing what happened.

19:19 For land creatures were changed into water creatures,
and swimming creatures moved onto land;

19:20 fire in water exceeded its own power,
and water forgot its nature of extinguishing;

19:21 flames, conversely, did not wither the flesh
of perishable animals walking among them,
nor the meltable, crystal-like kind of heavenly food that readily melts.

19:22 For in every way, Lord, Thou didst magnify and glorify Thy people,
and didst not disregard them, standing beside them at every time and place.
