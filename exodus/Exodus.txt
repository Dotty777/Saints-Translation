EXODUS 1

1:1 These are the names of the sons of Israel who entered Egypt with Jacob their father; each came with his whole household:
1:2 Reuben, Simeon, Levi, and Judah;
1:3 Issachar, Zebulun, and Benjamin;
1:4 Dan and Naphtali, Gad and Asher.
1:5 Now Joseph was in Egypt; and all the souls descended from Jacob were seventy-five.
1:6 And Joseph died, and all his brothers, and all that generation.
1:7 And the sons of Israel increased and multiplied, and became numerous and exceedingly, exceedingly strong; and the land multiplied them.
1:8 And another king arose over Egypt, who did not know Joseph.
1:9 And he said to his nation, “Behold, the people of the sons of Israel are a great multitude, and are stronger than we are.
1:10 Come, therefore, let us deal craftily with them, lest they multiply; and when war befalls us, they also join our adversaries, wage war against us, and leave the land.”
1:11 He set overseers of labour over them to afflict them with their work. They built fortified cities for Pharaoh: Pithom, Rameses, and On, which is the City of the Sun.
1:12 But the more they humbled them, the more they multiplied and grew exceedingly, exceedingly strong; and the Egyptians loathed the sons of Israel.
1:13 The Egyptians oppressed the sons of Israel by force.
1:14 They made their lives painful with hard labour in clay and brickmaking, and with all the work in the plains, in every task in which they enslaved them by force.
1:15 The king of the Egyptians spoke to the midwives of the Hebrews, one named Sepphora and the other Puah.
1:16 And he said, “When you attend the Hebrew women in childbirth, and they are about to give birth, if the child is male, kill him; but if the child is female, preserve her.”
1:17 But the midwives feared God. They did not do as the king of Egypt had commanded them, and they kept the boys alive.
1:18 And the king of Egypt called the midwives and said to them, “Why have you done this thing, and kept the male children alive?”
1:19 The midwives said to Pharaoh, “The Hebrew women are not like the Egyptian women, for they give birth before the midwives come to them.” And they bore children.
1:20 God dealt well with the midwives, and the people multiplied and became very strong.
1:21 And because the midwives feared God, they made houses for themselves.
1:22 And Pharaoh commanded all his people, saying, “Cast every male child born to the Hebrews into the river, but keep every female child alive.”

EXODUS 2

2:1 Now there was a man of the tribe of Levi, who took one of the daughters of Levi and had her as his wife.
2:2 She conceived and bore a male child. Seeing that he was beautiful, they hid him for three months.
2:3 When they could no longer hide him, his mother took a basket for him and coated it with bitumen-pitch. She put the child in it and placed it in the marsh beside the river.
2:4 His sister watched from a distance to learn what would happen to him.
2:5 Pharaoh’s daughter came down to bathe at the river while her attendants walked beside it. She saw the basket in the marsh, sent her attendant, and had it brought to her.
2:6 And she opened it, and saw a child weeping in the basket; and Pharaoh’s daughter had compassion on him, and said, “This is one of the Hebrew children.”
2:7 And his sister said to Pharaoh’s daughter, “Do you want me to call a nursing woman from among the Hebrews, that she may nurse the child for you?”
2:8 Pharaoh’s daughter said to her, “Go.” The young woman went and called the child’s mother.
2:9 And Pharaoh’s daughter said to her, “Keep this child for me, and nurse him for me, and I will give you your wages.” And the woman took the child and nursed him.
2:10 When the child grew, she brought him to Pharaoh’s daughter, and he became her son. She named him Moses, saying, “I took him up out of the water.”
2:11 During those many days, when Moses had grown, he went out to his brothers, the sons of Israel. Seeing their suffering, he saw an Egyptian man striking a Hebrew, one of his own brothers, the sons of Israel.
2:12 He looked this way and that, and, seeing no one, struck down the Egyptian and hid him in the sand.
2:13 And he went out on the second day, and saw two Hebrew men fighting; and he said to the one who did wrong, “Why do you strike your neighbour?”
2:14 But he said, “Who made you a ruler and judge over us? Do you want to kill me, as you killed the Egyptian yesterday?” And Moses was afraid, and said, “Has this matter become so plainly known?”
2:15 Pharaoh heard about this and sought to kill Moses. Moses withdrew from Pharaoh’s presence and settled in the land of Midian. Having come to the land of Midian, he sat down by the well.
2:16 The priest of Midian had seven daughters who tended the sheep of their father Jethro. They came and drew water until they filled the troughs to water the sheep of their father Jethro.
2:17 The shepherds came and drove them away, but Moses rose and rescued them, drew water for them, and watered their sheep.
2:18 And they came to Reuel their father, and he said to them, “Why have you returned so quickly today?”
2:19 They said, “An Egyptian man rescued us from the shepherds. He drew water for us and watered our sheep.”
2:20 And he said to his daughters, “And where is he? Why have you left the man behind? Call him, therefore, that he may eat bread.”
2:21 Moses settled with the man, who gave him his daughter Zipporah as his wife.
2:22 And the woman conceived and bore a son; and Moses called his name Gershom, saying, “I am a sojourner in a foreign land.”
2:23 After those many days the king of Egypt died. The sons of Israel groaned deeply because of their labour and cried out, and their cry because of their labour rose to God.
2:24 God heard their groaning, and God remembered His covenant with Abraham, Isaac, and Jacob.
2:25 God looked upon the sons of Israel, and He became known to them.

EXODUS 3

3:1 Moses was tending the sheep of Jethro his father-in-law, the priest of Midian. He led the sheep toward the wilderness and came to Mount Horeb.
3:2 An angel of the Lord appeared to him in a flame of fire from the bush. He saw that the bush was burning with fire, yet it was not consumed.
3:3 And Moses said, “I will turn aside and see this great sight, why the bush is not consumed.”
3:4 When the Lord saw that he was approaching to look, the Lord called to him from the bush, “Moses, Moses!” He replied, “What is it?”
3:5 And He said, “Do not come near here. Untie the footwear from your feet; for the place where you stand is holy ground.”
3:6 And He said to him, “I am the God of your father, the God of Abraham, and the God of Isaac, and the God of Jacob.” And Moses turned his face away, for he feared to look before God.
3:7 And the Lord said to Moses, “I have surely seen the affliction of My people in Egypt, and have heard their cry because of their taskmasters; for I know their pain.
3:8 I have come down to rescue them from the hand of the Egyptians and bring them out of that land into a good and spacious land, a land flowing with milk and honey, to the place of the Canaanites, Hittites, Amorites, Perizzites, Girgashites, Hivites, and Jebusites.
3:9 And now, behold, the cry of the sons of Israel has come to Me; and I have seen the oppression with which the Egyptians oppress them.
3:10 Come now, and I will send you to Pharaoh king of Egypt; and you shall bring My people, the sons of Israel, out of the land of Egypt.”
3:11 Moses said to God, “Who am I, that I should go to Pharaoh, king of Egypt, and bring the sons of Israel out of the land of Egypt?”
3:12 And God said to Moses, “I will be with you; and this shall be the sign to you that I send you: when you have brought My people out of Egypt, you shall serve God upon this mountain.”
3:13 And Moses said to God, “Behold, I shall go to the sons of Israel and say to them, ‘The God of your fathers has sent me to you’; and they shall ask me, ‘What is His name?’ What shall I say to them?”
3:14 And God said to Moses, “I am the One Who Is.” And He said, “This is what you shall say to the sons of Israel: ‘The One Who Is has sent me to you.’”
3:15 And God said again to Moses, “This is what you shall say to the sons of Israel: ‘The Lord, the God of your fathers, the God of Abraham, and the God of Isaac, and the God of Jacob, has sent me to you.’ This is My everlasting name, and My memorial from generation to generation.
3:16 Go, therefore, gather the council of elders of the sons of Israel, and say to them, ‘The Lord, the God of your fathers, the God of Abraham, and the God of Isaac, and the God of Jacob, has appeared to me, saying, “I have surely visited you, and seen all that has befallen you in Egypt.
3:17 And I have said, I will bring you up out of the affliction of the Egyptians, into the land of the Canaanites, and Hittites, and Amorites, and Perizzites, and Girgashites, and Hivites, and Jebusites, into a land flowing with milk and honey.”’
3:18 And they shall listen to your voice; and you and the council of elders of Israel shall go in to Pharaoh king of Egypt, and you shall say to him, ‘The God of the Hebrews has called us. Let us therefore go a journey of three days into the wilderness, that we may sacrifice to our God.’
3:19 I know that Pharaoh, king of Egypt, will not let you go unless by a strong hand.
3:20 I will stretch out My hand and strike the Egyptians with all My wonders that I will perform among them. Afterward he will send you away.
3:21 And I will give this people favour before the Egyptians; and when you depart, you shall not go empty-handed.
3:22 Every woman shall ask of her neighbour and of her fellow lodger articles of silver and gold, and clothing; and you shall put them upon your sons and upon your daughters, and you shall plunder the Egyptians.”

EXODUS 4

4:1 And Moses answered and said, “If they do not believe me or listen to my voice, for they will say, ‘God has not appeared to you,’ what shall I say to them?”
4:2 And the Lord said to him, “What is this in your hand?” And he said, “A staff.”
4:3 He said, “Throw it on the ground.” He threw it on the ground, and it became a serpent; Moses fled from it.
4:4 And the Lord said to Moses, “Stretch out your hand, and take it by the tail.” And he stretched out his hand, and took it by the tail; and it became a staff in his hand.
4:5 “That they may believe you, that the Lord, the God of their fathers, the God of Abraham, and the God of Isaac, and the God of Jacob, has appeared to you.”
4:6 And the Lord said again to him, “Put your hand into your bosom.” And he put his hand into his bosom, and brought his hand out of his bosom; and his hand became as snow.
4:7 And He said, “Put your hand again into your bosom.” And he put his hand into his bosom, and brought it out of his bosom; and it was restored again to the colour of his flesh.
4:8 “And if they do not believe you or listen to the voice of the first sign, they shall believe you through the voice of the latter sign.
4:9 And if they do not believe you through these two signs or listen to your voice, you shall take some water from the river and pour it upon the dry ground; and the water which you take from the river shall become blood upon the dry ground.”
4:10 And Moses said to the Lord, “I beseech Thee, Lord, I have not been capable—not yesterday, nor the day before, nor since Thou didst begin to speak to Thy servant; for I am weak of voice and slow of tongue.”
4:11 The Lord said to Moses, “Who gave a human being a mouth? Who made one hard of hearing or deaf, seeing or blind? Is it not I, God?
4:12 Now therefore go, and I will open your mouth, and teach you what you are to speak.”
4:13 And Moses said, “I beseech Thee, Lord, appoint another who is able, whom Thou wilt send.”
4:14 And the Lord was kindled with anger against Moses, and said, “Behold, is Aaron the Levite not your brother? I know that he will surely speak to you; and behold, he shall come forth to meet you, and when he sees you, he shall rejoice within himself.
4:15 And you shall speak to him, and put My words in his mouth; and I will open your mouth and his mouth, and teach you what you shall do.
4:16 And he shall speak for you to the people, and he shall be your mouth; but you shall be for him in matters pertaining to God.
4:17 And you shall take in your hand this staff which was turned into a serpent, with which you shall perform the signs.”
4:18 Moses went back to Jethro his father-in-law and said, “I will return to my brothers in Egypt to see whether they are still alive.” Jethro said to Moses, “Go in health.”
4:19 And after those many days the king of Egypt died. And the Lord said to Moses in Midian, “Go, depart into Egypt; for all who sought your life are dead.”
4:20 Moses took his wife and children, set them on the beasts of burden, and returned toward Egypt. Moses took the staff he had from God in his hand.
4:21 And the Lord said to Moses, “As you go and return to Egypt, see that you perform before Pharaoh all the wonders which I have put in your hands. But I will harden his heart, and he shall by no means let the people go.
4:22 And you shall say to Pharaoh, ‘Thus says the Lord: Israel is My firstborn son.
4:23 And I said to you, “Let My people go, that they may serve Me.” If therefore you will not let them go, behold, I will kill your firstborn son.’”
4:24 On the way, at the lodging place, an angel of the Lord met him and sought to kill him.
4:25 And Zipporah took a stone, and circumcised the foreskin of her son, and fell at the feet, and said, “The blood of my child’s circumcision has ceased.”
4:26 And he departed from him, because she said, “The blood of my child’s circumcision has ceased.”
4:27 The Lord said to Aaron, “Go into the wilderness to meet Moses.” He went and met him at the mountain of God, and they kissed each other.
4:28 Moses told Aaron all the words of the Lord with which He had sent him, and all the signs He had commanded him.
4:29 Moses and Aaron went and gathered the council of elders of the sons of Israel.
4:30 Aaron spoke all these words God had spoken to Moses and performed the signs before the people.
4:31 And the people believed and rejoiced, because God had visited the sons of Israel, and because He had seen their affliction; and the people bowed down and worshipped.

EXODUS 5

5:1 Afterward Moses and Aaron went in to Pharaoh and said, “Thus says the Lord, the God of Israel: ‘Let My people go so that they may hold a feast for Me in the wilderness.’”
5:2 Pharaoh said, “Who is He, that I should obey His voice and let the sons of Israel go? I do not know the Lord, and I will not let Israel go.”
5:3 They said to him, “The God of the Hebrews has called us. We will go a three days’ journey into the wilderness to sacrifice to our God, lest death or slaughter meet us.”
5:4 And the king of Egypt said to them, “Moses and Aaron, why do you turn my people from their works? Each of you, go back to your work.”
5:5 And Pharaoh said, “Behold, the people are now numerous; let us not therefore give them rest from their works.”
5:6 Pharaoh commanded the people’s taskmasters and scribes:
5:7 “The people shall no longer be given straw for making bricks as before. Let them go and gather straw for themselves.
5:8 And you shall lay upon them the appointed number of bricks which they make each day; you shall not reduce it at all. For they are idle; therefore they cry out, saying, ‘Let us go and sacrifice to our God.’
5:9 Let these men’s tasks be heavier. Let them attend to them and pay no attention to empty words.”
5:10 The taskmasters and scribes pressed them and said to the people, “Thus says Pharaoh: ‘I will no longer give you straw.
5:11 Go yourselves, and gather straw for yourselves wherever you may find it; for your appointed number shall not be reduced.’”
5:12 The people scattered throughout the land of Egypt to gather stubble for straw.
5:13 The taskmasters pressed them, saying, “Complete the work due each day, as when straw was provided for you.”
5:14 And the scribes of the people of the sons of Israel, whom Pharaoh’s overseers had set over them, were scourged, and asked, “Why have you not completed your appointed number of bricks, as before, including that of today?”
5:15 And the scribes of the sons of Israel came in and cried to Pharaoh, saying, “Why do you treat your household slaves this way?
5:16 Straw is not given to your household slaves, yet they tell us to make bricks; and behold, your servants have been scourged. In this way you will wrong your own people.”
5:17 And he said to them, “You are idle; you are idlers! Therefore you say, ‘Let us go and sacrifice to our God.’
5:18 Now therefore go and work; for straw shall not be given to you, yet you shall deliver the appointed number of bricks.”
5:19 And the scribes of the sons of Israel saw themselves in trouble, saying, “You shall not reduce the number of bricks due each day.”
5:20 As they came out from Pharaoh, they met Moses and Aaron, who were coming to meet them.
5:21 And they said to them, “May God look upon you and judge, for you have made our smell loathsome before Pharaoh and before his attendants, putting a sword into his hands to kill us.”
5:22 And Moses returned to the Lord and said, “Lord, why hast Thou afflicted this people? And why hast Thou sent me?
5:23 For since I went to Pharaoh to speak in Thy name, he has afflicted this people; and Thou hast not delivered Thy people.”

EXODUS 6

6:1 And the Lord said to Moses, “Now you shall see what I will do to Pharaoh; for with a mighty hand he shall let them go, and with an uplifted arm he shall drive them out of his land.”
6:2 God spoke to Moses and said to him, “I am the Lord.
6:3 I appeared to Abraham, Isaac, and Jacob as their God, but I did not reveal My name Lord to them.
6:4 I established My covenant with them, to give them the land of the Canaanites, the land where they sojourned and lived as foreigners.
6:5 I have heard the groaning of the sons of Israel, whom the Egyptians enslave, and I have remembered your covenant.
6:6 Go, say to the sons of Israel: ‘I am the Lord. I will bring you out from the Egyptians’ domination and deliver you from slavery. I will ransom you with an uplifted arm and with great judgment.
6:7 And I will take you to Myself for a people, and I will be your God; and you shall know that I am the Lord your God, who brought you out from the oppression of the Egyptians.
6:8 I will bring you into the land concerning which I stretched out My hand to give it to Abraham, Isaac, and Jacob. I will give it to you as an inheritance. I am the Lord.’”
6:9 Moses spoke thus to the sons of Israel, but they did not listen to him because of their discouragement and hard labour.
6:10 The Lord spoke to Moses, saying,
6:11 “Go in and speak to Pharaoh, king of Egypt, so that he will let the sons of Israel leave his land.”
6:12 And Moses spoke before the Lord, saying, “Behold, the sons of Israel have not listened to me; how then shall Pharaoh listen to me? And I am without eloquence.”
6:13 The Lord spoke to Moses and Aaron and charged them concerning Pharaoh, king of Egypt, so that the sons of Israel would be sent out of the land of Egypt.
6:14 These are the heads of their ancestral houses. The sons of Reuben, Israel’s firstborn: Hanoch, Pallu, Hezron, and Carmi. This is the kindred of Reuben.
6:15 The sons of Simeon: Jemuel, Jamin, Ohad, Jachin, Zohar, and Shaul, the son of a Phoenician woman. These are the families of the sons of Simeon.
6:16 These are the names of the sons of Levi according to their kindreds: Gershon, Kohath, and Merari. Levi lived one hundred and thirty-seven years.
6:17 These are the sons of Gershon: Libni and Shimei, the houses of their family.
6:18 The sons of Kohath: Amram, Izhar, Hebron, and Uzziel. Kohath lived one hundred and thirty years.
6:19 The sons of Merari: Mahli and Mushi. These are the ancestral houses of Levi according to their kindreds.
6:20 Amram took Jochebed, the daughter of his father’s brother, as his wife, and she bore him Aaron, Moses, and their sister Miriam. Amram lived one hundred and thirty-two years.
6:21 The sons of Izhar: Korah, Nepheg, and Zichri.
6:22 The sons of Uzziel: Elzaphan and Sithri.
6:23 Aaron took Elisheba, daughter of Amminadab and sister of Nahshon, as his wife. She bore him Nadab, Abihu, Eleazar, and Ithamar.
6:24 The sons of Korah: Assir, Elkanah, and Abiasaph. These are the generations of Korah.
6:25 Eleazar son of Aaron took one of Putiel’s daughters as his wife, and she bore him Phinehas. These are the heads of the Levites’ family according to their generations.
6:26 These are Aaron and Moses whom God told to bring the sons of Israel out of the land of Egypt with their forces.
6:27 They were the ones who spoke to Pharaoh, king of Egypt, and brought the sons of Israel out of the land of Egypt: Aaron himself and Moses.
6:28 On the day when the Lord spoke to Moses in the land of Egypt,
6:29 the Lord spoke to Moses, saying, “I am the Lord. Speak to Pharaoh king of Egypt all that I say to you.”
6:30 And Moses said before the Lord, “Behold, I am weak of voice; how then shall Pharaoh listen to me?”

EXODUS 7

7:1 And the Lord said to Moses, “Behold, I have made you a god to Pharaoh; and Aaron your brother shall be your prophet.
7:2 You shall speak to him all that I command you; and Aaron your brother shall speak to Pharaoh, that he may let the sons of Israel go out of his land.
7:3 I will harden Pharaoh’s heart and multiply My signs and My wonders in the land of Egypt.
7:4 Pharaoh will not listen to you. I will lay My hand on Egypt and bring out My people, the sons of Israel, from the land of Egypt with My power and great vengeance.
7:5 All the Egyptians shall know that I am the Lord when I stretch out My hand over Egypt and bring the sons of Israel out from among them.”
7:6 Moses and Aaron did as the Lord commanded them; so they did.
7:7 Moses was eighty years old and his brother Aaron eighty-three when he spoke to Pharaoh.
7:8 The Lord spoke to Moses and Aaron, saying,
7:9 “If Pharaoh speaks to you, saying, ‘Give us a sign or a wonder,’ you shall say to Aaron your brother, ‘Take the staff, and cast it upon the ground before Pharaoh and before his attendants, and it shall become a dragon.’”
7:10 Moses and Aaron went before Pharaoh and his attendants and did as the Lord commanded. Aaron threw his staff before Pharaoh and his attendants, and it became a dragon.
7:11 Pharaoh summoned the wise men of Egypt and the sorcerers. The Egyptian enchanters did the same by their sorceries.
7:12 Each threw down his staff, and they became dragons. But Aaron’s staff swallowed their staffs.
7:13 Pharaoh’s heart grew strong, and he did not listen to them, as the Lord had said to them.
7:14 The Lord said to Moses, “Pharaoh’s heart has become heavy, so that he will not let the people go.
7:15 Go to Pharaoh in the morning; behold, he goes out to the water. And you shall stand to meet him upon the bank of the river, and take in your hand the staff which was turned into a serpent.
7:16 And you shall say to him, ‘The Lord, the God of the Hebrews, has sent me to you, saying, “Let My people go, that they may serve Me in the wilderness.” And behold, until now you have not listened.
7:17 Thus says the Lord: “By this you shall know that I am the Lord: behold, I strike with the staff in My hand upon the water in the river, and it shall be turned to blood.
7:18 The fish in the river will die, the river will stink, and the Egyptians will be unable to drink its water.”’”
7:19 And the Lord said to Moses, “Say to Aaron your brother, ‘Take your staff, and stretch out your hand over the waters of Egypt, over their rivers, and over their canals, and over their marshes, and over all their standing water, and it shall become blood.’” And there was blood throughout all the land of Egypt, both in wood and in stone.
7:20 Moses and Aaron did as the Lord commanded. He raised his staff and struck the river’s water before Pharaoh and his attendants, and all the water in the river turned to blood.
7:21 The fish in the river died, and the river stank. The Egyptians could not drink its water, and there was blood throughout the land of Egypt.
7:22 The Egyptian enchanters did the same by their sorceries. Pharaoh’s heart grew hard, and he did not listen to them, as the Lord had said.
7:23 Pharaoh turned and went into his house and did not set his mind even on this.
7:24 All the Egyptians dug around the river to drink water, and they could not drink water from the river.
7:25 Seven days passed after the Lord struck the river.
7:26 And the Lord said to Moses, “Go in to Pharaoh, and you shall say to him, ‘Thus says the Lord: Let My people go, that they may serve Me.
7:27 But if you will not let them go, behold, I will strike all your borders with frogs.
7:28 And the river shall pour forth frogs; and they shall come up into your houses, and into your bedchambers, and upon your beds, and into the houses of your attendants and your people, and into your dough and your ovens.
7:29 And the frogs shall come up upon you, and upon your attendants, and upon your people.’”

EXODUS 8

8:1 And the Lord said to Moses, “Say to Aaron your brother, ‘Stretch out the staff in your hand over the rivers, and over the canals, and over the marshes, and bring up the frogs.’”
8:2 Aaron stretched out his hand over Egypt’s waters and brought up the frogs. The frogs were brought up and covered the land of Egypt.
8:3 The Egyptian enchanters did the same by their sorceries and brought frogs up over the land of Egypt.
8:4 Pharaoh called Moses and Aaron and said, “Pray to the Lord for me, and let Him remove the frogs from me and my people. I will let them go so that they may sacrifice to the Lord.”
8:5 And Moses said to Pharaoh, “Appoint a time for me to pray for you, and for your attendants, and for your people, to destroy the frogs from you and from your people and from your houses; only in the river shall they remain.”
8:6 And he said, “Tomorrow.” And he answered, “According to your word; that you may know that there is no other except the Lord.
8:7 And the frogs shall be removed from you, and from your houses, and from the farmsteads, and from your attendants, and from your people; only in the river shall they remain.”
8:8 Moses and Aaron went out from Pharaoh. Moses cried to the Lord concerning the appointed limit for the frogs, as Pharaoh had set it.
8:9 The Lord did as Moses said. The frogs died in the houses, farmsteads, and fields.
8:10 They gathered them in heaps upon heaps, and the land stank.
8:11 When Pharaoh saw that there was relief, his heart became heavy and he did not listen to them, as the Lord had said.
8:12 And the Lord said to Moses, “Say to Aaron, ‘Stretch out the staff in your hand, and strike the dust of the earth; and there shall be gnats upon men and upon four-footed animals, and throughout all the land of Egypt.’”
8:13 Aaron stretched out the staff in his hand and struck the earth’s dust, and gnats came upon people and four-footed animals. Gnats appeared in all the earth’s dust throughout the land of Egypt.
8:14 The enchanters tried by their sorceries to bring forth gnats, but they could not. The gnats were on people and four-footed animals.
8:15 The enchanters said to Pharaoh, “This is the finger of God.” But Pharaoh’s heart was hardened, and he did not listen to them, as the Lord had said.
8:16 And the Lord said to Moses, “Rise early in the morning, and stand before Pharaoh; and behold, he shall go out to the water. And you shall say to him, ‘Thus says the Lord: Let My people go, that they may serve Me in the wilderness.
8:17 But if you will not let My people go, behold, I will send dog-flies upon you, and upon your attendants, and upon your people, and upon your houses; and the houses of the Egyptians shall be filled with dog-flies, and also the ground on which they stand.
8:18 And in that day I will marvellously distinguish the land of Goshen, in which My people dwell, so that no dog-flies shall be there; that you may know that I am the Lord, the Lord of all the earth.
8:19 And I will put a distinction between My people and your people; tomorrow this sign shall be upon the land.’”
8:20 The Lord did so. A multitude of dog-flies came into Pharaoh’s houses, his attendants’ houses, and all the land of Egypt. The land was ruined by the dog-flies.
8:21 Pharaoh called Moses and Aaron and said, “Go, sacrifice to your God within the land.”
8:22 Moses said, “This cannot be done, for we would sacrifice to the Lord our God what the Egyptians regard as abominations. If we sacrifice these before their eyes, we shall be stoned.
8:23 We will go a three days’ journey into the wilderness and sacrifice to the Lord our God, as He told us.”
8:24 And Pharaoh said, “I will let you go, and you shall sacrifice to the Lord your God in the wilderness; only you shall not go far away. Pray therefore to the Lord for me.”
8:25 And Moses said, “Behold, I will go out from you and pray to God; and tomorrow the dog-flies shall depart from you, and from your attendants, and from your people. Only do not deceive again, Pharaoh, by refusing to let the people go to sacrifice to the Lord.”
8:26 Moses went out from Pharaoh and prayed to God.
8:27 The Lord did as Moses said and removed the dog-flies from Pharaoh, his attendants, and his people. Not one remained.
8:28 Pharaoh made his heart heavy this time also and was unwilling to let the people go.

EXODUS 9

9:1 And the Lord said to Moses, “Go in to Pharaoh, and you shall say to him, ‘Thus says the Lord, the God of the Hebrews: Let My people go, that they may serve Me.
9:2 But if you will not let My people go, and still hold them,
9:3 behold, the hand of the Lord shall be upon your livestock in the plains, upon the horses, and upon the beasts of burden, and upon the camels, and upon the cattle, and upon the sheep: an exceedingly great death.
9:4 At that time I will make a marvellous distinction between the Egyptians’ livestock and the sons of Israel’s livestock. Nothing belonging to the sons of Israel shall die.’”
9:5 God appointed a time, saying, “Tomorrow the Lord will do this in the land.”
9:6 The Lord did this the next day. All the Egyptians’ livestock died, but none of the sons of Israel’s livestock died.
9:7 Pharaoh saw that none of the sons of Israel’s livestock had died. Yet Pharaoh’s heart became heavy, and he did not let the people go.
9:8 The Lord said to Moses and Aaron, “Take handfuls of soot from a furnace, and let Moses scatter it toward heaven before Pharaoh and his attendants.
9:9 Let it become fine dust over all the land of Egypt. Boils, with blisters breaking out, shall come upon people and four-footed animals, on people and four-footed animals throughout Egypt.”
9:10 He took soot from the furnace before Pharaoh. Moses scattered it toward heaven, and boils with blisters broke out on people and four-footed animals.
9:11 The sorcerers could not stand before Moses because of the boils, for the boils were on the sorcerers and throughout the land of Egypt.
9:12 The Lord hardened Pharaoh’s heart, and he did not listen to them, as the Lord had commanded.
9:13 And the Lord said to Moses, “Rise early in the morning, and stand before Pharaoh, and you shall say to him, ‘Thus says the Lord, the God of the Hebrews: Let My people go, that they may serve Me.
9:14 For at this time I will send all My blows upon your heart, and upon your attendants and your people, that you may know that there is none other like Me in all the earth.
9:15 For now I will stretch out My hand and strike you and your people with death, and you shall be blotted out from the earth.
9:16 And for this reason you were preserved, that I might show My strength in you, and that My name might be proclaimed throughout all the earth.
9:17 Do you still act against My people, refusing to let them go?
9:18 At this time tomorrow I will rain down very abundant hail, such as has never been in Egypt from the day it was created until today.
9:19 Now therefore hurry to gather your livestock, and all that you have in the plain; for all the men and livestock found in the plain, who have not entered a house, shall die when the hail falls upon them.’”
9:20 He among Pharaoh’s attendants who feared the word of the Lord gathered his livestock into the houses.
9:21 But he who gave no heed in his mind to the word of the Lord left the livestock in the plains.
9:22 And the Lord said to Moses, “Stretch out your hand toward heaven, and there shall be hail throughout all the land of Egypt, upon men, and upon livestock, and upon every plant upon the earth.”
9:23 Moses stretched out his hand toward heaven. The Lord sent thunder and hail, and fire ran over the earth. The Lord rained hail upon all the land of Egypt.
9:24 There was hail, with fire flaming amid the hail. The hail was very, very abundant, such as had never been in Egypt since a nation came to be there.
9:25 Throughout Egypt the hail struck people and livestock. It struck every plant in the plains and shattered all the trees there.
9:26 Only in the land of Goshen, where the sons of Israel were, was there no hail.
9:27 Pharaoh sent for Moses and Aaron and said to them, “This time I have sinned. The Lord is righteous, and I and my people are ungodly.
9:28 Pray therefore to the Lord for me, and let the thunder of God, and the hail, and the fire cease; and I will let you go, and you shall remain no longer.”
9:29 And Moses said to him, “When I go out of the city, I will spread out my hands to the Lord; and the thunder shall cease, and there shall be no more hail or rain, that you may know that the earth is the Lord’s.
9:30 But as for you and your attendants, I know that you have not yet feared the Lord.”
9:31 The flax and barley were struck down, for the barley was standing ready and the flax was setting seed.
9:32 The wheat and spelt were not struck down, for they were late crops.
9:33 Moses left Pharaoh and went out of the city. He spread out his hands to the Lord, and the thunder and hail ceased. Rain no longer fell upon the earth.
9:34 When Pharaoh saw that the rain, hail, and thunder had ceased, he sinned again and made his heart and his attendants’ hearts heavy.
9:35 Pharaoh’s heart was hardened, and he did not let the sons of Israel go, as the Lord had spoken to Moses.

EXODUS 10

10:1 The Lord said to Moses, “Go to Pharaoh, for I have hardened his heart and his attendants’ hearts, so that these signs may come upon them in succession,
10:2 that you may tell in the ears of your children, and of your children’s children, how I have mocked the Egyptians, and My signs which I have performed among them; and you shall know that I am the Lord.”
10:3 And Moses and Aaron went in before Pharaoh, and said to him, “Thus says the Lord, the God of the Hebrews: How long will you refuse to reverence Me? Let My people go, that they may serve Me.
10:4 But if you will not let My people go, behold, at this hour tomorrow I will bring many locusts upon all your borders.
10:5 And they shall cover the face of the land, and you shall not be able to see the land; and they shall devour all that remains of the land which the hail left to you, and every tree growing for you upon the land.
10:6 And your houses shall be filled, and the houses of your attendants, and all the houses throughout the land of the Egyptians, as neither your fathers nor their forefathers have ever seen, from the day they came upon the earth to this day.” And Moses turned and went out from Pharaoh.
10:7 And Pharaoh’s attendants said to him, “How long shall this be a hindrance to us? Let the men go, that they may serve their God. Or do you want to know that Egypt is destroyed?”
10:8 Moses and Aaron were brought back to Pharaoh. He said to them, “Go, serve your God. But who are those who will go?”
10:9 Moses said, “We will go with our young men and our elders, with our sons and our daughters, and with our sheep and our cattle, for it is a feast of the Lord our God.”
10:10 And he said to them, “So be it, the Lord be with you! When I let you go, shall I also let your households go? Behold, evil lies before you.
10:11 Not so; let the men go, and serve God, for this is what you seek.” And they drove them out from Pharaoh’s presence.
10:12 And the Lord said to Moses, “Stretch out your hand over the land of Egypt, and let locusts come up upon the land; and they shall devour every plant of the land, and all the fruit of the trees which the hail has left.”
10:13 Moses raised his staff toward heaven, and the Lord brought a south wind upon the land all that day and all that night. When morning came, the south wind carried up the locusts.
10:14 It brought them up over all the land of Egypt, and they settled throughout Egypt’s territory in immense numbers. There had never been such locusts before, nor would there be afterward.
10:15 They covered the surface of the land, and the land was ruined. They devoured all the vegetation of the land and all the fruit of the trees that the hail had left. Nothing green remained on the trees or among the vegetation of the field throughout the land of Egypt.
10:16 Pharaoh hurried to call Moses and Aaron and said, “I have sinned before the Lord your God and against you.
10:17 Forgive my sin this once more, and pray to the Lord your God to remove this death from me.”
10:18 Moses went out from Pharaoh and prayed to God.
10:19 The Lord turned a very strong wind from the sea. It lifted the locusts and cast them into the Red Sea. Not one locust remained in all Egypt’s territory.
10:20 The Lord hardened Pharaoh’s heart, and he did not let the sons of Israel go.
10:21 And the Lord said to Moses, “Stretch out your hand toward heaven, and let there be darkness upon the land of Egypt, darkness that may be felt.”
10:22 Moses stretched out his hand toward heaven, and there was darkness, gloom, and a tempest throughout the land of Egypt for three days.
10:23 No one saw his brother for three days, and no one rose from his bed for three days. But all the sons of Israel had light everywhere they lived.
10:24 Pharaoh called Moses and Aaron and said, “Go, serve the Lord your God. Only leave your sheep and your cattle behind. Let your household go with you.”
10:25 And Moses said, “You shall also give us whole burnt offerings and sacrifices, which we shall offer to the Lord our God.
10:26 Our livestock must also go with us. We will not leave a hoof behind, for we must take some of them to serve the Lord our God. We will not know with what we must serve the Lord our God until we arrive there.”
10:27 The Lord hardened Pharaoh’s heart, and he was unwilling to let them go.
10:28 And Pharaoh said, “Depart from me! Take care not to see my face again; for on the day you appear before me, you shall die.”
10:29 And Moses said, “You have spoken; I will appear before your face no more.”

EXODUS 11

11:1 The Lord said to Moses, “I will bring one more plague upon Pharaoh and Egypt. Afterward he will let you go from here. When he lets you go, he will drive you out altogether, with everything.
11:2 Therefore speak secretly in the people’s hearing, and let each man ask his neighbour and each woman her neighbour for articles of silver and gold and for clothing.”
11:3 The Lord gave His people favour in the Egyptians’ sight, and they lent to them. The man Moses became very great in the sight of the Egyptians, Pharaoh, and all his attendants.
11:4 Moses said, “Thus says the Lord: About midnight I will enter the midst of Egypt.
11:5 And every firstborn in the land of Egypt shall die, from the firstborn of Pharaoh who sits upon the throne, to the firstborn of the female slave beside the mill, and to the firstborn of every beast.
11:6 There shall be a great cry throughout the land of Egypt, such as there has never been and never will be again.
11:7 But among all the sons of Israel, from man to beast, not a dog shall growl with its tongue; that you may know how marvellously the Lord distinguishes between the Egyptians and Israel.
11:8 And all these servants of yours shall come down to me, and bow before me, saying, ‘Go out, you and all your people whom you lead.’ And after this I will go out.” And Moses departed from Pharaoh in anger.
11:9 The Lord said to Moses, “Pharaoh will not listen to you, so that I may greatly multiply My signs and wonders in the land of Egypt.”
11:10 Moses and Aaron performed all these signs and wonders in the land of Egypt before Pharaoh. The Lord hardened Pharaoh’s heart, and he was unwilling to send the sons of Israel out of the land of Egypt.

EXODUS 12

12:1 The Lord spoke to Moses and Aaron in the land of Egypt, saying,
12:2 “This month shall be the beginning of months for you; it shall be the first month of the year for you.
12:3 Speak to the whole congregation of the sons of Israel, saying: On the tenth day of this month, each shall take a sheep for the ancestral households, one sheep for each household.
12:4 If those in the house are too few to be sufficient for a sheep, he shall join with his nearest neighbour. According to the number of persons, each person’s sufficient portion shall be reckoned for the sheep.
12:5 Your sheep shall be without blemish, a male a year old; you shall take it from the lambs and from the young goats.
12:6 And you shall keep it until the fourteenth day of this month; and the whole multitude of the congregation of the sons of Israel shall slaughter it toward evening.
12:7 They shall take some of the blood and put it on the two doorposts and the lintel of the houses in which they eat them.
12:8 They shall eat the meat that night, roasted over fire, and eat unleavened bread with bitter herbs.
12:9 You shall eat none of it raw, nor boiled in water, but roasted with fire, its head together with its feet and inward parts.
12:10 You shall leave none of it until morning, and you shall not break a bone of it. Whatever remains until morning you shall burn with fire.
12:11 And this is how you shall eat it: your loins girded, your sandals upon your feet, and your staffs in your hands; and you shall eat it in haste. It is a Passover to the Lord.
12:12 I will go through the land of Egypt that night and strike every firstborn in the land of Egypt, both human and animal. Against all the Egyptians’ gods I will execute vengeance. I am the Lord.
12:13 And the blood shall be a sign to you upon the houses in which you are; and I will see the blood, and shelter you; and no plague of destruction shall be among you when I strike the land of Egypt.
12:14 This day shall be a memorial for you. You shall celebrate it as a feast to the Lord throughout all your generations; you shall celebrate it as a perpetual statute.
12:15 For seven days you shall eat unleavened bread, and from the first day you shall remove leaven out of your houses; whoever eats leaven, from the first day to the seventh day, that soul shall be destroyed from Israel.
12:16 The first day shall be called holy, and the seventh day shall be a holy appointed day for you. You shall do no servile work on them, except what must be done for each person; that alone may be done for you.
12:17 You shall keep this commandment, for on this day I will bring your host out of the land of Egypt. You shall observe this day throughout your generations as a perpetual statute.
12:18 Beginning on the fourteenth day of the first month, from evening until the evening of the twenty-first day of the month, you shall eat unleavened bread.
12:19 For seven days no leaven shall be found in your houses; whoever eats anything leavened, that soul shall be destroyed from the congregation of Israel, whether a resident foreigner or a native of the land.
12:20 You shall eat nothing leavened. In all your dwellings you shall eat unleavened bread.”
12:21 Moses called the whole council of elders of the sons of Israel and said to them, “Go and take a sheep for your families, and slaughter the Passover.
12:22 Take a bunch of hyssop, dip it in the blood beside the door, and touch the lintel and both doorposts with the blood beside the door. None of you shall go out of the door of his house until morning.
12:23 The Lord will pass through to strike the Egyptians. He will see the blood on the lintel and both doorposts, and the Lord will pass by the doorway. He will not allow the destroyer to enter your houses to strike.
12:24 And you shall keep this word as a statute for yourself and for your sons for ever.
12:25 And when you enter into the land which the Lord shall give to you, as He has spoken, you shall keep this service.
12:26 When your sons say to you, ‘What is this service?’
12:27 you shall say to them, ‘This Passover is a sacrifice to the Lord, because He sheltered the houses of the sons of Israel in Egypt when He struck the Egyptians and delivered our houses.’” The people bowed down and worshipped.
12:28 The sons of Israel went and did so. As the Lord had commanded Moses and Aaron, so they did.
12:29 At midnight the Lord struck every firstborn in the land of Egypt, from the firstborn of Pharaoh who sat on the throne to the firstborn of the captive woman in the pit, and the firstborn of every animal of the livestock.
12:30 Pharaoh rose during the night, with all his attendants and all the Egyptians. There was a great cry throughout the land of Egypt, for there was no house without someone dead.
12:31 And Pharaoh called Moses and Aaron by night, and said to them, “Rise and depart from among my people, both you and the sons of Israel; go, serve the Lord your God, as you say.
12:32 Take your sheep and your cattle, and go. Bless me also.”
12:33 The Egyptians pressed the people hard to drive them quickly out of the land, for they said, “We are all dying!”
12:34 The people took their dough before it was leavened, with their kneaded batches wrapped in their garments on their shoulders.
12:35 The sons of Israel did as Moses had instructed them. They asked the Egyptians for articles of silver and gold and for clothing.
12:36 The Lord gave His people favour in the Egyptians’ sight, and they lent to them. Thus they plundered the Egyptians.
12:37 The sons of Israel departed from Rameses for Succoth, about six hundred thousand men on foot, apart from the household company.
12:38 A great mixed multitude also went up with them, together with sheep and cattle and livestock in great abundance.
12:39 They baked the dough they had brought from Egypt into unleavened hearth-cakes, because it was not leavened. The Egyptians had driven them out, and they could not stay; nor had they prepared provisions for the journey.
12:40 The time the sons of Israel lived in the land of Egypt and in the land of Canaan was four hundred and thirty years.
12:41 After the four hundred and thirty years, all the Lord’s host went out from the land of Egypt.
12:42 It is a night vigil for the Lord, to bring them out of the land of Egypt. This same night is a vigil for the Lord for all the sons of Israel throughout their generations.
12:43 The Lord said to Moses and Aaron, “This is the law of the Passover: no foreigner shall eat of it.
12:44 And every man’s household slave, or one bought with silver, you shall circumcise; and then he shall eat of it.
12:45 A sojourner or a hired worker shall not eat of it.
12:46 It shall be eaten in one house. You shall not take any of the meat outside the house, and you shall not break a bone of it.
12:47 The whole congregation of the sons of Israel shall observe it.
12:48 But if a resident foreigner comes to you to keep the Passover to the Lord, you shall circumcise every male belonging to him; and then he shall draw near to keep it, and shall be as the native of the land. No uncircumcised person shall eat of it.
12:49 There shall be one law for the native, and for the resident foreigner who has come among you.”
12:50 The sons of Israel did so. As the Lord had commanded Moses and Aaron concerning them, so they did.
12:51 On that day the Lord brought the sons of Israel out of the land of Egypt with their host.

EXODUS 13

13:1 The Lord spoke to Moses, saying,
13:2 “Consecrate to Me every firstborn, the first offspring opening every womb among the sons of Israel, both human and animal. It is Mine.”
13:3 And Moses said to the people, “Remember this day, in which you came out of the land of Egypt, out of the house of bondage; for with a mighty hand the Lord brought you out from here; and no leaven shall be eaten.
13:4 For today you go out, in the month of new grain.
13:5 And when the Lord your God brings you into the land of the Canaanites, and Hittites, and Hivites, and Girgashites, and Amorites, and Perizzites, and Jebusites, which He swore to your fathers to give you, a land flowing with milk and honey, you shall keep this service in this month.
13:6 You shall eat unleavened bread for six days, and on the seventh day there shall be a feast of the Lord.
13:7 For seven days you shall eat unleavened bread; no leavened thing shall be seen with you, neither shall there be leaven with you within all your borders.
13:8 And you shall declare to your son in that day, saying, ‘It is because of this that the Lord God acted for me when I came out of Egypt.’
13:9 And it shall be to you a sign upon your hand, and a memorial before your eyes, that the law of the Lord may be in your mouth; for with a mighty hand the Lord God brought you out of Egypt.
13:10 You shall keep this law at the appointed times of the seasons, from year to year.
13:11 And when the Lord your God brings you into the land of the Canaanites, as He swore to your fathers, and gives it to you,
13:12 you shall set apart to the Lord all that opens the womb, the males; every male that opens the womb among the herds or among your livestock, whatever shall be born to you, you shall consecrate to the Lord.
13:13 Every donkey that opens the womb you shall exchange for a sheep; and if you do not exchange it, you shall redeem it. Every firstborn of man among your sons you shall redeem.
13:14 And if your son asks you later, saying, ‘What is this?’ you shall say to him, ‘With a mighty hand the Lord brought us out of the land of Egypt, out of the house of bondage.
13:15 When Pharaoh stubbornly refused to let us go, He killed every firstborn in the land of Egypt, both human firstborn and firstborn of the livestock. Therefore I sacrifice to the Lord every male that opens the womb, and every firstborn of my sons I will redeem.’
13:16 And it shall be a sign upon your hand, and a fixed reminder before your eyes; for with a mighty hand the Lord brought you out of Egypt.”
13:17 When Pharaoh let the people go, God did not guide them by the road through the land of the Philistines, because it was near. For God said, “The people may change their minds when they see war and return to Egypt.”
13:18 God led the people around by the road into the wilderness toward the Red Sea. The sons of Israel went up from the land of Egypt in the fifth generation.
13:19 Moses took Joseph’s bones with him, for Joseph had made the sons of Israel swear an oath, saying, “The Lord will surely visit you, and you shall carry my bones up from here with you.”
13:20 The sons of Israel departed from Succoth and camped at Etham, beside the wilderness.
13:21 God led them by day in a pillar of cloud to show them the way, and by night in a pillar of fire.
13:22 The pillar of cloud by day and the pillar of fire by night did not cease before all the people.

EXODUS 14

14:1 The Lord spoke to Moses, saying,
14:2 “Speak to the sons of Israel, that they turn back and encamp opposite the settlement, between Migdol and the sea, opposite Baal-zephon; before them you shall encamp by the sea.
14:3 Pharaoh will say to his people, ‘These sons of Israel are wandering in the land, for the wilderness has shut them in.’
14:4 I will harden Pharaoh’s heart, and he will pursue them. I will be glorified through Pharaoh and all his army, and all the Egyptians will know that I am the Lord.” They did so.
14:5 The king of the Egyptians was told that the people had fled. Pharaoh and his attendants had a change of heart toward the people and said, “What have we done, sending the sons of Israel away so that they no longer serve us?”
14:6 Pharaoh harnessed his chariots and took all his people with him.
14:7 He took six hundred chosen chariots, all the Egyptians’ horse force, and officers over all.
14:8 The Lord hardened the heart of Pharaoh king of Egypt and of his attendants, and he pursued the sons of Israel. The sons of Israel were going out with a high hand.
14:9 The Egyptians pursued them and found them camped by the sea. All Pharaoh’s horse force and chariots, his horsemen, and his army were opposite the settlement, opposite Baal-zephon.
14:10 Pharaoh drew near. The sons of Israel looked up and saw the Egyptians camped behind them. They were very afraid, and the sons of Israel cried out to the Lord.
14:11 And they said to Moses, “Was it because there were no tombs in the land of Egypt that you brought us out to put us to death in the wilderness? What is this that you have done to us, bringing us out of Egypt?
14:12 Was this not the word which we spoke to you in Egypt, saying, ‘Leave us, that we may serve the Egyptians’? For it is better for us to serve the Egyptians than to die in this wilderness.”
14:13 And Moses said to the people, “Take courage; stand, and behold the salvation from God, which He shall accomplish for us today. For as you have seen the Egyptians today, you shall see them no more for ever.
14:14 The Lord shall fight for you, and you shall keep silence.”
14:15 And the Lord said to Moses, “Why do you cry to Me? Speak to the sons of Israel, that they set forth.
14:16 And lift up with your staff, and stretch out your hand over the sea, and divide it; and let the sons of Israel enter into the midst of the sea upon dry ground.
14:17 Behold, I will harden the heart of Pharaoh and of all the Egyptians, and they will enter after them. I will be glorified through Pharaoh and all his army, his chariots, and his horses.
14:18 All the Egyptians will know that I am the Lord when I am glorified through Pharaoh, his chariots, and his horses.”
14:19 The angel of God, who went before the camp of the sons of Israel, moved and went behind them. The pillar of cloud also moved from before them and stood behind them.
14:20 It came between the Egyptians’ camp and Israel’s camp and stood there. There were darkness and gloom, and the night passed. They did not mingle with one another all night.
14:21 Moses stretched out his hand over the sea. The Lord drove the sea back with a violent south wind all night and made the sea dry. The water was split.
14:22 The sons of Israel entered the midst of the sea on dry ground, and the water was a wall for them on the right and a wall on the left.
14:23 The Egyptians pursued them, and all Pharaoh’s horses, the chariots, and the riders entered after them into the midst of the sea.
14:24 During the morning watch, the Lord looked on the Egyptians’ camp in a pillar of fire and cloud and threw the Egyptians’ camp into confusion.
14:25 He bound the axles of their chariots and drove them with force. The Egyptians said, “Let us flee from Israel, for the Lord is fighting for them against the Egyptians.”
14:26 And the Lord said to Moses, “Stretch out your hand over the sea, and let the water return and cover the Egyptians, both their chariots and their riders.”
14:27 Moses stretched out his hand over the sea, and toward daybreak the water returned to its place. The Egyptians fled beneath the water, and the Lord shook the Egyptians into the midst of the sea.
14:28 The water returned and covered the chariots, the riders, and all Pharaoh’s force that had entered the sea after them. Not one of them remained.
14:29 But the sons of Israel walked on dry ground in the midst of the sea, and the water was a wall for them on the right and a wall on the left.
14:30 The Lord rescued Israel that day from the Egyptians’ hand, and Israel saw the Egyptians dead on the seashore.
14:31 Israel saw the great hand in what the Lord had done to the Egyptians. The people feared the Lord and trusted God and His attendant Moses.

EXODUS 15

15:1 Then Moses and the sons of Israel sang this song to God, saying: “Let us sing to the Lord, for He has been gloriously glorified; horse and rider He has cast into the sea.
15:2 He has become a helper and a shelter to me for salvation; He is my God, and I will glorify Him; the God of my father, and I will exalt Him.
15:3 The Lord shatters wars; the Lord is His name.
15:4 Pharaoh’s chariots and his host He has cast into the sea; chosen riders, officers, He has drowned in the Red Sea.
15:5 With the sea He covered them; they sank into the deep as a stone.
15:6 Thy right hand, O Lord, has been glorified in strength; Thy right hand, O Lord, has shattered enemies.
15:7 And in the abundance of Thy glory Thou didst shatter Thine adversaries; Thou didst send forth Thy wrath, and it devoured them as stubble.
15:8 And by the breath of Thy wrath the water parted; the waters stood fast as a wall; the waves stood fast in the midst of the sea.
15:9 The enemy said, ‘I will pursue and overtake; I will divide the spoil; I will fill my soul; I will slay with my sword; my hand shall have dominion.’
15:10 Thou didst send forth Thy wind, and the sea covered them; they sank as lead in mighty waters.
15:11 Who is like Thee among the gods, O Lord? Who is like Thee, glorified among the holy ones, marvellous in glories, working wonders?
15:12 Thou didst stretch forth Thy right hand; the earth swallowed them.
15:13 In Thy righteousness Thou didst guide this people of Thine whom Thou didst redeem; in Thy strength Thou didst comfort them to Thy holy dwelling.
15:14 The nations heard and were angry; pangs took hold of the inhabitants of Philistia.
15:15 Then the leaders of Edom hastened; and trembling seized the rulers of Moab; all the inhabitants of Canaan melted away.
15:16 May fear and trembling fall upon them; by the greatness of Thine arm let them become as stone, until Thy people pass over, O Lord, until this people of Thine whom Thou hast purchased pass over.
15:17 Bring them in, and plant them in the mountain of Thine inheritance, in Thy prepared dwelling which Thou hast wrought, O Lord; the sanctuary, O Lord, which Thy hands have made ready.
15:18 The Lord reigns for ever and ever, and beyond.”
15:19 For Pharaoh’s horses entered the sea with chariots and riders, and the Lord brought the water of the sea over them. But the sons of Israel walked on dry ground in the midst of the sea.
15:20 Miriam the prophetess, Aaron’s sister, took a timbrel in her hand, and all the women went out after her with timbrels and dances.
15:21 And Miriam led them in song, saying, “Let us sing to the Lord, for He has been gloriously glorified; horse and rider He has cast into the sea.”
15:22 Moses led the sons of Israel onward from the Red Sea and brought them into the wilderness of Shur. They travelled three days in the wilderness without finding water to drink.
15:23 They came to Marah but could not drink its water because it was bitter. Therefore that place was named Bitterness.
15:24 The people murmured against Moses, saying, “What shall we drink?”
15:25 And Moses cried to the Lord; and the Lord showed him a piece of wood, and he cast it into the water, and the water was made sweet. There He appointed ordinances and judgments for him, and there He tested him,
15:26 and said, “If you diligently listen to the voice of the Lord your God, and do what is pleasing before Him, and give ear to His commandments, and keep all His ordinances, I will bring upon you none of the diseases which I brought upon the Egyptians; for I am the Lord who heals you.”
15:27 They came to Elim, where there were twelve springs of water and seventy palm trunks, and they camped there beside the water.

EXODUS 16

16:1 They journeyed from Elim, and the whole congregation of the sons of Israel came to the wilderness of Sin, between Elim and Sinai, on the fifteenth day of the second month after leaving the land of Egypt.
16:2 The whole congregation of the sons of Israel murmured against Moses and Aaron.
16:3 The sons of Israel said to them, “If only we had died, struck by the Lord in the land of Egypt, when we sat by the meat pots and ate our fill of bread! You have brought us into this wilderness to kill this whole congregation with hunger.”
16:4 The Lord said to Moses, “Behold, I am raining bread from heaven for you. The people shall go out and gather each day’s portion, so that I may test whether they will walk in My law or not.
16:5 On the sixth day they shall prepare what they bring in, and it shall be twice what they gather each day.”
16:6 Moses and Aaron said to the whole congregation of the sons of Israel, “In the evening you shall know that the Lord brought you out of the land of Egypt,
16:7 and in the morning you shall see the glory of the Lord, when He hears your murmuring against God. And what are we, that you murmur against us?”
16:8 And Moses said, “When the Lord gives you meat to eat in the evening, and bread in the morning until you are full, it is because the Lord has heard your murmuring with which you murmur against us. And what are we? For your murmuring is not against us, but against God.”
16:9 Moses said to Aaron, “Tell the whole congregation of the sons of Israel, ‘Come near before God, for He has heard your murmuring.’”
16:10 As Aaron spoke to the whole congregation of the sons of Israel, they turned toward the wilderness, and the Lord’s glory appeared in a cloud.
16:11 The Lord spoke to Moses, saying,
16:12 “I have heard the sons of Israel’s murmuring. Tell them: Toward evening you shall eat meat, and in the morning you shall have your fill of bread. You shall know that I am the Lord your God.”
16:13 Evening came, and quail came up and covered the camp. In the morning, as the dew around the camp was subsiding,
16:14 there on the surface of the wilderness was something fine, like white coriander, like frost on the ground.
16:15 When the sons of Israel saw it, they said to one another, “What is this?” For they did not know what it was. Moses said to them, “This is the bread the Lord has given you to eat.
16:16 This is what the Lord commanded: Gather from it, each for those belonging to him, an omer per person according to your number. Each of you shall gather with your tent companions.”
16:17 The sons of Israel did so and gathered, some much and some little.
16:18 They measured it by the omer. The one with much had no excess, and the one with little had no lack. Each gathered for those belonging to his household.
16:19 Moses said to them, “Let no one leave any of it until morning.”
16:20 They did not listen to Moses. Some left part of it until morning, and it swarmed with worms and stank. Moses was embittered against them.
16:21 They gathered it morning by morning, each what he needed. When the sun grew hot, it melted.
16:22 On the sixth day they gathered twice what was needed, two omers per person. All the congregation’s leaders came in and reported it to Moses.
16:23 And Moses said to them, “This is the word which the Lord has spoken: Tomorrow is a Sabbath, a holy rest to the Lord. What you would bake, bake; and what you would boil, boil; and all that remains, lay up to keep until morning.”
16:24 They left some of it until morning, as Moses instructed them. It did not stink, and no worm was in it.
16:25 Moses said, “Eat today, for today is a Sabbath to the Lord. It will not be found in the field.
16:26 You shall gather for six days, but the seventh day is a Sabbath; there will be none on it.”
16:27 On the seventh day some of the people went out to gather, but they found none.
16:28 And the Lord said to Moses, “How long will you refuse to listen to My commandments and My law?
16:29 Behold, the Lord has given you this day, the Sabbath. Therefore on the sixth day He gave you bread for two days. Each of you shall stay in your houses; no one shall go out from his place on the seventh day.”
16:30 The people kept Sabbath on the seventh day.
16:31 The sons of Israel called it Man. It was like coriander seed, white, and tasted like a cake with honey.
16:32 And Moses said, “This is the word which the Lord has commanded: Fill an omer with manna to keep throughout your generations, that they may see the bread which you ate in the wilderness, when the Lord brought you out of the land of Egypt.”
16:33 Moses said to Aaron, “Take one golden jar, put an omerful of manna in it, and set it before God to keep throughout your generations.”
16:34 As the Lord instructed Moses, Aaron set it before the Testimony to keep.
16:35 The sons of Israel ate manna for forty years, until they came to the inhabited land. They ate manna until they reached the region of Phoenicia.
16:36 An omer was one-tenth of the three measures.

EXODUS 17

17:1 The whole congregation of the sons of Israel set out from the wilderness of Sin by their encampments, at the Lord’s word, and camped at Rephidim. There was no water for the people to drink.
17:2 And the people reviled Moses, saying, “Give us water, that we may drink.” And Moses said to them, “Why do you revile me? And why do you test the Lord?”
17:3 And the people thirsted there for water, and murmured against Moses, saying, “Why have you brought us up out of Egypt, to kill us and our children and our livestock with thirst?”
17:4 Moses cried out to the Lord, “What shall I do with this people? A little more, and they will stone me to death!”
17:5 And the Lord said to Moses, “Go before this people, and take with you some of the elders of the people; and take in your hand the staff with which you struck the river, and go.
17:6 Behold, I stand before you there upon the rock in Horeb; and you shall strike the rock, and water shall come forth from it, and My people shall drink.” And Moses did so before the sons of Israel.
17:7 He named that place Testing and Reviling because the sons of Israel reviled and tested the Lord, saying, “Is the Lord among us or not?”
17:8 Amalek came and fought with Israel at Rephidim.
17:9 And Moses said to Joshua, “Choose for yourself able men, and go out and draw up for battle against Amalek tomorrow; and behold, I shall stand upon the summit of the hill, with the staff of God in my hand.”
17:10 Joshua did as Moses had told him and went out to take his stand against Amalek. Moses, Aaron, and Hur went up to the top of the hill.
17:11 Whenever Moses raised his hands, Israel prevailed; whenever he lowered his hands, Amalek prevailed.
17:12 Moses’ hands were heavy. They took a stone, put it beneath him, and he sat on it. Aaron and Hur steadied his hands, one on each side, so that Moses’ hands remained supported until sunset.
17:13 Joshua routed Amalek and all his people with the slaughter of the sword.
17:14 The Lord said to Moses, “Write this as a memorial in a book and deliver it in Joshua’s hearing: I will utterly blot out Amalek’s remembrance from under heaven.”
17:15 Moses built an altar to the Lord and named it The Lord Is My Refuge.
17:16 For with a hidden hand the Lord fights against Amalek from generation to generation.

EXODUS 18

18:1 Jethro, the priest of Midian and Moses’ father-in-law, heard all that the Lord had done for Israel His own people, for the Lord had brought Israel out of Egypt.
18:2 Jethro, Moses’ father-in-law, took Zipporah, Moses’ wife, after she had been sent away,
18:3 and his two sons. One of them was named Gershom, for Moses had said, “I was a sojourner in a foreign land.”
18:4 The second was named Eliezer, for he had said, “My father’s God was my helper and delivered me from Pharaoh’s hand.”
18:5 Jethro, Moses’ father-in-law, went out with the sons and the wife to Moses in the wilderness, where he camped at God’s mountain.
18:6 Moses was told, “Behold, your father-in-law Jethro comes to you, and your wife and your two sons with him.”
18:7 Moses went out to meet his father-in-law, bowed before him, and kissed him. They greeted one another, and he brought him into the tent.
18:8 Moses told his father-in-law everything the Lord had done to Pharaoh and the Egyptians for Israel’s sake, all the hardship they had met on the journey, and how the Lord had delivered them from Pharaoh’s hand and the Egyptians’ hand.
18:9 Jethro was amazed at all the good the Lord had done for them in delivering them from the Egyptians’ hand and Pharaoh’s hand.
18:10 Jethro said, “Blessed be the Lord, for He delivered His people from the Egyptians’ hand and Pharaoh’s hand.
18:11 Now I know that the Lord is greater than all gods, because of this, because they attacked them.”
18:12 Jethro, Moses’ father-in-law, took whole burnt offerings and sacrifices for God. Aaron and all Israel’s elders came to eat bread with Moses’ father-in-law before God.
18:13 After the next day Moses sat to judge the people, and all the people stood before Moses from morning until evening.
18:14 And when Jethro saw all that he did to the people, he said, “What is this that you do to the people? Why do you sit alone, while all the people stand before you from morning until evening?”
18:15 Moses said to his father-in-law, “The people come to me to seek judgment from God.
18:16 When they have a dispute and come to me, I judge each case and instruct them in God’s ordinances and His law.”
18:17 And Moses’ father-in-law said to him, “You are not doing this rightly.
18:18 You shall be worn out with unbearable exhaustion, both you and all this people who are with you; this matter is heavy for you, and you cannot do it alone.
18:19 Now therefore listen to me, and I will counsel you, and God shall be with you. Represent the people in matters pertaining to God, and bring their words to God.
18:20 And you shall solemnly instruct them in the ordinances of God and His law, and show them the ways in which they shall walk, and the works which they shall do.
18:21 And choose for yourself from all the people able men who fear God, righteous men who hate arrogance; and you shall set them over the people as rulers of thousands, and rulers of hundreds, and rulers of fifties, and rulers of tens.
18:22 And they shall judge the people at all times; the weighty matter they shall bring to you, but the lesser judgments they shall judge themselves; and they shall lighten your burden, and bear it with you.
18:23 If you do this thing, God shall strengthen you, and you shall be able to stand; and all this people shall come to their own place in peace.”
18:24 Moses listened to his father-in-law and did all that he said.
18:25 Moses chose capable men from all Israel and appointed them over the people as leaders of thousands, hundreds, fifties, and tens.
18:26 They judged the people at all times. They brought every weighty matter to Moses, but judged every light matter themselves.
18:27 Moses sent his father-in-law on his way, and he went to his own land.

EXODUS 19

19:1 In the third month after the sons of Israel left the land of Egypt, on that day they came to the wilderness of Sinai.
19:2 They journeyed from Rephidim and came to the wilderness of Sinai. Israel camped there opposite the mountain.
19:3 And Moses went up to the mountain of God; and God called to him from the mountain, saying, “This is what you shall say to the house of Jacob, and declare to the sons of Israel:
19:4 ‘You yourselves have seen all that I have done to the Egyptians, and I bore you as upon eagles’ wings, and brought you to Myself.
19:5 Now therefore, if you diligently listen to My voice, and keep My covenant, you shall be to Me a treasured people from among all the nations; for all the earth is Mine.
19:6 And you shall be to Me a royal priesthood and a holy nation.’ These are the words which you shall speak to the sons of Israel.”
19:7 Moses came, called the people’s elders, and set before them all these words which God had commanded him.
19:8 All the people answered with one accord, “We will do everything God has said, and we will obey.” Moses brought the people’s words to God.
19:9 And the Lord said to Moses, “Behold, I come to you in a pillar of cloud, that the people may hear Me speaking to you, and believe you for ever.” And Moses declared the words of the people to the Lord.
19:10 The Lord said to Moses, “Go down, solemnly warn the people, and purify them today and tomorrow. Let them wash their garments
19:11 and be ready for the third day, for on the third day the Lord will come down on Mount Sinai in the sight of all the people.
19:12 And you shall set bounds for the people all around, saying, ‘Take care not to go up the mountain or touch any part of it; whoever touches the mountain shall surely die.
19:13 No hand shall touch him; for he shall surely be stoned, or shot through with a dart; whether beast or man, he shall not live. When the sounds and the trumpets and the cloud depart from the mountain, they shall go up upon the mountain.’”
19:14 Moses went down from the mountain to the people and consecrated them, and they washed their garments.
19:15 He said to the people, “Be ready for three days. Do not approach a woman.”
19:16 Toward dawn on the third day there were thunder and lightning and a dark cloud over Mount Sinai. The trumpet sounded loudly, and all the people in the camp were terrified.
19:17 Moses brought the people out of the camp to meet God, and they stood beneath the mountain.
19:18 Mount Sinai was entirely covered with smoke because God had descended on it in fire. The smoke rose like the smoke of a furnace, and all the people were greatly astonished.
19:19 The trumpet’s sounds grew much stronger. Moses spoke, and God answered him with a voice.
19:20 The Lord descended on Mount Sinai, to the top of the mountain. The Lord called Moses to the top of the mountain, and Moses went up.
19:21 God said to Moses, “Go down and solemnly warn the people, lest they come near God to gaze and a multitude of them fall.
19:22 Let the priests who approach the Lord God consecrate themselves, lest the Lord depart from them.”
19:23 And Moses said to God, “The people cannot come up to Mount Sinai; for Thou hast solemnly warned us, saying, ‘Set bounds about the mountain, and consecrate it.’”
19:24 And the Lord said to him, “Go, descend, and come up, you and Aaron with you; but do not let the priests and the people force their way up to God, lest the Lord destroy some of them.”
19:25 Moses went down to the people and spoke to them.

EXODUS 20

20:1 The Lord spoke all these words, saying,
20:2 “I am the Lord your God, who brought you out of the land of Egypt, out of the house of bondage.
20:3 You shall have no other gods except Me.
20:4 You shall not make for yourself an idol, nor any likeness of things in heaven above, or in the earth beneath, or in the waters under the earth.
20:5 You shall not bow down to them, neither shall you serve them; for I am the Lord your God, a jealous God, repaying the sins of fathers upon children to the third and fourth generation of those who hate Me,
20:6 and showing mercy to thousands, to those who love Me and keep My ordinances.
20:7 You shall not take the name of the Lord your God in vain; for the Lord shall by no means hold him guiltless who takes His name in vain.
20:8 Remember the Sabbath day, to keep it holy.
20:9 For six days you shall labour and do all your works;
20:10 but the seventh day is a Sabbath to the Lord your God. You shall do no work on it: you, and your son, and your daughter, your male slave, and your female slave, your ox, and your beast of burden, and all your livestock, and the resident foreigner who dwells with you.
20:11 For in six days the Lord made heaven and earth and the sea and everything in them, and rested on the seventh day. Therefore the Lord blessed the seventh day and made it holy.
20:12 Honour your father and your mother, that it may be well with you, and that your days may be long upon the good land which the Lord your God gives you.
20:13 You shall not commit adultery.
20:14 You shall not steal.
20:15 You shall not murder.
20:16 You shall not bear false witness against your neighbour.
20:17 You shall not covet your neighbour’s wife. You shall not covet your neighbour’s house, nor his field, nor his male slave, nor his female slave, nor his ox, nor his beast of burden, nor any of his livestock, nor anything that is your neighbour’s.”
20:18 All the people saw the sound, the flashes, the trumpet’s sound, and the smoking mountain. All the people were afraid and stood far away.
20:19 And they said to Moses, “Speak to us yourself, and do not let God speak to us, lest we die.”
20:20 And Moses said to them, “Take courage; for God has come to you to test you, that His fear may be in you, so that you do not sin.”
20:21 The people stood far away, while Moses entered the thick darkness where God was.
20:22 And the Lord said to Moses, “This is what you shall say to the house of Jacob, and declare to the sons of Israel: ‘You have seen that I have spoken to you from heaven.
20:23 You shall not make gods of silver for yourselves, nor shall you make gods of gold for yourselves.
20:24 You shall make an altar of earth to Me and sacrifice on it your whole burnt offerings and your sacrifices of deliverance, your sheep and your calves, in every place where I name My name; and I will come to you and bless you.
20:25 But if you make Me an altar of stones, you shall not build with hewn stones; for you have laid your blade upon them, and they are defiled.
20:26 You shall not go up by steps to My altar, lest you uncover your nakedness on it.’”

EXODUS 21

21:1 “And these are the ordinances which you shall set before them.
21:2 If you acquire a Hebrew slave, he shall serve you for six years; and in the seventh year he shall go out free, without payment.
21:3 If he comes in alone, he shall go out alone; but if a wife comes in with him, his wife shall also go out with him.
21:4 But if his master gives him a wife, and she bears him sons or daughters, the wife and the children shall be his master’s, and he shall go out alone.
21:5 But if the slave answers and says, ‘I love my master, and my wife, and my children; I will not go out free,’
21:6 his master shall bring him before God’s tribunal, and then bring him to the door, to the doorpost. His master shall pierce his ear with an awl, and he shall serve him for ever.
21:7 And if a man sells his own daughter as a household slave, she shall not go out as the female slaves go out.
21:8 If she does not please her master, who pledged her to himself, he shall redeem her; he has no right to sell her to a foreign nation, because he has broken faith with her.
21:9 But if he pledges her to his son, he shall deal with her according to the ordinance for daughters.
21:10 And if he takes another wife to himself, he shall not deprive her of her necessities, her clothing, or her conjugal companionship.
21:11 But if he does not do these three things for her, she shall go out freely, without silver.
21:12 And if a man strikes another, and he dies, the one who struck him shall surely be put to death.
21:13 But if he did not act willingly, and God delivered him into his hands, I will give you a place where the slayer shall flee.
21:14 But if a man attacks his neighbour to kill him by deceit, and flees for refuge, you shall take him from My altar to put him to death.
21:15 He who strikes his father or his mother shall surely be put to death.
21:16 He who reviles his father or his mother shall surely die.
21:17 And if a man steals one of the sons of Israel, and overpowers him and sells him, and he is found with him, he shall surely die.
21:18 And if two men quarrel, and one strikes his neighbour with a stone or with his fist, and he does not die, but lies on his bed,
21:19 if the man rises and walks outside with a staff, the one who struck him shall be guiltless; only he shall pay for his lost labour and his healing.
21:20 And if a man strikes his male slave or his female slave with a rod, and the slave dies under his hands, he shall surely be avenged by judgment.
21:21 But if the slave survives a day or two, he shall not be avenged; for he is his silver.
21:22 And if two men fight and strike a woman with child, and her child comes out not fully formed, he shall surely be fined; as the woman’s husband shall impose on him, he shall pay according to an assessment.
21:23 But if it was fully formed, he shall give life for life,
21:24 eye for eye, tooth for tooth, hand for hand, foot for foot,
21:25 burn for burn, wound for wound, bruise for bruise.
21:26 And if a man strikes the eye of his household slave or of his female slave, and blinds it, he shall set them free for their eye.
21:27 And if he knocks out the tooth of his household slave or of his female slave, he shall set them free for their tooth.
21:28 And if a bull gores a man or a woman, and the person dies, the bull shall surely be stoned, and its flesh shall not be eaten; but the owner of the bull shall be guiltless.
21:29 But if the bull was in the habit of goring before, and its owner has been warned, and he does not remove it, and it kills a man or a woman, the bull shall be stoned, and its owner also shall die.
21:30 But if a ransom is imposed on him, he shall give for the ransom of his life all that they impose on him.
21:31 Whether it gores a son or a daughter, they shall deal with him according to this ordinance.
21:32 And if the bull gores a male slave or a female slave, he shall give thirty didrachmas of silver to their master, and the bull shall be stoned.
21:33 And if a man opens a pit, or hews out a pit, and does not cover it, and a calf or donkey falls in it,
21:34 the owner of the pit shall make restitution. He shall give silver to the animal’s owner, and the dead animal shall be his.
21:35 And if a man’s bull gores his neighbour’s bull, and it dies, they shall sell the living bull and divide its silver; and they shall also divide the dead bull.
21:36 But if the bull is known to have been in the habit of goring before, and its owner has been warned, and he does not remove it, he shall repay bull for bull; and the dead bull shall be his.
21:37 And if a man steals a calf or a sheep, and slaughters it or sells it, he shall repay five calves for the calf, and four sheep for the sheep.”

EXODUS 22

22:1 “And if the thief is found breaking in, and is struck and dies, there is no murder in his case.
22:2 But if the sun has risen upon him, he is guilty; he shall die in return. And if he has nothing, let him be sold for the theft.
22:3 But if he is caught, and the stolen thing is found alive in his hand, from donkey to sheep, he shall repay them double.
22:4 And if a man grazes a field or a vineyard, and lets his beast graze another field, he shall repay from his own field according to its yield; but if it grazes the whole field, he shall repay the best of his field and the best of his vineyard.
22:5 And if fire breaks out and catches thorns, and burns a threshing floor, or standing grain, or a plain, he who kindled the fire shall make restitution.
22:6 And if a man gives his neighbour silver or goods to keep, and they are stolen from the man’s house, if the thief is found, he shall repay double.
22:7 But if the thief is not found, the master of the house shall come before God and swear that he has not dealt wickedly with any of his neighbour’s deposit.
22:8 In every alleged wrong concerning a calf, beast of burden, sheep, garment, or any disputed loss, whatever it may be, the case of both parties shall come before God. Whoever is convicted through God shall repay his neighbour double.
22:9 And if a man gives his neighbour a beast of burden, or a calf, or a sheep, or any beast to keep, and it is injured, or dies, or is taken captive, and no one knows,
22:10 an oath by God shall be between them that he has not acted dishonestly concerning any of his neighbour’s deposit. The owner shall accept it, and he shall not make restitution.
22:11 But if it was stolen from him, he shall make restitution to its owner.
22:12 If it was taken by a wild animal, he shall bring the owner to the prey, and he shall not make restitution.
22:13 And if a man borrows from his neighbour, and it is injured, or dies, or is taken captive, and its owner is not with it, he shall make restitution.
22:14 But if its owner is with it, he shall not make restitution; and if it is hired, it shall be reckoned against its hire.
22:15 And if a man deceives a virgin who is not betrothed, and lies with her, he shall surely give the marriage payment for her to be his wife.
22:16 But if her father firmly refuses, and is unwilling to give her to him as a wife, he shall pay silver to the father according to the marriage payment for virgins.
22:17 You shall not preserve sorcerers alive.
22:18 Everyone who lies with a beast, you shall surely put to death.
22:19 He who sacrifices to gods, except to the Lord alone, shall surely be destroyed.
22:20 And you shall not ill-treat a resident foreigner, neither shall you afflict him; for you were resident foreigners in the land of Egypt.
22:21 You shall not mistreat any widow or orphan.
22:22 But if you afflict them, and they cry aloud to Me, I will surely listen to their voice,
22:23 I will be angry with wrath and kill you with the sword. Your wives will become widows and your children orphans.
22:24 And if you lend silver to your poor brother beside you, you shall not press him hard, neither shall you lay interest upon him.
22:25 And if you take your neighbour’s garment in pledge, you shall restore it to him before the setting of the sun;
22:26 for this is his covering, his only garment for his nakedness; What shall he sleep in? If therefore he cries to Me, I will listen to him; for I am merciful.
22:27 You shall not revile gods, neither shall you speak evil of the rulers of your people.
22:28 You shall not delay the firstfruits of your threshing floor and your winepress; you shall give Me the firstborn of your sons.
22:29 You shall do the same with your calf, and your sheep, and your beast of burden; it shall be under its mother for seven days, and on the eighth day you shall give it to Me.
22:30 You shall be holy people to Me. You shall not eat flesh taken by wild animals; throw it to the dog.”

EXODUS 23

23:1 “You shall not receive an empty report. You shall not agree with the unrighteous to become an unrighteous witness.
23:2 You shall not join the many in evil; neither shall you join a multitude in turning aside with the many, to turn aside judgment.
23:3 Neither shall you favour a poor man in judgment.
23:4 And if you meet your enemy’s ox or his beast of burden wandering, you shall surely turn it back and restore it to him.
23:5 And if you see your enemy’s beast of burden fallen beneath its load, you shall not pass it by; but you shall raise it up together with him.
23:6 You shall not pervert the judgment of a poor man in his cause.
23:7 You shall keep far away from every unrighteous charge; the innocent and the righteous you shall not kill, neither shall you justify the ungodly for gifts.
23:8 And you shall receive no gifts; for gifts blind the eyes of those who see, and corrupt righteous words.
23:9 And you shall not afflict a resident foreigner; for you know the soul of the resident foreigner, since you yourselves were resident foreigners in the land of Egypt.
23:10 For six years you shall sow your land, and gather its produce;
23:11 but in the seventh you shall grant it release, and leave it; and the poor of your nation shall eat, and what they leave the wild beasts shall eat. You shall do the same with your vineyard and your olive grove.
23:12 For six days you shall do your works, but on the seventh day there shall be rest, that your ox and your beast of burden may rest, and that the son of your female slave and the resident foreigner may be refreshed.
23:13 Keep everything I have said to you. Do not call to mind the name of other gods or let it be heard from your mouth.
23:14 Three times a year you shall keep a feast to Me.
23:15 Keep the feast of unleavened bread; for seven days you shall eat unleavened bread, as I commanded you, at the appointed time in the month of new grain; for in it you came out of Egypt. You shall not appear before Me empty-handed.
23:16 And you shall keep the feast of harvest, of the firstfruits of your labours which you sow in your field; and the feast of completion as the year goes out, when you gather your labours out of your field.
23:17 Every male of yours shall appear three times in the year before the Lord your God.
23:18 For when I cast out nations before you, and enlarge your borders, you shall not sacrifice the blood of My offering with leaven, neither shall the fat of My feast remain until morning.
23:19 You shall bring the firstfruits of the first produce of your land into the house of the Lord your God. You shall not boil a lamb in its mother’s milk.
23:20 And behold, I send My angel before your face, to keep you in the way, that he may bring you into the land which I have prepared for you.
23:21 Take care, and listen to him, and do not disobey him; for he shall not yield before you, for My name is upon him.
23:22 If you diligently listen to My voice, and you do all that I command you, and you keep My covenant, you shall be to Me a treasured people from among all the nations; for all the earth is Mine, and you shall be to Me a royal priesthood and a holy nation. You shall say these words to the sons of Israel: ‘If you diligently listen to My voice, and you do all that I say to you, I will be an enemy to your enemies, and oppose those who oppose you.’
23:23 For My angel shall go before you, and bring you to the Amorite, and Hittite, and Perizzite, and Canaanite, and Girgashite, and Hivite, and Jebusite; and I will destroy them.
23:24 You shall not bow down to their gods, neither shall you serve them, nor do according to their works; but you shall utterly overthrow them, and utterly shatter their pillars.
23:25 And you shall serve the Lord your God, and I will bless your bread, and your wine, and your water; and I will turn sickness away from you.
23:26 None shall be unfruitful or barren in your land; I will fulfil the number of your days.
23:27 And I will send fear before you, and confound all the nations among whom you come; and I will make all your adversaries fugitives.
23:28 And I will send hornets before you, and they shall drive out the Amorites, and Hivites, and Canaanites, and Hittites from before you.
23:29 I will not drive them out in one year, lest the land become desolate, and the beasts of the land multiply against you;
23:30 little by little I will drive them out from before you, until you increase and inherit the land.
23:31 And I will set your borders from the Red Sea to the sea of the Philistines, and from the wilderness to the great river Euphrates; and I will deliver the inhabitants of the land into your hands, and cast them out from before you.
23:32 You shall make no covenant with them, nor with their gods;
23:33 neither shall they dwell in your land, lest they cause you to sin against Me; for if you serve their gods, these shall be a stumbling block to you.”

EXODUS 24

24:1 And He said to Moses, “Come up to the Lord, you and Aaron, and Nadab and Abihu, and seventy of the elders of Israel; and they shall bow down to the Lord from afar.
24:2 Moses alone shall approach God. They shall not approach, and the people shall not come up with them.”
24:3 Moses came in and related to the people all God’s words and ordinances. All the people answered with one voice, “We will do and obey everything the Lord has spoken.”

31:15 For six days you shall do your work, but the seventh day is a Sabbath, a holy rest to the Lord. Whoever does work on the seventh day shall surely be put to death.
31:16 The sons of Israel shall keep the Sabbaths, observing them throughout their generations: an everlasting covenant.

EXODUS 32

32:3 All the people took off the gold earrings from their ears and brought them to Aaron.

32:5 When Aaron saw it, he built an altar opposite it and proclaimed, “Tomorrow is a feast of the Lord.”
32:6 He rose early the next day, offered whole burnt offerings, and brought a sacrifice of deliverance. The people sat down to eat and drink and rose up to play.
32:7 The Lord spoke to Moses, saying, “Go quickly from here and come down, for your people, whom you led out of the land of Egypt, have acted lawlessly.
32:8 They have quickly transgressed the way you commanded them. They have made themselves a calf, bowed down to it, sacrificed to it, and said, ‘These are your gods, Israel, who brought you up from the land of Egypt.’
32:10 Now leave Me alone, and in the heat of My wrath against them I will destroy them. I will make you into a great nation.”
32:11 Moses entreated the Lord God and said, “Why, O Lord, art Thou angry with Thy people, whom Thou didst lead out of the land of Egypt with great strength and Thine uplifted arm?
32:12 Let not the Egyptians say, ‘He led them out with evil intent, to kill them in the mountains and consume them from the earth.’ Cease from Thy fierce anger and be merciful concerning Thy people’s evil,
32:13 remembering Abraham, Isaac, and Jacob, Thy servants, to whom Thou didst swear by Thyself and speak, saying, ‘I will greatly multiply your seed as the stars of heaven in multitude’; and all this land which Thou didst say Thou wouldest give to their seed, they shall possess for ever.”
32:14 The Lord was appeased concerning the calamity He had said He would bring upon His people.
32:15 Moses turned and came down the mountain with the two tablets of the testimony in his hands, stone tablets written on both sides, on the one side and the other.

32:17 When Joshua heard the people shouting, he said to Moses, “There is a sound of war in the camp!”
32:18 He said, “It is not the sound of those leading a song of victory or of defeat; it is the sound of those leading a wine-song that I hear.”
32:19 As he approached the camp, he saw the calf and the dancing. In his anger Moses threw the two tablets from his hands and shattered them at the mountain’s foot.
32:20 He took the calf they had made, burned it in the fire, ground it fine, scattered it on the water, and made the sons of Israel drink it.
32:21 Moses said to Aaron, “What did this people do to you, that you brought so great a sin upon them?”
32:22 Aaron said to Moses, “Do not be angry, my lord. You know this people’s impulse.
32:23 They said to me, ‘Make us gods to go before us. As for this Moses, the man who led us out of Egypt, we do not know what has happened to him.’
32:24 So I told them, ‘Whoever has gold ornaments, take them off.’ They gave them to me, I threw them into the fire, and this calf came out.”
32:25 Moses saw that the people were scattered, for Aaron had scattered them, to the delight of their enemies.
32:26 Moses stood at the camp’s gate and said, “Whoever is for the Lord, come to me!” All the sons of Levi gathered to him.
32:27 He said to them, “Thus says the Lord, the God of Israel: ‘Each of you put his sword at his thigh. Go back and forth through the camp from gate to gate, and kill, each his brother, each his neighbour, and each the one nearest to him.’”
32:28 The sons of Levi did as Moses told them. As many as three thousand men of the people fell that day.
32:29 Moses said to them, “You have filled your hands today to the Lord, each at the cost of his son or brother, that a blessing may be given to you.”
32:30 After the next day Moses said to the people, “You have committed a great sin. Now I will go up to God, that I may make propitiation for your sin.”
32:31 Moses returned to the Lord and said, “I beseech Thee, O Lord: this people has committed a great sin and made themselves gods of gold.
32:32 Now, if Thou wilt forgive their sin, forgive it; but if not, blot me out of Thy book which Thou hast written.”
32:33 The Lord said to Moses, “If anyone has sinned before Me, I will blot him out of My book.
32:34 Now go down and lead this people to the place I told you. Behold, My angel goes before your face. On the day I visit, I will bring their sin upon them.”
32:35 The Lord struck the people because of the making of the calf Aaron made.

EXODUS 33

33:1 The Lord said to Moses, “Go up from here, you and your people whom you led out of the land of Egypt, to the land I swore to Abraham, Isaac, and Jacob, saying, ‘I will give it to your seed.’
33:2 I will send My angel with you, before your face, and he shall drive out the Amorites, Hittites, Perizzites, Girgashites, Hivites, and Jebusites.
33:3 I will bring you into a land flowing with milk and honey. But I will not go up with you, lest I consume you on the way, for you are a stiff-necked people.”
33:4 When the people heard this grievous word, they mourned in mourning clothes.
33:5 The Lord said to the sons of Israel, “You are a stiff-necked people. Beware lest I bring another blow upon you and consume you. Now remove your splendid robes and ornaments, and I will show you what I will do to you.”
33:6 The sons of Israel removed their ornaments and splendid clothing from Mount Horeb onward.
33:7 Moses took his tent and pitched it outside the camp, far from the camp, and it was called the Tent of Testimony. Everyone seeking the Lord would go out to the tent outside the camp.

33:9 When Moses entered the tent, the pillar of cloud descended and stood at the tent’s door, and He spoke with Moses.
33:10 All the people saw the pillar of cloud standing at the tent’s entrance. They rose and bowed down, each at his own tent’s entrance.
33:11 The Lord spoke to Moses face to face, as a man speaks to his friend. Then Moses returned to the camp, but his young attendant Joshua son of Nun did not leave the tent.
33:12 Moses said to the Lord, “Behold, Thou sayest to me, ‘Bring up this people,’ but Thou hast not shown me whom Thou wilt send with me. Thou hast said, ‘I know you above all, and you have grace with Me.’
33:13 If then I have found grace before Thee, reveal Thyself to me, that I may see Thee with understanding and find grace before Thee, and know that this great nation is Thy people.”
33:14 He said, “I Myself will go before you, and I will give you rest.”
33:15 Moses said to Him, “If Thou Thyself dost not go, do not bring me up from here.
33:16 How shall it truly be known that I and Thy people have found grace with Thee, unless Thou goest with us? Then I and Thy people shall be glorified above all the nations upon the earth.”
33:17 The Lord said to Moses, “I will also do this thing which you have asked, for you have found grace before Me, and I know you above all.”
33:18 Moses said, “Show me Thy glory.”
33:19 He said, “I will pass before you in My glory and proclaim My name, Lord, before you. I will have mercy on whom I will have mercy, and compassion on whom I will have compassion.”
33:20 He said, “You cannot see My face, for no man shall see My face and live.”
33:21 The Lord said, “Behold, there is a place beside Me; you shall stand upon the rock.
33:22 When My glory passes by, I will put you in a cleft of the rock and cover you with My hand until I have passed.
33:23 Then I will remove My hand, and you shall see what is behind Me, but My face shall not appear to you.”

34:3 Let no one come up with you or be seen anywhere on the mountain. Neither sheep nor cattle shall graze near that mountain.”

34:5 The Lord descended in a cloud, stood beside him there, and called upon the name of the Lord.
34:6 The Lord passed before him and proclaimed, “The Lord God, compassionate and merciful, slow to anger, abundant in mercy and true,

34:8 Moses quickly bowed to the ground and worshipped.

34:10 The Lord said to Moses, “Behold, I establish a covenant with you. Before all your people I will do glorious things never done in all the earth or among any nation. All the people among whom you are shall see the works of the Lord, for what I will do to you is marvellous.
34:11 Observe all that I command you. Behold, I am driving out before you the Amorites, Canaanites, Hittites, Perizzites, Hivites, Girgashites, and Jebusites.
34:12 Take care, lest you make a covenant with the inhabitants of the land you are entering, lest it become a stumbling block to you among you.
34:13 Tear down their altars, break their pillars, cut down their sacred groves, and burn their gods’ carved images with fire.
34:14 You shall not bow down to another god, for the Lord God, whose name is Jealous, is a jealous God.
34:15 Make no covenant with the foreign inhabitants of the land, lest they prostitute themselves to their gods, sacrifice to their gods, invite you, and you eat of their sacrifices,
34:16 and take their daughters for your sons and give your daughters to their sons, and your daughters prostitute themselves to their gods and lead your sons to prostitute themselves to their gods.
34:17 You shall not make cast gods for yourself.

34:21 For six days you shall work, but on the seventh you shall rest; at sowing time and harvest you shall rest.
34:22 Keep the Feast of Weeks for Me, at the beginning of the wheat harvest, and the Feast of Ingathering at the middle of the year.
34:23 Three times in the year all your males shall appear before the Lord, the God of Israel.
34:24 When I drive nations out before your face and enlarge your borders, no one shall desire your land when you go up to appear before the Lord your God three times in the year.
34:25 You shall not offer the blood of My offerings with leaven, nor shall the sacrifices of the Passover feast remain until morning.
34:26 Bring the first produce of your land into the house of the Lord your God. You shall not offer a lamb in its mother’s milk.”
34:27 The Lord said to Moses, “Write these words for yourself, for according to these words I have made a covenant with you and Israel.”
34:28 Moses was there before the Lord forty days and forty nights, eating no bread and drinking no water. He wrote these words on the covenant tablets, the Ten Words.
34:29 As Moses came down from the mountain with the two tablets in his hands, he did not know that the appearance of the skin of his face had been glorified through speaking with Him.
34:30 Aaron and all Israel’s elders saw that the appearance of the skin of Moses’ face had been glorified, and they were afraid to approach him.
34:31 Moses called to them, and Aaron and all the assembly’s leaders returned to him. Moses spoke with them.
34:32 Afterward all the sons of Israel approached him, and he commanded them everything the Lord had spoken to him on Mount Sinai.
34:33 When Moses finished speaking with them, he put a veil over his face.
34:34 Whenever Moses entered before the Lord to speak with Him, he removed the veil until he came out. Then he told all the sons of Israel what the Lord had commanded him.
34:35 The sons of Israel saw that Moses’ face had been glorified. Moses replaced the veil over his face until he entered to converse with Him.

40:4 Bring in the table and set out its presentation. Bring in the lampstand and put its lamps on it.
40:5 Place the gold altar for burning incense before the ark, and put a screening veil over the doorway of the Tent of Testimony.
40:6 Place the altar of offerings by the doors of the Tent of Testimony.
40:8 Set the tent about, and sanctify everything belonging to it all around.
40:9 Take the anointing oil and anoint the tent and everything in it. Sanctify it and all its utensils, and it shall be holy.
40:10 Anoint the altar of offerings and all its utensils. Sanctify the altar, and it shall be most holy.
40:12 Bring Aaron and his sons to the doors of the Tent of Testimony and wash them with water.
40:13 Clothe Aaron in the holy robes, anoint him, and sanctify him, and he shall serve Me as priest.
40:14 Bring his sons and clothe them in tunics.
40:15 Anoint them as you anointed their father, and they shall serve Me as priests. They shall have an anointing for priesthood for ever, throughout their generations.”
40:16 Moses did everything the Lord had commanded him; so he did.
40:17 In the first month of the second year after they came out of Egypt, at the new moon, the tent was erected.
40:18 Moses erected the tent, placed its capitals, inserted its bars, and erected its pillars.
40:19 He spread the curtains over the tent and placed the tent covering above it, as the Lord commanded Moses.
40:20 He took the testimonies and put them into the ark, and placed the carrying poles beneath the ark.
40:21 He brought the ark into the tent, put up the screening veil, and screened the ark of the testimony, as the Lord commanded Moses.
40:22 He placed the table in the Tent of Testimony, on its north side, outside the tent’s veil.
40:23 He set the bread of the Presence upon it before the Lord, as the Lord commanded Moses.
40:24 He placed the lampstand in the Tent of Testimony, on the south side of the tent.
40:25 He put its lamps in place before the Lord, as the Lord commanded Moses.
40:26 He placed the gold altar in the Tent of Testimony opposite the veil,
40:27 and burned the compounded incense on it, as the Lord commanded Moses.
40:29 He placed the altar of offerings by the doors of the tent.
40:33 He erected the court around the tent and the altar. Moses completed all the work.
40:34 The cloud covered the Tent of Testimony, and the tent was filled with the glory of the Lord.
40:35 Moses could not enter the Tent of Testimony because the cloud overshadowed it and the tent was filled with the glory of the Lord.
40:36 Whenever the cloud rose from the tent, the sons of Israel set out with their belongings.
40:37 If the cloud did not rise, they did not set out until the day it rose.
40:38 For a cloud was over the tent by day, and fire was over it by night, in the sight of all Israel throughout all their journeys.
