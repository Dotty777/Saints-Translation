Exodus 1

Read this chapter on staging · Book beginning

1:1 These are the names of the sons of Israel who entered Egypt with Jacob their father; each came with his whole household:

1:2 Reuben, Simeon, Levi, and Judah;

1:3 Issachar, Zebulun, and Benjamin;

1:4 Dan and Naphtali, Gad and Asher.

1:5 Now Joseph was in Egypt; and all the souls descended from Jacob were seventy-five.

1:6 And Joseph died, and all his brothers, and all that generation.

1:7 And the sons of Israel increased and multiplied, and became numerous and exceedingly, exceedingly strong; and the land multiplied them.

1:8 And another king arose over Egypt, who did not know Joseph.

1:9 And he said to his nation, “Behold, the people of the sons of Israel are a great multitude, and are stronger than we are.

1:10 Come, therefore, let us deal craftily with them, lest they multiply; and when war befalls us, they also join our adversaries, wage war against us, and leave the land.”

1:11 He set overseers of labour over them to afflict them with their work. They built fortified cities for Pharaoh: Pithom, Rameses, and On, which is the City of the Sun.

1:12 But the more they humbled them, the more they multiplied and grew exceedingly, exceedingly strong; and the Egyptians loathed the sons of Israel.

1:13 The Egyptians oppressed the sons of Israel by force.

1:14 They made their lives painful with hard labour in clay and brickmaking, and with all the work in the plains, in every task in which they enslaved them by force.

1:15 The king of the Egyptians spoke to the midwives of the Hebrews, one named Sepphora and the other Puah.

1:16 And he said, “When you attend the Hebrew women in childbirth, and they are about to give birth, if the child is male, kill him; but if the child is female, preserve her.”

1:17 But the midwives feared God. They did not do as the king of Egypt had commanded them, and they kept the boys alive.

1:18 And the king of Egypt called the midwives and said to them, “Why have you done this thing, and kept the male children alive?”

1:19 The midwives said to Pharaoh, “The Hebrew women are not like the Egyptian women, for they give birth before the midwives come to them.” And they bore children.

1:20 God dealt well with the midwives, and the people multiplied and became very strong.

1:21 And because the midwives feared God, they made houses for themselves.

1:22 And Pharaoh commanded all his people, saying, “Cast every male child born to the Hebrews into the river, but keep every female child alive.”

No new full critical-apparatus or Göttingen collation was made. Independent linguistic review, a name and technical-term review, and human reading aloud remain outstanding. Unresolved inherited readings remain identified in the notes; the revision is not independent publication approval.

3. Reading the English Thou and thee are singular; ye and you are plural. Thy and thine, and my and mine, follow the guide’s sound rule. Clear divine pronouns are capitalized. Ordinary narrative past forms remain where they read naturally. The Greek historical present is often rendered as English narrative past. Seed, soul, hand, heart, covenant, and similar concrete or theological terms are retained where the construction supports them. Soul can mean a life or a person. Slave is retained where the legal status is slavery. Ancient measures and monetary units are not modern currency equivalents. Quotation marks are editorial. Chapters 21–23 and 25–29 are punctuated as separate reading excerpts from continuing divine speech; the chapter boundary does not imply a change of speaker. Ordinary narrative past forms are retained where clear.

4. Verse numbering The manuscript contains 1,165 separately numbered units, following the publisher’s displayed Greek chapter and verse labels. These include 28:29a and 35:12a. Labels not displayed separately in the consulted text are 25:6; 28:23–28; 32:9; 35:8, 15, 17–18; and 40:7, 11, 28, 30–32. No replacement text has been inserted at those numbers. Especially in chapters 36–39, the displayed Greek arrangement differs from familiar Hebrew-based arrangements. The inherited English grouped several verse labels; they are separated here so that each displayed source unit can be located.

5. Exodus 1 and 2 The seventy-five souls of 1:5 and On, the City of the Sun, in 1:11 follow this Greek text. At 1:21, the plural verb and reflexive expression support the midwives making houses for themselves; the sentence is not silently recast as God making houses for them. At 2:25, the wording concerning God making Himself known is retained. The narrative’s own statements govern these choices.

Exodus 2

Read this chapter on staging · Book beginning

2:1 Now there was a man of the tribe of Levi, who took one of the daughters of Levi and had her as his wife.

2:2 She conceived and bore a male child. Seeing that he was beautiful, they hid him for three months.

2:3 When they could no longer hide him, his mother took a basket for him and coated it with bitumen-pitch. She put the child in it and placed it in the marsh beside the river.

2:4 His sister watched from a distance to learn what would happen to him.

2:5 Pharaoh’s daughter came down to bathe at the river while her attendants walked beside it. She saw the basket in the marsh, sent her attendant, and had it brought to her.

2:6 And she opened it, and saw a child weeping in the basket; and Pharaoh’s daughter had compassion on him, and said, “This is one of the Hebrew children.”

2:7 And his sister said to Pharaoh’s daughter, “Do you want me to call a nursing woman from among the Hebrews, that she may nurse the child for you?”

2:8 Pharaoh’s daughter said to her, “Go.” The young woman went and called the child’s mother.

2:9 And Pharaoh’s daughter said to her, “Keep this child for me, and nurse him for me, and I will give you your wages.” And the woman took the child and nursed him.

2:10 When the child grew, she brought him to Pharaoh’s daughter, and he became her son. She named him Moses, saying, “I took him up out of the water.”

2:11 During those many days, when Moses had grown, he went out to his brothers, the sons of Israel. Seeing their suffering, he saw an Egyptian man striking a Hebrew, one of his own brothers, the sons of Israel.

2:12 He looked this way and that, and, seeing no one, struck down the Egyptian and hid him in the sand.

2:13 And he went out on the second day, and saw two Hebrew men fighting; and he said to the one who did wrong, “Why do you strike your neighbour?”

2:14 But he said, “Who made you a ruler and judge over us? Do you want to kill me, as you killed the Egyptian yesterday?” And Moses was afraid, and said, “Has this matter become so plainly known?”

2:15 Pharaoh heard about this and sought to kill Moses. Moses withdrew from Pharaoh’s presence and settled in the land of Midian. Having come to the land of Midian, he sat down by the well.

2:16 The priest of Midian had seven daughters who tended the sheep of their father Jethro. They came and drew water until they filled the troughs to water the sheep of their father Jethro.

2:17 The shepherds came and drove them away, but Moses rose and rescued them, drew water for them, and watered their sheep.

2:18 And they came to Reuel their father, and he said to them, “Why have you returned so quickly today?”

2:19 They said, “An Egyptian man rescued us from the shepherds. He drew water for us and watered our sheep.”

2:20 And he said to his daughters, “And where is he? Why have you left the man behind? Call him, therefore, that he may eat bread.”

2:21 Moses settled with the man, who gave him his daughter Zipporah as his wife.

2:22 And the woman conceived and bore a son; and Moses called his name Gershom, saying, “I am a sojourner in a foreign land.”

2:23 After those many days the king of Egypt died. The sons of Israel groaned deeply because of their labour and cried out, and their cry because of their labour rose to God.

2:24 God heard their groaning, and God remembered His covenant with Abraham, Isaac, and Jacob.

2:25 God looked upon the sons of Israel, and He became known to them.

Exodus 3

Read this chapter on staging · Book beginning

3:1 Moses was tending the sheep of Jethro his father-in-law, the priest of Midian. He led the sheep toward the wilderness and came to Mount Horeb.

3:2 An angel of the Lord appeared to him in a flame of fire from the bush. He saw that the bush was burning with fire, yet it was not consumed.

3:3 And Moses said, “I will turn aside and see this great sight, why the bush is not consumed.”

3:4 When the Lord saw that he was approaching to look, the Lord called to him from the bush, “Moses, Moses!” He replied, “What is it?”

3:5 And He said, “Do not come near here. Untie the footwear from your feet; for the place where you stand is holy ground.”

3:6 And He said to him, “I am the God of your father, the God of Abraham, and the God of Isaac, and the God of Jacob.” And Moses turned his face away, for he feared to look before God.

3:7 And the Lord said to Moses, “I have surely seen the affliction of My people in Egypt, and have heard their cry because of their taskmasters; for I know their pain.

3:8 I have come down to rescue them from the hand of the Egyptians and bring them out of that land into a good and spacious land, a land flowing with milk and honey, to the place of the Canaanites, Hittites, Amorites, Perizzites, Girgashites, Hivites, and Jebusites.

3:9 And now, behold, the cry of the sons of Israel has come to Me; and I have seen the oppression with which the Egyptians oppress them.

3:10 Come now, and I will send you to Pharaoh king of Egypt; and you shall bring My people, the sons of Israel, out of the land of Egypt.”

3:11 Moses said to God, “Who am I, that I should go to Pharaoh, king of Egypt, and bring the sons of Israel out of the land of Egypt?”

3:12 And God said to Moses, “I will be with you; and this shall be the sign to you that I send you: when you have brought My people out of Egypt, you shall serve God upon this mountain.”

3:13 And Moses said to God, “Behold, I shall go to the sons of Israel and say to them, ‘The God of your fathers has sent me to you’; and they shall ask me, ‘What is His name?’ What shall I say to them?”

3:14 And God said to Moses, “I am the One Who Is.” And He said, “This is what you shall say to the sons of Israel: ‘The One Who Is has sent me to you.’”

3:15 And God said again to Moses, “This is what you shall say to the sons of Israel: ‘The Lord, the God of your fathers, the God of Abraham, and the God of Isaac, and the God of Jacob, has sent me to you.’ This is My everlasting name, and My memorial from generation to generation.

3:16 Go, therefore, gather the council of elders of the sons of Israel, and say to them, ‘The Lord, the God of your fathers, the God of Abraham, and the God of Isaac, and the God of Jacob, has appeared to me, saying, “I have surely visited you, and seen all that has befallen you in Egypt.

3:17 And I have said, I will bring you up out of the affliction of the Egyptians, into the land of the Canaanites, and Hittites, and Amorites, and Perizzites, and Girgashites, and Hivites, and Jebusites, into a land flowing with milk and honey.”’

3:18 And they shall listen to your voice; and you and the council of elders of Israel shall go in to Pharaoh king of Egypt, and you shall say to him, ‘The God of the Hebrews has called us. Let us therefore go a journey of three days into the wilderness, that we may sacrifice to our God.’

3:19 I know that Pharaoh, king of Egypt, will not let you go unless by a strong hand.

3:20 I will stretch out My hand and strike the Egyptians with all My wonders that I will perform among them. Afterward he will send you away.

3:21 And I will give this people favour before the Egyptians; and when you depart, you shall not go empty-handed.

3:22 Every woman shall ask of her neighbour and of her fellow lodger articles of silver and gold, and clothing; and you shall put them upon your sons and upon your daughters, and you shall plunder the Egyptians.”

Exodus 4

Read this chapter on staging · Book beginning

4:1 And Moses answered and said, “If they do not believe me or listen to my voice, for they will say, ‘God has not appeared to you,’ what shall I say to them?”

4:2 And the Lord said to him, “What is this in your hand?” And he said, “A staff.”

4:3 He said, “Throw it on the ground.” He threw it on the ground, and it became a serpent; Moses fled from it.

4:4 And the Lord said to Moses, “Stretch out your hand, and take it by the tail.” And he stretched out his hand, and took it by the tail; and it became a staff in his hand.

4:5 “That they may believe you, that the Lord, the God of their fathers, the God of Abraham, and the God of Isaac, and the God of Jacob, has appeared to you.”

4:6 And the Lord said again to him, “Put your hand into your bosom.” And he put his hand into his bosom, and brought his hand out of his bosom; and his hand became as snow.

4:7 And He said, “Put your hand again into your bosom.” And he put his hand into his bosom, and brought it out of his bosom; and it was restored again to the colour of his flesh.

4:8 “And if they do not believe you or listen to the voice of the first sign, they shall believe you through the voice of the latter sign.

4:9 And if they do not believe you through these two signs or listen to your voice, you shall take some water from the river and pour it upon the dry ground; and the water which you take from the river shall become blood upon the dry ground.”

4:10 And Moses said to the Lord, “I beseech Thee, Lord, I have not been capable—not yesterday, nor the day before, nor since Thou didst begin to speak to Thy servant; for I am weak of voice and slow of tongue.”

4:11 The Lord said to Moses, “Who gave a human being a mouth? Who made one hard of hearing or deaf, seeing or blind? Is it not I, God?

4:12 Now therefore go, and I will open your mouth, and teach you what you are to speak.”

4:13 And Moses said, “I beseech Thee, Lord, appoint another who is able, whom Thou wilt send.”

4:14 And the Lord was kindled with anger against Moses, and said, “Behold, is Aaron the Levite not your brother? I know that he will surely speak to you; and behold, he shall come forth to meet you, and when he sees you, he shall rejoice within himself.

4:15 And you shall speak to him, and put My words in his mouth; and I will open your mouth and his mouth, and teach you what you shall do.

4:16 And he shall speak for you to the people, and he shall be your mouth; but you shall be for him in matters pertaining to God.

4:17 And you shall take in your hand this staff which was turned into a serpent, with which you shall perform the signs.”

4:18 Moses went back to Jethro his father-in-law and said, “I will return to my brothers in Egypt to see whether they are still alive.” Jethro said to Moses, “Go in health.”

4:19 And after those many days the king of Egypt died. And the Lord said to Moses in Midian, “Go, depart into Egypt; for all who sought your life are dead.”

4:20 Moses took his wife and children, set them on the beasts of burden, and returned toward Egypt. Moses took the staff he had from God in his hand.

4:21 And the Lord said to Moses, “As you go and return to Egypt, see that you perform before Pharaoh all the wonders which I have put in your hands. But I will harden his heart, and he shall by no means let the people go.

4:22 And you shall say to Pharaoh, ‘Thus says the Lord: Israel is My firstborn son.

4:23 And I said to you, “Let My people go, that they may serve Me.” If therefore you will not let them go, behold, I will kill your firstborn son.’”

4:24 On the way, at the lodging place, an angel of the Lord met him and sought to kill him.

4:25 And Zipporah took a stone, and circumcised the foreskin of her son, and fell at the feet, and said, “The blood of my child’s circumcision has ceased.”

4:26 And he departed from him, because she said, “The blood of my child’s circumcision has ceased.”

4:27 The Lord said to Aaron, “Go into the wilderness to meet Moses.” He went and met him at the mountain of God, and they kissed each other.

4:28 Moses told Aaron all the words of the Lord with which He had sent him, and all the signs He had commanded him.

4:29 Moses and Aaron went and gathered the council of elders of the sons of Israel.

4:30 Aaron spoke all these words God had spoken to Moses and performed the signs before the people.

4:31 And the people believed and rejoiced, because God had visited the sons of Israel, and because He had seen their affliction; and the people bowed down and worshipped.
