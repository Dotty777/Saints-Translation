Song of Songs 1

Read this chapter on staging

1:1The Song of Songs, which belongs to Solomon.

1:2Let him kiss me with the kisses of his mouth, for your breasts are better than wine.

1:3The fragrance of your perfumes surpasses all spices; your name is perfume poured out. That is why young women loved you.

1:4They drew you; we will run after you into the fragrance of your perfumes. The king brought me into his chamber. Let us exult and rejoice in you; we will love your breasts more than wine. Uprightness loved you.

1:5 I am black and beautiful, daughters of Jerusalem, as the tents of Kedar, as the tent coverings of Solomon.

1:6Do not look at me because I have been darkened, because the sun looked upon me. My mother’s sons fought against me; they made me a keeper in vineyards. My own vineyard I did not keep.

1:7Tell me, you whom my soul loved, where you pasture, where you rest at noon, lest I become like a woman covering herself beside your companions’ herds.

1:8If you do not know yourself, beautiful among women, go out along the heels of the flocks and pasture your young goats beside the shepherds’ tents.

1:9I have compared you, my companion, to my mare among Pharaoh’s chariots.

1:10How beautiful your cheeks have become, like turtledoves, and your neck like necklaces!

1:11We will make you likenesses of gold with dots of silver.

1:12 While the king was reclining, my nard gave its fragrance.

1:13 My kinsman is to me a bundle of liquid myrrh; he will lodge between my breasts.

1:14 My kinsman is to me a cluster of henna in the vineyards of En-gedi.

1:15Behold, you are beautiful, my companion! Behold, you are beautiful; your eyes are doves.

1:16Behold, you are beautiful, my kinsman, and indeed lovely! Moreover, our bed is shaded.

1:17 The beams of our houses are cedars; our ceiling panels are cypresses.

Saints English wording notes

1:2 · breast

Meaning. The bodily image in the speaker's praise.

Wording. Retain breasts and the shift from him to thy within the utterance. Do not replace bodily language with an abstract devotional term or assign a speaker identity solely to avoid the shift.

English comparison. Saints, Brenton, and DRC name breasts; KJV has love. DRC Canticle 1:1 corresponds to Greek Song 1:2 because it numbers the opening differently.

1:5 · beautiful

Meaning. The speaker's simultaneous description of colour and beauty.

Wording. Prefer and as the unmarked force of the Greek conjunction. Keep both affirmations and avoid making dark colour inherently contrary to beauty. Read the sun and vineyard context in the next verse separately.

English comparison. KJV, Brenton, and DRC use but. Saints uses and. An adversative construal should be identified as interpretation rather than assumed from the English tradition.

1:6 · vineyard

Meaning. The vineyard the speaker says she failed to guard.

Wording. Preserve the shift from vineyards entrusted to her to her own singular vineyard. The image may bear further poetic associations, but these belong in interpretation, not an explicit anatomical substitution.

English comparison. All witnesses keep vineyard. They differ in how they express the sun's effect and the brothers' hostility; those surrounding differences must remain attached to their own clauses.

1:13 · kinsman

Meaning. A kinship word used for the beloved figure in the poem.

Wording. Retain the inherited kinsman provisionally and explain the lover's role. The lexical kinship form does not by itself prove a biological relationship; beloved is a contextual alternative that loses that surface form.

English comparison. Saints and Brenton use kinsman; KJV and DRC beloved. The myrrh and lodging-between-breasts image remains bodily and poetic.

Song of Songs 2

Read this chapter on staging

2:1 I am a flower of the plain, a lily of the valleys.

2:2 Like a lily among thorns, so is my companion among the daughters.

2:3 Like an apple tree among the trees of the woodland, so is my kinsman among the sons. I desired his shade and sat there; his fruit was sweet in my throat.

2:4 Bring me into the house of wine; arrange love over me.

2:5 Strengthen me with sweet cakes, heap apples about me, for I am wounded by love.

2:6 His left hand is beneath my head, and his right hand will embrace me.

2:7I have adjured you, daughters of Jerusalem, by the powers and by the strengths of the field: do not awaken or stir up love until it wishes.

2:8The voice of my kinsman! Behold, he comes, leaping over the mountains, bounding over the hills.

2:9My kinsman is like a gazelle or a young stag upon the mountains of Bethel. Behold, he stands behind our wall, peering through the windows, looking out through the lattice.

2:10My kinsman answers and says to me: Rise, come, my companion, my beautiful one, my dove,

2:11for behold, winter has passed; the rain has gone, departed,

2:12the blossoms have appeared in the land; the time of pruning has arrived; the voice of the turtledove has been heard in our land.

2:13The fig tree has brought forth its early figs; the vines are flowering and have given fragrance. Rise, come, my companion, my beautiful one, my dove,

2:14and come, my dove, in the shelter of the rock, close to the outer wall. Show me your face and let me hear your voice, for your voice is sweet and your face lovely.

2:15 Catch for us the little foxes that destroy vineyards, for our vines are flowering.

2:16My kinsman is mine, and I am his, he who pastures among the lilies,

2:17until the day breathes and the shadows move. Turn back, my kinsman; be like a gazelle or a young stag upon mountains of hollows.

Saints English wording notes

2:1 · flower of the plain

Meaning. An unspecified flower associated with open level country.

Wording. Retain the broad plant term and the landscape description. Keep lily in the parallel clause; neither member authorizes a precise modern botanical identification without evidence.

English comparison. KJV names the rose of Sharon. Saints and Brenton have flower of the plain; DRC flower of the field. The Greek phrase does not supply the proper name Sharon.

2:4 · house of wine

Meaning. The wine-associated setting into which the speaker asks to be brought.

Wording. Preserve the two plural commands bring me and arrange love over me. The setting can be glossed as a place for wine; the Greek clause does not explicitly name a banner.

English comparison. KJV and DRC recount he brought me, whereas Greek and Brenton command bring me. KJV's banner differs from the Greek arranging verb and its object love.

2:5 · wounded

Meaning. Love's effect described through the image of a wound.

Wording. Retain the wound image and feminine speaker. The first provision in this verse is lexically difficult: the inherited sweet cakes needs further review against perfumes and flowers; this record does not settle that object.

English comparison. Brenton has wounded with love, KJV sick of love, and DRC languish with love. Their food or fragrance wording differs sharply, so these objects must not be harmonized automatically.

2:7 · adjure

Meaning. Bind the addressed daughters by a solemn charge.

Wording. Adjure preserves the oath-like force; I have adjured records the tense more closely. Keep the Greek powers and strengths of the field and explain the uncertain oath formula without importing animals as explicit nouns.

English comparison. KJV and DRC name deer; Brenton powers and virtues. Virtues here means powers or strengths, not a catalogue of moral qualities. The closing verb has no expressed personal subject.

2:15 · fox

Meaning. The animals threatening the vineyards in the poem.

Wording. Keep the animals and the plural request catch for us. Spiritual applications to vices may be useful reception, but the translation should preserve the vineyard scene and its uncertain horticultural details.

English comparison. All witnesses retain foxes. KJV and Brenton describe tender grapes, Saints flowering vines, and DRC a flourished vineyard; the closing plant verb needs its own contextual judgment.

Song of Songs 3

Read this chapter on staging

3:1 On my bed at night I sought the one my soul loved; I sought him but did not find him. I called him, but he did not answer me.

3:2 I will rise now and go around the city, through the marketplaces and squares, and seek the one my soul loved. I sought him but did not find him.

3:3The watchmen going around the city found me. “Have you perhaps seen the one my soul loved?”

3:4 Scarcely had I passed them when I found the one my soul loved. I held him and will not let him go until I have brought him into my mother’s house, into the chamber of the one who conceived me.

3:5I have adjured you, daughters of Jerusalem, by the powers and by the strengths of the field: do not awaken or stir up love until it wishes.

3:6 Who is this coming up from the wilderness like columns of smoke, perfumed with myrrh and frankincense, with every powder of the perfumer?

3:7 Behold, Solomon’s couch! Sixty mighty men surround it, from the mighty men of Israel,

3:8all holding a sword, trained for war, each with his sword upon his thigh, against terror in the night.

3:9 King Solomon made himself a carrying chair from the timber of Lebanon.

3:10 He made its pillars silver and its reclining support gold; its step was purple, its interior paved with stone—love from the daughters of Jerusalem.

3:11 Go out and look at King Solomon wearing the crown with which his mother crowned him on his wedding day, on the day his heart rejoiced.

Saints English wording notes

3:1 · seek

Meaning. The speaker's repeated search for the beloved.

Wording. Preserve the repetition and retain the Greek closing call without an answer. The emotional movement comes from these simple actions; ornament should not add new pursuit or reunion events.

English comparison. KJV and DRC end after not finding him. Saints and Brenton also preserve calling him and receiving no answer, as in the checked Greek.

Song of Songs 4

Read this chapter on staging

4:1Behold, you are beautiful, my companion! Behold, you are beautiful; your eyes are doves apart from your silence. Your hair is like herds of goats that have appeared from Gilead.

4:2Your teeth are like herds of shorn sheep that came up from bathing, all bearing twins, with none childless among them.

4:3Your lips are like a scarlet cord, and your speech is lovely. Your cheek is like a pomegranate rind apart from your silence.

4:4Your neck is like David’s tower, built for thalpioth; a thousand shields hang upon it, all the missiles of mighty men.

4:5Your two breasts are like two fawns, twins of a gazelle, grazing among lilies.

4:6Until the day breathes and the shadows move, I will go to the mountain of myrrh and the hill of Lebanon.

4:7You are altogether beautiful, my companion, and there is no blemish in you.

4:8Come from Lebanon, bride, come from Lebanon. You will come and pass through from the beginning of faith, from the summit of Senir and Hermon, from lions’ enclosures, from leopards’ mountains.

4:9You have stirred our hearts, my sister, bride; you have stirred our hearts with one of your eyes, with one ornament of your neck.

4:10How beautiful your breasts have become, my sister, bride! How much more beautiful your breasts than wine, and the fragrance of your garments than all spices!

4:11Your lips drip honeycomb sweetness, bride; honey and milk are beneath your tongue, and the fragrance of your garments is like the fragrance of Lebanon.

4:12 A garden enclosed is my sister, my bride; a garden enclosed, a fountain sealed.

4:13Your outgrowths are an orchard of pomegranates with tree fruits, henna with nard,

4:14 nard and saffron, aromatic reed and cinnamon, with all the trees of Lebanon, myrrh, aloth, with all the finest perfumes,

4:15 a fountain of gardens, a well of living water rushing from Lebanon.

4:16 Awake, north wind, and come, south wind! Blow through my garden, and let my spices flow. Let my kinsman come down into his garden and eat its tree fruits.

Saints English wording notes

4:12 · sealed

Meaning. A spring pictured as sealed within the enclosed-garden comparison.

Wording. Keep fountain and the sealing image together with the repeated garden. The passage's poetic or later spiritual implications should be explained without replacing its visible objects.

English comparison. Saints, Brenton, and DRC repeat garden; KJV changes the second member to spring shut up. The Greek retains the repeated garden before the sealed fountain.

Song of Songs 5

Read this chapter on staging

5:1 I entered my garden, my sister, bride; I gathered my myrrh with my spices. I ate my bread with my honey; I drank my wine with my milk. Eat, companions, and drink and become drunk, brothers!

5:2I sleep, but my heart keeps watch. The voice of my kinsman—he knocks upon the door! Open to me, my sister, my companion, my dove, my perfect one, for my head is filled with dew and my curls with the drops of night.

5:3 I took off my tunic; how could I put it on? I washed my feet; how could I soil them?

5:4 My kinsman extended his hand from the opening, and my belly was stirred for him.

5:5 I rose to open to my kinsman; my hands dripped myrrh, my fingers rich myrrh, upon the handles of the bolt.

5:6 I opened to my kinsman; my kinsman had gone. My soul went out at his word. I sought him but did not find him; I called him, but he did not answer me.

5:7 The guards going around the city found me; they struck me, they wounded me. The guards of the walls took my light covering from me.

5:8I have adjured you, daughters of Jerusalem, by the powers and by the strengths of the field: if you find my kinsman, what will you tell him? That I am wounded by love.

5:9What makes your kinsman more than another kinsman, beautiful among women? What makes your kinsman more than another kinsman, that you have adjured us this way?

5:10 My kinsman is white and ruddy, selected from myriads.

5:11 His head is gold and phaz; his curls are fir trees, black as a raven,

5:12 his eyes are like doves beside full waters, bathed in milk, seated beside full waters,

5:13 his cheeks are like bowls of spice growing fragrant substances; his lips are lilies dripping rich myrrh,

5:14 his hands are sculpted gold filled with tharsis; his belly is an ivory tablet upon sapphire stone,

5:15 his legs are marble pillars founded upon golden bases; his appearance is like Lebanon, choice as cedars,

5:16 his throat is sweetness, and all of him is desire. This is my kinsman, and this is my companion, daughters of Jerusalem.

Saints English wording notes

5:10 · ruddy

Meaning. A colour description of the beloved figure.

Wording. Retain both colour terms; ruddy means reddish or red-toned in this praise. Do not turn the words into a modern racial classification or supply a biological explanation of the complexion.

English comparison. All four witnesses preserve white and ruddy. Saints and Brenton retain myriads in the following selection phrase, where the other witnesses use thousands or ten thousand.

Song of Songs 7

Read this chapter on staging

7:1Return, return, Shulammite! Return, return, and we will look at you. What will you see in the Shulammite? She comes like dances of the camps.

7:2How beautiful your steps in sandals have become, daughter of Nadab! The rhythms of your thighs are like necklaces, the work of a craftsman’s hands.

7:3Your navel is a sculpted mixing bowl, not lacking mixed wine; your belly is a heap of grain fenced about with lilies.

7:4Your two breasts are like two fawns, twins of a gazelle.

7:5Your neck is like an ivory tower; your eyes are like pools in Heshbon at the gates of the daughter of many. Your nose is like Lebanon’s tower looking toward Damascus.

7:6Your head upon you is like Carmel, and the braid of your head is like purple; a king is bound in passageways.

7:7How beautiful and how delightful you have become, love, in your delights!

7:8This stature of yours has been compared to a palm tree, and your breasts to clusters.

7:9I said: I will climb the palm tree; I will grasp its heights. Then your breasts will be like clusters of the vine, and the fragrance of your nose like apples,

7:10and your throat like good wine, going straight to my kinsman, suited to my lips and teeth.

7:11 I am my kinsman’s, and his turning is toward me.

7:12 Come, my kinsman, let us go out into the field; let us lodge in the villages.

7:13Let us rise early for the vineyards; let us see whether the vine has blossomed, whether the bloom has flowered, whether the pomegranates have flowered. There I will give you my breasts.

7:14The mandrakes have given fragrance, and at our doors are all tree fruits, new together with old, which I have kept for you, my kinsman.

Saints English wording notes

Wording. Singular address uses thou, thee, thy, and thine; plural address uses ye, you, and your. Source-sensitive images, inherited numbering, and uncertainties are retained.

Song of Songs 8

Read this chapter on staging

8:1Who might make you my kinsman, nursing at my mother’s breasts? Finding you outside, I will kiss you, and indeed they will not despise me.

8:2I will take you, bring you into my mother’s house and into the chamber of the one who conceived me. I will give you spiced wine to drink, the juice of my pomegranates.

8:3 His left hand is beneath my head, and his right hand will embrace me.

8:4I have adjured you, daughters of Jerusalem, by the powers and by the strengths of the field: why awaken or stir up love before it wishes?

8:5Who is this coming up, made white, leaning upon her kinsman? Beneath the apple tree I awakened you. There your mother labored with you; there she who bore you labored.

8:6Set me as a seal upon your heart, as a seal upon your arm, for love is strong as death, jealousy harsh as Hades. Its wings are wings of fire, its flames.

8:7Much water will not be able to quench love, nor will rivers flood it. If a man gives all his livelihood for love, they will utterly despise him.

8:8We have a little sister, and she has no breasts. What shall we do for our sister on the day someone speaks concerning her?

8:9 If she is a wall, let us build silver battlements upon her; and if she is a door, let us mark out a cedar plank upon her.

8:10I am a wall, and my breasts are like towers. I was in his eyes like one who finds peace.

8:11 Solomon had a vineyard at Baal-hamon; he gave his vineyard to keepers. Each will bring a thousand pieces of silver for its fruit.

8:12My own vineyard is before me. The thousand are yours, Solomon, and the two hundred belong to those who keep its fruit.

8:13You who sit in gardens, companions are attending to your voice; let me hear it.

8:14 Flee, my kinsman, and be like a gazelle or a young stag upon mountains of spices.

Saints English wording notes

8:6 · jealousy

Meaning. The intense exclusive passion compared with the realm of death.

Wording. Keep love and jealousy as related but distinct terms, with Hades rather than an unlabelled modern sense of hell. The difficult wing or shaft imagery in the final clause requires separate review.

English comparison. KJV and Brenton compare jealousy with the grave, DRC with hell. The English witnesses differ among coals, shafts, and lamps in the final image; these are not one settled Greek object.

Declared Greek chapter · Lexical decision carried forward from the supplied Saints Biblical Lexicon, 26 September 2026; independent interpretation review remains outstanding.

8:7 · quench

Meaning. Extinguish love through the image of water overcoming fire.

Wording. Retain quench and the parallel inability of rivers to overwhelm love. Preserve the distinction between love and the later offer of all one's livelihood; the poem's image supplies its own intensity.

English comparison. DRC opens with charity where the others say love. Its concluding pronouns and agency also differ from Saints and the Greek; charity here need not mean almsgiving.

Current Bespoke editorial notes

The newer Bespoke Saints English (1).pdf governs, with accepted Genesis 1–11 as the English benchmark. It supersedes the older Founding Style Guide where their pronoun rules conflict: Thou/Thee/Thy address God directly; ordinary address uses you/your.

Keep the desired kinship, the chamber of the one who conceived me, the future embrace, and the last oath as a question rather than harmonizing it with earlier commands. Retain made white without adding from the wilderness, both labor clauses, seal/heart/arm, jealousy and Hades, repeated wings of fire, water/rivers, livelihood and the person despised. Gives/will despise in 7 makes the prospective conditional explicit. Keep the little sister, wall/door options, figures of one thousand and two hundred and the final flight. The person addressed in 13 is grammatically masculine in the consulted Greek; no divine addressee is asserted.
