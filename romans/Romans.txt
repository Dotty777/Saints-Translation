ROMANS 1

1:1 Paul, a slave of Christ Jesus, called as an apostle, set apart for the gospel of God,
1:2 which He promised beforehand through His prophets in holy Scriptures,
1:3 concerning His Son, who came from David’s seed according to the flesh,
1:4 who was designated Son of God in power according to the Spirit of holiness by resurrection from the dead, Jesus Christ our Lord,
1:5 through whom we received grace and apostleship for the obedience of faith among all the nations for His name,
1:6 among whom you also are, called by Jesus Christ:
1:7 to all who are in Rome, beloved of God, called as saints: grace to you and peace from God our Father and the Lord Jesus Christ.
1:8 First, I thank my God through Jesus Christ for all of you, because your faith is proclaimed in the whole world.
1:9 For God, whom I serve in my spirit in the gospel of His Son, is my witness that I remember you without ceasing,
1:10 always in my prayers asking whether somehow now at last I may be granted a way, by the will of God, to come to you.
1:11 For I long to see you, to share with you some spiritual gift so that you may be strengthened,
1:12 that is, to be encouraged together among you through one another’s faith, both yours and mine.
1:13 And I do not want you to be unaware, brothers, that I often planned to come to you—and was hindered until now—so that I might have some fruit among you also, just as among the other nations.
1:14 I am a debtor both to Greeks and to those who do not speak Greek, both to the wise and to the senseless.
1:15 Thus, for my part, I am eager to proclaim the gospel also to you who are in Rome.
1:16 For I am not ashamed of the gospel, for it is God’s power for salvation to everyone who believes, to the Jew first and also to the Greek.
1:17 For in it the righteousness of God is revealed from faith to faith, as it is written, “But the righteous one will live by faith.”
1:18 For God’s wrath is revealed from heaven against all ungodliness and unrighteousness of people who suppress the truth in unrighteousness,
1:19 because what is known of God is plain among them, for God made it plain to them.
1:20 For His invisible qualities, both His eternal power and divinity, have been clearly seen since the creation of the world, understood through the things made, so that they are without excuse,
1:21 because, although they knew God, they did not glorify Him as God or give thanks, but became futile in their thoughts, and their senseless heart was darkened.
1:22 Claiming to be wise, they became foolish,
1:23 and exchanged the glory of the incorruptible God for the likeness of an image of corruptible humanity, and of birds and four-footed animals and creeping things.
1:24 Therefore God handed them over, in the desires of their hearts, to impurity, so that their bodies would be dishonored among them,
1:25 who exchanged the truth of God for the lie, and revered and worshiped the creation instead of the Creator, who is blessed throughout the ages. Amen.
1:26 For this reason God handed them over to dishonorable passions; for their females exchanged natural relations for those contrary to nature,
1:27 and likewise the males, leaving natural relations with the female, burned in their desire for one another, males with males committing what is shameful and receiving in themselves the due penalty for their error.
1:28 And just as they did not see fit to retain God in their knowledge, God handed them over to an unfit mind, to do what is not fitting:
1:29 filled with all unrighteousness, wickedness, greed, evil; full of envy, murder, strife, deceit, malice; whisperers,
1:30 slanderers, haters of God, insolent, proud, boastful, inventors of evils, disobedient to parents,
1:31 without understanding, faithless, without natural affection, merciless;
1:32 who, knowing God’s righteous decree, that those who practice such things are worthy of death, not only do them, but also approve those who practice them.

ROMANS 2

2:1 Therefore you are without excuse, O man—whoever you are who judges. For when you judge another, you condemn yourself, because you who judge practice the same things.
2:2 And we know that God’s judgment against those who practice such things is according to truth.
2:3 But do you think, O man—you who judge those who practice such things and do them yourself—that you will escape God’s judgment?
2:4 Or do you despise the wealth of His kindness and forbearance and patience, not knowing that God’s kindness leads you to repentance?
2:5 But by your hardness and unrepentant heart, you store up wrath for yourself on the day of wrath and revelation of God’s righteous judgment,
2:6 who will repay each according to his works:
2:7 to those who, through perseverance in good work, seek glory and honor and incorruptibility, eternal life;
2:8 but to those acting from selfish ambition, who disobey the truth but obey unrighteousness, wrath and fury,
2:9 affliction and distress upon every human soul that does evil—the Jew first and also the Greek;
2:10 but glory and honor and peace to everyone who does good, to the Jew first and also to the Greek.
2:11 For there is no partiality with God.
2:12 For all who sinned without law will also perish without law; and all who sinned under law will be judged through law.
2:13 For it is not the hearers of law who are righteous before God, but the doers of law will be justified.
2:14 For whenever Gentiles who do not have law do by nature the things of the law, these, though not having law, are a law to themselves,
2:15 who show the work of the law written in their hearts, their conscience bearing witness with it, and their reasonings among themselves accusing or even defending,
2:16 on the day when God judges people’s secrets according to my gospel through Christ Jesus.
2:17 But if you bear the name Jew, and rest upon law and boast in God,
2:18 and know His will and approve the things that matter, being instructed from the law,
2:19 and are confident that you yourself are a guide of the blind, a light to those in darkness,
2:20 an instructor of the senseless, a teacher of infants, having in the law the embodiment of knowledge and truth—
2:21 you then who teach another, do you not teach yourself? You who proclaim that one must not steal, do you steal?
2:22 You who say that one must not commit adultery, do you commit adultery? You who abhor idols, do you rob temples?
2:23 You who boast in law, do you dishonor God through transgression of the law?
2:24 For “the name of God is blasphemed among the nations because of you,” just as it is written.
2:25 For circumcision benefits you if you practice law; but if you are a transgressor of law, your circumcision has become uncircumcision.
2:26 So if the uncircumcised person keeps the law’s righteous requirements, will not his uncircumcision be reckoned as circumcision?
2:27 And the one uncircumcised by nature who fulfills the law will judge you who, with the written letter and circumcision, are a transgressor of law.
2:28 For the Jew is not the one outwardly so, nor is circumcision what is outward in flesh;
2:29 but the Jew is the one inwardly so, and circumcision is of the heart, in Spirit, not in letter, whose praise is not from people but from God.

ROMANS 3

3:1 What, then, is the Jew’s advantage, or what is the benefit of circumcision?
3:2 Much in every way. First, indeed, because they were entrusted with the oracles of God.
3:3 For what if some were unfaithful? Will their unfaithfulness nullify God’s faithfulness?
3:4 May it never be! But let God be true, and every human being a liar, just as it is written, “So that Thou mayest be justified in Thy words, and wilt prevail when Thou art judged.”
3:5 But if our unrighteousness demonstrates God’s righteousness, what shall we say? Is God unrighteous when He brings wrath? I speak in human terms.
3:6 May it never be! For then how will God judge the world?
3:7 But if God’s truth abounded through my falsehood to His glory, why am I still judged as a sinner?
3:8 And why not say—as we are slandered and as some claim we say—“Let us do evil things so that good things may come”? Their condemnation is just.
3:9 What, then? Are we better off? Not at all; for we have already charged both Jews and Greeks with all being under sin,
3:10 just as it is written: “There is no righteous one, not even one;
3:11 there is no one who understands; there is no one who seeks God.
3:12 All turned aside; together they became useless. There is no one who shows kindness; there is not even one.
3:13 Their throat is an opened tomb; with their tongues they practiced deceit; the poison of asps is under their lips;
3:14 their mouth is full of cursing and bitterness.
3:15 Their feet are swift to pour out blood;
3:16 ruin and misery are in their ways,
3:17 and the way of peace they did not know.
3:18 There is no fear of God before their eyes.”
3:19 And we know that whatever the law says, it speaks to those under the law, so that every mouth may be stopped and the whole world may become answerable to God;
3:20 because by works of law no flesh will be justified before Him, for through law comes knowledge of sin.
3:21 But now, apart from law, God’s righteousness has been revealed, witnessed by the law and the prophets:
3:22 God’s righteousness through faith in Jesus Christ, for all who believe. For there is no distinction,
3:23 for all sinned and fall short of God’s glory,
3:24 being justified freely by His grace through the redemption that is in Christ Jesus,
3:25 whom God set forth as a propitiation in His blood, through faith, to demonstrate His righteousness because of the passing over of sins committed beforehand,
3:26 in God’s forbearance, to demonstrate His righteousness at the present time, so that He may be righteous and justify the one who has faith in Jesus.
3:27 Where, then, is boasting? It was excluded. Through what kind of law? Of works? No, but through a law of faith.
3:28 For we reckon that a person is justified by faith apart from works of law.
3:29 Or is God the God of Jews only? Is He not also of Gentiles? Yes, also of Gentiles,
3:30 since God is one, who will justify the circumcised from faith and the uncircumcised through faith.
3:31 Do we then nullify law through faith? May it never be! Rather, we establish law.

ROMANS 4

4:1 What, then, shall we say Abraham, our forefather according to the flesh, has found?
4:2 For if Abraham was justified by works, he has something to boast of—but not before God.
4:3 For what does the Scripture say? “And Abraham believed God, and it was reckoned to him as righteousness.”
4:4 Now to the one who works, the wage is not reckoned according to grace, but according to what is owed.
4:5 But to the one who does not work, but believes in the One who justifies the ungodly, his faith is reckoned as righteousness,
4:6 just as David also speaks of the blessedness of the person to whom God reckons righteousness apart from works:
4:7 “Blessed are those whose lawless deeds were forgiven and whose sins were covered;
4:8 blessed is the man whose sin the Lord certainly will not reckon.”
4:9 Is this blessedness, then, upon the circumcised, or also upon the uncircumcised? For we say, “Faith was reckoned to Abraham as righteousness.”
4:10 How, then, was it reckoned? While he was circumcised, or uncircumcised? Not while circumcised, but while uncircumcised.
4:11 And he received the sign of circumcision, a seal of the righteousness of the faith he had while uncircumcised, so that he might be father of all who believe while uncircumcised, so that righteousness might be reckoned to them also,
4:12 and father of the circumcised, to those who are not only circumcised but also walk in the footsteps of the faith our father Abraham had while uncircumcised.
4:13 For the promise to Abraham or to his seed that he would be heir of the world was not through law, but through the righteousness of faith.
4:14 For if those from law are heirs, faith has been emptied and the promise has been nullified;
4:15 for the law brings wrath, but where there is no law, neither is there transgression.
4:16 For this reason it is from faith, so that it may be according to grace, so that the promise may be secure to all the seed—not only to the one from the law, but also to the one from Abraham’s faith. He is father of us all,
4:17 just as it is written, “I have made you father of many nations,” before the God whom he believed, who gives life to the dead and calls the things that are not as though they are.
4:18 Against hope, he believed in hope, so that he might become father of many nations, according to what had been said, “So shall your seed be.”
4:19 And without growing weak in faith, he considered his own body, already deadened as it was, being about a hundred years old, and the deadness of Sarah’s womb;
4:20 but with regard to God’s promise he did not waver in unbelief, but was strengthened in faith, giving glory to God,
4:21 and being fully assured that what He has promised He is also able to do.
4:22 Therefore it was also reckoned to him as righteousness.
4:23 But “it was reckoned to him” was not written for his sake alone,
4:24 but also for our sake, to whom it is to be reckoned, who believe in the One who raised Jesus our Lord from the dead,
4:25 who was handed over for our trespasses and was raised for our justification.

ROMANS 5

5:1 Therefore, having been justified from faith, we have peace with God through our Lord Jesus Christ,
5:2 through whom we have also obtained access by faith into this grace in which we stand; and we boast in hope of God’s glory.
5:3 And not only this, but we also boast in afflictions, knowing that affliction produces perseverance,
5:4 and perseverance, proven character, and proven character, hope.
5:5 And hope does not put us to shame, because God’s love has been poured out in our hearts through the Holy Spirit who was given to us.
5:6 For while we were still weak, even then Christ died at the appointed time for the ungodly.
5:7 For rarely will someone die for a righteous person; though perhaps for the good person someone even dares to die.
5:8 But God demonstrates His own love toward us in that, while we were still sinners, Christ died for us.
5:9 Much more, then, having now been justified in His blood, we will be saved through Him from wrath.
5:10 For if, while we were enemies, we were reconciled to God through the death of His Son, much more, having been reconciled, we will be saved in His life.
5:11 And not only this, but we also boast in God through our Lord Jesus Christ, through whom we have now received reconciliation.
5:12 For this reason, just as through one person sin entered the world, and through sin, death, and so death passed to all people, because all sinned—
5:13 for before the law came, sin was in the world, but sin is not charged to account when there is no law;
5:14 nevertheless death reigned from Adam until Moses, even over those who did not sin in the likeness of Adam’s transgression, who is a type of the One to come.
5:15 But the gift is not like the trespass. For if by the one man’s trespass the many died, much more did God’s grace and the gift in the grace of the one Man, Jesus Christ, abound to the many.
5:16 And the gift is not like what came through one who sinned; for the judgment from one trespass resulted in condemnation, but the gift from many trespasses resulted in justification.
5:17 For if, by the one man’s trespass, death reigned through the one, much more will those who receive the abundance of grace and the gift of righteousness reign in life through the One, Jesus Christ.
5:18 So then, as through one trespass there was condemnation for all people, so also through one righteous act there is justification of life for all people.
5:19 For just as through the disobedience of the one person the many were made sinners, so also through the obedience of the One the many will be made righteous.
5:20 But law came in alongside, so that the trespass might increase; but where sin increased, grace abounded beyond measure,
5:21 so that, just as sin reigned in death, so also grace might reign through righteousness for eternal life through Jesus Christ our Lord.

ROMANS 6

6:1 What, then, shall we say? Should we remain in sin so that grace may increase?
6:2 May it never be! We who died to sin, how will we still live in it?
6:3 Or do you not know that all of us who were baptized into Christ Jesus were baptized into His death?
6:4 Therefore we were buried with Him through baptism into death, so that, just as Christ was raised from the dead through the Father’s glory, so we also might walk in newness of life.
6:5 For if we have become united with the likeness of His death, we will also be united with that of His resurrection,
6:6 knowing this, that our old self was crucified with Him, so that the body of sin might be rendered powerless, so that we would no longer be enslaved to sin;
6:7 for the one who died has been justified from sin.
6:8 And if we died with Christ, we believe that we will also live with Him,
6:9 knowing that Christ, having been raised from the dead, no longer dies; death no longer rules over Him.
6:10 For the death He died, He died to sin once for all; but the life He lives, He lives to God.
6:11 So also reckon yourselves to be dead to sin, but alive to God in Christ Jesus.
6:12 Therefore do not let sin reign in your mortal body, so that you obey its desires;
6:13 Do not present the parts of your body to sin as weapons of unrighteousness, but present yourselves to God as those alive from the dead, and the parts of your body to God as weapons of righteousness.
6:14 For sin will not rule over you, for you are not under law, but under grace.
6:15 What, then? Should we sin because we are not under law, but under grace? May it never be!
6:16 Do you not know that when you present yourselves to someone as slaves to obey, you are slaves of the one whom you obey, either of sin leading to death, or of obedience leading to righteousness?
6:17 But thanks be to God that, though you were slaves of sin, you obeyed from the heart the pattern of teaching to which you were handed over;
6:18 and, having been set free from sin, you were enslaved to righteousness.
6:19 I speak in human terms because of the weakness of your flesh. For just as you presented the parts of your body as slaves to impurity and to lawlessness leading to lawlessness, so now present the parts of your body as slaves to righteousness leading to sanctification.
6:20 For when you were slaves of sin, you were free from righteousness.
6:21 What fruit, then, did you have at that time from the things of which you are now ashamed? For the end of those things is death.
6:22 But now, having been set free from sin and enslaved to God, you have your fruit leading to sanctification, and the end, eternal life.
6:23 For sin’s wages are death, but God’s gift is eternal life in Christ Jesus our Lord.

ROMANS 7

7:1 Or do you not know, brothers—for I speak to those who know law—that the law rules over a person for as long as he lives?
7:2 For the married woman is bound by law to her living husband; but if the husband dies, she has been released from the law of the husband.
7:3 So then, while the husband lives, she will be called an adulteress if she belongs to another man; but if the husband dies, she is free from the law, so that she is not an adulteress when she belongs to another man.
7:4 So, my brothers, you also were put to death to the law through the body of Christ, so that you might belong to Another, the One raised from the dead, so that we might bear fruit for God.
7:5 For when we were in the flesh, the sinful passions aroused through the law were at work in the parts of our body, to bear fruit for death.
7:6 But now we were released from the law, having died to that in which we were held, so that we serve in the newness of the Spirit and not in the oldness of the letter.
7:7 What, then, shall we say? Is the law sin? May it never be! Rather, I did not know sin except through law; for I would not have known desire if the law had not said, “You shall not desire.”
7:8 But sin, taking opportunity through the commandment, produced every desire in me; for apart from law sin is dead.
7:9 And I was once alive apart from law; but when the commandment came, sin came to life,
7:10 and I died; and the commandment that was for life proved to bring me death.
7:11 For sin, taking opportunity through the commandment, deceived me and through it killed me.
7:12 So the law is holy, and the commandment holy and righteous and good.
7:13 Did the good, then, become death to me? May it never be! But sin did, so that it might appear as sin, producing death in me through the good, so that through the commandment sin might become sinful beyond measure.
7:14 For we know that the law is spiritual; but I am of flesh, sold under sin.
7:15 For I do not understand what I do; for I do not practice what I want, but what I hate, this I do.
7:16 But if I do what I do not want, I agree with the law that it is good.
7:17 But now it is no longer I who do it, but sin dwelling in me.
7:18 For I know that good does not dwell in me—that is, in my flesh. For the will is there, but doing what is good is not.
7:19 For I do not do the good that I want, but the evil that I do not want, this I practice.
7:20 But if I do what I do not want, it is no longer I who do it, but sin dwelling in me.
7:21 So I find this law: when I want to do the good, evil is at hand for me.
7:22 For I delight in God’s law in my inner self,
7:23 but I see another law in the parts of my body, waging war against the law of my mind and taking me captive in the law of sin that is in the parts of my body.
7:24 Wretched person that I am! Who will rescue me from the body of this death?
7:25 Thanks be to God through Jesus Christ our Lord. So then I myself, with the mind, serve God’s law, but with the flesh, sin’s law.

ROMANS 8

8:1 So then, there is now no condemnation for those in Christ Jesus.
8:2 For the law of the Spirit of life in Christ Jesus set you free from the law of sin and death.
8:3 For what the law could not do, in that it was weak through the flesh, God did: sending His own Son in the likeness of sinful flesh and concerning sin, He condemned sin in the flesh,
8:4 so that the law’s righteous requirement might be fulfilled in us who walk not according to flesh, but according to Spirit.
8:5 For those who are according to flesh set their minds on the things of the flesh, but those according to Spirit, on the things of the Spirit.
8:6 For the mind of the flesh is death, but the mind of the Spirit is life and peace,
8:7 because the mind of the flesh is hostility toward God; for it does not submit to God’s law, nor indeed can it.
8:8 And those who are in flesh cannot please God.
8:9 But you are not in flesh, but in Spirit, if indeed God’s Spirit dwells in you. But if anyone does not have Christ’s Spirit, that person does not belong to Him.
8:10 But if Christ is in you, the body is dead because of sin, but the Spirit is life because of righteousness.
8:11 And if the Spirit of the One who raised Jesus from the dead dwells in you, the One who raised Christ from the dead will also give life to your mortal bodies through His Spirit dwelling in you.
8:12 So then, brothers, we are debtors—not to the flesh, to live according to flesh;
8:13 for if you live according to flesh, you are going to die; but if by Spirit you put to death the body’s practices, you will live.
8:14 For all who are led by God’s Spirit are sons of God.
8:15 For you did not receive a spirit of slavery leading again to fear, but you received the Spirit of adoption as sons, in whom we cry, “Abba, Father.”
8:16 The Spirit Himself bears witness with our spirit that we are God’s children.
8:17 And if children, then also heirs: heirs of God and joint heirs with Christ, if indeed we suffer with Him so that we may also be glorified with Him.
8:18 For I reckon that the sufferings of the present time are not worth comparing with the glory that is to be revealed in us.
8:19 For creation eagerly awaits the revealing of God’s sons;
8:20 for creation was subjected to futility, not willingly, but because of the One who subjected it, in hope
8:21 that creation itself also will be set free from slavery to corruption into the freedom of the glory of God’s children.
8:22 For we know that the whole creation groans together and labors in birth pangs together until now;
8:23 and not only creation, but we ourselves also, having the firstfruits of the Spirit, groan within ourselves, awaiting adoption as sons, the redemption of our body.
8:24 For in hope we were saved. But hope that is seen is not hope; for who hopes for what he sees?
8:25 But if we hope for what we do not see, we await it with perseverance.
8:26 And likewise the Spirit also helps our weakness; for we do not know what to pray for as we ought, but the Spirit Himself intercedes with groanings beyond words.
8:27 And the One who searches hearts knows the mind of the Spirit, because He intercedes for saints according to God.
8:28 And we know that all things work together for good for those who love God, those who are called according to purpose.
8:29 Because those whom He foreknew, He also predestined to be conformed to the image of His Son, so that He might be firstborn among many brothers;
8:30 and those whom He predestined, these He also called; and those whom He called, these He also justified; and those whom He justified, these He also glorified.
8:31 What, then, shall we say to these things? If God is for us, who is against us?
8:32 He who did not spare His own Son, but handed Him over for us all, how will He not also graciously give us all things with Him?
8:33 Who will bring a charge against God’s chosen ones? God is the One who justifies.
8:34 Who is the one who condemns? Christ Jesus is the One who died—more than that, was raised—who is also at God’s right hand, who also intercedes for us.
8:35 Who will separate us from Christ’s love? Affliction, or distress, or persecution, or famine, or nakedness, or danger, or sword?
8:36 Just as it is written, “For Thy sake we are put to death all day long; we were reckoned as sheep for slaughter.”
8:37 But in all these things we more than conquer through the One who loved us.
8:38 For I am convinced that neither death nor life, neither angels nor rulers, neither things present nor things to come nor powers,
8:39 neither height nor depth nor any other created thing will be able to separate us from God’s love in Christ Jesus our Lord.

ROMANS 9

9:1 I speak truth in Christ; I do not lie, my conscience bearing witness with me in the Holy Spirit,
9:2 that I have great sorrow and unceasing anguish in my heart.
9:3 For I could wish that I myself were accursed, away from Christ, for the sake of my brothers, my kinsmen according to the flesh,
9:4 who are Israelites. Theirs are the adoption as sons and the glory and the covenants and the giving of the law and the worship and the promises;
9:5 theirs are the fathers, and from them comes the Christ according to the flesh, who is over all, God blessed throughout the ages. Amen.
9:6 But it is not as though God’s word has failed. For not all who are from Israel are Israel;
9:7 neither, because they are Abraham’s seed, are they all children; but, “In Isaac your seed will be called.”
9:8 That is, the children of the flesh are not thereby God’s children, but the children of the promise are reckoned as seed.
9:9 For this is a word of promise: “At this time I will come, and Sarah will have a son.”
9:10 And not only this, but also Rebecca, having conceived by one man, Isaac our father—
9:11 for when they had not yet been born or done anything good or bad, so that God’s purpose according to election might remain,
9:12 not from works but from the One who calls, it was said to her, “The older will serve the younger,”
9:13 just as it is written, “Jacob I loved, but Esau I hated.”
9:14 What, then, shall we say? Is there unrighteousness with God? May it never be!
9:15 For He says to Moses, “I will have mercy on whomever I have mercy, and I will have compassion on whomever I have compassion.”
9:16 So then, it depends not on the one who wills, nor on the one who runs, but on God who has mercy.
9:17 For the Scripture says to Pharaoh, “For this very purpose I raised you up, so that I might demonstrate My power in you, and so that My name might be proclaimed in all the earth.”
9:18 So then, He has mercy on whom He wills, and whom He wills He hardens.
9:19 You will then say to me, “Why, then, does He still find fault? For who has resisted His purpose?”
9:20 But who are you, O man, to answer back to God? Will what is formed say to the One who formed it, “Why didst Thou make me this way?”
9:21 Or does the potter not have authority over the clay, from the same lump to make one vessel for honor and another for dishonor?
9:22 But what if God, willing to demonstrate wrath and to make His power known, endured with much patience vessels of wrath fitted for destruction,
9:23 and did so to make known the wealth of His glory upon vessels of mercy, which He prepared beforehand for glory,
9:24 even us whom He also called, not only from Jews but also from Gentiles?
9:25 As He also says in Hosea, “I will call those who are not My people ‘My people,’ and her who is not beloved ‘beloved’;
9:26 and in the place where it was said to them, ‘You are not My people,’ there they will be called sons of the living God.”
9:27 But Isaiah cries out concerning Israel, “Though the number of Israel’s sons be like the sand of the sea, the remnant will be saved;
9:28 for the Lord will carry out a word on the earth, completing it and cutting it short.”
9:29 And just as Isaiah has said beforehand, “Unless the Lord Sabaoth had left us seed, we would have become like Sodom and would have been made like Gomorrah.”
9:30 What, then, shall we say? That Gentiles who were not pursuing righteousness attained righteousness, namely the righteousness from faith;
9:31 but Israel, pursuing a law of righteousness, did not attain law.
9:32 Why? Because it was not from faith, but as though from works. They stumbled against the stone of stumbling,
9:33 just as it is written, “Behold, I lay in Zion a stone of stumbling and a rock of offense, and the one who believes in Him will not be put to shame.”

ROMANS 10

10:1 Brothers, my heart’s desire and my prayer to God for them are that they may be saved.
10:2 For I bear them witness that they have zeal for God, but not according to knowledge;
10:3 for, not knowing God’s righteousness and seeking to establish their own righteousness, they did not submit to God’s righteousness.
10:4 For Christ is the end of law for righteousness to everyone who believes.
10:5 For Moses writes about the righteousness from the law: the person who does these things will live by them.
10:6 But the righteousness from faith speaks in this way: “Do not say in your heart, ‘Who will ascend into heaven?’”—that is, to bring Christ down;
10:7 or, “‘Who will descend into the abyss?’”—that is, to bring Christ up from the dead.
10:8 But what does it say? “The word is near you, in your mouth and in your heart”—that is, the word of faith that we proclaim.
10:9 Because if you confess Jesus as Lord with your mouth, and believe in your heart that God raised Him from the dead, you will be saved;
10:10 for with the heart one believes for righteousness, and with the mouth one confesses for salvation.
10:11 For the Scripture says, “Everyone who believes in Him will not be put to shame.”
10:12 For there is no distinction between Jew and Greek, for the same Lord is Lord of all, being rich toward all who call upon Him;
10:13 for “everyone who calls upon the name of the Lord will be saved.”
10:14 How, then, are they to call upon One in whom they have not believed? And how are they to believe in One whom they have not heard? And how are they to hear without someone proclaiming?
10:15 And how are they to proclaim unless they are sent? Just as it is written, “How beautiful are the feet of those announcing good things!”
10:16 But not all obeyed the gospel. For Isaiah says, “Lord, who believed our report?”
10:17 So faith is from hearing, and hearing through the word of Christ.
10:18 But I say, did they not hear? Indeed they did: “Their sound went out into all the earth, and their words to the ends of the inhabited world.”
10:19 But I say, did Israel not know? First Moses says, “I will make you jealous by what is not a nation; by a senseless nation I will provoke you to anger.”
10:20 And Isaiah speaks boldly and says, “I was found among those who did not seek Me; I became known to those who did not ask for Me.”
10:21 But concerning Israel He says, “All day long I stretched out My hands toward a disobedient and contrary people.”

ROMANS 11

11:1 I say, then, has God rejected His people? May it never be! For I too am an Israelite, from Abraham’s seed, of Benjamin’s tribe.
11:2 God has not rejected His people whom He foreknew. Or do you not know what the Scripture says in the passage about Elijah, how he pleads with God against Israel?
11:3 “Lord, they killed Thy prophets, they demolished Thy altars, and I alone was left, and they seek my life.”
11:4 But what does the divine response say to him? “I left for Myself seven thousand men who did not bend the knee to Baal.”
11:5 So then, at the present time also there has come to be a remnant according to an election of grace.
11:6 But if by grace, it is no longer from works; otherwise grace is no longer grace.
11:7 What, then? What Israel seeks, it did not obtain; but the chosen obtained it, and the rest were hardened,
11:8 just as it is written, “God gave them a spirit of stupor, eyes not to see and ears not to hear, until this very day.”
11:9 And David says, “Let their table become a snare and a trap and a stumbling block and a repayment to them;
11:10 let their eyes be darkened so as not to see, and bend their back continually.”
11:11 I say, then, did they stumble so that they might fall? May it never be! But by their trespass salvation has come to the Gentiles, to make them jealous.
11:12 But if their trespass is the world’s wealth and their loss the Gentiles’ wealth, how much more their fullness!
11:13 And I speak to you Gentiles. Insofar as I am an apostle of Gentiles, I glorify my ministry,
11:14 if somehow I may make my own flesh jealous and save some of them.
11:15 For if their rejection is the world’s reconciliation, what will their acceptance be except life from the dead?
11:16 And if the firstfruits are holy, so is the lump; and if the root is holy, so are the branches.
11:17 But if some of the branches were broken off, and you, being a wild olive, were grafted in among them and came to share in the olive tree’s rich root,
11:18 do not boast over the branches. But if you boast, it is not you who bear the root, but the root bears you.
11:19 You will then say, “Branches were broken off so that I might be grafted in.”
11:20 Well: they were broken off because of unbelief, but you stand by faith. Do not be haughty, but fear;
11:21 for if God did not spare the natural branches, neither will He spare you.
11:22 See, then, God’s kindness and severity: severity upon those who fell, but God’s kindness upon you, if you remain in the kindness; otherwise you too will be cut off.
11:23 And they also, if they do not remain in unbelief, will be grafted in; for God is able to graft them in again.
11:24 For if you were cut from what is by nature a wild olive, and contrary to nature were grafted into a cultivated olive, how much more will these natural branches be grafted into their own olive tree!
11:25 For I do not want you to be unaware, brothers, of this mystery, so that you may not be wise in your own eyes: that a partial hardening has come upon Israel until the fullness of the Gentiles enters,
11:26 and in this way all Israel will be saved, just as it is written: “The Deliverer will come from Zion; He will turn away ungodliness from Jacob.
11:27 And this is My covenant with them, when I take away their sins.”
11:28 With regard to the gospel, they are enemies for your sake; but with regard to election, beloved for the fathers’ sake.
11:29 For God’s gifts and calling are irrevocable.
11:30 For just as you once disobeyed God, but now received mercy through their disobedience,
11:31 so they also now disobeyed, so that through the mercy shown to you they also may now receive mercy.
11:32 For God confined all in disobedience so that He might have mercy on all.
11:33 O depth of God’s wealth and wisdom and knowledge! How unsearchable His judgments and untraceable His ways!
11:34 “For who knew the Lord’s mind? Or who became His counselor?
11:35 Or who gave to Him first, and it will be repaid to him?”
11:36 Because from Him and through Him and to Him are all things. To Him be the glory throughout the ages. Amen.

ROMANS 12

12:1 Therefore I urge you, brothers, by the mercies of God, to present your bodies as a living sacrifice, holy and pleasing to God—your rational worship.
12:2 And do not be conformed to this age, but be transformed by the renewal of the mind, so that you may discern what God’s will is: the good and pleasing and perfect.
12:3 For through the grace given me I say to everyone among you not to think more highly than one ought to think, but to think with sound judgment, as God apportioned to each a measure of faith.
12:4 For as we have many parts in one body, and the parts do not all have the same function,
12:5 so we, the many, are one body in Christ, and each one is a member of the others.
12:6 And since we have gifts differing according to the grace given to us: if prophecy, let it be according to the proportion of faith;
12:7 if service, in serving; if one teaches, in teaching;
12:8 if one exhorts, in exhortation; the one who shares, with generosity; the one who leads, with diligence; the one who shows mercy, with cheerfulness.
12:9 Let love be without hypocrisy. Abhor what is evil; cling to what is good.
12:10 In brotherly love be tenderly devoted to one another; take the lead in honoring one another;
12:11 do not be slack in zeal; be fervent in spirit; serve the Lord;
12:12 rejoice in hope; persevere in affliction; persist in prayer;
12:13 share in the needs of the saints; pursue hospitality.
12:14 Bless those who persecute you; bless, and do not curse.
12:15 Rejoice with those who rejoice; weep with those who weep.
12:16 Have the same mind toward one another. Do not set your minds on lofty things, but associate with the lowly. Do not become wise in your own eyes.
12:17 Repay no one evil for evil. Take thought for what is honorable before all people.
12:18 If possible, as far as it depends on you, live at peace with all people.
12:19 Do not avenge yourselves, beloved, but make room for wrath; for it is written, “Vengeance is Mine; I will repay, says the Lord.”
12:20 But if your enemy is hungry, feed him; if he is thirsty, give him a drink; for by doing this you will heap coals of fire upon his head.
12:21 Do not be conquered by evil, but conquer evil with good.

ROMANS 13

13:1 Let every soul submit to the higher authorities, for there is no authority except from God, and those that exist have been appointed by God.
13:2 Therefore the one who sets himself against authority has resisted God’s ordering, and those who resist will receive judgment upon themselves.
13:3 For rulers are not a terror to good work, but to evil. Do you want to be free from fear of the authority? Do the good, and you will have praise from it;
13:4 for it is God’s servant to you for good. But if you do evil, be afraid; for it does not bear the sword without purpose. For it is God’s servant, an avenger who brings wrath on the one who practices evil.
13:5 Therefore it is necessary to submit, not only because of wrath, but also because of conscience.
13:6 For this reason you also pay taxes, for they are God’s ministers, devoted to this very task.
13:7 Pay everyone what is owed: tax to whom tax is due, duty to whom duty is due, fear to whom fear is due, honor to whom honor is due.
13:8 Owe no one anything except to love one another; for the one who loves the other has fulfilled law.
13:9 For “You shall not commit adultery,” “You shall not murder,” “You shall not steal,” “You shall not desire,” and if there is any other commandment, it is summed up in this word: “You shall love your neighbor as yourself.”
13:10 Love does no evil to the neighbor; therefore love is the fulfillment of law.
13:11 And do this knowing the time, that it is already the hour for you to rise from sleep; for now salvation is nearer to us than when we believed.
13:12 The night has advanced, and the day has drawn near. Therefore let us put away the works of darkness and put on the weapons of light.
13:13 Let us walk honorably as in daytime, not in revelries and drunkenness, not in sexual promiscuity and licentiousness, not in strife and jealousy;
13:14 but put on the Lord Jesus Christ, and do not make provision for the flesh to satisfy its desires.

ROMANS 14

14:1 Welcome the one who is weak in faith, but not to dispute over opinions.
14:2 One believes he may eat all things, but the weak person eats vegetables.
14:3 Do not let the one who eats despise the one who does not eat; and do not let the one who does not eat judge the one who eats, for God welcomed him.
14:4 Who are you to judge another’s household slave? To his own master he stands or falls; and he will be made to stand, for the Lord is able to make him stand.
14:5 For one judges one day above another, but another judges every day alike. Let each be fully assured in his own mind.
14:6 The one who regards the day regards it for the Lord. And the one who eats eats for the Lord, for he gives thanks to God; and the one who does not eat refrains for the Lord and gives thanks to God.
14:7 For none of us lives for himself, and none dies for himself;
14:8 for if we live, we live for the Lord, and if we die, we die for the Lord. So whether we live or die, we are the Lord’s.
14:9 For to this end Christ died and lived, so that He might be Lord of both the dead and the living.
14:10 But why do you judge your brother? Or you also, why do you despise your brother? For we will all stand before God’s judgment seat,
14:11 for it is written, “As I live, says the Lord, every knee will bend to Me, and every tongue will confess to God.”
14:12 So then, each of us will give an account of himself to God.
14:13 Therefore let us no longer judge one another; but judge this instead: not to place a stumbling block or a snare for a brother.
14:14 I know and am convinced in the Lord Jesus that nothing is unclean in itself; except that to the one who reckons something unclean, to that person it is unclean.
14:15 For if your brother is grieved because of food, you no longer walk according to love. Do not destroy with your food the one for whom Christ died.
14:16 Therefore do not let your good be spoken of as evil.
14:17 For God’s kingdom is not eating and drinking, but righteousness and peace and joy in the Holy Spirit;
14:18 for the one who serves Christ in this is pleasing to God and approved by people.
14:19 So then, let us pursue what makes for peace and what builds up one another.
14:20 Do not tear down God’s work for the sake of food. All things indeed are clean, but it is evil for a person to eat in a way that causes stumbling.
14:21 It is good not to eat meat or drink wine or do anything by which your brother stumbles.
14:22 The faith you have, keep to yourself before God. Blessed is the one who does not judge himself in what he approves.
14:23 But the one who doubts, if he eats, has been condemned, because it is not from faith; and everything that is not from faith is sin.

ROMANS 15

15:1 And we who are strong ought to bear the weaknesses of those without strength, and not to please ourselves.
15:2 Let each of us please our neighbor for his good, to build him up.
15:3 For even Christ did not please Himself; but just as it is written, “The reproaches of those who reproach Thee fell upon Me.”
15:4 For whatever was written beforehand was written for our instruction, so that through perseverance and through the encouragement of the Scriptures we might have hope.
15:5 And may the God of perseverance and encouragement grant you to have the same mind among one another according to Christ Jesus,
15:6 so that with one accord and one mouth you may glorify the God and Father of our Lord Jesus Christ.
15:7 Therefore welcome one another, just as Christ also welcomed you, to God’s glory.
15:8 For I say that Christ has become a servant of the circumcision for God’s truth, to confirm the promises of the fathers,
15:9 and for the Gentiles to glorify God for mercy, just as it is written, “For this reason I will give thanks to Thee among the Gentiles, and sing praise to Thy name.”
15:10 And again it says, “Rejoice, Gentiles, with His people.”
15:11 And again, “Praise the Lord, all you Gentiles, and let all the peoples praise Him.”
15:12 And again Isaiah says, “There will be the root of Jesse, and the One who rises to rule Gentiles; upon Him Gentiles will hope.”
15:13 And may the God of hope fill you with all joy and peace in believing, so that you may abound in hope in the power of the Holy Spirit.
15:14 And I myself am convinced about you, my brothers, that you yourselves also are full of goodness, filled with all knowledge, able also to admonish one another.
15:15 But I wrote more boldly to you on some points, to remind you again, because of the grace given me by God
15:16 to be a minister of Christ Jesus to the Gentiles, performing priestly service with God’s gospel, so that the offering of the Gentiles may become acceptable, sanctified in the Holy Spirit.
15:17 Therefore I have reason to boast in Christ Jesus concerning the things of God;
15:18 for I will not dare to speak of anything that Christ did not work through me for the Gentiles’ obedience, in word and deed,
15:19 in the power of signs and wonders, in the power of the Spirit of God, so that from Jerusalem and around as far as Illyricum I have fulfilled the proclamation of Christ’s gospel,
15:20 and so making it my ambition to proclaim the gospel where Christ has not been named, so that I would not build on another’s foundation,
15:21 but just as it is written, “Those to whom nothing about Him was announced will see, and those who have not heard will understand.”
15:22 For this reason I was also often hindered from coming to you;
15:23 but now, no longer having a place in these regions, and having a longing for many years to come to you,
15:24 whenever I travel to Spain—for I hope to see you as I pass through, and to be sent onward there by you, if I may first be filled in some measure by your company—
15:25 but now I am traveling to Jerusalem, serving the saints.
15:26 For Macedonia and Achaia were pleased to make a contribution for the poor among the saints in Jerusalem.
15:27 For they were pleased, and they are their debtors; for if the Gentiles shared in their spiritual things, they also owe them service in material things.
15:28 Therefore, having completed this and sealed this fruit for them, I will depart by way of you for Spain.
15:29 And I know that when I come to you, I will come in the fullness of Christ’s blessing.
15:30 And I urge you, brothers, through our Lord Jesus Christ and through the love of the Spirit, to struggle together with me in prayers to God for me,
15:31 so that I may be rescued from those who disobey in Judea, and my service for Jerusalem may become acceptable to the saints,
15:32 so that, coming to you in joy through God’s will, I may find rest together with you.
15:33 And may the God of peace be with you all. Amen.
