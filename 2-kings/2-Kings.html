2 Kings 1

Read this chapter on staging

Ahaziah Sends to Baal-zebub

1:1Moab broke faith with Israel after Ahab died.

1:2Ahaziah fell through the lattice in his upper room in Samaria and became ill. He sent messengers and said to them, ‘Come, and inquire of Baal Fly, the god of Ekron, whether I shall recover from this illness of mine.’ And they went to inquire through him.

1:3An angel of the Lord spoke to Elijah the Tishbite, saying, ‘Rise, come to meet the messengers of Ahaziah king of Samaria, and say to them: Is it because there is no God in Israel that you are going to inquire of Baal Fly, the god of Ekron?’

1:4‘It shall not be so! For thus says the Lord: The bed onto which you have gone up—you will not come down from it, for there you will surely die.’ And Elijah went and spoke to them.

1:5The messengers returned to him, and he said to them, ‘Why have you returned?’

1:6They said to him, ‘A man came up to meet us and said to us: Come, return to the king who sent you and tell him, Thus says the Lord: Is it because there is no God in Israel that you are going to inquire of Baal Fly, the god of Ekron? It shall not be so! The bed onto which you have gone up—you will not come down from it, for you will surely die.’

1:7He spoke to them, saying, ‘What was the character of the man who came up to meet you and spoke these words to you?’

1:8They said to him, ‘A hairy man, with a leather belt fastened around his waist.’ He said, ‘This is Elijah the Tishbite.’

Fire from Heaven upon the Captains of Fifty

1:9He sent to him a commander, a captain of fifty, with his fifty men. He went up and came to him, and there Elijah was, sitting on the top of the mountain. The captain of fifty spoke to him and said, ‘Man of God, the king has summoned you. Come down!’

1:10Elijah answered and said to the captain of fifty, ‘If I am a man of God, fire will come down from heaven and consume you and your fifty men.’ Fire came down from heaven and consumed him and his fifty men.

1:11The king again sent to him another captain of fifty with his fifty men. The captain went up and spoke to him, saying, ‘Man of God, thus says the king: Come down quickly!’

1:12Elijah answered and spoke to him, saying, ‘If I am a man of God, fire will come down from heaven and consume you and your fifty men.’ Fire came down from heaven and consumed him and his fifty men.

1:13The king again sent a commander, a third captain of fifty, with his fifty men. The third captain of fifty came to him, bent his knees before Elijah, and pleaded with him, saying, ‘Man of God, please let my life and the lives of these fifty slaves of yours be honored in your eyes.’

1:14‘Behold, fire came down from heaven and consumed the first two captains of fifty and their fifty men. But now please let the lives of your slaves be honored in your eyes.’

The Death of Ahaziah King of Israel

1:15An angel of the Lord spoke to Elijah and said, ‘Go down with him; do not be afraid of them.’ Elijah rose and went down with him to the king.

1:16Elijah spoke to him and said, ‘Thus says the Lord: Why did you send messengers to inquire of Baal Fly, the god of Ekron? It shall not be so! The bed onto which you have gone up—you will not come down from it, for you will surely die.’

1:17He died according to the word of the Lord that Elijah had spoken.

1:18The rest of the acts of Ahaziah, all that he did—behold, are these not written in the book of the daily records for the kings of Israel?

Jehoram Son of Ahab Reigns

1:18aJehoram son of Ahab reigns over Israel in Samaria for twelve years, beginning in the eighteenth year of Jehoshaphat king of Judah.

1:18bHe did what was evil before the Lord, though not as his brothers did, nor as his mother did.

1:18cHe removed the pillars of Baal that his father had made and shattered them. Yet he clung to the sins of the house of Jeroboam, who caused Israel to sin; he did not turn away from them.

1:18dThe Lord was stirred to anger against the house of Ahab.

2 Kings 2

Read this chapter on staging

Elijah and Elisha Go Down from Gilgal

2:1When the Lord was about to take Elijah up in a tumult, as into heaven, Elijah and Elisha went from Gilgal.

2:2Elijah said to Elisha, ‘Please stay here, for the Lord has sent me as far as Bethel.’ Elisha said, ‘As the Lord lives, and as you live, I will not leave you.’ So they came to Bethel.

2:3The sons of the prophets who were in Bethel came to Elisha and said to him, ‘Do you know that this day the Lord is taking your master away from above your head?’ He said, ‘I too know it. Be silent.’

2:4Elijah said to Elisha, ‘Please stay here, for the Lord has sent me to Jericho.’ Elisha said, ‘As the Lord lives, and as you live, I will not leave you.’ So they came to Jericho.

2:5The sons of the prophets who were in Jericho approached Elisha and said to him, ‘Do you know that this day the Lord is taking your master away from above your head?’ He said, ‘Indeed, I too know it. Be silent.’

2:6Elijah said to him, ‘Please stay here, for the Lord has sent me as far as the Jordan.’ Elisha said, ‘As the Lord lives, and as you live, I will not leave you.’ So both of them went on.

2:7Fifty men, sons of the prophets, stood opposite them at a distance, while both of them stood beside the Jordan.

The Parting of the Jordan

2:8Elijah took his sheepskin, rolled it up, and struck the water. The water divided to this side and that, and both of them crossed over on barren ground.

2:9When they had crossed, Elijah said to Elisha, ‘Ask what I should do for you before I am taken up from you.’ Elisha said, ‘Please let a double portion of your spirit be upon me.’

2:10Elijah said, ‘You have made a difficult request. If you see me being taken up from you, it will be so for you; but if not, it certainly will not happen.’

Elijah Taken Up in the Whirlwind

2:11As they went, walking and speaking, behold—a chariot of fire and horses of fire separated the two of them, and Elijah was taken up in a tumult, as into heaven.

2:12Elisha was watching and crying, ‘Father, father! Israel’s chariot and its horseman!’ He saw him no more. He seized his clothes and tore them into two pieces.

2:13He picked up Elijah’s sheepskin, which had fallen upon Elisha, and Elisha returned and stood on the edge of the Jordan.

2:14He took Elijah’s sheepskin, which had fallen upon him, and struck the water, but it did not separate. He said, ‘Where is the God of Elijah—Appho?’ He struck the waters, and they split to this side and that, and Elisha crossed over.

The Sons of the Prophets Seek Elijah

2:15The sons of the prophets in Jericho saw him from opposite him and said, ‘The spirit of Elijah has come to rest upon Elisha.’ They came to meet him and bowed before him to the ground.

2:16They said to him, ‘Behold, with your servants are fifty capable men. Please let them go and search for your master, in case a spirit of the Lord has lifted him and cast him into the Jordan, or onto one of the mountains, or onto one of the hills.’ Elisha said, ‘Do not send them.’

2:17They pressed him until he was ashamed, and he said, ‘Send them.’ They sent fifty men, who searched for three days but did not find him.

2:18They returned to him while he was staying in Jericho, and Elisha said, ‘Did I not tell you, Do not go?’

The Healing of the Waters

2:19The men of the city said to Elisha, ‘Behold, the city’s settlement is good, as the lord sees, but the waters are bad and the land is bereaved of offspring.’

2:20Elisha said, ‘Bring me a new small water jar and put salt in it.’ They brought it to him.

2:21Elisha went out to the outlet of the waters, threw salt there, and said, ‘Thus says the Lord: I have healed these waters. No longer will death or loss of offspring come from there.’

2:22The waters have remained healed to this day, according to the word Elisha spoke.

The Boys of Bethel

2:23He went up from there to Bethel. As he was going up along the road, small children came out from the city and made fun of him, saying to him, ‘Go up, baldhead! Go up!’

2:24He turned back toward them, saw them, and cursed them in the name of the Lord. Behold, two bears came out of the woodland and tore open forty-two of the children.

2:25He went from there to Mount Carmel, and from there he returned to Samaria.

2 Kings 3

Read this chapter on staging

Jehoram King of Israel

3:1Jehoram son of Ahab became king in Israel in the eighteenth year of Jehoshaphat king of Judah, and he reigned twelve years.

3:2He did what was evil in the eyes of the Lord, though not as his father and not as his mother had done. He removed the pillars of Baal that his father had made.

3:3Yet he clung to the sin of Jeroboam son of Nebat, who caused Israel to sin; he did not turn away from it.

Moab Breaks Faith

3:4Mesha king of Moab was a noqed. At the rising, he rendered to the king of Israel a hundred thousand lambs and a hundred thousand rams in their fleeces.

3:5After Ahab died, the king of Moab broke faith with the king of Israel.

3:6King Jehoram went out from Samaria that day and mustered Israel.

3:7He went and sent word to Jehoshaphat king of Judah, saying, ‘The king of Moab has broken faith with me. Will you go with me to Moab for war?’ He said, ‘I will go up. As I am, so are you; my people are as your people, my horses as your horses.’

3:8He said, ‘By which road shall I go up?’ He said, ‘By the wilderness road of Edom.’

3:9The king of Israel, the king of Judah, and the king of Edom went on. They made a circuit of seven days’ journey, but there was no water for the camp or the livestock at their feet.

3:10The king of Israel said, ‘Alas! The Lord has called the three kings who are passing through to give them into the hand of Moab.’

Elisha Sought in the Wilderness

3:11Jehoshaphat said, ‘Is there no prophet of the Lord here, through whom we may inquire of the Lord?’ One of the attendants of the king of Israel answered, ‘Elisha son of Shaphat is here, who poured water on Elijah’s hands.’

3:12Jehoshaphat said, ‘A word of the Lord is with him.’ The king of Israel, Jehoshaphat king of Judah, and the king of Edom went down to him.

3:13Elisha said to the king of Israel, ‘What have I to do with you? Go to your father’s prophets.’ The king of Israel said to him, ‘No! For the Lord has called the three kings together to hand them over into the hands of Moab.’

3:14Elisha said, ‘As the Lord of hosts lives, before whom I stand, if I were not showing regard for Jehoshaphat king of Judah, I would neither look toward you nor see you.’

3:15‘But now bring me a player of strings.’ As the player plucked the strings, the hand of the Lord came upon him.

3:16He said, ‘Thus says the Lord: Make this torrent bed pit after pit.’

3:17‘For thus says the Lord: You will see no wind and see no rain, yet this torrent bed will be filled with water. You will drink—you, your possessions, and your livestock.’

3:18‘This is a light thing in the eyes of the Lord; I will also hand Moab over into your hand.’

3:19‘You will strike every fortified city. You will bring down every good tree, block every spring of water, and make every good plot useless with stones.’

The Waters and the Defeat of Moab

3:20In the morning, when the sacrifice was going up, look—waters were coming from the direction of Edom, and the land filled with water.

3:21All Moab heard that the kings had come up to fight them. They raised a cry, from everyone wearing a belt and upward, and stood at the border.

3:22They rose early in the morning, and the sun rose over the waters. From opposite them Moab saw the waters fiery red, like blood.

3:23They said, ‘This is blood from the sword! The kings have fought and each has struck his neighbor. Now, Moab, to the spoils!’

3:24They entered Israel’s camp. Israel rose and struck Moab, and they fled before them. They kept going in and striking Moab.

3:25They tore down the cities. Each man threw his stone onto every good plot, filling it. They blocked every spring of water and brought down every good tree, until the stones of the wall were left demolished. The slingers surrounded it and struck it.

The King of Moab Offers His Son

3:26The king of Moab saw that the battle had become too strong for him. He took with him seven hundred men with drawn swords to break through to the king of Edom, but they could not.

3:27He took his firstborn son, who had reigned in his place, and offered him as a whole burnt offering on the wall. Great regret came upon Israel, and they withdrew from him and returned to the land.

2 Kings 4

Read this chapter on staging

The Widow's Jar of Oil

4:1A woman from among the sons of the prophets cried to Elisha, saying, ‘Your slave, my husband, has died. You know that he was a slave who feared the Lord. Now the lender has come to take my two sons as his slaves.’

4:2Elisha said, ‘What should I do for you? Tell me what you have in the house.’ She said, ‘Your slave woman has nothing in the house except oil with which I may anoint myself.’

4:3He said to her, ‘Go, ask for vessels from outside, from all your neighbors—empty vessels, not a few.’

4:4‘Then go inside and shut the door behind yourself and your sons. Pour into these vessels, and take away each full one.’

4:5She went away from him and did so. She shut the door behind herself and her sons. They kept bringing vessels to her, and she kept pouring,

4:6until the vessels were full. She said to her sons, ‘Bring me another vessel.’ They said to her, ‘There is no other vessel.’ Then the oil stopped.

4:7She came and reported to the man of God. Elisha said, ‘Go, sell the oil, and pay your interest. You and your sons can live on the remaining oil.’

The Shunammite Woman

4:8One day Elisha passed through Shunem. A prominent woman was there, and she pressed him to eat bread. As he continued coming there over time, he would turn aside to eat there.

4:9The woman said to her husband, ‘Behold, I know that this is a holy man of God who continually passes by us.’

4:10‘Please let us make a small upstairs place for him and put a bed, a table, a stool, and a lampstand there for him. When he comes to us, he can turn aside there.’

4:11One day he came there, turned aside to the upper room, and slept there.

4:12He said to Gehazi his attendant, ‘Call this Shunammite woman for me.’ He called her, and she stood before him.

4:13He said to him, ‘Please say to her: Behold, you have shown us all this extraordinary concern. What should be done for you? Do you have a matter for the king or the commander of the army?’ She said, ‘I am living among my own people.’

4:14He said, ‘What should be done for her?’ Gehazi his attendant said, ‘Indeed, she has no son, and her husband is an old man.’

4:15He called her, and she stood beside the door.

4:16Elisha said to her, ‘At this season, as the season comes alive, you will be holding a son.’ She said, ‘No, my lord! Do not deceive your slave woman.’

4:17The woman conceived and bore a son at that season, as the season came alive, just as Elisha had told her.

The Death of the Shunammite's Son

4:18The child grew strong. When the child went out to his father, to the reapers,

4:19he said to his father, ‘My head, my head!’ His father said to the attendant, ‘Carry him to his mother.’

4:20He carried him to his mother. The boy lay on her knees until noon, and died.

4:21She carried him up and laid him on the bed of the man of God, shut the door behind him, and went out.

4:22She called her husband and said, ‘Please send me one of the attendants and one of the female donkeys, so that I may run to the man of God and return.’

4:23He said, ‘Why are you going to him this day? It is neither new moon nor Sabbath.’ She said, ‘Peace.’

4:24She saddled the female donkey and said to her attendant, ‘Lead on, go. Do not hold me back from riding unless I tell you.

4:25Come, and you will go and come to the man of God at Mount Carmel.’ When Elisha saw her coming, he said to Gehazi his attendant, ‘Behold, there is that Shunammite woman.’

4:26‘Now run to meet her and say: Is there peace with you? Is there peace with your husband? Is there peace with the child?’ She said, ‘Peace.’

4:27She came to Elisha at the mountain and seized his feet. Gehazi approached to push her away, but Elisha said, ‘Leave her alone, for her soul is in pain within her. The Lord has hidden it from me and has not told me.’

4:28She said, ‘Did I ask my lord for a son? Did I not say, Do not play me false?’

4:29Elisha said to Gehazi, ‘Fasten your belt, take my staff in your hand, and come. If you meet a man, do not bless him, and if a man blesses you, do not answer him. Place my staff on the child’s face.’

4:30The child’s mother said, ‘As the Lord lives, and as you live, I will not leave you.’ Elisha rose and went behind her.

4:31Gehazi went ahead of her and placed the staff on the child’s face, but there was no voice and no hearing. He returned to meet him and reported, ‘The child has not been raised.’

Elisha Raises the Child

4:32Elisha entered the house, and there was the child, dead, lying on his bed.

4:33Elisha entered the house, shut the door behind the two of them, and prayed to the Lord.

4:34He went up and lay upon the child, putting his mouth on his mouth, his eyes on his eyes, and his hands on his hands. He bent over him, and the child’s flesh was warmed.

4:35He returned and walked in the house this way and that. He went up and bent over the child as many as seven times, and the child opened his eyes.

4:36Elisha called out to Gehazi and said, ‘Call this Shunammite woman.’ He called, and she came in to him. Elisha said, ‘Take your son.’

4:37The woman came in, fell at his feet, and bowed to the ground. She took her son and went out.

Death in the Cauldron

4:38Elisha returned to Gilgal. There was famine in the land, and the sons of the prophets were sitting before him. Elisha said to his attendant, ‘Set on the large cauldron and prepare a cooked dish for the sons of the prophets.’

4:39One went out into the field to gather arioth. He found a vine in the field and gathered wild round fruit from it, filling his garment. He put it into the cauldron of the cooked dish, for they did not recognize it.

4:40He poured it out for the men to eat. As they ate from the cooked dish, look—they cried out, ‘Death is in the cauldron, man of God!’ They could not eat it.

4:41He said, ‘Bring flour and put it into the cauldron.’ Elisha said to Gehazi the attendant, ‘Pour it out for the people, and let them eat.’ There was no longer anything harmful there in the cauldron.

The Twenty Barley Loaves

4:42A man passed through from Baithsarisa and brought the man of God twenty barley loaves of firstfruits and cakes of dried fruit. He said, ‘Give them to the people, and let them eat.’

4:43His minister said, ‘How can I put this before a hundred men?’ He said, ‘Give it to the people, and let them eat, for thus says the Lord: They will eat and leave some over.’

4:44They ate and left some over, according to the word of the Lord.

2 Kings 5

Read this chapter on staging

Naaman the Syrian

5:1Naaman, commander of Syria’s army, was a great man before his master and admired in his presence, for through him the Lord had given deliverance to Syria. The man was powerful in strength, but afflicted with leprosy.

5:2Lightly equipped Syrians had gone out and taken captive a young girl from the land of Israel. She attended Naaman’s wife.

5:3She said to her mistress, ‘If only my master were before the prophet of God who is in Samaria! Then he would free him from his leprosy.’

5:4She went in and reported to her own master, saying, ‘Thus and thus the girl from the land of Israel has spoken.’

5:5The king of Syria said to Naaman, ‘Come, go in, and I will send a letter to the king of Israel.’ He went, taking with him ten talents of silver, six thousand gold coins, and ten changes of robes.

5:6He brought the letter to the king of Israel. It said, ‘Now, when this letter reaches you, behold, I have sent you Naaman my slave, and you shall free him from his leprosy.’

5:7When the king of Israel read the letter, he tore his garments and said, ‘Am I God, to put to death and bring to life, that this man sends to me to free a man from his leprosy? Please understand and see: this man is looking for a pretext against me.’

5:8When Elisha heard that the king of Israel had torn his garments, he sent word to the king of Israel, saying, ‘Why have you torn your garments? Please let Naaman come to me, and let him know that there is a prophet in Israel.’

Wash Seven Times in the Jordan

5:9Naaman came with a horse and chariot and stood at the doors of Elisha’s house.

5:10Elisha sent a messenger to him, saying, ‘Go and bathe seven times in the Jordan. Your flesh will be restored to you, and you will be cleansed.’

5:11Naaman became angry and went away, saying, ‘Behold, I thought that he would come out to me, stand, call on the name of his God, place his hand on the place, and remove the leprous condition.’

5:12‘Are not Abanah and Pharpar, the rivers of Damascus, better than the Jordan and all the waters of Israel? Could I not go and bathe in them and be cleansed?’ He turned aside and went away in anger.

5:13His attendants approached and spoke to him: ‘The prophet has told you a great thing; will you not do it? And he has said to you, Bathe and be cleansed.’

5:14And Naaman went down, and dipped himself in the Jordan seven times, according to the word of Elisha; and his flesh was restored as the flesh of a little child, and he was cleansed.

Naaman's Confession and Elisha's Refusal

5:15He returned to Elisha, he and all his company. He came and stood and said, ‘Behold, now I know that there is no God in all the earth except in Israel. Now accept the blessing from your slave.’

5:16Elisha said, ‘As the Lord lives, before whom I stand, I will not accept it.’ He pressed him to accept, but he would not comply.

5:17Naaman said, ‘If not, please let your slave be given a pair of mules’ load, and you shall give me some of the red earth, for your slave will no longer offer whole burnt offering or sacrifice to other gods, but to the Lord alone.’

5:18‘The Lord will be propitious to your slave when my master enters the house of Remman to bow to him and leans on my arm, and I bow in the house of Remman when he bows in the house of Remman. The Lord will indeed be propitious to your slave in this matter.’

5:19Elisha said to Naaman, ‘Go in peace.’ He went away from him a debratha of the land.

The Greed of Gehazi

5:20Gehazi, Elisha’s attendant, said, ‘Behold, my master has spared this Naaman the Syrian by not taking from his hand what he brought. As the Lord lives, I will run after him and take something from him.’

5:21Gehazi pursued Naaman. Naaman saw him running after him and turned back from the chariot to meet him.

5:22He said, ‘Peace. My master sent me, saying: Behold, two young men from the sons of the prophets have now come to me from Mount Ephraim. Please give them a talent of silver and two changes of robes.’

5:23Naaman said, ‘Take two talents of silver.’ He took them in two bags, with two changes of robes, and placed them on two of his attendants, who carried them ahead of him.

5:24They came to the dark place. He took the goods from their hands and set them in a house, then sent the men away.

5:25He went in and stood beside his master. Elisha said to him, ‘From where, Gehazi?’ Gehazi said, ‘Your slave has not gone here or there.’

5:26Elisha said to him, ‘Did not my heart go with you when the man turned from the chariot to meet you? Now you have taken the silver and now you have taken the garments, and with it you will acquire gardens and olive groves and vineyards and sheep and cattle and male servants and female servants.’

5:27‘Naaman’s leprosy will cling to you and to your offspring for ever.’ He went out from his presence afflicted with leprosy, like snow.

2 Kings 6

Read this chapter on staging

The Floating Axe Head

6:1The sons of the prophets said to Elisha, ‘Behold, the place where we dwell before you is too narrow for us.’

6:2‘Please let us go as far as the Jordan, and let each man take one beam from there. Let us build for ourselves there, to dwell there.’ He said, ‘Go on.’

6:3One of them said, ‘Please come with your slaves.’ He said, ‘I will go.’

6:4He went with them. They came to the Jordan and cut the timber.

6:5Behold, as one of them was bringing down a beam, the iron fell out into the water. He cried, ‘Alas, lord! It was borrowed!’

6:6The man of God said, ‘Where did it fall?’ He showed him the place. He chipped off a piece of wood and threw it there, and the iron floated.

6:7He said, ‘Lift it up for yourself.’ He stretched out his hand and took it.

The Syrian Ambushes Revealed

2 Kings 11

Read this chapter on staging

Athaliah Destroys the Royal Seed

11:1Athaliah, Ahaziah’s mother, saw that her sons had died, and destroyed all the royal offspring.

11:2Jehosheba, daughter of King Joram and sister of Ahaziah, took Joash, her brother’s son, and stole him away from among the king’s sons who were being put to death—him and his nurse—in the chamber of the beds. She hid him from Athaliah, and he was not put to death.

11:3He remained with her in the Lord’s house, hidden for six years, while Athaliah was reigning over the land.

Jehoiada Brings Out the King's Son

11:4In the seventh year Jehoiada the priest sent for the captains of hundreds, the Chorri and the Rasim. He brought them to him into the Lord’s house, made a covenant of the Lord with them, put them under oath before the Lord, and Jehoiada showed them the king’s son.

11:5He commanded them, saying, ‘This is what you shall do: let a third of you enter on the Sabbath, and you shall guard the king’s house at the gateway,

11:6and a third shall be at the Gate of the Roads, and a third at the gate behind the runners. Keep the watch over the house.

11:7The two divisions among you, everyone going out on the Sabbath, shall keep watch over the Lord’s house for the king.

11:8Surround the king on every side, each man with his equipment in his hand. Whoever enters the saderoth shall die.’ And he was with the king when he went out and came in.

11:9The captains of hundreds did everything Jehoiada the discerning one commanded. Each took his men entering on the Sabbath, together with those going out on the Sabbath, and came to Jehoiada the priest.

11:10The priest gave the captains of hundreds King David’s lances and threefold weapons that were in the Lord’s house.

11:11The runners stood, each man with his equipment in his hand, from the house’s right flank to its left flank, beside the altar and the house, all around the king.

11:12He brought out the king’s son and put the nezer and the testimony upon him. He made him king and anointed him, and they clapped their hands and said, ‘Long live the king!’

The Death of Athaliah

11:13Athaliah heard the sound of the running people and entered among the people in the Lord’s house.

11:14She looked, and there was the king standing by the pillar according to the ordinance, with the singers and trumpets beside the king. All the people of the land were rejoicing and sounding trumpets. Athaliah tore her garments and cried, ‘Conspiracy! Conspiracy!’

11:15Jehoiada the priest commanded the captains of hundreds, the army’s overseers, and said to them, ‘Bring her out from inside the saderoth. Whoever enters after her shall certainly be put to death by the sword.’ For the priest had said, ‘She must not die in the Lord’s house.’

11:16They laid hands on her. She entered by the way of the horses’ entrance of the king’s house, and died there.

The Covenant and the Pulling Down of Baal's House

11:17Jehoiada made a covenant between the Lord, the king, and the people, that they should be the Lord’s people, and between the king and the people.

11:18All the people of the land entered Baal’s house and pulled it down. They thoroughly shattered its altars and images, and killed Mattan, Baal’s priest, before the altars. The priest appointed overseers for the Lord’s house.

11:19He took the captains of hundreds, the Chorri, the Rasim, and all the people of the land. They brought the king down from the Lord’s house. He entered the king’s house by the way of the Runners’ Gate, and they seated him on the throne of the kings.

11:20All the people of the land rejoiced, and the city became quiet. They had put Athaliah to death by the sword in the king’s house.

2 Kings 12

Read this chapter on staging

Joash King of Judah

12:1Joash was seven years old when he became king.

12:2In Jehu’s seventh year Joash became king, and he reigned forty years in Jerusalem. His mother’s name was Abia of Beersheba.

12:3Joash did what was upright before the Lord all the days in which Jehoiada the priest enlightened him.

12:4Yet the high places were not removed, and the people were still sacrificing and burning incense there at the high places.

The Silver for the Repair of the House

12:5Joash said to the priests, ‘All the silver of the holy things received into the Lord’s house—silver of assessment, each man taking silver of assessment, and all silver that may arise in a man’s heart to bring into the Lord’s house—

12:6let the priests receive it for themselves, each from their sale, and they shall strengthen the bedek of the house wherever bedek is found.’

12:7In the twenty-third year of King Joash, the priests had not strengthened the bedek of the house.

12:8King Joash called Jehoiada the priest and the priests and said to them, ‘Why are you not strengthening the bedek of the house? Now take no silver from your sales, but give it for the bedek of the house.’

12:9The priests agreed neither to take silver from the people nor to strengthen the bedek of the house.

12:10Jehoiada the priest took a chest, bored a hole in its board, and placed it beside Iamibin, in the house of a man of the Lord’s house. The priests guarding the doorpost put in all the silver found in the Lord’s house.

12:11Whenever they saw that there was much silver in the chest, the king’s scribe and the high priest came up. They bound up and counted the silver found in the Lord’s house.

12:12They placed the prepared silver in the hands of those doing the work, the overseers of the Lord’s house. They paid it out to the carpenters and builders working on the Lord’s house,

12:13to the wall builders and stone quarriers, to acquire timber and quarried stones to make firm the bedek of the Lord’s house, for everything spent on strengthening the house.

12:14But silver doors, nails, bowls, trumpets, or any gold or silver vessel were not to be made for the Lord’s house from the silver brought into the Lord’s house.

12:15They would give it to those doing the work, and with it they strengthened the Lord’s house.

12:16They did not call the men to account into whose hands they placed the silver to give to those doing the work, for they acted in their trustworthiness.

12:17Silver for sin and silver for trespass, whatever was brought into the Lord’s house, belonged to the priests.

Hazael Turns Against Jerusalem

12:18Then Hazael king of Syria went up, fought against Gath, and seized it. Hazael set his face to go up against Jerusalem.

12:19Joash king of Judah took all the holy things that Jehoshaphat, Joram, and Ahaziah, his fathers and kings of Judah, had consecrated, his own holy things, and all the gold found in the treasuries of the Lord’s house and the king’s house. He sent them to Hazael king of Syria, and he went away from Jerusalem.

The Conspiracy Against Joash

12:20The rest of the acts of Joash, and everything he did—behold, are these not written in the book of the daily records for the kings of Judah?

12:21His slaves rose, bound together a conspiracy, and struck Joash in the house of Mallo in Gaalla.

12:22Jezichar son of Jemuath and Jezebuth—his son Shomer—his slaves, struck him, and he died. They buried him with his fathers in the city of David, and Amaziah his son became king in his place.

2 Kings 13

Read this chapter on staging

Jehoahaz King of Israel

13:1In the twenty-third year of Joash son of Ahaziah, king of Judah, Jehoahaz son of Jehu became king in Samaria, for seventeen years.

13:2He did evil in the Lord’s eyes and followed the sins of Jeroboam son of Nebat, who caused Israel to sin; he did not turn away from them.

13:3The Lord was fiercely angry with Israel, and He gave them into the hand of Hazael king of Syria and into the hand of the son of Ader, Hazael’s son, all those days.

13:4Jehoahaz entreated the Lord’s face, and the Lord listened to him, for He saw Israel’s affliction, because the king of Syria afflicted them.

13:5The Lord gave Israel deliverance, and Israel came out from under Syria’s hand. The Israelites lived in their dwellings as in former days.

13:6Nevertheless, they did not turn away from the sins of Jeroboam’s house, who caused Israel to sin; they walked in them. Moreover, the grove stood in Samaria.

13:7For no troops were left to Jehoahaz except fifty horsemen, ten chariots, and ten thousand foot soldiers, because the king of Syria destroyed them, and they made them like dust for trampling.

13:8The rest of Jehoahaz’s deeds, all that he did, and his mighty acts—are these not written in the book of the chronicles of the kings of Israel?

13:9Jehoahaz lay down with his fathers, and they buried him with his fathers in Samaria. His son Joash became king in his place.

Joash King of Israel

13:10In the thirty-seventh year of Joash king of Judah, Joash son of Jehoahaz became king over Israel in Samaria, for sixteen years.

13:11He did evil in the Lord’s eyes. He did not turn away from every sin of Jeroboam son of Nebat, who caused Israel to sin; he walked in them.

13:12The rest of Joash’s deeds, all that he did, and his mighty acts, which he performed with Amaziah king of Judah—are these not written in the book of the chronicles of the kings of Israel?

13:13Joash lay down with his fathers, and Jeroboam sat on his throne in Samaria with the sons of Israel.

The Last Sign of Elisha

13:14Elisha fell ill with the sickness from which he died. Joash king of Israel came down to him, wept over his face, and said, ‘Father, father! Israel’s chariot and its horseman!’

13:15Elisha said to him, ‘Take a bow and arrows.’ He brought a bow and arrows to him.

13:16He said to the king, ‘Put your hand on the bow.’ Joash put his hand on the bow, and Elisha placed his hands on the king’s hands.

13:17He said, ‘Open the window toward the east.’ He opened it. Elisha said, ‘Shoot!’ He shot. He said, ‘An arrow of deliverance for the Lord, an arrow of deliverance against Syria! You shall strike Syria at Aphek until it is finished.’

13:18Elisha said to him, ‘Take bows.’ He took them. He said to the king of Israel, ‘Strike the ground.’ The king struck three times and stopped.

13:19The man of God was grieved at him and said, ‘If you had struck five or six times, then you would have struck Syria until it was finished. But now you shall strike Syria three times.’

The Death of Elisha and the Man Revived

13:20Elisha died, and they buried him. Moab’s lightly equipped raiders came into the land when the year came round.

13:21As they were burying the man, suddenly they saw the lightly equipped raider. They threw the man into Elisha’s grave. He went and touched Elisha’s bones, came to life, and stood on his feet.

Israel Delivered from Syria

13:22Hazael afflicted Israel all Jehoahaz’s days.

13:23The Lord had mercy on them, pitied them, and looked toward them because of His covenant with Abraham, Isaac, and Jacob. The Lord was unwilling to destroy them and did not cast them from His presence.

13:24Hazael king of Syria died, and his son, the son of Ader, became king in his place.

13:25Joash son of Jehoahaz took back from the son of Ader, Hazael’s son, the towns he had taken in war from his father Jehoahaz. Joash struck him three times and recovered Israel’s towns.

2 Kings 14

Read this chapter on staging

Amaziah King of Judah

14:1In the second year of Joash son of Jehoahaz, king of Israel, Amaziah son of Joash, king of Judah, became king.

14:2He was twenty-five years old when he became king, and he reigned twenty-nine years in Jerusalem. His mother’s name was Joadin, from Jerusalem.

14:3He did what was right in the Lord’s eyes, though not like his father David. He did in accordance with everything his father Joash had done.

14:4Only he did not remove the high places; the people still sacrificed and burned incense at the high places.

14:5When the kingdom was firmly in his hand, he struck down his servants who had struck down his father.

14:6But he did not put the killers’ sons to death, as written in the book of Moses’ laws, as the Lord commanded: ‘Fathers shall not die for sons, and sons shall not die for fathers; each shall die only for his own sins.’

14:7He struck down ten thousand of Edom in Gaimela, captured the Rock in battle, and named it Kathoel, to this day.

The Thistle and the Cedar

14:8Then Amaziah sent messengers to Joash son of Jehoahaz son of Jehu, king of Israel, saying, ‘Come, let us face one another.’

14:9Joash king of Israel sent to Amaziah king of Judah, saying, ‘The thornbush in Lebanon sent to the cedar in Lebanon: “Give your daughter to my son as a wife.” But the wild animals in Lebanon passed by and trampled the thornbush.

14:10You have indeed struck Idumea, and your heart has lifted you up. Enjoy glory while sitting at home! Why contend in your wickedness? You will fall, you and Judah with you.’

14:11Amaziah did not listen. The king of Israel went up, and he and Amaziah king of Judah faced one another at Baithsamys in Judah.

14:12Judah stumbled before Israel, and each man fled to his dwelling.

14:13Joash son of Jehoahaz, king of Israel, captured Amaziah son of Joash son of Ahaziah, king of Judah, at Baithsamys. He came to Jerusalem and pulled down four hundred cubits of Jerusalem’s wall, from the Ephraim Gate to the Corner Gate.

14:14He took the gold and silver, all the vessels found in the Lord’s house and in the treasuries of the king’s house, and the sons of the mixed unions, and returned to Samaria.

14:15The rest of Joash’s deeds, what he did in his might, how he fought Amaziah king of Judah—are these not written in the book of the chronicles of the kings of Israel?

14:16Joash lay down with his fathers and was buried in Samaria with the kings of Israel. His son Jeroboam became king in his place.

The Death of Amaziah

14:17Amaziah son of Joash, king of Judah, lived fifteen years after the death of Joash son of Jehoahaz, king of Israel.

14:18The rest of Amaziah’s deeds and everything he did—are these not written in the book of the chronicles of the kings of Judah?

14:19They formed a conspiracy against him in Jerusalem, and he fled to Lachish. They sent after him to Lachish and put him to death there.

14:20They carried him on horses, and he was buried in Jerusalem with his fathers in David’s city.

Azariah Made King

14:21All the people of Judah took Azariah—he was sixteen years old—and made him king in place of his father Amaziah.

14:22He built Ailoth and restored it to Judah after the king lay down with his fathers.

Jeroboam Son of Joash King of Israel

14:23In the fifteenth year of Amaziah son of Joash, king of Judah, Jeroboam son of Joash became king over Israel in Samaria, for forty-one years.

14:24He did evil before the Lord. He did not turn away from any of the sins of Jeroboam son of Nebat, who caused Israel to sin.

14:25He shifted Israel’s boundary from the entrance of Aimath to the Sea of the Arabah, according to the word of the Lord, Israel’s God, which He spoke through His servant Jonah son of Amathi, the prophet from Gethchober.

14:26For the Lord saw Israel’s humiliation was very bitter, and they were few, hemmed in, impoverished, and abandoned; there was no one helping Israel.

14:27The Lord had not spoken of blotting out Israel’s seed from beneath heaven, and He saved them through Jeroboam son of Joash.

14:28The rest of Jeroboam’s deeds, all that he did, his mighty acts, all his warfare, and how he restored Damascus and Aimath to Judah in Israel—are these not written in the book of the chronicles of the kings of Israel?

14:29Jeroboam lay down with his fathers, with Israel’s kings, and Azariah son of Amaziah became king in place of his father.

Azariah King of Judah

15:1In the twenty-seventh year of Jeroboam king of Israel, Azariah son of Amaziah, king of Judah, became king.

15:2He was sixteen years old when he became king, and he reigned fifty-two years in Jerusalem. His mother’s name was Chalia, from Jerusalem.

15:3He did what was right in the Lord’s eyes, in accordance with everything his father Amaziah had done.

15:4Only he did not remove the high places; the people still sacrificed and burned incense at the high places.

Zechariah King of Israel

15:9He did evil in the Lord’s eyes as his fathers had done. He did not turn away from the sins of Jeroboam son of Nebat, who caused Israel to sin.

15:10Sellum son of Jabis and Keblaam conspired against him, struck him, and put him to death; Sellum became king in his place.

15:11The rest of Zechariah’s deeds—behold, they are written in the book of the chronicles of the kings of Israel.

Sellum King of Israel

Manaem King of Israel

15:22Manaem lay down with his fathers, and his son Phakeias became king in his place.

15:24He did evil in the Lord’s eyes. He did not turn away from the sins of Jeroboam son of Nebat, who caused Israel to sin.

15:25Phakee son of Romelias, his third-ranking officer, conspired against him and struck him in Samaria, in front of the king’s house, with Argob and Aria. With him were fifty men from the four hundred. He put him to death and became king in his place.

15:26The rest of Phakeias’s deeds and all that he did—behold, they are written in the book of the chronicles of the kings of Israel.

Phakee King of Israel

15:27In the fifty-second year of Azariah king of Judah, Phakee son of Romelias became king over Israel in Samaria, for twenty years.

15:28He did evil in the Lord’s eyes. He did not turn away from any of the sins of Jeroboam son of Nebat, who caused Israel to sin.

15:29In the days of Phakee king of Israel, Thaglathphellasar king of the Assyrians came and took Ain, Abelbaithamaacha, Janoch, Kenez, Asor, Gilead, and Galilee, all Naphtali’s land, and deported them to the Assyrians.

15:30Hoshea son of Ela formed a conspiracy against Phakee son of Romelias, struck him, put him to death, and became king in his place, in the twentieth year of Jotham son of Azariah.

15:31The rest of Phakee’s deeds and all that he did—behold, they are written in the book of the chronicles of the kings of Israel.

Jotham King of Judah

15:32In the second year of Phakee son of Romelias, king of Israel, Jotham son of Azariah, king of Judah, became king.

15:33He was twenty-five years old when he became king, and he reigned sixteen years in Jerusalem. His mother’s name was Jerusa daughter of Sadok.

15:34He did what was right in the Lord’s eyes, in accordance with everything his father Ozias had done.

15:35Only he did not remove the high places; the people still sacrificed and burned incense at the high places. He built the upper gate of the Lord’s house.

15:36The rest of Jotham’s deeds and everything he did—are these not written in the book of the chronicles of the kings of Judah?

15:37In those days the Lord began sending Raasson king of Syria and Phakee son of Romelias against Judah.

15:38Jotham lay down with his fathers and was buried with his fathers in the city of his father David. His son Ahaz became king in his place.

2 Kings 16

Read this chapter on staging

Ahaz King of Judah

16:1In the seventeenth year of Phakee son of Romelias, Ahaz son of Jotham, king of Judah, became king.

16:2Ahaz was twenty years old when he became king, and he reigned sixteen years in Jerusalem. He did not faithfully do what was right in the eyes of the Lord his God, as his father David had done.

16:3He walked in the way of Jeroboam son of Nebat, king of Israel. He even led his son through fire, according to the abominations of the nations whom the Lord removed before the Israelites.

16:4He sacrificed and burned incense at the high places, on the hills, and under every leafy tree.

The Siege by Raasson and Phakee

16:5Then Raasson king of Syria and Phakee son of Romelias, king of Israel, went up to Jerusalem for war. They besieged Ahaz but could not wage the battle successfully.

16:6At that time Raasson king of Syria restored Ailath to Syria and expelled the Judeans from Ailath. Idumeans came to Ailath and lived there to this day.

16:7Ahaz sent messengers to Thaglathphellasar king of the Assyrians, saying, ‘I am your slave and your son. Come up and save me from the hand of the king of Syria and the hand of the king of Israel, who are rising against me.’

16:8Ahaz took the silver and gold found in the treasuries of the Lord’s house and the king’s house, and sent gifts to the king.

16:9The king of the Assyrians listened to him. The king of the Assyrians went up against Damascus, captured it, deported it, and put Raasson to death.

The Altar Copied from Damascus

16:10King Ahaz went to Damascus to meet Thaglathphellasar king of the Assyrians. He saw the altar in Damascus, and King Ahaz sent Uriah the priest the altar’s likeness and design for all its construction.

16:11Uriah the priest built the altar in accordance with everything King Ahaz sent from Damascus.

16:12The king saw the altar and went up onto it.

16:13He burned his whole burnt offering, his sacrifice, and his drink offering, and poured the blood of his peace offerings against the altar.

16:14He moved the bronze altar that stood before the Lord from the front of the Lord’s house, from between the altar and the Lord’s house, and placed it at the altar’s north side.

16:15King Ahaz commanded Uriah the priest: ‘On the great altar offer the morning whole burnt offering and evening sacrifice, the king’s whole burnt offering and sacrifice, and the whole burnt offering of all the people, their sacrifice, and their drink offering. Pour all blood of whole burnt offering and all blood of sacrifice against it. The bronze altar shall be for me for the morning.’

16:16Uriah the priest did in accordance with everything King Ahaz commanded him.

16:17King Ahaz cut apart the enclosures of the mechonoth and removed the basin from them. He took the Sea down from the bronze oxen beneath it and placed it on a stone base.

16:18He built the foundation of the seat in the Lord’s house, and he turned the king’s outer entrance into the Lord’s house because of the king of the Assyrians.

The Death of Ahaz

16:19The rest of Ahaz’s deeds, what he did—are these not written in the book of the chronicles of the kings of Judah?

2 Kings 22

Read this chapter on staging

Josiah King of Judah

22:1Josiah was eight years old when he became king, and he reigned thirty-one years in Jerusalem. His mother’s name was Jedida daughter of Edeia, from Basouroth.

22:2He did what was right in the Lord’s eyes and walked in every way of his father David, turning aside neither right nor left.

The Book of the Law Found

22:3In King Josiah’s eighteenth year, in the eighth month, the king sent Sapphan son of Eselias son of Mesollam, the scribe of the Lord’s house, saying,

22:4‘Go up to Chelkias the high priest and seal the silver brought into the Lord’s house, which those guarding the doorpost collected from the people.

22:5Let them give it into the hand of the workers appointed in the Lord’s house.’ He gave it to the workers in the Lord’s house to strengthen the house’s bedek:

22:6to the carpenters, builders, and wall builders, and to acquire timber and quarried stones to strengthen the house’s bedek.

22:7But they did not demand an account from them for the silver given to them, for they act faithfully.

22:8Chelkias the high priest said to Sapphan the scribe, ‘I found a book of the law in the Lord’s house.’ Chelkias gave the book to Sapphan, and he read it.

22:9He brought it to King Josiah, reported to the king, and said, ‘Your servants have melted down the silver found in the Lord’s house and given it into the hand of the workers appointed in the Lord’s house.’

22:10Sapphan the scribe told the king, ‘Chelkias the priest gave me a book.’ Sapphan read it before the king.

Josiah Tears His Garments

22:11When the king heard the words of the book of the law, he tore his garments.

22:12The king commanded Chelkias the priest, Achikam son of Sapphan, Achobor son of Michaias, Sapphan the scribe, and Asaia the king’s servant:

22:13‘Come, seek the Lord for me, for all the people, and for all Judah concerning the words of this book that has been found. For great is the Lord’s anger which burns among us, because our fathers did not listen to this book’s words, to do everything written against us.’

The Word of Holda the Prophetess

22:14Chelkias the priest, Achikam, Achobor, Sapphan, and Asaias went to Olda the prophetess, wife of Sellem son of Thekoue son of Araas, keeper of the garments. She lived in Jerusalem in the masena. They spoke to her.

22:15She said to them, ‘Thus says the Lord, Israel’s God: Tell the man who sent you to me:

22:16Thus says the Lord: Behold, I am bringing disasters on this place and its inhabitants, all the words of the book which the king of Judah has read,

22:17because they abandoned Me and burned incense to other gods, provoking Me with the works of their hands. My wrath shall burn against this place and shall not be quenched.

22:18But to the king of Judah who sent you to seek the Lord, say this: Thus says the Lord, Israel’s God: The words you have heard—

22:19because your heart was softened and you did feel shame before the Lord when you heard what I spoke against this place and its inhabitants, that they should become an obliteration and a curse, and because you did rend your garments and did weep before Me, I also have heard, says the Lord.

22:20Not so! Behold, I am adding you to your fathers, and you shall be gathered into your grave in peace. None of all the disasters I am bringing upon this place shall appear before your eyes.’ They reported the word to the king.

2 Kings 23

Read this chapter on staging

The Covenant Renewed

23:1The king sent and gathered to himself all the elders of Judah and Jerusalem.

23:2The king went up to the Lord’s house, with every man of Judah and all who inhabited Jerusalem, the priests, the prophets, and all the people, from small to great. He read in their hearing all the words of the covenant book found in the Lord’s house.

23:3The king stood beside the pillar and made a covenant before the Lord: to follow the Lord and keep His commandments, testimonies, and ordinances with the whole heart and whole soul, upholding the words of this covenant, the things written in this book. All the people stood in the covenant.

The Cleansing of the House and the Land

23:4The king commanded Chelkias the high priest, the priests of the second rank, and the doorpost guards to bring out of the Lord’s sanctuary all the vessels made for Baal, for the grove, and for all heaven’s host. He burned them outside Jerusalem in the sademoth of Kidron and took their dust to Bethel.

23:5He put an end to the chomarim whom Judah’s kings had appointed, who burned incense at the high places and in Judah’s cities and the places around Jerusalem, and to those burning incense to Baal, the sun, the moon, the mazouroth, and all heaven’s host.

23:6He brought the grove out of the Lord’s house, outside Jerusalem to the Kidron torrent. He burned it in the Kidron torrent, reduced it to dust, and threw its dust into the grave of the sons of the people.

23:7He pulled down the house of the kadesim in the Lord’s house, where the women were weaving chettiin for the grove.

23:8He brought up all the priests from Judah’s cities and defiled the high places where the priests burned incense, from Gabaa to Beersheba. He pulled down the house of the gates beside the doorway of the gate of Joshua, the city’s ruler, on a man’s left at the city gate.

23:9Nevertheless, the priests of the high places did not go up to the Lord’s altar in Jerusalem; they only ate unleavened bread among their brothers.

23:10He defiled Tapheth in the valley of the son of Ennom, for a man to lead his son, and a man his daughter, through fire to Moloch.

23:11He removed the horses which Judah’s kings had given to the sun, at the entrance of the Lord’s house, toward the treasury of Nathan the king, the eunuch, in the pharourim. He burned the sun’s chariot with fire.

23:12The king pulled down the altars on the roof of Ahaz’s upper chamber which Judah’s kings had made, and the altars Manasseh had made in the two courts of the Lord’s house. He tore them down from there and threw their dust into the Kidron torrent.

23:13The king defiled the house facing Jerusalem, to the right of Mount Mosoath, which Solomon king of Israel had built for Astarte, the Sidonians’ offensive thing, for Chamos, Moab’s offensive thing, and for Molchol, the Ammonites’ abomination.

23:14He shattered the pillars, destroyed the groves, and filled their sites with human bones.

The Altar at Bethel Torn Down

23:15Moreover, he tore down the altar at Bethel, the high place Jeroboam son of Nabat had made, who caused Israel to sin—both that altar and the high place. He shattered its stones, reduced it to dust, and burned the grove.

23:16Josiah turned aside and saw the tombs there in the city. He sent and took the bones from the tombs, burned them on the altar, and defiled it, according to the Lord’s word which the man of God spoke while Jeroboam stood at the festival beside the altar. Turning, he lifted his eyes toward the tomb of the man of God who had spoken these words.

23:17He said, ‘What is that rocky marker I see?’ The city’s men told him, ‘It is the man of God who came out of Judah and proclaimed these words which he proclaimed against Bethel’s altar.’

23:18He said, ‘Leave it alone. Let no one move his bones.’ His bones were spared together with the bones of the prophet who came from Samaria.

23:19Josiah also removed all the high-place houses in Samaria’s cities which Israel’s kings had made to provoke the Lord. He carried out in them all the works he had carried out at Bethel.

23:20He sacrificed all the priests of the high places who were there upon the altars, and burned human bones upon them. Then he returned to Jerusalem.

The Passover Kept

23:21The king commanded all the people, ‘Keep Passover to the Lord our God, as written in this covenant book.’

23:22For this Passover had not occurred since the days of the judges who judged Israel, throughout all the days of Israel’s kings and Judah’s kings.

23:23Only in King Josiah’s eighteenth year did this Passover to the Lord take place in Jerusalem.

23:24King Josiah also removed the charmers, the knowing ones, the theraphin, the idols, and all the offensive things present in Judah’s land and Jerusalem, to uphold the law’s words written in the book Chelkias the priest found in the Lord’s house.

23:25Before him there was no king like him who returned to the Lord with his whole heart, his whole soul, and all his strength, according to all Moses’ law; and after him none like him arose.

The Lord Does Not Turn from His Anger

23:26Nevertheless, the Lord did not turn from His great burning wrath which burned against Judah because of the provocations with which Manasseh had provoked Him.

23:27The Lord said, ‘I shall also remove Judah from My presence, as I removed Israel. I shall reject this city which I chose, Jerusalem, and the house of which I said, “My name shall be there.”’

23:28The rest of Josiah’s acts and everything he did—are these not written in the book of the chronicles of Judah’s kings?

The Death of Josiah at Mageddo

23:29In his days Pharaoh Nechao king of Egypt went up against the king of the Assyrians to the River Euphrates. Josiah went to meet him, and Nechao killed him at Mageddo when he saw him.

23:30His servants transported him dead from Mageddo, brought him to Jerusalem, and buried him in his grave in David’s city. The people of the land took Joachas son of Josiah, anointed him, and made him king in his father’s place.

Joachas King of Judah

23:31Joachas was twenty-three years old when he became king, and he reigned three months in Jerusalem. His mother’s name was Amital daughter of Jeremiah, from Lemna.

23:32He did what was evil in the Lord’s eyes, according to everything his fathers had done.

23:33Pharaoh Nechao removed him at Deblatha in Emath’s land so that he would not reign in Jerusalem. He imposed a penalty on the land of one hundred talents of silver and one hundred talents of gold.

Joakim King of Judah

23:34Pharaoh Nechao made Eliakim, son of Josiah king of Judah, king over them in place of his father Josiah and changed his name to Joakim. He took Joachas and brought him into Egypt, and he died there.

23:35Joakim gave the silver and gold to Pharaoh, but assessed the land to provide the silver at Pharaoh’s command. Each according to his assessment, they gave the silver and gold with the people of the land, to give to Pharaoh Nechao.

23:36Joakim was twenty-five years old when he became king, and he reigned eleven years in Jerusalem. His mother’s name was Jeldaph daughter of Phedeia, from Rouma.

23:37He did what was evil in the Lord’s eyes, according to everything his fathers had done.

Nabouchodonosor Comes Up Against Judah

24:1In his days Nabouchodonosor king of Babylon came up. Joakim became his slave for three years, then turned and broke faith with him.

24:2He sent against him the lightly equipped raiders of the Chaldeans, Syria, Moab, and the Ammonites. He sent them out in Judah’s land to prevail, according to the Lord’s word which He spoke through His servants the prophets.

24:3Nevertheless, this came upon Judah because of the Lord’s anger, to remove it from His presence because of Manasseh’s sins, according to everything he had done.

24:4He also shed innocent blood and filled Jerusalem with innocent blood, and the Lord was unwilling to be propitiated.

24:5The rest of Joakim’s acts and everything he did—behold, are these not written in the book of the chronicles of Judah’s kings?

24:6Joakim lay down with his fathers, and his son Joakim became king in his place.

24:7The king of Egypt did not again leave his land, because the king of Babylon had taken everything belonging to the king of Egypt from Egypt’s torrent to the River Euphrates.

Joakim Son of Joakim King of Judah

24:8Joakim was eighteen years old when he became king, and he reigned three months in Jerusalem. His mother’s name was Nestha daughter of Ellanathan, from Jerusalem.

24:9He did what was evil in the Lord’s eyes, according to everything his father had done.

The First Deportation

24:10At that time Nabouchodonosor king of Babylon came up to Jerusalem, and the city came under siege.

24:11Nabouchodonosor king of Babylon entered the city while his servants were besieging it.

24:12Joakim king of Judah went out to the king of Babylon, he, his servants, his mother, his rulers, and his eunuchs. The king of Babylon took him in the eighth year of his reign.

24:13He brought out from there all the treasures of the Lord’s house and the treasures of the king’s house. He cut apart all the gold vessels Solomon king of Israel had made in the Lord’s sanctuary, according to the Lord’s word.

24:14He deported Jerusalem, all the rulers, and the mighty in strength, taking ten thousand captives, and every craftsman and one who shuts up. None remained except the land’s poor.

24:15He deported Joakim to Babylon, along with the king’s mother, the king’s wives, and his eunuchs. He led the land’s powerful men into deportation from Jerusalem to Babylon,

24:16and all the men of the force, seven thousand, and the craftsmen and those who shut up, one thousand—all mighty men who waged war. The king of Babylon brought them into deportation to Babylon.

24:17The king of Babylon made Maththanias, his son, king in his place and gave him the name Sedekias.

Sedekias King of Judah

24:18Sedekias was twenty-one years old when he became king, and he reigned eleven years in Jerusalem. His mother’s name was Amital daughter of Jeremiah.

24:19He did what was evil before the Lord, according to everything Joakim had done.

24:20For this came upon Jerusalem and Judah because of the Lord’s anger, until He cast them from His presence. Sedekias broke faith with the king of Babylon.

2 Kings 25

Read this chapter on staging

The Siege of Jerusalem

25:1In the ninth year of his reign, in the tenth month, Nabouchodonosor king of Babylon came with all his force against Jerusalem. He camped against it and built an enclosing wall against it all around.

25:2The city remained under siege until King Sedekias’s eleventh year.

25:3On the ninth of the month the famine grew severe in the city, and there were no loaves for the people of the land.

The Flight and Capture of Sedekias

25:4The city was breached, and all the men of war went out by night by way of the gate between the walls, the one belonging to the king’s garden, with the Chaldeans surrounding the city. He went by the way of the Araba.

25:5The Chaldean force pursued the king and overtook him in Araboth Jericho, and all his force scattered away from him.

25:6They seized the king and brought him to the king of Babylon at Deblatha, and he pronounced judgment upon him.

25:7He slaughtered Sedekias’s sons before his eyes. He blinded Sedekias’s eyes, bound him with fetters, and brought him to Babylon.

The Burning of the House of the Lord

25:8In the fifth month, on the seventh of the month, in the nineteenth year of Nabouchodonosor king of Babylon, Nabouzardan, the chief cook who stood before the king of Babylon, came to Jerusalem.

25:9He set fire to the Lord’s house, the king’s house, and all Jerusalem’s houses; every house was set ablaze by

25:10the chief cook.

25:11Nabouzardan, the chief cook, removed the rest of the people left in the city, those who had gone over to the king of Babylon, and the remaining support.

25:12The chief cook left some of the land’s poor as vinedressers and as gabin.

The Vessels Carried to Babylon

25:13The Chaldeans shattered the bronze pillars in the Lord’s house, the mechonoth, and the bronze sea in the Lord’s house, and carried their bronze to Babylon.

25:14He took the cauldrons, the iamin, the bowls, the incense dishes, and all the bronze utensils with which they ministered.

25:15The chief cook took the firepans and the gold and silver bowls,

25:16the two pillars, the one sea, and the mechonoth which Solomon made for the Lord’s house. The bronze of all the objects had no reckoned weight.

25:17One pillar was eighteen cubits high, with the bronze chothar upon it. The chothar was three cubits high, with sabacha and pomegranates around the chothar, all bronze. The second pillar was the same, with the sabacha.

The Officers Put to Death at Deblatha

25:18The chief cook took Saraias the first priest, Sophonias, son of the second rank, and the three doorpost guards,

25:19and from the city he took one eunuch who supervised the warriors, five men who saw the king’s face who were found in the city, the scribe of the commander of the force who drew up the people of the land, and sixty men of the people of the land found in the city.

25:20Nabouzardan, the chief cook, took them and led them to the king of Babylon at Deblatha.

25:21The king of Babylon struck them and put them to death at Deblatha in Aimath’s land. Judah was deported from its land.

Godolias Appointed and Slain

25:22As for the people remaining in Judah’s land whom Nabouchodonosor king of Babylon had left, he appointed over them Godolias son of Achikam son of Saphan.

25:23All the commanders of the force, they and their men, heard that the king of Babylon had appointed Godolias. They came to Godolias at Massephath: Ishmael son of Nathanias, Joanan son of Karee, Saraias son of Thanemath the Netophathite, and Jezonias son of the Machathi, they and their men.

25:24Godolias swore to them and their men and said, ‘Do not fear the passing of the Chaldeans. Settle in the land and serve the king of Babylon, and it shall go well for you.’

25:25In the seventh month Ishmael son of Nathanias son of Elisama, of the kings’ offspring, came with ten men. He struck Godolias—who died—and the Judeans and Chaldeans who were with him at Massephath.

25:26All the people, from small to great, and the commanders of the forces arose and entered Egypt, for they feared the Chaldeans.

Joakim Released in Babylon

25:27In the thirty-seventh year of the deportation of Joakim king of Judah, in the twelfth month, on the twenty-seventh of the month, Euilmarodach king of Babylon, in the year of his reign, lifted the head of Joakim king of Judah and brought him out of his prison house.

25:28He spoke kindly to him and placed his throne above the thrones of the kings who were with him in Babylon,

25:29and he changed his prison garments, and he ate bread continually before him all the days of his life.

25:30His table provision was given to him continually from the king’s house, each day’s portion on its day, all the days of his life.
