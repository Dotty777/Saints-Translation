2 KINGS 1

1:1 Moab broke faith with Israel after Ahab died.
1:2 Ahaziah fell through the lattice in his upper room in Samaria and became ill. He sent messengers and said to them, ‘Come, and inquire of Baal Fly, the god of Ekron, whether I shall recover from this illness of mine.’ And they went to inquire through him.
1:3 An angel of the Lord spoke to Elijah the Tishbite, saying, ‘Rise, come to meet the messengers of Ahaziah king of Samaria, and say to them: Is it because there is no God in Israel that you are going to inquire of Baal Fly, the god of Ekron?’
1:4 ‘It shall not be so! For thus says the Lord: The bed onto which you have gone up—you will not come down from it, for there you will surely die.’ And Elijah went and spoke to them.
1:5 The messengers returned to him, and he said to them, ‘Why have you returned?’
1:6 They said to him, ‘A man came up to meet us and said to us: Come, return to the king who sent you and tell him, Thus says the Lord: Is it because there is no God in Israel that you are going to inquire of Baal Fly, the god of Ekron? It shall not be so! The bed onto which you have gone up—you will not come down from it, for you will surely die.’
1:7 He spoke to them, saying, ‘What was the character of the man who came up to meet you and spoke these words to you?’
1:8 They said to him, ‘A hairy man, with a leather belt fastened around his waist.’ He said, ‘This is Elijah the Tishbite.’
1:9 He sent to him a commander, a captain of fifty, with his fifty men. He went up and came to him, and there Elijah was, sitting on the top of the mountain. The captain of fifty spoke to him and said, ‘Man of God, the king has summoned you. Come down!’
1:10 Elijah answered and said to the captain of fifty, ‘If I am a man of God, fire will come down from heaven and consume you and your fifty men.’ Fire came down from heaven and consumed him and his fifty men.
1:11 The king again sent to him another captain of fifty with his fifty men. The captain went up and spoke to him, saying, ‘Man of God, thus says the king: Come down quickly!’
1:12 Elijah answered and spoke to him, saying, ‘If I am a man of God, fire will come down from heaven and consume you and your fifty men.’ Fire came down from heaven and consumed him and his fifty men.
1:13 The king again sent a commander, a third captain of fifty, with his fifty men. The third captain of fifty came to him, bent his knees before Elijah, and pleaded with him, saying, ‘Man of God, please let my life and the lives of these fifty slaves of yours be honored in your eyes.’
1:14 ‘Behold, fire came down from heaven and consumed the first two captains of fifty and their fifty men. But now please let the lives of your slaves be honored in your eyes.’
1:15 An angel of the Lord spoke to Elijah and said, ‘Go down with him; do not be afraid of them.’ Elijah rose and went down with him to the king.
1:16 Elijah spoke to him and said, ‘Thus says the Lord: Why did you send messengers to inquire of Baal Fly, the god of Ekron? It shall not be so! The bed onto which you have gone up—you will not come down from it, for you will surely die.’
1:17 He died according to the word of the Lord that Elijah had spoken.
1:18 The rest of the acts of Ahaziah, all that he did—behold, are these not written in the book of the daily records for the kings of Israel?
1:18a Jehoram son of Ahab reigns over Israel in Samaria for twelve years, beginning in the eighteenth year of Jehoshaphat king of Judah.
1:18b He did what was evil before the Lord, though not as his brothers did, nor as his mother did.
1:18c He removed the pillars of Baal that his father had made and shattered them. Yet he clung to the sins of the house of Jeroboam, who caused Israel to sin; he did not turn away from them.
1:18d The Lord was stirred to anger against the house of Ahab.

2 KINGS 2

2:1 When the Lord was about to take Elijah up in a tumult, as into heaven, Elijah and Elisha went from Gilgal.
2:2 Elijah said to Elisha, ‘Please stay here, for the Lord has sent me as far as Bethel.’ Elisha said, ‘As the Lord lives, and as you live, I will not leave you.’ So they came to Bethel.
2:3 The sons of the prophets who were in Bethel came to Elisha and said to him, ‘Do you know that this day the Lord is taking your master away from above your head?’ He said, ‘I too know it. Be silent.’
2:4 Elijah said to Elisha, ‘Please stay here, for the Lord has sent me to Jericho.’ Elisha said, ‘As the Lord lives, and as you live, I will not leave you.’ So they came to Jericho.
2:5 The sons of the prophets who were in Jericho approached Elisha and said to him, ‘Do you know that this day the Lord is taking your master away from above your head?’ He said, ‘Indeed, I too know it. Be silent.’
2:6 Elijah said to him, ‘Please stay here, for the Lord has sent me as far as the Jordan.’ Elisha said, ‘As the Lord lives, and as you live, I will not leave you.’ So both of them went on.
2:7 Fifty men, sons of the prophets, stood opposite them at a distance, while both of them stood beside the Jordan.
2:8 Elijah took his sheepskin, rolled it up, and struck the water. The water divided to this side and that, and both of them crossed over on barren ground.
2:9 When they had crossed, Elijah said to Elisha, ‘Ask what I should do for you before I am taken up from you.’ Elisha said, ‘Please let a double portion of your spirit be upon me.’
2:10 Elijah said, ‘You have made a difficult request. If you see me being taken up from you, it will be so for you; but if not, it certainly will not happen.’
2:11 As they went, walking and speaking, behold—a chariot of fire and horses of fire separated the two of them, and Elijah was taken up in a tumult, as into heaven.
2:12 Elisha was watching and crying, ‘Father, father! Israel’s chariot and its horseman!’ He saw him no more. He seized his clothes and tore them into two pieces.
2:13 He picked up Elijah’s sheepskin, which had fallen upon Elisha, and Elisha returned and stood on the edge of the Jordan.
2:14 He took Elijah’s sheepskin, which had fallen upon him, and struck the water, but it did not separate. He said, ‘Where is the God of Elijah—Appho?’ He struck the waters, and they split to this side and that, and Elisha crossed over.
2:15 The sons of the prophets in Jericho saw him from opposite him and said, ‘The spirit of Elijah has come to rest upon Elisha.’ They came to meet him and bowed before him to the ground.
2:16 They said to him, ‘Behold, with your servants are fifty capable men. Please let them go and search for your master, in case a spirit of the Lord has lifted him and cast him into the Jordan, or onto one of the mountains, or onto one of the hills.’ Elisha said, ‘Do not send them.’
2:17 They pressed him until he was ashamed, and he said, ‘Send them.’ They sent fifty men, who searched for three days but did not find him.
2:18 They returned to him while he was staying in Jericho, and Elisha said, ‘Did I not tell you, Do not go?’
2:19 The men of the city said to Elisha, ‘Behold, the city’s settlement is good, as the lord sees, but the waters are bad and the land is bereaved of offspring.’
2:20 Elisha said, ‘Bring me a new small water jar and put salt in it.’ They brought it to him.
2:21 Elisha went out to the outlet of the waters, threw salt there, and said, ‘Thus says the Lord: I have healed these waters. No longer will death or loss of offspring come from there.’
2:22 The waters have remained healed to this day, according to the word Elisha spoke.
2:23 He went up from there to Bethel. As he was going up along the road, small children came out from the city and made fun of him, saying to him, ‘Go up, baldhead! Go up!’
2:24 He turned back toward them, saw them, and cursed them in the name of the Lord. Behold, two bears came out of the woodland and tore open forty-two of the children.
2:25 He went from there to Mount Carmel, and from there he returned to Samaria.

2 KINGS 3

3:1 Jehoram son of Ahab became king in Israel in the eighteenth year of Jehoshaphat king of Judah, and he reigned twelve years.
3:2 He did what was evil in the eyes of the Lord, though not as his father and not as his mother had done. He removed the pillars of Baal that his father had made.
3:3 Yet he clung to the sin of Jeroboam son of Nebat, who caused Israel to sin; he did not turn away from it.
3:4 Mesha king of Moab was a noqed. At the rising, he rendered to the king of Israel a hundred thousand lambs and a hundred thousand rams in their fleeces.
3:5 After Ahab died, the king of Moab broke faith with the king of Israel.
3:6 King Jehoram went out from Samaria that day and mustered Israel.
3:7 He went and sent word to Jehoshaphat king of Judah, saying, ‘The king of Moab has broken faith with me. Will you go with me to Moab for war?’ He said, ‘I will go up. As I am, so are you; my people are as your people, my horses as your horses.’
3:8 He said, ‘By which road shall I go up?’ He said, ‘By the wilderness road of Edom.’
3:9 The king of Israel, the king of Judah, and the king of Edom went on. They made a circuit of seven days’ journey, but there was no water for the camp or the livestock at their feet.
3:10 The king of Israel said, ‘Alas! The Lord has called the three kings who are passing through to give them into the hand of Moab.’
3:11 Jehoshaphat said, ‘Is there no prophet of the Lord here, through whom we may inquire of the Lord?’ One of the attendants of the king of Israel answered, ‘Elisha son of Shaphat is here, who poured water on Elijah’s hands.’
3:12 Jehoshaphat said, ‘A word of the Lord is with him.’ The king of Israel, Jehoshaphat king of Judah, and the king of Edom went down to him.
3:13 Elisha said to the king of Israel, ‘What have I to do with you? Go to your father’s prophets.’ The king of Israel said to him, ‘No! For the Lord has called the three kings together to hand them over into the hands of Moab.’
3:14 Elisha said, ‘As the Lord of hosts lives, before whom I stand, if I were not showing regard for Jehoshaphat king of Judah, I would neither look toward you nor see you.’
3:15 ‘But now bring me a player of strings.’ As the player plucked the strings, the hand of the Lord came upon him.
3:16 He said, ‘Thus says the Lord: Make this torrent bed pit after pit.’
3:17 ‘For thus says the Lord: You will see no wind and see no rain, yet this torrent bed will be filled with water. You will drink—you, your possessions, and your livestock.’
3:18 ‘This is a light thing in the eyes of the Lord; I will also hand Moab over into your hand.’
3:19 ‘You will strike every fortified city. You will bring down every good tree, block every spring of water, and make every good plot useless with stones.’
3:20 In the morning, when the sacrifice was going up, look—waters were coming from the direction of Edom, and the land filled with water.
3:21 All Moab heard that the kings had come up to fight them. They raised a cry, from everyone wearing a belt and upward, and stood at the border.
3:22 They rose early in the morning, and the sun rose over the waters. From opposite them Moab saw the waters fiery red, like blood.
3:23 They said, ‘This is blood from the sword! The kings have fought and each has struck his neighbor. Now, Moab, to the spoils!’
3:24 They entered Israel’s camp. Israel rose and struck Moab, and they fled before them. They kept going in and striking Moab.
3:25 They tore down the cities. Each man threw his stone onto every good plot, filling it. They blocked every spring of water and brought down every good tree, until the stones of the wall were left demolished. The slingers surrounded it and struck it.
3:26 The king of Moab saw that the battle had become too strong for him. He took with him seven hundred men with drawn swords to break through to the king of Edom, but they could not.
3:27 He took his firstborn son, who had reigned in his place, and offered him as a whole burnt offering on the wall. Great regret came upon Israel, and they withdrew from him and returned to the land.

2 KINGS 4

4:1 A woman from among the sons of the prophets cried to Elisha, saying, ‘Your slave, my husband, has died. You know that he was a slave who feared the Lord. Now the lender has come to take my two sons as his slaves.’
4:2 Elisha said, ‘What should I do for you? Tell me what you have in the house.’ She said, ‘Your slave woman has nothing in the house except oil with which I may anoint myself.’
4:3 He said to her, ‘Go, ask for vessels from outside, from all your neighbors—empty vessels, not a few.’
4:4 ‘Then go inside and shut the door behind yourself and your sons. Pour into these vessels, and take away each full one.’
4:5 She went away from him and did so. She shut the door behind herself and her sons. They kept bringing vessels to her, and she kept pouring,
4:6 until the vessels were full. She said to her sons, ‘Bring me another vessel.’ They said to her, ‘There is no other vessel.’ Then the oil stopped.
4:7 She came and reported to the man of God. Elisha said, ‘Go, sell the oil, and pay your interest. You and your sons can live on the remaining oil.’
4:8 One day Elisha passed through Shunem. A prominent woman was there, and she pressed him to eat bread. As he continued coming there over time, he would turn aside to eat there.
4:9 The woman said to her husband, ‘Behold, I know that this is a holy man of God who continually passes by us.’
4:10 ‘Please let us make a small upstairs place for him and put a bed, a table, a stool, and a lampstand there for him. When he comes to us, he can turn aside there.’
4:11 One day he came there, turned aside to the upper room, and slept there.
4:12 He said to Gehazi his attendant, ‘Call this Shunammite woman for me.’ He called her, and she stood before him.
4:13 He said to him, ‘Please say to her: Behold, you have shown us all this extraordinary concern. What should be done for you? Do you have a matter for the king or the commander of the army?’ She said, ‘I am living among my own people.’
4:14 He said, ‘What should be done for her?’ Gehazi his attendant said, ‘Indeed, she has no son, and her husband is an old man.’
4:15 He called her, and she stood beside the door.
4:16 Elisha said to her, ‘At this season, as the season comes alive, you will be holding a son.’ She said, ‘No, my lord! Do not deceive your slave woman.’
4:17 The woman conceived and bore a son at that season, as the season came alive, just as Elisha had told her.
4:18 The child grew strong. When the child went out to his father, to the reapers,
4:19 he said to his father, ‘My head, my head!’ His father said to the attendant, ‘Carry him to his mother.’
4:20 He carried him to his mother. The boy lay on her knees until noon, and died.
4:21 She carried him up and laid him on the bed of the man of God, shut the door behind him, and went out.
4:22 She called her husband and said, ‘Please send me one of the attendants and one of the female donkeys, so that I may run to the man of God and return.’
4:23 He said, ‘Why are you going to him this day? It is neither new moon nor Sabbath.’ She said, ‘Peace.’
4:24 She saddled the female donkey and said to her attendant, ‘Lead on, go. Do not hold me back from riding unless I tell you.
4:25 Come, and you will go and come to the man of God at Mount Carmel.’ When Elisha saw her coming, he said to Gehazi his attendant, ‘Behold, there is that Shunammite woman.’
4:26 ‘Now run to meet her and say: Is there peace with you? Is there peace with your husband? Is there peace with the child?’ She said, ‘Peace.’
4:27 She came to Elisha at the mountain and seized his feet. Gehazi approached to push her away, but Elisha said, ‘Leave her alone, for her soul is in pain within her. The Lord has hidden it from me and has not told me.’
4:28 She said, ‘Did I ask my lord for a son? Did I not say, Do not play me false?’
4:29 Elisha said to Gehazi, ‘Fasten your belt, take my staff in your hand, and come. If you meet a man, do not bless him, and if a man blesses you, do not answer him. Place my staff on the child’s face.’
4:30 The child’s mother said, ‘As the Lord lives, and as you live, I will not leave you.’ Elisha rose and went behind her.
4:31 Gehazi went ahead of her and placed the staff on the child’s face, but there was no voice and no hearing. He returned to meet him and reported, ‘The child has not been raised.’
4:32 Elisha entered the house, and there was the child, dead, lying on his bed.
4:33 Elisha entered the house, shut the door behind the two of them, and prayed to the Lord.
4:34 He went up and lay upon the child, putting his mouth on his mouth, his eyes on his eyes, and his hands on his hands. He bent over him, and the child’s flesh was warmed.
4:35 He returned and walked in the house this way and that. He went up and bent over the child as many as seven times, and the child opened his eyes.
4:36 Elisha called out to Gehazi and said, ‘Call this Shunammite woman.’ He called, and she came in to him. Elisha said, ‘Take your son.’
4:37 The woman came in, fell at his feet, and bowed to the ground. She took her son and went out.
4:38 Elisha returned to Gilgal. There was famine in the land, and the sons of the prophets were sitting before him. Elisha said to his attendant, ‘Set on the large cauldron and prepare a cooked dish for the sons of the prophets.’
4:39 One went out into the field to gather arioth. He found a vine in the field and gathered wild round fruit from it, filling his garment. He put it into the cauldron of the cooked dish, for they did not recognize it.
4:40 He poured it out for the men to eat. As they ate from the cooked dish, look—they cried out, ‘Death is in the cauldron, man of God!’ They could not eat it.
4:41 He said, ‘Bring flour and put it into the cauldron.’ Elisha said to Gehazi the attendant, ‘Pour it out for the people, and let them eat.’ There was no longer anything harmful there in the cauldron.
4:42 A man passed through from Baithsarisa and brought the man of God twenty barley loaves of firstfruits and cakes of dried fruit. He said, ‘Give them to the people, and let them eat.’
4:43 His minister said, ‘How can I put this before a hundred men?’ He said, ‘Give it to the people, and let them eat, for thus says the Lord: They will eat and leave some over.’
4:44 They ate and left some over, according to the word of the Lord.

2 KINGS 5

5:1 Naaman, commander of Syria’s army, was a great man before his master and admired in his presence, for through him the Lord had given deliverance to Syria. The man was powerful in strength, but afflicted with leprosy.
5:2 Lightly equipped Syrians had gone out and taken captive a young girl from the land of Israel. She attended Naaman’s wife.
5:3 She said to her mistress, ‘If only my master were before the prophet of God who is in Samaria! Then he would free him from his leprosy.’
5:4 She went in and reported to her own master, saying, ‘Thus and thus the girl from the land of Israel has spoken.’
5:5 The king of Syria said to Naaman, ‘Come, go in, and I will send a letter to the king of Israel.’ He went, taking with him ten talents of silver, six thousand gold coins, and ten changes of robes.
5:6 He brought the letter to the king of Israel. It said, ‘Now, when this letter reaches you, behold, I have sent you Naaman my slave, and you shall free him from his leprosy.’
5:7 When the king of Israel read the letter, he tore his garments and said, ‘Am I God, to put to death and bring to life, that this man sends to me to free a man from his leprosy? Please understand and see: this man is looking for a pretext against me.’
5:8 When Elisha heard that the king of Israel had torn his garments, he sent word to the king of Israel, saying, ‘Why have you torn your garments? Please let Naaman come to me, and let him know that there is a prophet in Israel.’
5:9 Naaman came with a horse and chariot and stood at the doors of Elisha’s house.
5:10 Elisha sent a messenger to him, saying, ‘Go and bathe seven times in the Jordan. Your flesh will be restored to you, and you will be cleansed.’
5:11 Naaman became angry and went away, saying, ‘Behold, I thought that he would come out to me, stand, call on the name of his God, place his hand on the place, and remove the leprous condition.’
5:12 ‘Are not Abanah and Pharpar, the rivers of Damascus, better than the Jordan and all the waters of Israel? Could I not go and bathe in them and be cleansed?’ He turned aside and went away in anger.
5:13 His attendants approached and spoke to him: ‘The prophet has told you a great thing; will you not do it? And he has said to you, Bathe and be cleansed.’
5:14 And Naaman went down, and dipped himself in the Jordan seven times, according to the word of Elisha; and his flesh was restored as the flesh of a little child, and he was cleansed.
5:15 He returned to Elisha, he and all his company. He came and stood and said, ‘Behold, now I know that there is no God in all the earth except in Israel. Now accept the blessing from your slave.’
5:16 Elisha said, ‘As the Lord lives, before whom I stand, I will not accept it.’ He pressed him to accept, but he would not comply.
5:17 Naaman said, ‘If not, please let your slave be given a pair of mules’ load, and you shall give me some of the red earth, for your slave will no longer offer whole burnt offering or sacrifice to other gods, but to the Lord alone.’
5:18 ‘The Lord will be propitious to your slave when my master enters the house of Remman to bow to him and leans on my arm, and I bow in the house of Remman when he bows in the house of Remman. The Lord will indeed be propitious to your slave in this matter.’
5:19 Elisha said to Naaman, ‘Go in peace.’ He went away from him a debratha of the land.
5:20 Gehazi, Elisha’s attendant, said, ‘Behold, my master has spared this Naaman the Syrian by not taking from his hand what he brought. As the Lord lives, I will run after him and take something from him.’
5:21 Gehazi pursued Naaman. Naaman saw him running after him and turned back from the chariot to meet him.
5:22 He said, ‘Peace. My master sent me, saying: Behold, two young men from the sons of the prophets have now come to me from Mount Ephraim. Please give them a talent of silver and two changes of robes.’
5:23 Naaman said, ‘Take two talents of silver.’ He took them in two bags, with two changes of robes, and placed them on two of his attendants, who carried them ahead of him.
5:24 They came to the dark place. He took the goods from their hands and set them in a house, then sent the men away.
5:25 He went in and stood beside his master. Elisha said to him, ‘From where, Gehazi?’ Gehazi said, ‘Your slave has not gone here or there.’
5:26 Elisha said to him, ‘Did not my heart go with you when the man turned from the chariot to meet you? Now you have taken the silver and now you have taken the garments, and with it you will acquire gardens and olive groves and vineyards and sheep and cattle and male servants and female servants.’
5:27 ‘Naaman’s leprosy will cling to you and to your offspring for ever.’ He went out from his presence afflicted with leprosy, like snow.

2 KINGS 6

6:1 The sons of the prophets said to Elisha, ‘Behold, the place where we dwell before you is too narrow for us.’
6:2 ‘Please let us go as far as the Jordan, and let each man take one beam from there. Let us build for ourselves there, to dwell there.’ He said, ‘Go on.’
6:3 One of them said, ‘Please come with your slaves.’ He said, ‘I will go.’
6:4 He went with them. They came to the Jordan and cut the timber.
6:5 Behold, as one of them was bringing down a beam, the iron fell out into the water. He cried, ‘Alas, lord! It was borrowed!’
6:6 The man of God said, ‘Where did it fall?’ He showed him the place. He chipped off a piece of wood and threw it there, and the iron floated.
6:7 He said, ‘Lift it up for yourself.’ He stretched out his hand and took it.
6:8 The king of Syria was at war in Israel. He consulted with his attendants, saying, ‘I will camp at this particular place, Elmoni.’
6:9 Elisha sent word to the king of Israel, saying, ‘Take care not to pass through this place, for Syria is concealed there.’
6:10 The king of Israel sent to the place of which Elisha had told him, and guarded himself from there—not once or twice.
6:11 The soul of the king of Syria was shaken over this matter. He called his attendants and said to them, ‘Will you not tell me who is betraying me to the king of Israel?’
6:12 One of his attendants said, ‘No, my lord king. Elisha, the prophet in Israel, reports to the king of Israel all the words you speak in the chamber of your bedroom.’
6:13 He said, ‘Go and see where he is, and I will send and take him.’ They reported to him, saying, ‘Behold, he is in Dothaim.’
6:14 He sent horse and chariot forces and a large army there. They came by night and encircled the city.
6:15 Elisha’s minister rose early and went out, and there was an army encircling the city, with horse and chariot forces. The attendant said to him, ‘Alas, lord! What shall we do?’
6:16 Elisha said, ‘Do not be afraid, for those with us are more numerous than those with them.’
6:17 Elisha prayed and said, ‘Lord, open the attendant’s eyes so that he may see.’ The Lord opened his eyes, and he saw: behold, the mountain was full of horses, and a chariot of fire was all around Elisha.
6:18 They came down to him, and Elisha prayed to the Lord, saying, ‘I pray Thee, strike this nation with blindness.’ He struck them with blindness according to Elisha’s word.
6:19 Elisha said to them, ‘This is not the city or the road. Follow me, and I will lead you to the man you seek.’ He led them to Samaria.
6:20 When they entered Samaria, Elisha said, ‘I pray Thee, Lord, open their eyes so they may see.’ The Lord opened their eyes, and they saw: behold, they were in the middle of Samaria.
6:21 When the king of Israel saw them, he said, ‘Shall I indeed strike them, father?’
6:22 He said, ‘You shall not strike them, except those whom you have captured with your sword and your bow—you strike those. Set loaves and water before them, and let them eat and drink and go to their master.’
6:23 He set a great spread before them. They ate and drank, and he sent them away. They went to their master, and Syria’s lightly equipped troops no longer came into the land of Israel.
6:24 After this, the son of Ader, king of Syria, gathered his whole army, went up, and laid siege to Samaria.
6:25 There was a great famine in Samaria. They besieged it until a donkey’s head cost fifty shekels of silver, and a quarter of a kab of pigeons’ dung cost five shekels of silver.
6:26 As the king of Israel was passing along the wall, a woman cried to him, saying, ‘Save me, lord king!’
6:27 He said to her, ‘May the Lord not save you! From where could I save you—from the threshing floor or from the winepress?’
6:28 The king said to her, ‘What is the matter with you?’ She said, ‘This woman said to me: Give your son, and we will eat him this day; and my son, we will eat him tomorrow.’
6:29 ‘We boiled my son and ate him. On the second day I said to her, Give your son, and let us eat him. But she hid her son.’
6:30 When the king of Israel heard the woman’s words, he tore his garments. He was passing along the wall, and the people saw sackcloth on his flesh underneath.
6:31 He said, ‘May God do thus to me, and add thus, if Elisha’s head remains on him this day.’
6:32 Elisha was sitting in his house, and the elders were sitting with him. The king sent a man ahead of him. Before the messenger reached him, Elisha said to the elders, ‘Do you know that this son of a murderer has sent to remove my head? Watch: when the messenger comes, shut the door and press him at the door. Is not the sound of his master’s feet behind him?’
6:33 While he was still speaking with them, behold, a messenger came down to him and said, ‘Behold, this calamity is from the Lord. Why should I wait for the Lord any longer?’

2 KINGS 7

7:1 Elisha said, ‘Hear the word of the Lord. Thus says the Lord: About this hour tomorrow, a measure of fine flour will sell for a shekel, and two measures of barley for a shekel, at the gates of Samaria.’
7:2 The officer on whose arm the king leaned answered Elisha, saying, ‘Behold, the Lord will make floodgates in heaven—surely this word cannot come true?’ Elisha said, ‘Behold, you will see it with your eyes, but you will not eat from there.’
7:3 Four leprous men were beside the city’s doorway. One said to his neighbor, ‘Why are we sitting here until we die?’
7:4 ‘If we say, Let us enter the city, the famine is in the city, and we will die there. If we sit here, we will die. Now come, let us throw ourselves upon the camp of Syria. If they keep us alive, we will live; if they kill us, we will die.’
7:5 They rose in the darkness to enter Syria’s camp. They came to a part of Syria’s camp, and behold, no man was there.
7:6 The Lord had made Syria’s camp hear the sound of a chariot, the sound of horse forces, and the sound of a great army. One said to his brother, ‘Now the king of Israel has hired the kings of the Hittites and the kings of Egypt against us, to come upon us.’
7:7 They rose and ran away in the darkness, abandoning their tents, their horses, and their donkeys in the camp just as it was, and fled for their lives.
7:8 These leprous men entered as far as part of the camp and entered one tent. They ate and drank, carried away silver, gold, and clothing from there, and went away. They returned, entered another tent, took things from there, and went and hid them.
7:9 One said to his neighbor, ‘We are not acting rightly. This day is a day of good news, yet we are silent and remain until morning light, and we will encounter lawlessness. Now come, let us go in and report to the king’s house.’
7:10 They came and cried toward the city gate and reported to them, saying, ‘We entered Syria’s camp, and behold, no man was there, not a human voice—only a tethered horse and a donkey, and their tents just as they were.’
7:11 The gatekeepers cried out and reported it inside the king’s house.
7:12 The king rose in the night and said to his attendants, ‘I will tell you what Syria has done to us. They know that we are hungry. They have left the camp and hidden in the field, saying: They will come out of the city; we will seize them alive and enter the city.’
7:13 One of his attendants answered, ‘Please let them take five of the remaining horses left here. Behold, they are like all the multitude of Israel that is failing. We will send there and see.’
7:14 They took two horse riders, and the king of Israel sent them after the king of Syria, saying, ‘Go and see.’
7:15 They followed them as far as the Jordan, and behold, the whole road was full of garments and equipment that Syria had thrown away in their panic. The messengers returned and reported to the king.
7:16 The people went out and plundered Syria’s camp. A measure of fine flour sold for a shekel, and two measures of barley for a shekel, according to the word of the Lord.
7:17 The king had placed the officer on whose arm the king leaned over the gate. The people trampled him in the gate, and he died, just as the man of God had said when the messenger came down to him.
7:18 It happened just as Elisha had said to the king: ‘Two measures of barley for a shekel, and a measure of fine flour for a shekel, will be available about this hour tomorrow at the gate of Samaria.’
7:19 The officer had answered Elisha, saying, ‘Behold, the Lord makes floodgates in heaven—surely this word cannot come true?’ Elisha had said, ‘Behold, you will see it with your eyes, but you will not eat from there.’
7:20 So it happened: the people trampled him in the gate, and he died.

8:3 After the end of seven years, the woman returned from the land of the foreigners to the city and came to cry to the king about her house and her fields.
8:4 The king was speaking to Gehazi, the attendant of Elisha the man of God, saying, ‘Please recount to me all the great things Elisha has done.’
8:5 As he was explaining to the king how he had revived a dead son, behold, the woman whose son Elisha had revived was crying to the king about her house and her fields. Gehazi said, ‘Lord king, this is the woman, and this is her son whom Elisha revived.’
8:6 The king questioned the woman, and she recounted it to him. The king assigned her one eunuch, saying, ‘Restore all her belongings and all the produce of her field from the day she left the land until now.’

8:8 The king said to Hazael, ‘Take a manaa in your hand and go to meet the man of God. Inquire of the Lord through him, saying, Will I recover from this illness of mine?’
8:9 Hazael went to meet him, taking a manaa in his hand and all the good things of Damascus, a load for forty camels. He came and stood before him and said to Elisha, ‘Your son, the son of Ader, king of Syria, sent me to you, saying, Will I recover from this illness of mine?’

8:11 He stood before his face and held it until shame, and the man of God wept.
8:12 Hazael said, ‘Why does my lord weep?’ He said, ‘Because I know all the evils you will do to the sons of Israel: you will send their strongholds into fire, kill their chosen men with the sword, dash down their infants, and tear open their pregnant women.’
8:13 Hazael said, ‘Who is your slave, a dead dog, that he should do this thing?’ Elisha said, ‘The Lord has shown me you reigning over Syria.’
8:14 He went away from Elisha and entered his master’s presence. He said to him, ‘What did Elisha say to you?’ He said, ‘He told me, You will surely live.’

8:19 Yet the Lord was unwilling to destroy Judah because of David His slave, as He had said He would give a lamp to him and to his sons all the days.
8:20 In his days Edom broke away from beneath Judah’s hand and made a king over themselves.

8:22 Edom has remained broken away from beneath Judah’s hand to this day. Lobena also broke away at that time.
8:23 The rest of the acts of Joram, and everything he did—behold, are these not written in the book of the daily records for the kings of Judah?
8:24 Joram lay down with his fathers and was buried with his fathers in the city of David his father. Ahaziah his son became king in his place.

8:26 Ahaziah was twenty-two years old when he became king, and he reigned one year in Jerusalem. His mother’s name was Athaliah, daughter of Omri king of Israel.
8:27 He walked in the way of the house of Ahab and did what was evil before the Lord, just as the house of Ahab did.
8:28 He went with Joram son of Ahab to war against Hazael king of the foreigners at Remmoth-gilead. The Syrians struck Joram.
8:29 King Joram returned to Jezreel to be treated for the wounds they had inflicted on him at Remmoth when he fought Hazael king of Syria. Ahaziah son of Joram went down to see Joram son of Ahab at Jezreel, because he was ill.

9:3 ‘Take the flask of oil, pour it over his head, and say: Thus says the Lord: I have anointed you king over Israel. Then open the door and flee; do not remain.’
9:4 The young man, the prophet, went to Remmoth-gilead
9:5 and entered, and there the army commanders were sitting. He said, ‘I have a word for you, commander.’ Jehu said, ‘For which of all of us?’ He said, ‘For you, commander.’

9:8 and at the hand of the whole house of Ahab. You shall destroy from Ahab’s house every one who urinates against a wall, both confined and abandoned in Israel.
9:9 I will make the house of Ahab like the house of Jeroboam son of Nebat and like the house of Baasha son of Ahijah.

9:15 and King Joram had returned to Jezreel to be treated for the wounds the Syrians had inflicted on him when he fought Hazael king of Syria. Jehu said, ‘If your life is with me, let no one escape from the city to go and report in Jezreel.’
9:16 Jehu rode on horseback, went on, and came down to Jezreel, for Joram king of Israel was being treated in Jezreel for the arrow wounds with which the Aramim had shot him at Rammath in the war against Hazael king of Syria, for he was powerful and a man of might. Ahaziah king of Judah had gone down to see Joram.

9:19 He sent a second horse rider. He came to him and said, ‘Thus says the king: Is there peace?’ Jehu said, ‘What have you to do with peace? Turn back behind me.’
9:20 The lookout reported, ‘He has reached them but has not returned.’ The driver was driving Jehu son of Nimshi, for he was in a deranged state.
9:21 Joram said, ‘Yoke up!’ A chariot was yoked. Joram king of Israel and Ahaziah king of Judah went out, each in his chariot, to meet Jehu. They found him in the portion of Naboth the Jezreelite.
9:22 When Joram saw Jehu, he said, ‘Is there peace, Jehu?’ Jehu said, ‘What peace? The prostitutions of Jezebel your mother and her many sorceries still remain.’
9:23 Joram turned his hands to flee and said to Ahaziah, ‘Deceit, Ahaziah!’

9:25 Jehu said to Badekar his officer, ‘Throw him into the portion of the field of Naboth the Jezreelite. For I remember you and me riding on paired teams behind Ahab his father, when the Lord took up this oracle against him, saying:
9:26 “Surely, alongside Naboth’s blood, I saw the blood of his sons yesterday, says the Lord, and I will repay him in this portion, says the Lord.” Now lift him up and throw him into the portion, according to the word of the Lord.’
9:27 Ahaziah king of Judah saw this and fled by the road of Baithaggan. Jehu pursued him and said, ‘Him too!’ He struck him in the chariot on the ascent of Gai, which is Ibleam. He fled to Megiddo and died there.

10:6 He wrote them a second letter, saying, ‘If you are for me and obey my voice, take the heads of the men, your master’s sons, and bring them to me at Jezreel about this hour tomorrow.’ The king’s sons were seventy men; these prominent men of the city were bringing them up.

10:8 The messenger came and reported, saying, ‘They have brought the heads of the king’s sons.’ He said, ‘Put them in two mounds beside the doorway of the gate until morning.’

10:13 and Jehu found the brothers of Ahaziah king of Judah. He said, ‘Who are you?’ They said, ‘We are Ahaziah’s brothers, and we have come down for the peace of the king’s sons and the ruling lady’s sons.’

10:15 He went from there and found Jonadab son of Rechab on the road coming to meet him, and blessed him. Jehu said to him, ‘Is your heart upright with my heart, as my heart is with your heart?’ Jonadab said, ‘It is.’ Jehu said, ‘If it is, give me your hand.’ He gave his hand, and he brought him up to himself onto the chariot.

10:20 Jehu said, ‘Consecrate a sacrificial festival for Baal.’ They proclaimed it.

Whoever stays away will not live.’ All Baal’s slaves, all his priests, and all his prophets came; not one man remained who did not arrive. They entered Baal’s house, and Baal’s house was filled from mouth to mouth.
10:22 Jehu said to the one over the house of Mesthaal, ‘Bring out garments for all Baal’s slaves.’ The dresser brought them out for them.
10:23 Jehu and Jonadab son of Rechab entered Baal’s house. He said to Baal’s slaves, ‘Search and see whether any of the Lord’s slaves are with you, for there must be only Baal’s slaves alone.’
10:24 He went in to perform the sacrifices and whole burnt offerings. Jehu stationed eighty men outside for himself and said, ‘If any man is saved from the men I bring into your hands, his life shall be in place of his life.’
10:25 When he finished performing the whole burnt offering, Jehu said to the runners and officers, ‘Go in and strike them. Let no man come out from them.’ They struck them with the edge of the sword. The runners and officers threw them out and went as far as the city of Baal’s house.

10:28 Jehu made Baal disappear from Israel.
10:29 Yet Jehu did not turn away from following the sins of Jeroboam son of Nebat, who caused Israel to sin: the golden heifers in Bethel and in Dan.
10:30 The Lord said to Jehu, ‘Because you have done well in doing what is upright in My eyes, and have done to Ahab’s house everything that was in My heart, sons of yours to the fourth generation will sit on Israel’s throne.’

10:32 In those days the Lord began cutting down within Israel, and Hazael struck them throughout Israel’s territory,
10:33 from the Jordan toward sunrise, all the land of Gilead belonging to Gad, Reuben, and Manasseh, from Aroer on the bank of the Arnon torrent, both Gilead and Bashan.
10:34 The rest of the acts of Jehu, everything he did, all his power, and the engagements he fought—are these not written in the book of the daily records for the kings of Israel?
10:35 Jehu lay down with his fathers, and they buried him in Samaria. Jehoahaz his son became king in his place.
10:36 The days Jehu reigned over Israel were twenty-eight years in Samaria.

12:3 Joash did what was upright before the Lord all the days in which Jehoiada the priest enlightened him.
12:4 Yet the high places were not removed, and the people were still sacrificing and burning incense there at the high places.

12:7 In the twenty-third year of King Joash, the priests had not strengthened the bedek of the house.

12:13 to the wall builders and stone quarriers, to acquire timber and quarried stones to make firm the bedek of the Lord’s house, for everything spent on strengthening the house.
12:14 But silver doors, nails, bowls, trumpets, or any gold or silver vessel were not to be made for the Lord’s house from the silver brought into the Lord’s house.
12:15 They would give it to those doing the work, and with it they strengthened the Lord’s house.
12:16 They did not call the men to account into whose hands they placed the silver to give to those doing the work, for they acted in their trustworthiness.

12:18 Then Hazael king of Syria went up, fought against Gath, and seized it. Hazael set his face to go up against Jerusalem.
12:19 Joash king of Judah took all the holy things that Jehoshaphat, Joram, and Ahaziah, his fathers and kings of Judah, had consecrated, his own holy things, and all the gold found in the treasuries of the Lord’s house and the king’s house. He sent them to Hazael king of Syria, and he went away from Jerusalem.
12:20 The rest of the acts of Joash, and everything he did—behold, are these not written in the book of the daily records for the kings of Judah?
12:21 His slaves rose, bound together a conspiracy, and struck Joash in the house of Mallo in Gaalla.
12:22 Jezichar son of Jemuath and Jezebuth—his son Shomer—his slaves, struck him, and he died. They buried him with his fathers in the city of David, and Amaziah his son became king in his place.

13:2 He did evil in the Lord’s eyes and followed the sins of Jeroboam son of Nebat, who caused Israel to sin; he did not turn away from them.
13:3 The Lord was fiercely angry with Israel, and He gave them into the hand of Hazael king of Syria and into the hand of the son of Ader, Hazael’s son, all those days.
13:4 Jehoahaz entreated the Lord’s face, and the Lord listened to him, for He saw Israel’s affliction, because the king of Syria afflicted them.
13:5 The Lord gave Israel deliverance, and Israel came out from under Syria’s hand. The Israelites lived in their dwellings as in former days.
13:6 Nevertheless, they did not turn away from the sins of Jeroboam’s house, who caused Israel to sin; they walked in them. Moreover, the grove stood in Samaria.
13:7 For no troops were left to Jehoahaz except fifty horsemen, ten chariots, and ten thousand foot soldiers, because the king of Syria destroyed them, and they made them like dust for trampling.
13:8 The rest of Jehoahaz’s deeds, all that he did, and his mighty acts—are these not written in the book of the chronicles of the kings of Israel?
13:9 Jehoahaz lay down with his fathers, and they buried him with his fathers in Samaria. His son Joash became king in his place.

13:11 He did evil in the Lord’s eyes. He did not turn away from every sin of Jeroboam son of Nebat, who caused Israel to sin; he walked in them.
13:12 The rest of Joash’s deeds, all that he did, and his mighty acts, which he performed with Amaziah king of Judah—are these not written in the book of the chronicles of the kings of Israel?
13:13 Joash lay down with his fathers, and Jeroboam sat on his throne in Samaria with the sons of Israel.
13:14 Elisha fell ill with the sickness from which he died. Joash king of Israel came down to him, wept over his face, and said, ‘Father, father! Israel’s chariot and its horseman!’
13:15 Elisha said to him, ‘Take a bow and arrows.’ He brought a bow and arrows to him.
13:16 He said to the king, ‘Put your hand on the bow.’ Joash put his hand on the bow, and Elisha placed his hands on the king’s hands.
13:17 He said, ‘Open the window toward the east.’ He opened it. Elisha said, ‘Shoot!’ He shot. He said, ‘An arrow of deliverance for the Lord, an arrow of deliverance against Syria! You shall strike Syria at Aphek until it is finished.’
13:18 Elisha said to him, ‘Take bows.’ He took them. He said to the king of Israel, ‘Strike the ground.’ The king struck three times and stopped.
13:19 The man of God was grieved at him and said, ‘If you had struck five or six times, then you would have struck Syria until it was finished. But now you shall strike Syria three times.’
13:20 Elisha died, and they buried him. Moab’s lightly equipped raiders came into the land when the year came round.
13:21 As they were burying the man, suddenly they saw the lightly equipped raider. They threw the man into Elisha’s grave. He went and touched Elisha’s bones, came to life, and stood on his feet.
13:22 Hazael afflicted Israel all Jehoahaz’s days.
13:23 The Lord had mercy on them, pitied them, and looked toward them because of His covenant with Abraham, Isaac, and Jacob. The Lord was unwilling to destroy them and did not cast them from His presence.
13:24 Hazael king of Syria died, and his son, the son of Ader, became king in his place.
13:25 Joash son of Jehoahaz took back from the son of Ader, Hazael’s son, the towns he had taken in war from his father Jehoahaz. Joash struck him three times and recovered Israel’s towns.

2 KINGS 14

14:1 In the second year of Joash son of Jehoahaz, king of Israel, Amaziah son of Joash, king of Judah, became king.
14:2 He was twenty-five years old when he became king, and he reigned twenty-nine years in Jerusalem. His mother’s name was Joadin, from Jerusalem.
14:3 He did what was right in the Lord’s eyes, though not like his father David. He did in accordance with everything his father Joash had done.
14:4 Only he did not remove the high places; the people still sacrificed and burned incense at the high places.
14:5 When the kingdom was firmly in his hand, he struck down his servants who had struck down his father.
14:6 But he did not put the killers’ sons to death, as written in the book of Moses’ laws, as the Lord commanded: ‘Fathers shall not die for sons, and sons shall not die for fathers; each shall die only for his own sins.’
14:7 He struck down ten thousand of Edom in Gaimela, captured the Rock in battle, and named it Kathoel, to this day.
14:8 Then Amaziah sent messengers to Joash son of Jehoahaz son of Jehu, king of Israel, saying, ‘Come, let us face one another.’
14:9 Joash king of Israel sent to Amaziah king of Judah, saying, ‘The thornbush in Lebanon sent to the cedar in Lebanon: “Give your daughter to my son as a wife.” But the wild animals in Lebanon passed by and trampled the thornbush.
14:10 You have indeed struck Idumea, and your heart has lifted you up. Enjoy glory while sitting at home! Why contend in your wickedness? You will fall, you and Judah with you.’
14:11 Amaziah did not listen. The king of Israel went up, and he and Amaziah king of Judah faced one another at Baithsamys in Judah.
14:12 Judah stumbled before Israel, and each man fled to his dwelling.
14:13 Joash son of Jehoahaz, king of Israel, captured Amaziah son of Joash son of Ahaziah, king of Judah, at Baithsamys. He came to Jerusalem and pulled down four hundred cubits of Jerusalem’s wall, from the Ephraim Gate to the Corner Gate.
14:14 He took the gold and silver, all the vessels found in the Lord’s house and in the treasuries of the king’s house, and the sons of the mixed unions, and returned to Samaria.
14:15 The rest of Joash’s deeds, what he did in his might, how he fought Amaziah king of Judah—are these not written in the book of the chronicles of the kings of Israel?
14:16 Joash lay down with his fathers and was buried in Samaria with the kings of Israel. His son Jeroboam became king in his place.
14:17 Amaziah son of Joash, king of Judah, lived fifteen years after the death of Joash son of Jehoahaz, king of Israel.
14:18 The rest of Amaziah’s deeds and everything he did—are these not written in the book of the chronicles of the kings of Judah?
14:19 They formed a conspiracy against him in Jerusalem, and he fled to Lachish. They sent after him to Lachish and put him to death there.
14:20 They carried him on horses, and he was buried in Jerusalem with his fathers in David’s city.
14:21 All the people of Judah took Azariah—he was sixteen years old—and made him king in place of his father Amaziah.
14:22 He built Ailoth and restored it to Judah after the king lay down with his fathers.
14:23 In the fifteenth year of Amaziah son of Joash, king of Judah, Jeroboam son of Joash became king over Israel in Samaria, for forty-one years.
14:24 He did evil before the Lord. He did not turn away from any of the sins of Jeroboam son of Nebat, who caused Israel to sin.
14:25 He shifted Israel’s boundary from the entrance of Aimath to the Sea of the Arabah, according to the word of the Lord, Israel’s God, which He spoke through His servant Jonah son of Amathi, the prophet from Gethchober.
14:26 For the Lord saw Israel’s humiliation was very bitter, and they were few, hemmed in, impoverished, and abandoned; there was no one helping Israel.
14:27 The Lord had not spoken of blotting out Israel’s seed from beneath heaven, and He saved them through Jeroboam son of Joash.
14:28 The rest of Jeroboam’s deeds, all that he did, his mighty acts, all his warfare, and how he restored Damascus and Aimath to Judah in Israel—are these not written in the book of the chronicles of the kings of Israel?
14:29 Jeroboam lay down with his fathers, with Israel’s kings, and Azariah son of Amaziah became king in place of his father.

2 KINGS 15

15:1 In the twenty-seventh year of Jeroboam king of Israel, Azariah son of Amaziah, king of Judah, became king.
15:2 He was sixteen years old when he became king, and he reigned fifty-two years in Jerusalem. His mother’s name was Chalia, from Jerusalem.
15:3 He did what was right in the Lord’s eyes, in accordance with everything his father Amaziah had done.
15:4 Only he did not remove the high places; the people still sacrificed and burned incense at the high places.
15:5 The Lord touched the king, and he was leprous until the day of his death. He reigned in the house of Apphousoth, while Jotham the king’s son was over the house, judging the people of the land.
15:6 The rest of Azariah’s deeds and all that he did—behold, are these not written in the book of the chronicles of the kings of Judah?
15:7 Azariah lay down with his fathers, and they buried him with his fathers in David’s city. His son Jotham became king in his place.
15:8 In the thirty-eighth year of Azariah king of Judah, Zechariah son of Jeroboam became king over Israel in Samaria, for six months.
15:9 He did evil in the Lord’s eyes as his fathers had done. He did not turn away from the sins of Jeroboam son of Nebat, who caused Israel to sin.
15:10 Sellum son of Jabis and Keblaam conspired against him, struck him, and put him to death; Sellum became king in his place.
15:11 The rest of Zechariah’s deeds—behold, they are written in the book of the chronicles of the kings of Israel.
15:12 The Lord’s word which He spoke to Jehu was: ‘Your sons to the fourth generation shall sit on Israel’s throne.’ And so it happened.
15:13 Sellum son of Jabis became king. In the thirty-ninth year of Azariah king of Judah, Sellum reigned a month of days in Samaria.
15:14 Manaem son of Gaddi went up from Tharsila, came to Samaria, struck Sellum son of Jabis in Samaria, and put him to death.
15:15 The rest of Sellum’s deeds and the conspiracy he formed—behold, they are written in the book of the chronicles of the kings of Israel.
15:16 Then Manaem struck Thersa, everything in it, and its territory from Thersa, because they did not open to him. He struck it and ripped open the pregnant women.
15:17 In the thirty-ninth year of Azariah king of Judah, Manaem son of Gaddi became king over Israel for ten years in Samaria.
15:18 He did evil in the Lord’s eyes. He did not turn away from any of the sins of Jeroboam son of Nebat, who caused Israel to sin.
15:19 In his days Phul king of the Assyrians went up against the land. Manaem gave Phul a thousand talents of silver so that his hand would be with him.
15:20 Manaem exacted the silver from Israel, from everyone mighty in strength, to give to the king of the Assyrians: fifty shekels for each man. The king of the Assyrians returned and did not remain there in the land.
15:21 The rest of Manaem’s deeds and all that he did—behold, are these not written in the book of the chronicles of the kings of Israel?
15:22 Manaem lay down with his fathers, and his son Phakeias became king in his place.
15:23 In the fiftieth year of Azariah king of Judah, Phakeias son of Manaem became king over Israel in Samaria, for two years.
15:24 He did evil in the Lord’s eyes. He did not turn away from the sins of Jeroboam son of Nebat, who caused Israel to sin.
15:25 Phakee son of Romelias, his third-ranking officer, conspired against him and struck him in Samaria, in front of the king’s house, with Argob and Aria. With him were fifty men from the four hundred. He put him to death and became king in his place.
15:26 The rest of Phakeias’s deeds and all that he did—behold, they are written in the book of the chronicles of the kings of Israel.
15:27 In the fifty-second year of Azariah king of Judah, Phakee son of Romelias became king over Israel in Samaria, for twenty years.
15:28 He did evil in the Lord’s eyes. He did not turn away from any of the sins of Jeroboam son of Nebat, who caused Israel to sin.
15:29 In the days of Phakee king of Israel, Thaglathphellasar king of the Assyrians came and took Ain, Abelbaithamaacha, Janoch, Kenez, Asor, Gilead, and Galilee, all Naphtali’s land, and deported them to the Assyrians.
15:30 Hoshea son of Ela formed a conspiracy against Phakee son of Romelias, struck him, put him to death, and became king in his place, in the twentieth year of Jotham son of Azariah.
15:31 The rest of Phakee’s deeds and all that he did—behold, they are written in the book of the chronicles of the kings of Israel.
15:32 In the second year of Phakee son of Romelias, king of Israel, Jotham son of Azariah, king of Judah, became king.
15:33 He was twenty-five years old when he became king, and he reigned sixteen years in Jerusalem. His mother’s name was Jerusa daughter of Sadok.
15:34 He did what was right in the Lord’s eyes, in accordance with everything his father Ozias had done.
15:35 Only he did not remove the high places; the people still sacrificed and burned incense at the high places. He built the upper gate of the Lord’s house.
15:36 The rest of Jotham’s deeds and everything he did—are these not written in the book of the chronicles of the kings of Judah?
15:37 In those days the Lord began sending Raasson king of Syria and Phakee son of Romelias against Judah.
15:38 Jotham lay down with his fathers and was buried with his fathers in the city of his father David. His son Ahaz became king in his place.

2 KINGS 16

16:1 In the seventeenth year of Phakee son of Romelias, Ahaz son of Jotham, king of Judah, became king.
16:2 Ahaz was twenty years old when he became king, and he reigned sixteen years in Jerusalem. He did not faithfully do what was right in the eyes of the Lord his God, as his father David had done.
16:3 He walked in the way of Jeroboam son of Nebat, king of Israel. He even led his son through fire, according to the abominations of the nations whom the Lord removed before the Israelites.
16:4 He sacrificed and burned incense at the high places, on the hills, and under every leafy tree.
16:5 Then Raasson king of Syria and Phakee son of Romelias, king of Israel, went up to Jerusalem for war. They besieged Ahaz but could not wage the battle successfully.
16:6 At that time Raasson king of Syria restored Ailath to Syria and expelled the Judeans from Ailath. Idumeans came to Ailath and lived there to this day.
16:7 Ahaz sent messengers to Thaglathphellasar king of the Assyrians, saying, ‘I am your slave and your son. Come up and save me from the hand of the king of Syria and the hand of the king of Israel, who are rising against me.’
16:8 Ahaz took the silver and gold found in the treasuries of the Lord’s house and the king’s house, and sent gifts to the king.
16:9 The king of the Assyrians listened to him. The king of the Assyrians went up against Damascus, captured it, deported it, and put Raasson to death.
16:10 King Ahaz went to Damascus to meet Thaglathphellasar king of the Assyrians. He saw the altar in Damascus, and King Ahaz sent Uriah the priest the altar’s likeness and design for all its construction.
16:11 Uriah the priest built the altar in accordance with everything King Ahaz sent from Damascus.
16:12 The king saw the altar and went up onto it.
16:13 He burned his whole burnt offering, his sacrifice, and his drink offering, and poured the blood of his peace offerings against the altar.
16:14 He moved the bronze altar that stood before the Lord from the front of the Lord’s house, from between the altar and the Lord’s house, and placed it at the altar’s north side.
16:15 King Ahaz commanded Uriah the priest: ‘On the great altar offer the morning whole burnt offering and evening sacrifice, the king’s whole burnt offering and sacrifice, and the whole burnt offering of all the people, their sacrifice, and their drink offering. Pour all blood of whole burnt offering and all blood of sacrifice against it. The bronze altar shall be for me for the morning.’
16:16 Uriah the priest did in accordance with everything King Ahaz commanded him.
16:17 King Ahaz cut apart the enclosures of the mechonoth and removed the basin from them. He took the Sea down from the bronze oxen beneath it and placed it on a stone base.
16:18 He built the foundation of the seat in the Lord’s house, and he turned the king’s outer entrance into the Lord’s house because of the king of the Assyrians.
16:19 The rest of Ahaz’s deeds, what he did—are these not written in the book of the chronicles of the kings of Judah?
16:20 Ahaz lay down with his fathers and was buried in David’s city. His son Hezekiah became king in his place.

2 KINGS 17

17:1 In the twelfth year of Ahaz king of Judah, Hoshea son of Ela became king in Samaria over Israel, for nine years.
17:2 He did evil in the Lord’s eyes, though not like the kings of Israel who were before him.
17:3 Salamanasar king of the Assyrians went up against him. Hoshea became his slave and paid him manaa.
17:4 The king of the Assyrians found wrongdoing in Hoshea, because he had sent messengers to Segor king of Egypt and had not brought manaa to the king of the Assyrians in that year. The king of the Assyrians besieged him and bound him in prison.
17:5 The king of the Assyrians went up through the whole land, went up to Samaria, and besieged it for three years.
17:6 In Hoshea’s ninth year the king of the Assyrians captured Samaria and deported Israel to the Assyrians. He settled them in Alae and Abor, rivers of Gozan, and Ore of the Medes.
17:7 This happened because the Israelites sinned against the Lord their God, who brought them up from Egypt, from under the hand of Pharaoh king of Egypt, and they feared other gods.
17:8 They walked in the ordinances of the nations whom the Lord removed before the Israelites; and Israel’s kings, all who acted so,
17:9 and all the Israelites who clothed themselves in words that were not so against the Lord their God, built themselves high places in all their cities, from watchtower to fortified city.
17:10 They set up pillars and groves for themselves on every high hill and under every leafy tree.
17:11 They burned incense there at all the high places like the nations whom the Lord deported before them. They made associates and engraved, to provoke the Lord.
17:12 They served the idols, concerning which the Lord had said to them, ‘You shall not do this thing to the Lord.’
17:13 The Lord warned Israel and Judah through all His prophets, every seer: ‘Turn from your evil ways and keep My commandments, My ordinances, and all the law I commanded your fathers, all that I sent to them through My servants the prophets.’
17:14 But they did not listen. They hardened their back more than the back of their fathers.
17:15 They did not keep His testimonies by which He warned them. They followed vain things and became vain, and followed the nations around them, concerning whom He had commanded them not to do such things.
17:16 They abandoned the commandments of the Lord their God. They made a casting for themselves, two heifers, made groves, bowed down to all heaven’s host, and served Baal.
17:17 They led their sons and daughters through fire, practiced divinations, read omens, and were sold to do evil in the Lord’s eyes, provoking Him.
17:18 The Lord was very angry with Israel and removed them from His presence. None remained except the tribe of Judah alone.
17:19 Judah also did not keep the commandments of the Lord their God; they walked in the ordinances Israel had made.
17:20 They rejected the Lord throughout all Israel’s seed; He shook them and gave them into the hands of those plundering them, until He cast them from His presence.
17:21 For Israel was apart from David’s house, and they made Jeroboam son of Nebat king. Jeroboam thrust Israel away from following the Lord and caused them to commit a great sin.
17:22 The Israelites walked in every sin Jeroboam committed; they did not turn away from it,
17:23 until the Lord removed Israel from His presence, as the Lord had spoken through all His servants the prophets. Israel was deported from its land to the Assyrians, to this day.
17:24 The king of the Assyrians brought from Babylon the one from Chountha, and from Aia, Aimath, and Seppharouain. They were settled in Samaria’s cities in place of the Israelites, took possession of Samaria, and lived in its cities.
17:25 At the beginning of their residence, they did not fear the Lord. The Lord sent lions among them, and they killed some of them.
17:26 They told the king of the Assyrians, ‘The nations you did deport and did resettle in Samaria’s cities do not know the judgment of the God of the land. He has sent lions among them, and behold, they are killing them, because they do not know the judgment of the God of the land.’
17:27 The king of the Assyrians commanded, ‘Take away from there; let them go and live there, and they shall enlighten them about the judgment of the God of the land.’
17:28 They brought one of the priests whom they had deported from Samaria, and he lived in Bethel. He enlightened them about how to fear the Lord.
17:29 But each nation made its own gods and placed them in the house of the high places which the Samaritans had made, the nations in their cities where they lived.
17:30 The men of Babylon made Sokchothbainith; the men of Chouth made Nerigel; the men of Aimath made Asimath.
17:31 The Evites made Eblazer and Tharthak, and the Seppharouain burned their sons in fire to Adramelech and Anemelech, gods of Seppharouain.
17:32 They feared the Lord and installed their abominations in the houses of the high places they had made in Samaria, each nation in the city where it lived. They feared the Lord and made priests of the high places for themselves, and performed rites for themselves in the house of the high places.
17:33 They feared the Lord while serving their own gods, according to the judgment of the nations from which he had deported them.
17:34 To this day they acted according to their judgment. They fear, and they act according to their ordinances, their judgment, the law, and the commandment which the Lord commanded Jacob’s sons, whom He named Israel.
17:35 The Lord made a covenant with them and commanded them, ‘You shall not fear other gods, bow down to them, serve them, or sacrifice to them,
17:36 but only the Lord, who brought you up from Egypt with great strength and a raised arm—Him you shall fear, to Him you shall bow down, and to Him you shall sacrifice.
17:37 The ordinances, judgments, law, and commandments He wrote for you, you shall take care to perform all the days; you shall not fear other gods.
17:38 You shall not forget the covenant He made with you, and you shall not fear other gods.
17:39 But you shall fear the Lord your God, and He will rescue you from all your enemies.
17:40 You shall not listen to their judgment, which they themselves practice.’
17:41 These nations feared the Lord while serving their carved images. Their sons and their sons’ sons also do as their fathers did, to this day.

2 KINGS 18

18:1 In the third year of Hoshea son of Ela, king of Israel, Hezekiah son of Ahaz, king of Judah, became king.
18:2 He was twenty-five years old when he became king, and he reigned twenty-nine years in Jerusalem. His mother’s name was Abou daughter of Zechariah.
18:3 He did what was right in the Lord’s eyes, in accordance with everything his father David had done.
18:4 He removed the high places, shattered all the pillars, and destroyed the groves and the bronze serpent Moses had made, because until those days the Israelites burned incense to it. He called it Neesthan.
18:5 He hoped in the Lord, Israel’s God. Among Judah’s kings there was none like him after him, nor among those before him.
18:6 He clung to the Lord, did not turn away from following Him, and kept His commandments which He commanded Moses.
18:7 The Lord was with him; in everything he did, he acted with understanding. He broke with the king of the Assyrians and did not serve him.
18:8 He struck the foreigners as far as Gaza and its boundary, from watchtower to fortified city.
18:9 In King Hezekiah’s fourth year—which was the seventh year of Hoshea son of Ela, king of Israel—Salamanassar king of the Assyrians went up against Samaria and besieged it.
18:10 He captured it at the end of three years, in Hezekiah’s sixth year—which was Hoshea king of Israel’s ninth year—and Samaria was captured.
18:11 The king of the Assyrians deported Samaria to the Assyrians and placed them in Alae, on the Abor, the river of Gozan, and in Ore of the Medes.
18:12 This was because they did not listen to the voice of the Lord their God but transgressed His covenant, everything Moses the Lord’s servant commanded. They neither listened nor acted.
18:13 In King Hezekiah’s fourteenth year, Sennacherib king of the Assyrians went up against Judah’s fortified cities and captured them.
18:14 Hezekiah king of Judah sent messengers to the king of the Assyrians at Lachish: ‘I have sinned. Withdraw from me; whatever you impose on me, I will bear.’ The king of the Assyrians imposed on Hezekiah king of Judah three hundred talents of silver and thirty talents of gold.
18:15 Hezekiah gave all the silver found in the Lord’s house and in the treasuries of the king’s house.
18:16 At that time Hezekiah cut apart the doors of the Lord’s sanctuary and the fixed supports which Hezekiah king of Judah had gilded, and gave them to the king of the Assyrians.
18:17 The king of the Assyrians sent Tharthan, Raphis, and Rhapsakes from Lachish to King Hezekiah against Jerusalem with a great force. They went up, came to Jerusalem, and stood at the aqueduct of the upper pool, on the road of the Fuller’s Field.
18:18 They called to Hezekiah. Eliakim son of Chelkias the steward, Somnas the scribe, and Joash son of Asaph the recorder went out to him.
18:19 Rhapsakes said to them, ‘Tell Hezekiah: Thus says the great king, the king of the Assyrians: What is this confidence in which you trust?
18:20 You said—only words of lips!—“Counsel and strength for war.” Now then, in whom do you trust, that you have broken with me?
18:21 Now behold, you have put your trust in this broken reed staff, Egypt. If a man supports himself on it, it enters his hand and pierces it. So is Pharaoh king of Egypt to all who trust him.
18:22 Since you said to me, “We trust the Lord God,” is He not the One whose high places and altars Hezekiah removed, saying to Judah and Jerusalem, “Before this altar you shall bow down in Jerusalem”?
18:23 Now join yourselves to my master, the king of the Assyrians. I will give you two thousand horses if you can provide riders for them.
18:24 How can you turn back the face of one district governor, one of my master’s least servants? Yet you hope in Egypt for chariots and horsemen!
18:25 Now have we come up without the Lord against this place to destroy it? The Lord said to me, “Go up against this land and destroy it.”’
18:26 Eliakim son of Chelkias, Somnas, and Joash said to Rhapsakes, ‘Please speak to your servants in Syrian, for we understand it. Do not speak with us in Judean. Why are you speaking within the hearing of the people on the wall?’
18:27 Rhapsakes said to them, ‘Has my master sent me to speak these words to your master and to you? Was it not to the men sitting on the wall, who are to eat their own dung and drink their own urine together with you?’
18:28 Rhapsakes stood and cried out loudly in Judean: ‘Hear the words of the great king of the Assyrians!
18:29 Thus says the king: Do not let Hezekiah lift you up with words, for he cannot rescue you from my hand.
18:30 Do not let Hezekiah make you hope in the Lord by saying, “The Lord will surely rescue us; this city will certainly not be handed into the hand of the king of the Assyrians.”
18:31 Do not listen to Hezekiah, for thus says the king of the Assyrians: Make a blessing with me and come out to me. Each man shall drink from his vine, eat from his fig tree, and drink water from his cistern,
18:32 until I come and take you to a land like your own, a land of grain, wine, bread, and vineyards, a land of oil-bearing olives and honey. You will live and certainly not die. Do not listen to Hezekiah, because he deceives you, saying, “The Lord will rescue us.”
18:33 Have the gods of the nations ever rescued, each his own country, from the hand of the king of the Assyrians?
18:34 Where is the god of Aimath and Arphad? Where is the god of Seppharouain? And have they rescued Samaria from my hand?
18:35 Who among all the gods of the lands has rescued their lands from my hand, that the Lord should rescue Jerusalem from my hand?’
18:36 They kept silent and did not answer him a word, for the king had commanded, ‘Do not answer him.’
18:37 Eliakim son of Chelkias the steward, Somnas the scribe, and Joash son of Asaph the recorder came to Hezekiah with torn garments and reported Rhapsakes’s words to him.

2 KINGS 19

19:1 When King Hezekiah heard, he tore his garments, put on sackcloth, and entered the Lord’s house.
19:2 He sent Eliakim the steward, Somnas the scribe, and the elders of the priests, clothed in sackcloth, to Isaiah the prophet, son of Amos.
19:3 They said to him, ‘Thus says Hezekiah: This day is a day of affliction, rebuke, and provocation. Children have reached the birth pains, but the woman giving birth has no strength.
19:4 Perhaps the Lord your God will hear all Rhapsakes’s words, whom his master the king of the Assyrians sent to reproach the living God and to blaspheme with words which the Lord your God has heard. Take up a prayer for the remnant that remains.’
19:5 King Hezekiah’s servants came to Isaiah.
19:6 Isaiah said to them, ‘Tell your master: Thus says the Lord: Do not fear the words you have heard, with which the king of the Assyrians’ young servants blasphemed.
19:7 Behold, I am putting a spirit within him. He shall hear a report and return to his land, and I shall bring him down by the sword in his land.’
19:8 Rhapsakes returned and found the king of the Assyrians fighting against Lomna, for he had heard that he had departed from Lachish.
19:9 He heard concerning Tharaka king of the Ethiopians, ‘Behold, he has come out to fight you.’ He returned and sent messengers to Hezekiah, saying,
19:10 ‘Do not let your God, in whom you trust, lift you up by saying, “Jerusalem will certainly not be handed into the hands of the king of the Assyrians.”
19:11 Behold, you have heard everything the kings of the Assyrians did to all the lands, devoting them to destruction. And will you be rescued?
19:12 Did the gods of the nations my fathers destroyed rescue them—Gozan, Charran, Raphes, and the sons of Edem in Thaesthen?
19:13 Where are the king of Aimath and the king of Arphad? And where are Seppharouain, Ana, and Ava?’
19:14 Hezekiah took the letters from the messengers’ hand and read them. He went up to the Lord’s house, and Hezekiah unfolded them before the Lord.
19:15 He said, ‘Lord, Israel’s God, seated upon the cherubim, Thou alone art God among all the kingdoms of the earth. Thou madest heaven and earth.
19:16 Incline Thine ear, Lord, and hear. Open Thine eyes, Lord, and see. Hear Sennacherib’s words, which he sent to reproach the living God.
19:17 For truly, Lord, the kings of the Assyrians have devastated the nations
19:18 and put their gods into the fire, for they are not gods but the work of human hands, wood and stones; so they destroyed them.
19:19 Now, Lord our God, save us from his hand, and all the kingdoms of the earth will know that Thou art the Lord, God alone.’
19:20 Isaiah son of Amos sent to Hezekiah: ‘Thus says the Lord, the God of hosts, Israel’s God: What you did pray to Me concerning Sennacherib king of the Assyrians, I have heard.
19:21 This is the word the Lord has spoken concerning him: Virgin daughter Zion despises you and sneers at you; daughter Jerusalem shakes her head at you.
19:22 Whom have you reproached and blasphemed? Against whom have you raised your voice? You have lifted your eyes on high against Israel’s Holy One!
19:23 Through your messengers you did reproach the Lord and did say, “With the multitude of my chariots I shall climb the mountain heights, Lebanon’s flanks. I cut down its cedar’s height, its choice cypresses, and came to the melon of its farthest part, the woodland of its Carmel.
19:24 I cooled and drank foreign waters, and with my foot’s tread I dried up all rivers of enclosure.”
19:25 I formed it; now I brought it. It became a carrying away into exiles of fighting men—fortified cities.
19:26 Their inhabitants grew weak in hand, cowered, and were ashamed. They became field grass or green herbage, rooftop grass, and a trampling before one standing.
19:27 I know your sitting, your going out, your coming in, and your anger against Me.
19:28 Because you were angry with Me and your arrogance rose to My ears, I shall put My hooks in your nostrils and a bridle in your lips, and turn you back by the road on which you came.
19:29 This shall be the sign for you: You shall eat this year what grows by itself, and in the second year what sprouts. In the third, sowing, harvest, and planting vineyards; you shall eat their fruit.
19:30 The rescued remnant of Judah’s house shall again take root downward and bear fruit upward.
19:31 For a remnant shall come out from Jerusalem, and one escaping from Mount Zion. The zeal of the Lord of hosts shall do this.
19:32 Not so! Thus says the Lord concerning the king of the Assyrians: He shall not enter this city or shoot an arrow there; no shield shall come before it, and he shall certainly not pour up a siege ramp against it.
19:33 By the road on which he came he shall return; he shall not enter this city, says the Lord.
19:34 I shall shield this city for My own sake and for the sake of David My servant.’
19:35 By night the Lord’s angel went out and struck one hundred eighty-five thousand in the Assyrians’ camp. When they rose early in the morning, behold, they were all dead bodies.
19:36 Sennacherib king of the Assyrians departed, went away, returned, and lived in Nineveh.
19:37 As he was bowing down in the house of Neserach his god, his sons Adramelech and Sarasar struck him with the sword and escaped to the land of Ararat. His son Asordan became king in his place.

2 KINGS 20

20:1 In those days Hezekiah became sick to the point of death. Isaiah son of Amos the prophet came to him and said, ‘Thus says the Lord: Give instructions for your house, for you are dying and shall not live.’
20:2 Hezekiah turned his face to the wall and prayed to the Lord,
20:3 ‘I pray Thee, Lord, remember how I walked before Thee in truth and with a full heart, and did what was good in Thine eyes.’ Hezekiah wept bitterly.
20:4 Isaiah was in the middle courtyard when the Lord’s word came to him:
20:5 ‘Return and tell Hezekiah, leader of My people: Thus says the Lord, the God of your father David: I have heard your prayer and seen your tears. Behold, I shall heal you; on the third day you shall go up to the Lord’s house,
20:6 and I shall add fifteen years to your days. I shall save you and this city from the hand of the king of the Assyrians, and shield this city for My own sake and for the sake of David My servant.’
20:7 He said, ‘Let them take a cake of figs and place it on the ulcer, and he will recover.’
20:8 Hezekiah said to Isaiah, ‘What is the sign that the Lord will heal me and I shall go up to the Lord’s house on the third day?’
20:9 Isaiah said, ‘This is the sign from the Lord that the Lord will do the word He has spoken: The shadow will advance ten steps, if it returns ten steps.’
20:10 Hezekiah said, ‘It is easy for the shadow to incline ten steps. No, let the shadow return ten steps backward.’
20:11 Isaiah the prophet cried to the Lord, and the shadow returned backward ten steps on the stairway.
20:12 At that time Marodachbaladan son of Baladan, king of Babylon, sent letters and manaa to Hezekiah, because he heard that Hezekiah was sick.
20:13 Hezekiah rejoiced over them and showed them the whole house of the nechotha: the silver, gold, spices, fine oil, the house of equipment, and everything found in his treasuries. There was nothing in his house or all his authority that Hezekiah did not show them.
20:14 Isaiah the prophet came to King Hezekiah and said, ‘What did these men say, and from where have they come to you?’ Hezekiah said, ‘They have come to me from a distant land, from Babylon.’
20:15 He said, ‘What have they seen in your house?’ He said, ‘They saw everything in my house. There was nothing in my house that I did not show them, and also the things in my treasuries.’
20:16 Isaiah said to Hezekiah, ‘Hear the Lord’s word:
20:17 Behold, days are coming when everything in your house, and what your fathers stored up to this day, shall be taken to Babylon. No word which the Lord spoke shall be left over.
20:18 He shall take your sons who come forth from you, whom you beget, and they shall be eunuchs in the house of the king of Babylon.’
20:19 Hezekiah said to Isaiah, ‘The word of the Lord which He spoke is good. Let there be peace in my days.’
20:20 The rest of Hezekiah’s deeds, all his might, and what he made—the fountain and aqueduct—and how he brought water into the city—are these not written in the book of the chronicles of the kings of Judah?
20:21 Hezekiah lay down with his fathers and was buried in David’s city. His son Manasseh became king in his place.

2 KINGS 21

21:1 Manasseh was twelve years old when he became king, and he reigned fifty-five years in Jerusalem. His mother’s name was Opsiba.
21:2 He did evil in the Lord’s eyes, according to the abominations of the nations whom the Lord removed before the Israelites.
21:3 He rebuilt the high places his father Hezekiah had pulled down, erected an altar for Baal, and made groves as Ahab king of Israel had done. He bowed down to all heaven’s host and served them.
21:4 He built an altar in the Lord’s house, as He had said, ‘In Jerusalem I shall place My name.’
21:5 He built an altar for all heaven’s host in the two courts of the Lord’s house.
21:6 He led his sons through fire, practiced divination from sounds, read omens, and appointed a charmer and knowing ones. He did much evil in the Lord’s eyes, provoking Him.
21:7 He placed the carved image of the grove in the house of which the Lord said to David and his son Solomon, ‘In this house and in Jerusalem, which I chose from all Israel’s tribes, I shall place My name there for ever.
21:8 I shall no longer shake Israel’s foot from the land I gave their fathers, for those who keep everything I commanded according to the whole commandment My servant Moses commanded them.’
21:9 But they did not listen. Manasseh led them astray to do evil in the Lord’s eyes beyond the nations whom the Lord obliterated before the Israelites.
21:10 The Lord spoke through His servants the prophets:
21:11 ‘Because Manasseh king of Judah has done these evil abominations, worse than everything the Amorite before him did, and has also caused Judah to sin through their idols,
21:12 not so! Thus says the Lord, Israel’s God: Behold, I am bringing disasters upon Jerusalem and Judah, so that both ears of everyone hearing shall ring.
21:13 I shall stretch over Jerusalem Samaria’s measure and the weight of Ahab’s house. I shall wipe Jerusalem as an alabaster jar is wiped, wiped and overturned on its face.
21:14 I shall reject the remnant of My inheritance and hand them into their enemies’ hands. They shall become plunder and booty for all their enemies,
21:15 because they did evil in My eyes and provoked Me from the day I brought their fathers out of Egypt until this day.’
21:16 Manasseh also poured out very much innocent blood, until he filled Jerusalem from end to end, besides his sins by which he caused Judah to do evil in the Lord’s eyes.
21:17 The rest of Manasseh’s deeds, all that he did, and the sin he committed—are these not written in the book of the chronicles of the kings of Judah?
21:18 Manasseh lay down with his fathers and was buried in the garden of his house, in Oza’s garden. His son Amon became king in his place.
21:19 Amon was twenty-two years old when he became king, and he reigned two years in Jerusalem. His mother’s name was Mesollam daughter of Arous, from Jeteba.
21:20 He did evil in the Lord’s eyes, as his father Manasseh had done.
21:21 He walked in every way his father had walked, served the idols his father had served, and bowed down to them.
21:22 He abandoned the Lord, the God of his fathers, and did not walk in the Lord’s way.
21:23 Amon’s servants conspired against him and put the king to death in his house.
21:24 All the people of the land struck down all who had conspired against King Amon. The people of the land made his son Josiah king in his place.
21:25 The rest of Amon’s deeds, what he did—behold, are these not written in the book of the chronicles of the kings of Judah?
21:26 They buried him in his tomb in Oza’s garden. His son Josiah became king in his place.

2 KINGS 22

22:1 Josiah was eight years old when he became king, and he reigned thirty-one years in Jerusalem. His mother’s name was Jedida daughter of Edeia, from Basouroth.
22:2 He did what was right in the Lord’s eyes and walked in every way of his father David, turning aside neither right nor left.
22:3 In King Josiah’s eighteenth year, in the eighth month, the king sent Sapphan son of Eselias son of Mesollam, the scribe of the Lord’s house, saying,
22:4 ‘Go up to Chelkias the high priest and seal the silver brought into the Lord’s house, which those guarding the doorpost collected from the people.
22:5 Let them give it into the hand of the workers appointed in the Lord’s house.’ He gave it to the workers in the Lord’s house to strengthen the house’s bedek:
22:6 to the carpenters, builders, and wall builders, and to acquire timber and quarried stones to strengthen the house’s bedek.
22:7 But they did not demand an account from them for the silver given to them, for they act faithfully.
22:8 Chelkias the high priest said to Sapphan the scribe, ‘I found a book of the law in the Lord’s house.’ Chelkias gave the book to Sapphan, and he read it.
22:9 He brought it to King Josiah, reported to the king, and said, ‘Your servants have melted down the silver found in the Lord’s house and given it into the hand of the workers appointed in the Lord’s house.’
22:10 Sapphan the scribe told the king, ‘Chelkias the priest gave me a book.’ Sapphan read it before the king.
22:11 When the king heard the words of the book of the law, he tore his garments.
22:12 The king commanded Chelkias the priest, Achikam son of Sapphan, Achobor son of Michaias, Sapphan the scribe, and Asaia the king’s servant:
22:13 ‘Come, seek the Lord for me, for all the people, and for all Judah concerning the words of this book that has been found. For great is the Lord’s anger which burns among us, because our fathers did not listen to this book’s words, to do everything written against us.’
22:14 Chelkias the priest, Achikam, Achobor, Sapphan, and Asaias went to Olda the prophetess, wife of Sellem son of Thekoue son of Araas, keeper of the garments. She lived in Jerusalem in the masena. They spoke to her.
22:15 She said to them, ‘Thus says the Lord, Israel’s God: Tell the man who sent you to me:
22:16 Thus says the Lord: Behold, I am bringing disasters on this place and its inhabitants, all the words of the book which the king of Judah has read,
22:17 because they abandoned Me and burned incense to other gods, provoking Me with the works of their hands. My wrath shall burn against this place and shall not be quenched.
22:18 But to the king of Judah who sent you to seek the Lord, say this: Thus says the Lord, Israel’s God: The words you have heard—
22:19 because your heart was softened and you did feel shame before the Lord when you heard what I spoke against this place and its inhabitants, that they should become an obliteration and a curse, and because you did rend your garments and did weep before Me, I also have heard, says the Lord.
22:20 Not so! Behold, I am adding you to your fathers, and you shall be gathered into your grave in peace. None of all the disasters I am bringing upon this place shall appear before your eyes.’ They reported the word to the king.

2 KINGS 23

23:1 The king sent and gathered to himself all the elders of Judah and Jerusalem.
23:2 The king went up to the Lord’s house, with every man of Judah and all who inhabited Jerusalem, the priests, the prophets, and all the people, from small to great. He read in their hearing all the words of the covenant book found in the Lord’s house.
23:3 The king stood beside the pillar and made a covenant before the Lord: to follow the Lord and keep His commandments, testimonies, and ordinances with the whole heart and whole soul, upholding the words of this covenant, the things written in this book. All the people stood in the covenant.
23:4 The king commanded Chelkias the high priest, the priests of the second rank, and the doorpost guards to bring out of the Lord’s sanctuary all the vessels made for Baal, for the grove, and for all heaven’s host. He burned them outside Jerusalem in the sademoth of Kidron and took their dust to Bethel.
23:5 He put an end to the chomarim whom Judah’s kings had appointed, who burned incense at the high places and in Judah’s cities and the places around Jerusalem, and to those burning incense to Baal, the sun, the moon, the mazouroth, and all heaven’s host.
23:6 He brought the grove out of the Lord’s house, outside Jerusalem to the Kidron torrent. He burned it in the Kidron torrent, reduced it to dust, and threw its dust into the grave of the sons of the people.
23:7 He pulled down the house of the kadesim in the Lord’s house, where the women were weaving chettiin for the grove.
23:8 He brought up all the priests from Judah’s cities and defiled the high places where the priests burned incense, from Gabaa to Beersheba. He pulled down the house of the gates beside the doorway of the gate of Joshua, the city’s ruler, on a man’s left at the city gate.
23:9 Nevertheless, the priests of the high places did not go up to the Lord’s altar in Jerusalem; they only ate unleavened bread among their brothers.
23:10 He defiled Tapheth in the valley of the son of Ennom, for a man to lead his son, and a man his daughter, through fire to Moloch.
23:11 He removed the horses which Judah’s kings had given to the sun, at the entrance of the Lord’s house, toward the treasury of Nathan the king, the eunuch, in the pharourim. He burned the sun’s chariot with fire.
23:12 The king pulled down the altars on the roof of Ahaz’s upper chamber which Judah’s kings had made, and the altars Manasseh had made in the two courts of the Lord’s house. He tore them down from there and threw their dust into the Kidron torrent.
23:13 The king defiled the house facing Jerusalem, to the right of Mount Mosoath, which Solomon king of Israel had built for Astarte, the Sidonians’ offensive thing, for Chamos, Moab’s offensive thing, and for Molchol, the Ammonites’ abomination.
23:14 He shattered the pillars, destroyed the groves, and filled their sites with human bones.
23:15 Moreover, he tore down the altar at Bethel, the high place Jeroboam son of Nabat had made, who caused Israel to sin—both that altar and the high place. He shattered its stones, reduced it to dust, and burned the grove.
23:16 Josiah turned aside and saw the tombs there in the city. He sent and took the bones from the tombs, burned them on the altar, and defiled it, according to the Lord’s word which the man of God spoke while Jeroboam stood at the festival beside the altar. Turning, he lifted his eyes toward the tomb of the man of God who had spoken these words.
23:17 He said, ‘What is that rocky marker I see?’ The city’s men told him, ‘It is the man of God who came out of Judah and proclaimed these words which he proclaimed against Bethel’s altar.’
23:18 He said, ‘Leave it alone. Let no one move his bones.’ His bones were spared together with the bones of the prophet who came from Samaria.
23:19 Josiah also removed all the high-place houses in Samaria’s cities which Israel’s kings had made to provoke the Lord. He carried out in them all the works he had carried out at Bethel.
23:20 He sacrificed all the priests of the high places who were there upon the altars, and burned human bones upon them. Then he returned to Jerusalem.
23:21 The king commanded all the people, ‘Keep Passover to the Lord our God, as written in this covenant book.’
23:22 For this Passover had not occurred since the days of the judges who judged Israel, throughout all the days of Israel’s kings and Judah’s kings.
23:23 Only in King Josiah’s eighteenth year did this Passover to the Lord take place in Jerusalem.
23:24 King Josiah also removed the charmers, the knowing ones, the theraphin, the idols, and all the offensive things present in Judah’s land and Jerusalem, to uphold the law’s words written in the book Chelkias the priest found in the Lord’s house.
23:25 Before him there was no king like him who returned to the Lord with his whole heart, his whole soul, and all his strength, according to all Moses’ law; and after him none like him arose.
23:26 Nevertheless, the Lord did not turn from His great burning wrath which burned against Judah because of the provocations with which Manasseh had provoked Him.
23:27 The Lord said, ‘I shall also remove Judah from My presence, as I removed Israel. I shall reject this city which I chose, Jerusalem, and the house of which I said, “My name shall be there.”’
23:28 The rest of Josiah’s acts and everything he did—are these not written in the book of the chronicles of Judah’s kings?
23:29 In his days Pharaoh Nechao king of Egypt went up against the king of the Assyrians to the River Euphrates. Josiah went to meet him, and Nechao killed him at Mageddo when he saw him.
23:30 His servants transported him dead from Mageddo, brought him to Jerusalem, and buried him in his grave in David’s city. The people of the land took Joachas son of Josiah, anointed him, and made him king in his father’s place.
23:31 Joachas was twenty-three years old when he became king, and he reigned three months in Jerusalem. His mother’s name was Amital daughter of Jeremiah, from Lemna.
23:32 He did what was evil in the Lord’s eyes, according to everything his fathers had done.
23:33 Pharaoh Nechao removed him at Deblatha in Emath’s land so that he would not reign in Jerusalem. He imposed a penalty on the land of one hundred talents of silver and one hundred talents of gold.
23:34 Pharaoh Nechao made Eliakim, son of Josiah king of Judah, king over them in place of his father Josiah and changed his name to Joakim. He took Joachas and brought him into Egypt, and he died there.
23:35 Joakim gave the silver and gold to Pharaoh, but assessed the land to provide the silver at Pharaoh’s command. Each according to his assessment, they gave the silver and gold with the people of the land, to give to Pharaoh Nechao.
23:36 Joakim was twenty-five years old when he became king, and he reigned eleven years in Jerusalem. His mother’s name was Jeldaph daughter of Phedeia, from Rouma.
23:37 He did what was evil in the Lord’s eyes, according to everything his fathers had done.

2 KINGS 24

24:1 In his days Nabouchodonosor king of Babylon came up. Joakim became his slave for three years, then turned and broke faith with him.
24:2 He sent against him the lightly equipped raiders of the Chaldeans, Syria, Moab, and the Ammonites. He sent them out in Judah’s land to prevail, according to the Lord’s word which He spoke through His servants the prophets.
24:3 Nevertheless, this came upon Judah because of the Lord’s anger, to remove it from His presence because of Manasseh’s sins, according to everything he had done.
24:4 He also shed innocent blood and filled Jerusalem with innocent blood, and the Lord was unwilling to be propitiated.
24:5 The rest of Joakim’s acts and everything he did—behold, are these not written in the book of the chronicles of Judah’s kings?
24:6 Joakim lay down with his fathers, and his son Joakim became king in his place.
24:7 The king of Egypt did not again leave his land, because the king of Babylon had taken everything belonging to the king of Egypt from Egypt’s torrent to the River Euphrates.
24:8 Joakim was eighteen years old when he became king, and he reigned three months in Jerusalem. His mother’s name was Nestha daughter of Ellanathan, from Jerusalem.
24:9 He did what was evil in the Lord’s eyes, according to everything his father had done.
24:10 At that time Nabouchodonosor king of Babylon came up to Jerusalem, and the city came under siege.
24:11 Nabouchodonosor king of Babylon entered the city while his servants were besieging it.
24:12 Joakim king of Judah went out to the king of Babylon, he, his servants, his mother, his rulers, and his eunuchs. The king of Babylon took him in the eighth year of his reign.
24:13 He brought out from there all the treasures of the Lord’s house and the treasures of the king’s house. He cut apart all the gold vessels Solomon king of Israel had made in the Lord’s sanctuary, according to the Lord’s word.
24:14 He deported Jerusalem, all the rulers, and the mighty in strength, taking ten thousand captives, and every craftsman and one who shuts up. None remained except the land’s poor.
24:15 He deported Joakim to Babylon, along with the king’s mother, the king’s wives, and his eunuchs. He led the land’s powerful men into deportation from Jerusalem to Babylon,
24:16 and all the men of the force, seven thousand, and the craftsmen and those who shut up, one thousand—all mighty men who waged war. The king of Babylon brought them into deportation to Babylon.
24:17 The king of Babylon made Maththanias, his son, king in his place and gave him the name Sedekias.
24:18 Sedekias was twenty-one years old when he became king, and he reigned eleven years in Jerusalem. His mother’s name was Amital daughter of Jeremiah.
24:19 He did what was evil before the Lord, according to everything Joakim had done.
24:20 For this came upon Jerusalem and Judah because of the Lord’s anger, until He cast them from His presence. Sedekias broke faith with the king of Babylon.

2 KINGS 25

25:1 In the ninth year of his reign, in the tenth month, Nabouchodonosor king of Babylon came with all his force against Jerusalem. He camped against it and built an enclosing wall against it all around.
25:2 The city remained under siege until King Sedekias’s eleventh year.
25:3 On the ninth of the month the famine grew severe in the city, and there were no loaves for the people of the land.
25:4 The city was breached, and all the men of war went out by night by way of the gate between the walls, the one belonging to the king’s garden, with the Chaldeans surrounding the city. He went by the way of the Araba.
25:5 The Chaldean force pursued the king and overtook him in Araboth Jericho, and all his force scattered away from him.
25:6 They seized the king and brought him to the king of Babylon at Deblatha, and he pronounced judgment upon him.
25:7 He slaughtered Sedekias’s sons before his eyes. He blinded Sedekias’s eyes, bound him with fetters, and brought him to Babylon.
25:8 In the fifth month, on the seventh of the month, in the nineteenth year of Nabouchodonosor king of Babylon, Nabouzardan, the chief cook who stood before the king of Babylon, came to Jerusalem.
25:9 He set fire to the Lord’s house, the king’s house, and all Jerusalem’s houses; every house was set ablaze by
25:10 the chief cook.
25:11 Nabouzardan, the chief cook, removed the rest of the people left in the city, those who had gone over to the king of Babylon, and the remaining support.
25:12 The chief cook left some of the land’s poor as vinedressers and as gabin.
25:13 The Chaldeans shattered the bronze pillars in the Lord’s house, the mechonoth, and the bronze sea in the Lord’s house, and carried their bronze to Babylon.
25:14 He took the cauldrons, the iamin, the bowls, the incense dishes, and all the bronze utensils with which they ministered.
25:15 The chief cook took the firepans and the gold and silver bowls,
25:16 the two pillars, the one sea, and the mechonoth which Solomon made for the Lord’s house. The bronze of all the objects had no reckoned weight.
25:17 One pillar was eighteen cubits high, with the bronze chothar upon it. The chothar was three cubits high, with sabacha and pomegranates around the chothar, all bronze. The second pillar was the same, with the sabacha.
25:18 The chief cook took Saraias the first priest, Sophonias, son of the second rank, and the three doorpost guards,
25:19 and from the city he took one eunuch who supervised the warriors, five men who saw the king’s face who were found in the city, the scribe of the commander of the force who drew up the people of the land, and sixty men of the people of the land found in the city.
25:20 Nabouzardan, the chief cook, took them and led them to the king of Babylon at Deblatha.
25:21 The king of Babylon struck them and put them to death at Deblatha in Aimath’s land. Judah was deported from its land.
25:22 As for the people remaining in Judah’s land whom Nabouchodonosor king of Babylon had left, he appointed over them Godolias son of Achikam son of Saphan.
25:23 All the commanders of the force, they and their men, heard that the king of Babylon had appointed Godolias. They came to Godolias at Massephath: Ishmael son of Nathanias, Joanan son of Karee, Saraias son of Thanemath the Netophathite, and Jezonias son of the Machathi, they and their men.
25:24 Godolias swore to them and their men and said, ‘Do not fear the passing of the Chaldeans. Settle in the land and serve the king of Babylon, and it shall go well for you.’
25:25 In the seventh month Ishmael son of Nathanias son of Elisama, of the kings’ offspring, came with ten men. He struck Godolias—who died—and the Judeans and Chaldeans who were with him at Massephath.
25:26 All the people, from small to great, and the commanders of the forces arose and entered Egypt, for they feared the Chaldeans.
25:27 In the thirty-seventh year of the deportation of Joakim king of Judah, in the twelfth month, on the twenty-seventh of the month, Euilmarodach king of Babylon, in the year of his reign, lifted the head of Joakim king of Judah and brought him out of his prison house.
25:28 He spoke kindly to him and placed his throne above the thrones of the kings who were with him in Babylon,
25:29 and he changed his prison garments, and he ate bread continually before him all the days of his life.
25:30 His table provision was given to him continually from the king’s house, each day’s portion on its day, all the days of his life.
