ACTS 1

1:1 In my first account, Theophilus, I wrote about all that Jesus began both to do and to teach
1:2 until the day when, after giving instructions through the Holy Spirit to the apostles whom He had chosen, He was taken up.
1:3 To them He also presented Himself alive after His suffering, with many proofs, appearing to them over forty days and speaking about the kingdom of God.
1:4 While gathered with them, He ordered them not to leave Jerusalem, but to wait for the Father’s promise, “which,” He said, “you heard from Me;
1:5 for John baptized with water, but you will be baptized in the Holy Spirit not many days from now.”
1:6 So those who had come together asked Him, “Lord, is this the time when Thou art restoring the kingdom to Israel?”
1:7 He said to them, “It is not for you to know times or seasons that the Father has set by His own authority.
1:8 But you will receive power when the Holy Spirit comes upon you, and you will be My witnesses in Jerusalem and [in] all Judea and Samaria, and to the end of the earth.”
1:9 After saying these things, He was lifted up as they watched, and a cloud took Him from their sight.
1:10 As they were gazing into heaven while He went, look—two men stood beside them in white garments.
1:11 They said, “Men of Galilee, why do you stand looking into heaven? This Jesus, who was taken up from you into heaven, will come in the same way as you saw Him go into heaven.”
1:12 Then they returned to Jerusalem from the mountain called Olivet, which is near Jerusalem, a Sabbath’s journey away.
1:13 When they entered, they went up to the upper room where they were staying: Peter and John and James and Andrew, Philip and Thomas, Bartholomew and Matthew, James son of Alphaeus, Simon the Zealot, and Judas son of James.
1:14 All these devoted themselves with one accord to prayer, together with women, Mary the mother of Jesus, and His brothers.
1:15 In those days Peter stood up among the brothers and said—about a hundred and twenty people were gathered together—
1:16 “Men, brothers, the Scripture had to be fulfilled, which the Holy Spirit spoke beforehand through David’s mouth concerning Judas, who became a guide to those who arrested Jesus;
1:17 for he was counted among us and received his allotted share in this ministry.”
1:18 Now this man acquired a field from the wages of wrongdoing, and falling headlong, he burst open in the middle, and all his entrails poured out.
1:19 This became known to all who lived in Jerusalem, so that the field was called in their own language Hakeldamach—that is, Field of Blood.
1:20 “For it is written in the book of Psalms: ‘Let his dwelling become deserted, and let there be no one living in it,’ and, ‘Let another take his office of oversight.’
1:21 Therefore, of the men who accompanied us throughout the time when the Lord Jesus went in and out among us,
1:22 beginning with John’s baptism until the day when He was taken up from us, one of these must become with us a witness of His resurrection.”
1:23 They put forward two: Joseph called Barsabbas, who was also named Justus, and Matthias.
1:24 They prayed and said, “Thou, Lord, who knowest everyone’s heart, show which one of these two Thou hast chosen
1:25 to take the place in this ministry and apostleship from which Judas turned aside to go to his own place.”
1:26 They cast lots for them, and the lot fell on Matthias, and he was counted with the eleven apostles.

ACTS 2

2:1 When the day of Pentecost arrived, they were all together in the same place.
2:2 Suddenly a sound came from heaven like a violent wind rushing along, and it filled the whole house where they were sitting.
2:3 Tongues like fire appeared to them, separating and settling on each one of them.
2:4 They were all filled with the Holy Spirit and began to speak in other tongues, as the Spirit gave them utterance.
2:5 Now there were Jews living in Jerusalem, devout men from every nation under heaven.
2:6 When this sound occurred, the crowd came together and was bewildered, because each one heard them speaking in his own language.
2:7 They were astonished and amazed, saying, “Look, are not all these people who are speaking Galileans?
2:8 How is it, then, that each of us hears in our own native language?
2:9 Parthians, Medes, and Elamites; those living in Mesopotamia, Judea and Cappadocia, Pontus and Asia,
2:10 Phrygia and Pamphylia, Egypt and the parts of Libya around Cyrene; visitors from Rome,
2:11 both Jews and converts; Cretans and Arabs—we hear them speaking in our own tongues about the mighty deeds of God.”
2:12 They were all astonished and perplexed, saying to one another, “What does this mean?”
2:13 But others mocked them and said, “They are full of sweet wine.”
2:14 Peter stood with the eleven, raised his voice, and addressed them: “Men of Judea and all who live in Jerusalem, let this be known to you, and listen closely to my words.
2:15 These people are not drunk, as you suppose, for it is the third hour of the day.
2:16 Rather, this is what was spoken through the prophet Joel:
2:17 ‘In the last days, God says, I will pour out of My Spirit upon all flesh. Your sons and your daughters will prophesy; your young men will see visions, and your elders will dream dreams.
2:18 Even upon My male slaves and My female slaves in those days I will pour out of My Spirit, and they will prophesy.
2:19 I will give wonders in heaven above and signs on the earth below: blood and fire and a cloud of smoke.
2:20 The sun will be changed into darkness and the moon into blood before the great and manifest day of the Lord comes.
2:21 And everyone who calls upon the name of the Lord will be saved.’
2:22 Men of Israel, hear these words: Jesus the Nazarene was a man attested to you by God through mighty works, wonders, and signs that God did through Him in your midst, as you yourselves know.
2:23 This man, handed over according to God’s determined purpose and foreknowledge, you nailed up and killed through the hands of lawless men.
2:24 God raised Him, releasing Him from the pangs of death, because it was impossible for Him to be held by it.
2:25 For David says concerning Him, ‘I kept the Lord before me always, for He is at my right hand so that I will not be shaken.
2:26 Therefore my heart rejoiced and my tongue exulted; moreover, my flesh will dwell in hope,
2:27 because Thou wilt not abandon my soul to Hades, nor allow Thy Holy One to see decay.
2:28 Thou hast made known to me the paths of life; Thou wilt fill me with gladness in Thy presence.’
2:29 Men, brothers, I may speak openly to you about the patriarch David: he both died and was buried, and his tomb is among us to this day.
2:30 Since he was a prophet and knew that God had sworn an oath to him to seat one from the fruit of his loins upon his throne,
2:31 he foresaw and spoke about the resurrection of the Christ: He was neither abandoned to Hades, nor did His flesh see decay.
2:32 God raised this Jesus, and all of us are witnesses of it.
2:33 Therefore, exalted to God’s right hand and having received from the Father the promised Holy Spirit, He has poured out what you both see and hear.
2:34 For David did not ascend into the heavens, but he himself says, ‘The Lord said to my Lord, “Sit at My right hand
2:35 until I make Thy enemies a footstool for Thy feet.”’
2:36 Therefore let the whole house of Israel know with certainty that God has made Him both Lord and Christ—this Jesus whom you crucified.”
2:37 When they heard this, they were pierced to the heart and said to Peter and the other apostles, “Men, brothers, what should we do?”
2:38 Peter said to them, “Repent, and let each one of you be baptized upon the name of Jesus Christ for the forgiveness of your sins, and you will receive the gift of the Holy Spirit.
2:39 For the promise is for you, for your children, and for all who are far away—as many as the Lord our God calls to Himself.”
2:40 With many other words he bore solemn witness and urged them, saying, “Be saved from this crooked generation.”
2:41 So those who welcomed his word were baptized, and about three thousand persons were added that day.
2:42 They devoted themselves to the apostles’ teaching and to fellowship, to the breaking of bread and to the prayers.
2:43 Awe came upon everyone, and many wonders and signs took place through the apostles.
2:44 All who believed were together and held everything in common.
2:45 They would sell their properties and possessions and distribute the proceeds to all, as anyone had need.
2:46 Day by day they devoted themselves with one accord to gathering in the temple, and breaking bread in their homes, they shared food with gladness and simplicity of heart,
2:47 praising God and enjoying favor with all the people. And day by day the Lord added those who were being saved to their gathering.

ACTS 3

3:1 Peter and John were going up to the temple at the ninth hour, the hour of prayer.
3:2 A man unable to walk from his mother’s womb was being carried there. They used to place him each day at the temple gate called Beautiful, to ask for alms from those entering the temple.
3:3 When he saw Peter and John about to enter the temple, he asked to receive alms.
3:4 Peter, together with John, looked intently at him and said, “Look at us.”
3:5 He paid attention to them, expecting to receive something from them.
3:6 But Peter said, “Silver and gold I do not have, but what I have I give you: in the name of Jesus Christ the Nazarene, get up and walk.”
3:7 Taking him by the right hand, he raised him up. At once his feet and ankles were strengthened.
3:8 Leaping up, he stood and began walking. He entered the temple with them, walking and leaping and praising God.
3:9 All the people saw him walking and praising God.
3:10 They recognized him as the one who used to sit for alms at the temple’s Beautiful Gate, and they were filled with wonder and astonishment at what had happened to him.
3:11 As he held on to Peter and John, all the people ran together to them at the portico called Solomon’s, utterly astonished.
3:12 When Peter saw this, he addressed the people: “Men of Israel, why do you marvel at this, or why do you stare at us as though we had made him walk by our own power or godliness?
3:13 The God of Abraham and the God of Isaac and the God of Jacob, the God of our fathers, has glorified His Servant Jesus, whom you handed over and denied before Pilate, when he had decided to release Him.
3:14 But you denied the Holy and Righteous One and asked for a murderer to be granted to you.
3:15 You killed the Author of life, whom God raised from the dead; we are witnesses of this.
3:16 Through faith in His name, His name has strengthened this man whom you see and know; and the faith that is through Him has given him this complete health before all of you.
3:17 And now, brothers, I know that you acted in ignorance, as did your rulers.
3:18 But what God announced beforehand through the mouth of all the prophets—that His Christ would suffer—He has fulfilled in this way.
3:19 Repent, therefore, and turn back, so that your sins may be wiped away,
3:20 so that times of refreshing may come from the presence of the Lord, and He may send the Christ appointed for you, Jesus,
3:21 whom heaven must receive until the times of restoration of all things of which God spoke through the mouth of His holy prophets from long ago.
3:22 Moses said, ‘The Lord your God will raise up for you a prophet like me from among your brothers. You shall listen to him in everything he says to you.
3:23 Every person who does not listen to that prophet will be destroyed from among the people.’
3:24 And all the prophets, from Samuel and those who followed—as many as spoke—also announced these days.
3:25 You are sons of the prophets and of the covenant God made with your fathers, saying to Abraham, ‘In your seed all the families of the earth will be blessed.’
3:26 God raised up His Servant and sent Him first to you, to bless you by turning each of you away from your wicked deeds.”

ACTS 4

4:1 While they were speaking to the people, the priests, the captain of the temple, and the Sadducees came upon them,
4:2 greatly troubled because they were teaching the people and proclaiming in Jesus the resurrection from the dead.
4:3 They laid hands on them and placed them in custody until the next day, for it was already evening.
4:4 But many of those who heard the word believed, and the number of the men came to about five thousand.
4:5 The next day their rulers, elders, and scribes gathered in Jerusalem,
4:6 together with Annas the high priest, Caiaphas, John, Alexander, and all who belonged to the high-priestly family.
4:7 They made them stand in their midst and asked, “By what power or by what name did you do this?”
4:8 Then Peter, filled with the Holy Spirit, said to them, “Rulers of the people and elders,
4:9 if we are being examined today concerning a good deed done to a disabled man, and by what means he has been made well,
4:10 let it be known to all of you and to all the people of Israel that in the name of Jesus Christ the Nazarene, whom you crucified, whom God raised from the dead—in Him this man stands before you in good health.
4:11 He is the stone despised by you, the builders, which has become the cornerstone.
4:12 Salvation is in no one else, for there is no other name under heaven given among human beings by which we must be saved.”
4:13 When they observed Peter’s and John’s boldness and realized that they were unlettered, ordinary men, they were amazed. They also recognized that they had been with Jesus.
4:14 Seeing the man who had been healed standing with them, they had nothing to say against it.
4:15 They ordered them to leave the council and conferred with one another,
4:16 saying, “What should we do with these men? That a notable sign has taken place through them is evident to everyone living in Jerusalem, and we cannot deny it.
4:17 But so that it spreads no further among the people, let us warn them with threats not to speak to anyone in this name again.”
4:18 They called them in and ordered them neither to speak nor to teach at all in the name of Jesus.
4:19 Peter and John answered them, “Judge whether it is right before God to listen to you rather than to God;
4:20 for we cannot keep from speaking about what we have seen and heard.”
4:21 After further threats, they released them, finding no way to punish them because of the people, since everyone was glorifying God for what had happened.
4:22 For the man in whom this sign of healing had occurred was more than forty years old.
4:23 After their release, they went to their own people and reported all that the chief priests and elders had said to them.
4:24 On hearing it, they lifted their voice together to God and said, “Master, Thou art the One who made heaven and earth and the sea and everything in them,
4:25 who through the Holy Spirit, by the mouth of our father David, Thy servant, said, ‘Why did nations rage and peoples devise empty things?
4:26 The kings of the earth took their stand, and the rulers gathered together against the Lord and against His Christ.’
4:27 For truly, in this city, both Herod and Pontius Pilate, with the Gentiles and the peoples of Israel, gathered against Thy holy Servant Jesus, whom Thou didst anoint,
4:28 to do whatever Thy hand and Thy purpose had determined beforehand should happen.
4:29 And now, Lord, look upon their threats, and grant Thy servants to speak Thy word with all boldness,
4:30 while Thou stretchest out Thy hand for healing, and signs and wonders take place through the name of Thy holy Servant Jesus.”
4:31 When they had prayed, the place where they were gathered was shaken. They were all filled with the Holy Spirit and spoke God’s word with boldness.
4:32 The multitude of those who believed was of one heart and soul. Not one of them claimed that anything he possessed was his own, but they held everything in common.
4:33 With great power the apostles gave their testimony to the resurrection of the Lord Jesus, and great grace was upon them all.
4:34 For there was not a needy person among them: all who owned lands or houses would sell them and bring the proceeds of what was sold,
4:35 laying them at the apostles’ feet; distribution was made to each as anyone had need.
4:36 Joseph, whom the apostles called Barnabas—which means Son of Encouragement—a Levite and a native of Cyprus,
4:37 sold a field he owned, brought the money, and laid it at the apostles’ feet.

ACTS 5

5:1 A man named Ananias, together with his wife Sapphira, sold a property.
5:2 He secretly kept back part of the price, with his wife also knowing about it, and brought a portion and laid it at the apostles’ feet.
5:3 Peter said, “Ananias, why has Satan filled your heart, so that you lied to the Holy Spirit and secretly kept back part of the price of the land?
5:4 While it remained unsold, did it not remain yours? And after it was sold, was it not under your authority? Why have you put this deed in your heart? You have not lied to human beings but to God.”
5:5 When Ananias heard these words, he fell down and breathed his last. Great fear came upon all who heard.
5:6 The younger men rose, wrapped him up, carried him out, and buried him.
5:7 About three hours later, his wife came in, not knowing what had happened.
5:8 Peter said to her, “Tell me, did you sell the land for this amount?” She said, “Yes, for that amount.”
5:9 Peter said to her, “Why did you both agree to test the Spirit of the Lord? Look, the feet of those who buried your husband are at the door, and they will carry you out.”
5:10 At once she fell at his feet and breathed her last. When the young men came in, they found her dead; they carried her out and buried her beside her husband.
5:11 Great fear came upon the whole Church and upon all who heard these things.
5:12 Many signs and wonders were taking place among the people through the apostles’ hands. They were all together with one accord in Solomon’s Portico.
5:13 None of the rest dared to join them, but the people held them in high esteem.
5:14 More and more believers were added to the Lord, multitudes of both men and women,
5:15 so that they even carried the sick into the streets and laid them on small beds and mats, so that as Peter came by, at least his shadow might fall upon one of them.
5:16 The crowd also came together from the towns around Jerusalem, bringing the sick and those troubled by unclean spirits, and they were all healed.
5:17 The high priest and all who were with him—the party of the Sadducees—rose up, filled with jealousy.
5:18 They laid hands on the apostles and placed them in the public prison.
5:19 But during the night an angel of the Lord opened the prison doors, led them out, and said,
5:20 “Go, stand in the temple, and speak to the people all the words of this life.”
5:21 When they heard this, they entered the temple at daybreak and began teaching. The high priest and those with him arrived, called together the council and the whole assembly of elders of the sons of Israel, and sent to the jail to have them brought in.
5:22 But when the officers arrived, they did not find them in the prison. They returned and reported,
5:23 “We found the jail securely locked and the guards standing at the doors, but when we opened it, we found no one inside.”
5:24 When the captain of the temple and the chief priests heard these words, they were perplexed about them, wondering what this would come to.
5:25 Then someone came and reported to them, “Look, the men you put in prison are standing in the temple and teaching the people.”
5:26 The captain then went with the officers and brought them, without force, for they feared the people might stone them.
5:27 They brought them and made them stand before the council. The high priest questioned them,
5:28 saying, “Did we not strictly command you not to teach in this name? Yet look—you have filled Jerusalem with your teaching and intend to bring this man’s blood upon us.”
5:29 Peter and the apostles answered, “We must obey God rather than human beings.
5:30 The God of our fathers raised Jesus, whom you killed by hanging Him on a tree.
5:31 God exalted Him to His right hand as Leader and Savior, to grant repentance to Israel and forgiveness of sins.
5:32 We are witnesses of these things, and so is the Holy Spirit, whom God has given to those who obey Him.”
5:33 When they heard this, they were enraged and wanted to kill them.
5:34 But a Pharisee named Gamaliel, a teacher of the law honored by all the people, stood in the council and ordered the men to be taken outside briefly.
5:35 He said to them, “Men of Israel, take care what you are about to do to these men.
5:36 For before these days Theudas arose, claiming to be somebody, and about four hundred men joined him. He was killed, and all who followed him were dispersed and came to nothing.
5:37 After him Judas the Galilean arose in the days of the registration and drew people away after him. He too perished, and all who followed him were scattered.
5:38 And now I tell you, keep away from these men and leave them alone. For if this plan or this work is of human origin, it will be overthrown;
5:39 but if it is from God, you will not be able to overthrow them. You might even be found fighting against God.” They were persuaded by him.
5:40 They summoned the apostles, beat them, ordered them not to speak in the name of Jesus, and released them.
5:41 So they left the council rejoicing that they had been counted worthy to suffer dishonor for the Name.
5:42 Every day, in the temple and in their homes, they never ceased teaching and proclaiming the good news of Jesus the Christ.

ACTS 6

6:1 In those days, as the disciples increased in number, a complaint arose from the Hellenists against the Hebrews, because their widows were being overlooked in the daily service.
6:2 The twelve called the multitude of the disciples together and said, “It is not fitting for us to leave the word of God to serve tables.
6:3 So, brothers, select from among you seven men of good reputation, full of the Spirit and wisdom, whom we will put in charge of this task.
6:4 But we will devote ourselves to prayer and to the service of the word.”
6:5 The proposal pleased the whole multitude. They chose Stephen, a man full of faith and the Holy Spirit, Philip, Prochorus, Nicanor, Timon, Parmenas, and Nicolaus, a convert from Antioch.
6:6 They presented them before the apostles, who prayed and laid their hands upon them.
6:7 The word of God grew, and the number of disciples in Jerusalem increased greatly. A large number of the priests also became obedient to the faith.
6:8 Stephen, full of grace and power, was doing great wonders and signs among the people.
6:9 But some from the synagogue called the Freedmen’s, including Cyrenians and Alexandrians and people from Cilicia and Asia, rose up and disputed with Stephen.
6:10 They could not withstand the wisdom and the Spirit with which he spoke.
6:11 Then they secretly persuaded men to say, “We have heard him speaking blasphemous words against Moses and God.”
6:12 They stirred up the people, the elders, and the scribes, and came upon him, seized him, and brought him to the council.
6:13 They set up false witnesses who said, “This man never stops speaking words against this holy place and the law.
6:14 For we have heard him say that this Jesus the Nazarene will destroy this place and change the customs Moses handed down to us.”
6:15 All who were seated in the council looked intently at him and saw his face like the face of an angel.

ACTS 7

7:1 The high priest said, “Are these things true?”
7:2 He answered, “Men, brothers and fathers, listen. The God of glory appeared to our father Abraham while he was in Mesopotamia, before he lived in Haran,
7:3 and said to him, ‘Leave your land and your relatives, and come to the land I will show you.’
7:4 Then he left the land of the Chaldeans and lived in Haran. From there, after his father died, God moved him to this land in which you now live.
7:5 He gave him no inheritance in it, not even a footstep’s space, but promised to give it to him as a possession and to his seed after him, although he had no child.
7:6 God spoke in this way: his seed would live as strangers in a foreign land, and they would enslave and mistreat them for four hundred years.
7:7 ‘But I will judge the nation they serve as slaves,’ God said, ‘and after these things they will come out and worship Me in this place.’
7:8 He gave him the covenant of circumcision. Thus Abraham fathered Isaac and circumcised him on the eighth day; Isaac fathered Jacob, and Jacob the twelve patriarchs.
7:9 The patriarchs, jealous of Joseph, sold him into Egypt. But God was with him,
7:10 rescued him from all his afflictions, and gave him favor and wisdom before Pharaoh, king of Egypt, who appointed him ruler over Egypt and his whole household.
7:11 Famine came upon all Egypt and Canaan, with great affliction, and our fathers could find no provisions.
7:12 When Jacob heard that there was grain in Egypt, he sent our fathers there the first time.
7:13 On the second visit Joseph made himself known to his brothers, and Joseph’s family became known to Pharaoh.
7:14 Joseph sent and summoned his father Jacob and all his relatives, seventy-five persons in all.
7:15 Jacob went down to Egypt, and he and our fathers died.
7:16 They were brought over to Shechem and placed in the tomb Abraham had bought for a price in silver from the sons of Hamor in Shechem.
7:17 As the time of the promise God had declared to Abraham drew near, the people grew and multiplied in Egypt,
7:18 until another king arose over Egypt, who did not know Joseph.
7:19 He dealt craftily with our people and mistreated our fathers, forcing them to expose their infants so that they would not be kept alive.
7:20 At that time Moses was born, and he was beautiful before God. He was brought up for three months in his father’s house.
7:21 When he was exposed, Pharaoh’s daughter took him up and brought him up as her own son.
7:22 Moses was educated in all the wisdom of the Egyptians and was powerful in his words and deeds.
7:23 When he reached the age of forty, it came into his heart to visit his brothers, the sons of Israel.
7:24 Seeing one of them being wronged, he defended him and avenged the oppressed man by striking down the Egyptian.
7:25 He supposed his brothers understood that God was giving them deliverance through his hand, but they did not understand.
7:26 The next day he came upon them as they were fighting and tried to reconcile them in peace, saying, ‘Men, you are brothers. Why are you wronging one another?’
7:27 But the one wronging his neighbor pushed him away and said, ‘Who appointed you ruler and judge over us?
7:28 Do you want to kill me as you killed the Egyptian yesterday?’
7:29 At this saying Moses fled and became a stranger in the land of Midian, where he fathered two sons.
7:30 When forty years had passed, an angel appeared to him in the wilderness of Mount Sinai, in the flame of a burning bush.
7:31 When Moses saw it, he marveled at the sight. As he approached to examine it, the Lord’s voice came:
7:32 ‘I am the God of your fathers, the God of Abraham and Isaac and Jacob.’ Moses trembled and did not dare to look closely.
7:33 The Lord said to him, ‘Remove the sandals from your feet, for the place on which you stand is holy ground.
7:34 I have indeed seen the mistreatment of My people in Egypt and heard their groaning, and I have come down to rescue them. Now come, let Me send you to Egypt.’
7:35 This Moses, whom they denied, saying, ‘Who appointed you ruler and judge?’—God has sent this very man as both ruler and redeemer, through the hand of the angel who appeared to him in the bush.
7:36 He led them out, doing wonders and signs in the land of Egypt, in the Red Sea, and in the wilderness for forty years.
7:37 This is the Moses who said to the sons of Israel, ‘God will raise up for you a prophet like me from among your brothers.’
7:38 He is the one who was in the assembly in the wilderness with the angel who spoke to him on Mount Sinai and with our fathers. He received living oracles to give to us,
7:39 but our fathers were unwilling to obey him. They pushed him away and turned in their hearts toward Egypt,
7:40 saying to Aaron, ‘Make us gods who will go before us. As for this Moses who led us out of the land of Egypt, we do not know what has happened to him.’
7:41 They made a calf in those days, offered sacrifice to the idol, and rejoiced in the works of their hands.
7:42 But God turned away and handed them over to worship the host of heaven, as it is written in the book of the prophets: ‘Did you offer Me slaughtered victims and sacrifices for forty years in the wilderness, house of Israel?
7:43 You took up the tent of Moloch and the star of your god Raiphan, the images you made to worship them. And I will deport you beyond Babylon.’
7:44 Our fathers had the Tent of Testimony in the wilderness, as the One who spoke to Moses directed him to make it according to the pattern he had seen.
7:45 Our fathers in their turn received it and brought it in with Joshua when they took possession of the nations’ land, whose people God drove out before our fathers. It remained until the days of David,
7:46 who found favor before God and asked to find a dwelling for the house of Jacob.
7:47 But Solomon built Him a house.
7:48 Yet the Most High does not dwell in things made by hands, as the prophet says:
7:49 ‘Heaven is My throne, and the earth the footstool of My feet. What kind of house will you build for Me, says the Lord, or what place is there for My rest?
7:50 Did not My hand make all these things?’
7:51 You stiff-necked people, uncircumcised in hearts and ears! You always resist the Holy Spirit; as your fathers did, so do you.
7:52 Which of the prophets did your fathers not persecute? They killed those who announced beforehand the coming of the Righteous One, whose betrayers and murderers you have now become—
7:53 you who received the law through the ordinances of angels and did not keep it.”
7:54 When they heard these things, they were cut to the heart with rage and ground their teeth at him.
7:55 But he, full of the Holy Spirit, gazed into heaven and saw the glory of God and Jesus standing at God’s right hand.
7:56 He said, “Look, I see the heavens opened and the Son of Man standing at God’s right hand.”
7:57 They cried out with a loud voice, stopped their ears, and rushed upon him together.
7:58 They drove him outside the city and began stoning him. The witnesses laid their garments at the feet of a young man called Saul.
7:59 They stoned Stephen as he called out, saying, “Lord Jesus, receive my spirit.”
7:60 Kneeling down, he cried with a loud voice, “Lord, do not hold this sin against them.” After saying this, he fell asleep.

ACTS 8

8:1 Saul approved of his killing. That day a great persecution arose against the Church in Jerusalem, and all except the apostles were scattered through the regions of Judea and Samaria.
8:2 Devout men buried Stephen and made great lamentation over him.
8:3 But Saul was ravaging the Church, entering house after house, dragging away both men and women, and handing them over to prison.
8:4 Those who were scattered went about proclaiming the word as good news.
8:5 Philip went down to the city of Samaria and proclaimed the Christ to them.
8:6 The crowds paid attention together to what Philip said, as they heard and saw the signs he was doing.
8:7 For unclean spirits came out of many who had them, crying with a loud voice, and many who were paralyzed or unable to walk were healed.
8:8 There was great joy in that city.
8:9 A man named Simon had previously practiced magic in the city and astonished the people of Samaria, claiming that he himself was someone great.
8:10 Everyone, from small to great, paid attention to him, saying, “This man is the Power of God that is called Great.”
8:11 They paid attention to him because he had astonished them with his magical practices for a long time.
8:12 But when they believed Philip as he proclaimed the good news about the kingdom of God and the name of Jesus Christ, both men and women were baptized.
8:13 Simon himself also believed, and after being baptized he stayed close to Philip. Seeing signs and great works of power taking place, he was astonished.
8:14 When the apostles in Jerusalem heard that Samaria had received the word of God, they sent Peter and John to them.
8:15 They went down and prayed for them to receive the Holy Spirit,
8:16 for He had not yet fallen upon any of them; they had only been baptized into the name of the Lord Jesus.
8:17 Then they laid their hands upon them, and they received the Holy Spirit.
8:18 When Simon saw that the Spirit was given through the laying on of the apostles’ hands, he offered them money,
8:19 saying, “Give me this authority too, so that anyone upon whom I lay my hands may receive the Holy Spirit.”
8:20 But Peter said to him, “May your silver perish with you, because you thought you could acquire the gift of God with money!
8:21 You have neither part nor allotted share in this matter, for your heart is not upright before God.
8:22 Repent, therefore, of this wickedness of yours, and plead with the Lord that perhaps the intention of your heart may be forgiven you.
8:23 For I see that you are in bitter gall and a bond of unrighteousness.”
8:24 Simon answered, “You yourselves plead with the Lord for me, so that none of the things you have spoken may come upon me.”
8:25 After bearing solemn witness and speaking the word of the Lord, they returned toward Jerusalem, proclaiming the good news in many Samaritan villages.
8:26 An angel of the Lord spoke to Philip, saying, “Rise and go south to the road that descends from Jerusalem to Gaza.” This is a desert road.
8:27 He rose and went. And look—an Ethiopian man, a eunuch and an official of Candace, queen of the Ethiopians, who was over all her treasury, had come to Jerusalem to worship.
8:28 He was returning, seated in his chariot, and reading the prophet Isaiah.
8:29 The Spirit said to Philip, “Go near and join this chariot.”
8:30 Philip ran up, heard him reading Isaiah the prophet, and said, “Do you understand what you are reading?”
8:31 He said, “How could I, unless someone guides me?” And he invited Philip to come up and sit with him.
8:32 The passage of Scripture he was reading was this: “Like a sheep He was led to slaughter, and as a lamb is silent before its shearer, so He does not open His mouth.
8:33 In His humiliation His judgment was taken away. Who will recount His generation? For His life is taken from the earth.”
8:34 The eunuch said to Philip, “I ask you, about whom does the prophet say this—about himself or about someone else?”
8:35 Philip opened his mouth and, beginning from this Scripture, proclaimed to him the good news of Jesus.
8:36 As they went along the road, they came to some water, and the eunuch said, “Look, water! What prevents me from being baptized?”
8:38 He ordered the chariot to stop. Both Philip and the eunuch went down into the water, and Philip baptized him.
8:39 When they came up out of the water, the Spirit of the Lord snatched Philip away, and the eunuch saw him no more, for he went on his way rejoicing.
8:40 Philip was found at Azotus, and as he passed through, he proclaimed the good news in all the towns until he came to Caesarea.

ACTS 9

9:1 Saul, still breathing threat and murder against the Lord’s disciples, went to the high priest
9:2 and asked him for letters to the synagogues in Damascus, so that if he found any belonging to the Way, both men and women, he might bring them bound to Jerusalem.
9:3 As he traveled and drew near Damascus, suddenly a light from heaven flashed around him.
9:4 He fell to the ground and heard a voice saying to him, “Saul, Saul, why are you persecuting Me?”
9:5 He said, “Who art Thou, Lord?” He answered, “I am Jesus, whom you are persecuting.
9:6 But rise and enter the city, and you will be told what you must do.”
9:7 The men traveling with him stood speechless, hearing the voice but seeing no one.
9:8 Saul rose from the ground, but although his eyes were open, he could see nothing. They led him by the hand and brought him into Damascus.
9:9 For three days he could not see, and he neither ate nor drank.
9:10 There was a disciple in Damascus named Ananias. The Lord said to him in a vision, “Ananias.” He said, “Here I am, Lord.”
9:11 The Lord said to him, “Rise, go to the street called Straight, and seek in Judas’s house a man named Saul from Tarsus. Look, he is praying,
9:12 and in a vision he has seen a man named Ananias come in and lay hands upon him so that he may regain his sight.”
9:13 Ananias answered, “Lord, I have heard from many about this man, how much evil he has done to Thy saints in Jerusalem.
9:14 Here too he has authority from the chief priests to bind all who call upon Thy name.”
9:15 But the Lord said to him, “Go, for this man is a chosen vessel for Me, to carry My name before Gentiles and kings and the sons of Israel.
9:16 For I will show him how much he must suffer for My name.”
9:17 Ananias went and entered the house. Laying his hands upon him, he said, “Brother Saul, the Lord has sent me—Jesus, who appeared to you on the road as you came—so that you may regain your sight and be filled with the Holy Spirit.”
9:18 At once something like scales fell from his eyes, and he regained his sight. He rose and was baptized,
9:19 and after taking food he regained strength. He stayed for some days with the disciples in Damascus.
9:20 Immediately he proclaimed Jesus in the synagogues, saying, “He is the Son of God.”
9:21 All who heard him were astonished and said, “Is this not the one who devastated those in Jerusalem who call upon this name? And had he not come here for this purpose, to bring them bound before the chief priests?”
9:22 But Saul grew stronger and confounded the Jews living in Damascus by demonstrating that Jesus is the Christ.
9:23 After many days had passed, the Jews conspired to kill him,
9:24 but their plot became known to Saul. They were also watching the gates day and night to kill him.
9:25 His disciples took him by night and let him down through the wall, lowering him in a hamper.
9:26 When he arrived in Jerusalem, he tried to join the disciples, but they all feared him, not believing that he was a disciple.
9:27 Barnabas took him and brought him to the apostles. He related to them how Saul had seen the Lord on the road, how He had spoken to him, and how Saul had spoken boldly in Damascus in the name of Jesus.
9:28 He was with them, going in and out in Jerusalem and speaking boldly in the name of the Lord.
9:29 He spoke and disputed with the Hellenists, but they attempted to kill him.
9:30 When the brothers learned this, they brought him down to Caesarea and sent him away to Tarsus.
9:31 So the Church throughout all Judea, Galilee, and Samaria had peace and was being built up. Walking in the fear of the Lord and in the encouragement of the Holy Spirit, it increased.
9:32 As Peter traveled among them all, he also went down to the saints who lived in Lydda.
9:33 There he found a man named Aeneas who had lain on a mat for eight years, for he was paralyzed.
9:34 Peter said to him, “Aeneas, Jesus Christ heals you. Rise and make your bed.” At once he rose.
9:35 All who lived in Lydda and Sharon saw him and turned to the Lord.
9:36 In Joppa there was a woman disciple named Tabitha, which translated is Dorcas. She was full of good works and acts of charity.
9:37 In those days she became ill and died. They washed her and laid her in an upper room.
9:38 Since Lydda was near Joppa, the disciples, hearing that Peter was there, sent two men to him, urging, “Do not delay in coming to us.”
9:39 Peter rose and went with them. When he arrived, they brought him into the upper room. All the widows stood beside him weeping and showing the tunics and garments Dorcas had made while she was with them.
9:40 Peter put everyone outside, knelt down, and prayed. Turning toward the body, he said, “Tabitha, rise.” She opened her eyes, saw Peter, and sat up.
9:41 He gave her his hand and raised her up. Then he called the saints and the widows and presented her alive.
9:42 This became known throughout Joppa, and many believed in the Lord.
9:43 He stayed in Joppa for many days with a certain Simon, a tanner.

ACTS 10

10:1 In Caesarea there was a man named Cornelius, a centurion of the cohort called the Italian,
10:2 devout and fearing God with his whole household, giving many alms to the people and praying to God continually.
10:3 About the ninth hour of the day, he clearly saw in a vision an angel of God come in to him and say, “Cornelius.”
10:4 He stared at him in fear and said, “What is it, sir?” The angel said to him, “Your prayers and your alms have ascended as a memorial before God.
10:5 Now send men to Joppa and summon a certain Simon who is called Peter.
10:6 He is staying with a certain Simon, a tanner, whose house is beside the sea.”
10:7 When the angel who spoke to him had gone, Cornelius called two of his household slaves and a devout soldier from among those who attended him.
10:8 He explained everything to them and sent them to Joppa.
10:9 The next day, while they were traveling and approaching the city, Peter went up onto the roof to pray, about the sixth hour.
10:10 He became hungry and wanted to eat. While they were preparing the food, a trance came upon him.
10:11 He saw heaven opened and something like a large sheet descending, being lowered by its four corners to the earth.
10:12 In it were all the four-footed animals and creeping things of the earth and birds of the sky.
10:13 A voice came to him: “Rise, Peter; slaughter and eat.”
10:14 Peter said, “By no means, Lord, for I have never eaten anything common and unclean.”
10:15 A voice came to him again, a second time: “What God has cleansed, you must not call common.”
10:16 This happened three times, and immediately the object was taken up into heaven.
10:17 While Peter was perplexed within himself about what the vision he had seen might mean, look—the men sent by Cornelius, having asked directions to Simon’s house, stood at the gate.
10:18 They called out and asked whether Simon, who was called Peter, was staying there.
10:19 While Peter was pondering the vision, the Spirit said to him, “Look, three men are seeking you.
10:20 Rise, go down, and go with them without hesitation, because I have sent them.”
10:21 Peter went down to the men and said, “I am the one you are seeking. What is the reason you have come?”
10:22 They said, “Cornelius, a centurion, a righteous and God-fearing man well spoken of by the whole Jewish nation, was directed by a holy angel to summon you to his house and hear words from you.”
10:23 So he invited them in and gave them lodging. The next day he rose and went away with them, and some of the brothers from Joppa accompanied him.
10:24 The following day he entered Caesarea. Cornelius was expecting them and had called together his relatives and close friends.
10:25 As Peter was about to enter, Cornelius met him, fell at his feet, and bowed before him.
10:26 But Peter raised him up, saying, “Rise; I too am a human being.”
10:27 While speaking with him, Peter entered and found many people gathered.
10:28 He said to them, “You know how forbidden it is for a Jewish man to associate with or visit someone of another people. Yet God has shown me to call no human being common or unclean.
10:29 Therefore I came without objection when summoned. So I ask, for what reason did you send for me?”
10:30 Cornelius said, “Four days ago, at this hour, I was praying in my house at the ninth hour, and look—a man stood before me in shining clothing.
10:31 He said, ‘Cornelius, your prayer has been heard, and your alms have been remembered before God.
10:32 Send, therefore, to Joppa and summon Simon who is called Peter. He is staying in the house of Simon the tanner beside the sea.’
10:33 So I sent to you at once, and you have done well to come. Now we are all here before God to hear everything the Lord has commanded you.”
10:34 Peter opened his mouth and said, “Truly I understand that God shows no partiality,
10:35 but in every nation, whoever fears Him and practices righteousness is acceptable to Him.
10:36 He sent the word to the sons of Israel, proclaiming good news of peace through Jesus Christ—He is Lord of all.
10:37 You know what took place throughout all Judea, beginning from Galilee after the baptism John proclaimed:
10:38 Jesus of Nazareth—how God anointed Him with the Holy Spirit and power. He went about doing good and healing all who were oppressed by the devil, because God was with Him.
10:39 We are witnesses of everything He did both in the country of the Jews and in Jerusalem. They also killed Him by hanging Him on a tree.
10:40 God raised Him on the third day and granted that He should appear,
10:41 not to all the people, but to witnesses chosen beforehand by God—to us, who ate and drank with Him after He rose from the dead.
10:42 He commanded us to proclaim to the people and bear solemn witness that He is the One appointed by God as judge of the living and the dead.
10:43 All the prophets bear witness to Him: everyone who believes in Him receives forgiveness of sins through His name.”
10:44 While Peter was still speaking these words, the Holy Spirit fell upon all who were hearing the message.
10:45 The believers from the circumcision who had come with Peter were astonished that the gift of the Holy Spirit had been poured out even upon the Gentiles,
10:46 for they heard them speaking in tongues and magnifying God. Then Peter said,
10:47 “Can anyone withhold the water so that these people, who have received the Holy Spirit just as we have, should not be baptized?”
10:48 He ordered them to be baptized in the name of Jesus Christ. Then they asked him to stay for some days.

ACTS 11

11:1 The apostles and the brothers throughout Judea heard that the Gentiles too had received the word of God.
11:2 When Peter went up to Jerusalem, those of the circumcision disputed with him,
11:3 saying, “You went in to uncircumcised men and ate with them.”
11:4 Peter began explaining to them in order, saying,
11:5 “I was in the city of Joppa praying, and in a trance I saw a vision: something like a large sheet descending, lowered by its four corners from heaven, and it came right to me.
11:6 I looked intently into it and examined it, and I saw the four-footed animals of the earth, wild animals, creeping things, and birds of the sky.
11:7 I also heard a voice saying to me, ‘Rise, Peter; slaughter and eat.’
11:8 But I said, ‘By no means, Lord, for nothing common or unclean has ever entered my mouth.’
11:9 A voice answered a second time from heaven, ‘What God has cleansed, you must not call common.’
11:10 This happened three times, and everything was drawn up again into heaven.
11:11 And look, at once three men stood at the house where we were, sent to me from Caesarea.
11:12 The Spirit told me to go with them, making no distinction. These six brothers also went with me, and we entered the man’s house.
11:13 He reported to us how he had seen the angel standing in his house and saying, ‘Send to Joppa and summon Simon who is called Peter,
11:14 who will speak words to you by which you and your whole household will be saved.’
11:15 As I began to speak, the Holy Spirit fell upon them just as upon us at the beginning.
11:16 I remembered the Lord’s word, how He said, ‘John baptized with water, but you will be baptized in the Holy Spirit.’
11:17 If, then, God gave them the same gift as He gave us when we believed in the Lord Jesus Christ, who was I that I could hinder God?”
11:18 When they heard these things, they fell silent and glorified God, saying, “Then God has granted even the Gentiles repentance leading to life.”
11:19 Those who had been scattered by the affliction that arose over Stephen traveled as far as Phoenicia, Cyprus, and Antioch, speaking the word to no one except Jews alone.
11:20 But some of them were men from Cyprus and Cyrene who came to Antioch and also spoke to the Hellenists, proclaiming the good news of the Lord Jesus.
11:21 The Lord’s hand was with them, and a great number believed and turned to the Lord.
11:22 The report about them reached the ears of the Church in Jerusalem, and they sent Barnabas to travel as far as Antioch.
11:23 When he arrived and saw the grace of God, he rejoiced and encouraged them all to remain with the Lord with steadfast purpose of heart,
11:24 for he was a good man, full of the Holy Spirit and faith. A considerable crowd was added to the Lord.
11:25 He went away to Tarsus to search for Saul,
11:26 and when he found him, he brought him to Antioch. For a whole year they gathered with the Church and taught a considerable crowd. It was in Antioch that the disciples were first called Christians.
11:27 In those days prophets came down from Jerusalem to Antioch.
11:28 One of them, named Agabus, stood up and indicated through the Spirit that a great famine would come over the whole inhabited world. This happened in the time of Claudius.
11:29 The disciples determined, each according to his means, to send relief to the brothers living in Judea.
11:30 This they did, sending it to the elders by the hand of Barnabas and Saul.

ACTS 12

12:1 About that time King Herod laid hands on some of the Church to mistreat them.
12:2 He killed James, the brother of John, with the sword.
12:3 When he saw that this pleased the Jews, he proceeded to arrest Peter as well. These were the days of Unleavened Bread.
12:4 He seized him and put him in prison, handing him over to four squads of four soldiers to guard him, intending to bring him out before the people after the Passover.
12:5 So Peter was kept in prison, but earnest prayer for him was being made to God by the Church.
12:6 On the night before Herod was to bring him out, Peter was sleeping between two soldiers, bound with two chains, while guards before the door kept watch over the prison.
12:7 And look—an angel of the Lord stood beside him, and light shone in the cell. Striking Peter’s side, he woke him and said, “Rise quickly.” The chains fell from his hands.
12:8 The angel said to him, “Fasten your belt and put on your sandals.” He did so. Then he said to him, “Wrap your cloak around you and follow me.”
12:9 Peter went out and followed. He did not know that what was happening through the angel was real; he thought he was seeing a vision.
12:10 They passed the first guard post and the second and came to the iron gate leading into the city. It opened for them of its own accord. They went out and continued along one street, and immediately the angel left him.
12:11 When Peter came to himself, he said, “Now I know truly that the Lord has sent His angel and rescued me from Herod’s hand and from everything the Jewish people were expecting.”
12:12 After considering this, he went to the house of Mary, the mother of John who was called Mark, where many had gathered and were praying.
12:13 When he knocked at the outer gate’s door, a slave girl named Rhoda came to answer.
12:14 Recognizing Peter’s voice, in her joy she did not open the gate, but ran inside and announced that Peter was standing before it.
12:15 They said to her, “You are out of your mind.” But she insisted that it was so. They said, “It is his angel.”
12:16 Peter kept knocking. When they opened the door, they saw him and were astonished.
12:17 He motioned with his hand for them to be silent and related how the Lord had brought him out of prison. He said, “Report these things to James and the brothers.” Then he left and went to another place.
12:18 When day came, there was no small disturbance among the soldiers over what had become of Peter.
12:19 Herod searched for him and did not find him. After examining the guards, he ordered them to be led away for execution. Then he went down from Judea to Caesarea and stayed there.
12:20 Herod was fiercely hostile toward the people of Tyre and Sidon. They came to him with one accord and, after winning over Blastus, the king’s chamberlain, asked for peace, because their country received its food from the king’s territory.
12:21 On an appointed day Herod put on royal clothing, sat on the tribunal, and delivered an address to them.
12:22 The populace shouted, “A god’s voice, and not a human being’s!”
12:23 Immediately an angel of the Lord struck him because he did not give the glory to God. He was eaten by worms and breathed his last.
12:24 But the word of God grew and multiplied.
12:25 Barnabas and Saul returned to Jerusalem after completing their service, and took along John who was called Mark.

ACTS 13

13:1 In the Church at Antioch there were prophets and teachers: Barnabas, Simeon who was called Niger, Lucius of Cyrene, Manaen who had been brought up with Herod the tetrarch, and Saul.
13:2 While they were ministering to the Lord and fasting, the Holy Spirit said, “Set apart for Me Barnabas and Saul for the work to which I have called them.”
13:3 Then, after fasting and praying and laying their hands upon them, they sent them away.
13:4 So, sent out by the Holy Spirit, they went down to Seleucia and from there sailed to Cyprus.
13:5 When they came to Salamis, they proclaimed the word of God in the Jewish synagogues. They also had John as an assistant.
13:6 They traveled through the whole island as far as Paphos and found a certain magician, a Jewish false prophet named Bar-Jesus,
13:7 who was with the proconsul Sergius Paulus, an intelligent man. The proconsul summoned Barnabas and Saul and sought to hear the word of God.
13:8 But Elymas the magician—for that is how his name is translated—opposed them, seeking to turn the proconsul away from the faith.
13:9 Saul, who was also called Paul, filled with the Holy Spirit, looked intently at him
13:10 and said, “You who are full of all deceit and all villainy, son of the devil, enemy of all righteousness—will you not stop making crooked the Lord’s straight ways?
13:11 Now look, the Lord’s hand is upon you, and you will be blind, unable to see the sun for a time.” Immediately mist and darkness fell upon him, and he went about seeking people to lead him by the hand.
13:12 When the proconsul saw what had happened, he believed, astonished at the teaching of the Lord.
13:13 Paul and his companions sailed from Paphos and came to Perga in Pamphylia. John left them and returned to Jerusalem.
13:14 They continued from Perga and arrived at Pisidian Antioch. On the Sabbath day they entered the synagogue and sat down.
13:15 After the reading of the law and the prophets, the synagogue rulers sent word to them, saying, “Men, brothers, if you have a word of encouragement for the people, speak.”
13:16 Paul rose, motioned with his hand, and said, “Men of Israel and you who fear God, listen.
13:17 The God of this people Israel chose our fathers and exalted the people during their stay as strangers in the land of Egypt. With an uplifted arm He led them out of it,
13:18 and for about forty years He put up with their conduct in the wilderness.
13:19 He overthrew seven nations in the land of Canaan and gave their land as an inheritance,
13:20 over about four hundred and fifty years. After these things He gave judges until Samuel the prophet.
13:21 Then they asked for a king, and God gave them Saul son of Kish, a man of the tribe of Benjamin, for forty years.
13:22 After removing him, He raised up David as king for them, to whom He also bore witness, saying, ‘I have found David son of Jesse, a man after My heart, who will do all My will.’
13:23 From this man’s seed, according to promise, God brought Israel a Savior, Jesus,
13:24 after John had proclaimed, before His coming, a baptism of repentance to all the people of Israel.
13:25 As John was completing his course, he said, ‘Who do you think I am? I am not that one. But look, One is coming after me, whose sandals I am not worthy to untie.’
13:26 Men, brothers, sons of Abraham’s family, and those among you who fear God: to us the message of this salvation has been sent.
13:27 For those living in Jerusalem and their rulers, failing to recognize Him and the voices of the prophets read every Sabbath, fulfilled those voices by condemning Him.
13:28 Although they found no cause for death, they asked Pilate to have Him killed.
13:29 When they had completed everything written about Him, they took Him down from the tree and laid Him in a tomb.
13:30 But God raised Him from the dead.
13:31 For many days He appeared to those who had gone up with Him from Galilee to Jerusalem, who are now His witnesses to the people.
13:32 We proclaim to you the good news of the promise made to the fathers:
13:33 God has fulfilled this for us, their children, by raising up Jesus, as it is also written in the second psalm: ‘Thou art My Son; today I have begotten Thee.’
13:34 God has spoken this way about raising Him from the dead, never again to return to decay: ‘I will give you the holy and sure blessings of David.’
13:35 Therefore He also says in another place, ‘Thou wilt not allow Thy Holy One to see decay.’
13:36 For David, after serving his own generation according to God’s purpose, fell asleep, was gathered to his fathers, and saw decay.
13:37 But the One whom God raised did not see decay.
13:38 Therefore let it be known to you, men, brothers, that through Him forgiveness of sins is proclaimed to you; and from everything from which you could not be justified in the law of Moses,
13:39 in Him everyone who believes is justified.
13:40 Take care, then, lest what is spoken in the prophets come upon you:
13:41 ‘Look, you scoffers, marvel and vanish, for I am doing a work in your days, a work you will never believe even if someone recounts it to you.’”
13:42 As they went out, the people urged that these words be spoken to them on the next Sabbath.
13:43 After the synagogue gathering was dismissed, many Jews and devout converts followed Paul and Barnabas, who spoke with them and urged them to remain in the grace of God.
13:44 On the coming Sabbath, almost the whole city gathered to hear the word of the Lord.
13:45 When the Jews saw the crowds, they were filled with jealousy and contradicted what Paul was saying, speaking abusively.
13:46 Paul and Barnabas spoke boldly and said, “It was necessary for God’s word to be spoken to you first. Since you reject it and judge yourselves unworthy of eternal life, look—we are turning to the Gentiles.
13:47 For the Lord has commanded us in this way: ‘I have appointed you as a light for Gentiles, so that you may bring salvation to the end of the earth.’”
13:48 When the Gentiles heard this, they rejoiced and glorified the word of the Lord, and as many as had been appointed to eternal life believed.
13:49 The word of the Lord spread through the whole region.
13:50 But the Jews stirred up the devout women of high standing and the leading men of the city, raised persecution against Paul and Barnabas, and drove them out of their territory.
13:51 They shook the dust from their feet against them and went to Iconium.
13:52 The disciples were filled with joy and the Holy Spirit.

ACTS 14

14:1 In Iconium they entered the Jewish synagogue together and spoke in such a way that a great multitude of both Jews and Greeks believed.
14:2 But the Jews who refused to believe stirred up the Gentiles and embittered their minds against the brothers.
14:3 So they stayed a considerable time, speaking boldly in reliance on the Lord, who bore witness to the word of His grace by granting signs and wonders to take place through their hands.
14:4 The people of the city were divided: some sided with the Jews and others with the apostles.
14:5 When an attempt was made by both Gentiles and Jews, together with their rulers, to mistreat and stone them,
14:6 they learned of it and fled to Lystra and Derbe, cities of Lycaonia, and the surrounding region.
14:7 There they continued proclaiming the good news.
14:8 In Lystra a man was sitting who had no strength in his feet, unable to walk from his mother’s womb; he had never walked.
14:9 He listened to Paul speaking. Paul looked intently at him and saw that he had faith to be made well,
14:10 and said with a loud voice, “Stand upright on your feet.” He leaped up and began walking.
14:11 When the crowds saw what Paul had done, they raised their voice, saying in Lycaonian, “The gods have come down to us in human likeness.”
14:12 They called Barnabas Zeus and Paul Hermes, because he was the leading speaker.
14:13 The priest of Zeus, whose shrine was before the city, brought bulls and garlands to the gates and wanted to offer sacrifice with the crowds.
14:14 When the apostles Barnabas and Paul heard this, they tore their garments and rushed out into the crowd, crying out
14:15 and saying, “Men, why are you doing these things? We too are human beings, of the same nature as you. We proclaim good news to you: turn from these worthless things to the living God, who made heaven and earth and the sea and everything in them.
14:16 In past generations He allowed all the nations to go their own ways.
14:17 Yet He did not leave Himself without witness in doing good, giving you rains from heaven and fruitful seasons, filling your hearts with food and gladness.”
14:18 Even by saying these things, they scarcely stopped the crowds from sacrificing to them.
14:19 Jews came from Antioch and Iconium, persuaded the crowds, stoned Paul, and dragged him outside the city, supposing he was dead.
14:20 But when the disciples surrounded him, he rose and entered the city. The next day he went with Barnabas to Derbe.
14:21 After proclaiming the good news to that city and making many disciples, they returned to Lystra, Iconium, and Antioch,
14:22 strengthening the souls of the disciples, encouraging them to remain in the faith, and saying, “Through many afflictions we must enter the kingdom of God.”
14:23 They appointed elders for them in each Church and, after praying with fasting, entrusted them to the Lord in whom they had believed.
14:24 They passed through Pisidia and came to Pamphylia.
14:25 After speaking the word in Perga, they went down to Attalia.
14:26 From there they sailed to Antioch, where they had been entrusted to the grace of God for the work they had now completed.
14:27 When they arrived and gathered the Church, they reported all that God had done with them and how He had opened a door of faith to the Gentiles.
14:28 They stayed quite a long time with the disciples.

ACTS 15

15:1 Some men came down from Judea and taught the brothers, “Unless you are circumcised according to the custom of Moses, you cannot be saved.”
15:2 When Paul and Barnabas had a serious disagreement and debate with them, they appointed Paul and Barnabas and some others from among them to go up to the apostles and elders in Jerusalem about this question.
15:3 Sent on their way by the Church, they passed through Phoenicia and Samaria, recounting the conversion of the Gentiles and bringing great joy to all the brothers.
15:4 When they arrived in Jerusalem, they were welcomed by the Church, the apostles, and the elders, and reported all that God had done with them.
15:5 But some believers from the party of the Pharisees rose and said, “It is necessary to circumcise them and command them to keep the law of Moses.”
15:6 The apostles and the elders gathered to consider this matter.
15:7 After much debate, Peter rose and said to them, “Men, brothers, you know that in earlier days God made a choice among you, that through my mouth the Gentiles should hear the word of the good news and believe.
15:8 God, who knows the heart, bore witness to them by giving them the Holy Spirit just as He did to us.
15:9 He made no distinction between us and them, cleansing their hearts by faith.
15:10 Now, therefore, why are you testing God by placing upon the disciples’ neck a yoke that neither our fathers nor we were able to bear?
15:11 Rather, we believe that we are saved through the grace of the Lord Jesus, in the same way as they are.”
15:12 The whole multitude became silent and listened to Barnabas and Paul relate all the signs and wonders God had done among the Gentiles through them.
15:13 After they fell silent, James spoke: “Men, brothers, listen to me.
15:14 Simeon has related how God first visited the Gentiles to take from them a people for His name.
15:15 With this the words of the prophets agree, as it is written:
15:16 ‘After these things I will return and rebuild David’s fallen tent. I will rebuild its ruins and set it upright,
15:17 so that the rest of humanity may seek the Lord, and all the nations upon whom My name has been called, says the Lord, who makes these things
15:18 known from long ago.’
15:19 Therefore I judge that we should not trouble those from the Gentiles who are turning to God,
15:20 but should write to them to abstain from the pollutions of idols, sexual immorality, what is strangled, and blood.
15:21 For since ancient times, people in every city have proclaimed Moses, and he is read in the synagogues every Sabbath.”
15:22 Then the apostles and elders, with the whole Church, decided to choose men from among them and send them to Antioch with Paul and Barnabas: Judas called Barsabbas, and Silas, leading men among the brothers.
15:23 They wrote by their hand: “The apostles and elders, your brothers, to the brothers from the Gentiles in Antioch, Syria, and Cilicia: greetings.
15:24 Since we have heard that some who went out from among us troubled you with words, unsettling your souls, although we gave them no instruction,
15:25 we have decided with one accord to choose men and send them to you with our beloved Barnabas and Paul,
15:26 men who have given over their lives for the name of our Lord Jesus Christ.
15:27 We have therefore sent Judas and Silas, who will themselves report the same things by word of mouth.
15:28 For it seemed good to the Holy Spirit and to us to place no further burden upon you except these necessary things:
15:29 to abstain from things sacrificed to idols, blood, things strangled, and sexual immorality. If you keep yourselves from these, you will do well. Farewell.”
15:30 After being sent off, they went down to Antioch, gathered the multitude, and delivered the letter.
15:31 When they read it, they rejoiced at its encouragement.
15:32 Judas and Silas, themselves also prophets, encouraged the brothers with many words and strengthened them.
15:33 After spending some time there, they were sent away in peace by the brothers to those who had sent them.
15:35 Paul and Barnabas remained in Antioch, teaching and proclaiming the word of the Lord as good news, along with many others.
15:36 After some days Paul said to Barnabas, “Let us return and visit the brothers in every city where we proclaimed the word of the Lord, to see how they are.”
15:37 Barnabas wanted to take along John, who was called Mark, as well.
15:38 But Paul considered it right not to take along the man who had withdrawn from them in Pamphylia and had not gone with them to the work.
15:39 A sharp disagreement arose, so that they separated from one another. Barnabas took Mark and sailed to Cyprus,
15:40 but Paul chose Silas and departed, entrusted by the brothers to the grace of the Lord.
15:41 He traveled through Syria and Cilicia, strengthening the Churches.

ACTS 16

16:1 He also came to Derbe and Lystra. There was a disciple there named Timothy, the son of a believing Jewish woman, but of a Greek father.
16:2 He was well spoken of by the brothers in Lystra and Iconium.
16:3 Paul wanted him to go with him, and took him and circumcised him because of the Jews in those places, for they all knew that his father was Greek.
16:4 As they traveled through the cities, they handed on to them for observance the decisions made by the apostles and elders in Jerusalem.
16:5 So the Churches were strengthened in the faith and increased in number each day.
16:6 They passed through Phrygia and the Galatian region, having been prevented by the Holy Spirit from speaking the word in Asia.
16:7 When they came near Mysia, they tried to go into Bithynia, but the Spirit of Jesus did not permit them.
16:8 Passing by Mysia, they went down to Troas.
16:9 During the night a vision appeared to Paul: a Macedonian man was standing and urging him, saying, “Cross over to Macedonia and help us.”
16:10 When he had seen the vision, we immediately sought to go out to Macedonia, concluding that God had called us to proclaim the good news to them.
16:11 We sailed from Troas and made a straight course to Samothrace, and on the next day to Neapolis,
16:12 and from there to Philippi, a city of the first district of Macedonia and a colony. We stayed in that city for some days.
16:13 On the Sabbath day we went outside the gate beside a river, where we thought there was a place of prayer. We sat down and spoke to the women who had gathered.
16:14 A woman named Lydia, a seller of purple goods from the city of Thyatira, who worshiped God, was listening. The Lord opened her heart to attend to what Paul was saying.
16:15 After she and her household were baptized, she urged us, saying, “If you have judged me faithful to the Lord, come into my house and stay.” And she prevailed upon us.
16:16 As we were going to the place of prayer, a slave girl with a spirit of divination met us. She brought her masters much profit by fortune-telling.
16:17 She followed Paul and us, crying out, “These men are servants of God Most High, who proclaim to you the way of salvation.”
16:18 She kept doing this for many days. Paul, greatly troubled, turned and said to the spirit, “I command you in the name of Jesus Christ to come out of her.” It came out that very hour.
16:19 When her masters saw that their hope of profit had gone out, they seized Paul and Silas and dragged them into the marketplace before the rulers.
16:20 They brought them before the magistrates and said, “These men are throwing our city into turmoil. They are Jews,
16:21 and they proclaim customs that we, being Romans, are not permitted to accept or practice.”
16:22 The crowd joined the attack against them. The magistrates tore off their garments and ordered them to be beaten with rods.
16:23 After inflicting many blows on them, they threw them into prison and ordered the jailer to keep them securely.
16:24 Having received such an order, he put them in the inner prison and secured their feet in the stocks.
16:25 About midnight Paul and Silas were praying and singing hymns to God, while the prisoners listened to them.
16:26 Suddenly there was a great earthquake, so that the jail’s foundations were shaken. At once all the doors opened, and everyone’s bonds were loosened.
16:27 The jailer awoke and saw the prison doors open. He drew his sword and was about to kill himself, supposing the prisoners had escaped.
16:28 But Paul cried with a loud voice, “Do yourself no harm, for we are all here.”
16:29 He called for lights, rushed inside, and fell trembling before Paul and Silas.
16:30 He brought them outside and said, “Sirs, what must I do to be saved?”
16:31 They said, “Believe in the Lord Jesus, and you will be saved, you and your household.”
16:32 They spoke the word of the Lord to him, together with all who were in his house.
16:33 At that very hour of the night he took them and washed their wounds. Immediately he and all his people were baptized.
16:34 He brought them up into his house, set food before them, and rejoiced with his whole household, having believed in God.
16:35 When day came, the magistrates sent the officers, saying, “Release those men.”
16:36 The jailer reported these words to Paul: “The magistrates have sent word for you to be released. Now come out and go in peace.”
16:37 But Paul said to them, “They beat us publicly without a verdict, although we are Roman citizens, and threw us into prison. And now are they sending us away secretly? No indeed! Let them come themselves and lead us out.”
16:38 The officers reported these words to the magistrates, and they were afraid when they heard that the men were Romans.
16:39 They came and appealed to them, led them out, and asked them to leave the city.
16:40 After leaving the prison, they went to Lydia’s house. They saw the brothers, encouraged them, and departed.

ACTS 17

17:1 After traveling through Amphipolis and Apollonia, they came to Thessalonica, where there was a Jewish synagogue.
17:2 As was his custom, Paul went in to them, and for three Sabbaths he reasoned with them from the Scriptures,
17:3 explaining and setting before them that the Christ had to suffer and rise from the dead, and saying, “This Jesus whom I proclaim to you is the Christ.”
17:4 Some of them were persuaded and joined Paul and Silas, as did a great multitude of devout Greeks and not a few of the leading women.
17:5 But the Jews became jealous and took some wicked men from the marketplace, gathered a mob, and threw the city into turmoil. They attacked Jason’s house and sought to bring Paul and Silas out to the populace.
17:6 When they did not find them, they dragged Jason and some brothers before the city officials, shouting, “These men who have upset the inhabited world have come here too,
17:7 and Jason has welcomed them. They all act against Caesar’s decrees, saying that there is another king, Jesus.”
17:8 They disturbed the crowd and the city officials who heard these things.
17:9 After taking security from Jason and the others, the officials released them.
17:10 The brothers immediately sent Paul and Silas away by night to Berea. When they arrived, they went into the Jewish synagogue.
17:11 These people were more noble-minded than those in Thessalonica: they welcomed the word with all eagerness, examining the Scriptures daily to see whether these things were so.
17:12 Many of them therefore believed, as did not a few Greek women of high standing and men.
17:13 But when the Jews from Thessalonica learned that the word of God had also been proclaimed by Paul in Berea, they came there too, stirring up and disturbing the crowds.
17:14 Then the brothers immediately sent Paul away to go as far as the sea, while Silas and Timothy remained there.
17:15 Those escorting Paul brought him as far as Athens. After receiving an order for Silas and Timothy to come to him as quickly as possible, they departed.
17:16 While Paul waited for them in Athens, his spirit was provoked within him as he observed that the city was full of idols.
17:17 So he reasoned in the synagogue with the Jews and the worshipers, and in the marketplace every day with those who happened to be there.
17:18 Some Epicurean and Stoic philosophers also engaged him. Some said, “What is this babbler trying to say?” Others said, “He seems to be a proclaimer of foreign divinities,” because he proclaimed the good news of Jesus and the resurrection.
17:19 They took him and brought him to the Areopagus, saying, “May we know what this new teaching is that you are speaking?
17:20 For you are bringing some strange things to our ears. We therefore wish to know what these things mean.”
17:21 Now all the Athenians and the foreigners staying there spent their free time doing nothing but telling or hearing the latest thing.
17:22 Paul stood in the midst of the Areopagus and said, “Men of Athens, I perceive that in every respect you are very religious.
17:23 For as I passed through and observed your objects of worship, I also found an altar inscribed, ‘To an unknown god.’ What you worship without knowing, this I proclaim to you.
17:24 The God who made the world and everything in it, being Lord of heaven and earth, does not dwell in sanctuaries made by hands.
17:25 Nor is He served by human hands as though He needed anything, since He Himself gives everyone life and breath and everything.
17:26 From one He made every nation of humanity to live upon the whole face of the earth, determining their appointed times and the boundaries of their dwelling,
17:27 so that they might seek God, if perhaps they might feel their way toward Him and find Him, though He is not far from each one of us.
17:28 For in Him we live and move and exist, as even some of your own poets have said, ‘For we too are His offspring.’
17:29 Since we are God’s offspring, we ought not to think that the divine nature is like gold or silver or stone, an image formed by human art and thought.
17:30 Therefore, having overlooked the times of ignorance, God now commands all people everywhere to repent,
17:31 because He has fixed a day on which He will judge the inhabited world in righteousness through a Man whom He appointed, giving assurance to everyone by raising Him from the dead.”
17:32 When they heard of a resurrection of the dead, some mocked, while others said, “We will hear you again about this.”
17:33 So Paul went out from their midst.
17:34 But some people joined him and believed, among them Dionysius the Areopagite, a woman named Damaris, and others with them.

ACTS 18

18:1 After these things he left Athens and came to Corinth.
18:2 He found a Jew named Aquila, a native of Pontus who had recently come from Italy with his wife Priscilla, because Claudius had ordered all the Jews to leave Rome. Paul went to them,
18:3 and because he practiced the same trade, he stayed with them and worked, for they were tentmakers by trade.
18:4 Every Sabbath he reasoned in the synagogue and sought to persuade both Jews and Greeks.
18:5 When Silas and Timothy came down from Macedonia, Paul devoted himself to the word, bearing solemn witness to the Jews that Jesus is the Christ.
18:6 When they opposed him and spoke abusively, he shook out his garments and said to them, “Your blood be upon your own heads. I am clean. From now on I will go to the Gentiles.”
18:7 He left there and entered the house of a man named Titius Justus, a worshiper of God, whose house was next to the synagogue.
18:8 Crispus, the synagogue ruler, believed in the Lord with his whole household. Many of the Corinthians who heard believed and were baptized.
18:9 The Lord said to Paul by a vision in the night, “Do not fear, but speak and do not be silent,
18:10 because I am with you, and no one will attack you to harm you, for I have many people in this city.”
18:11 He stayed a year and six months, teaching the word of God among them.
18:12 While Gallio was proconsul of Achaia, the Jews rose together against Paul and brought him before the tribunal,
18:13 saying, “This man persuades people to worship God contrary to the law.”
18:14 As Paul was about to open his mouth, Gallio said to the Jews, “If it were some wrongdoing or wicked crime, O Jews, I would have reason to hear you out.
18:15 But if these are questions about a word, names, and your own law, see to it yourselves. I do not wish to be a judge of these things.”
18:16 He drove them away from the tribunal.
18:17 They all seized Sosthenes, the synagogue ruler, and beat him before the tribunal. Gallio cared about none of these things.
18:18 Paul stayed many more days, then took leave of the brothers and sailed for Syria, with Priscilla and Aquila. He had cut his hair at Cenchreae, for he had a vow.
18:19 They arrived at Ephesus, and he left them there. He himself entered the synagogue and reasoned with the Jews.
18:20 When they asked him to stay for a longer time, he did not agree.
18:21 Instead, he took leave of them, saying, “I will return to you again, God willing,” and sailed from Ephesus.
18:22 He landed at Caesarea, went up and greeted the Church, and went down to Antioch.
18:23 After spending some time there, he departed and traveled from place to place through the Galatian region and Phrygia, strengthening all the disciples.
18:24 A Jew named Apollos, a native of Alexandria, came to Ephesus. He was an eloquent man, powerful in the Scriptures.
18:25 He had been instructed in the way of the Lord, and being fervent in spirit, he spoke and taught accurately the things concerning Jesus, though he knew only John’s baptism.
18:26 He began to speak boldly in the synagogue. When Priscilla and Aquila heard him, they took him aside and explained the way of God to him more accurately.
18:27 When he wished to cross over to Achaia, the brothers encouraged him and wrote to the disciples to welcome him. On arriving, he greatly helped those who had believed through grace,
18:28 for he vigorously refuted the Jews in public, demonstrating through the Scriptures that Jesus is the Christ.

ACTS 19

19:1 While Apollos was in Corinth, Paul passed through the upper regions and came down to Ephesus. He found some disciples
19:2 and said to them, “Did you receive the Holy Spirit when you believed?” They said to him, “We have not even heard whether there is a Holy Spirit.”
19:3 He said, “Into what, then, were you baptized?” They said, “Into John’s baptism.”
19:4 Paul said, “John baptized with a baptism of repentance, telling the people to believe in the One coming after him, that is, in Jesus.”
19:5 When they heard this, they were baptized into the name of the Lord Jesus.
19:6 When Paul laid his hands upon them, the Holy Spirit came upon them, and they spoke in tongues and prophesied.
19:7 There were about twelve men in all.
19:8 He entered the synagogue and spoke boldly for three months, reasoning and persuading concerning the kingdom of God.
19:9 But when some became hardened and refused to believe, speaking evil of the Way before the multitude, he withdrew from them and separated the disciples, reasoning daily in the lecture hall of Tyrannus.
19:10 This continued for two years, so that all who lived in Asia heard the word of the Lord, both Jews and Greeks.
19:11 God was doing extraordinary mighty works through Paul’s hands,
19:12 so that even cloths or aprons from contact with his skin were carried to the sick, and their diseases left them and the evil spirits went out.
19:13 Some itinerant Jewish exorcists also attempted to invoke the name of the Lord Jesus over those who had evil spirits, saying, “I solemnly charge you by the Jesus whom Paul proclaims.”
19:14 Seven sons of a certain Sceva, a Jewish chief priest, were doing this.
19:15 But the evil spirit answered them, “Jesus I know, and Paul I recognize, but who are you?”
19:16 The man in whom the evil spirit was leaped upon them, mastered both of them, and overpowered them, so that they fled from that house naked and wounded.
19:17 This became known to all the Jews and Greeks living in Ephesus. Fear fell upon them all, and the name of the Lord Jesus was magnified.
19:18 Many who had believed came, confessing and reporting their practices.
19:19 A considerable number who had practiced magic brought their books together and burned them before everyone. They calculated their prices and found the total to be fifty thousand in silver.
19:20 In this way the word of the Lord grew mightily and prevailed.
19:21 After these things were completed, Paul resolved in spirit to pass through Macedonia and Achaia and go to Jerusalem, saying, “After I have been there, I must also see Rome.”
19:22 He sent two of those who assisted him, Timothy and Erastus, to Macedonia, while he himself remained in Asia for a time.
19:23 About that time a serious disturbance arose concerning the Way.
19:24 A silversmith named Demetrius, who made silver shrines of Artemis, provided considerable business for the craftsmen.
19:25 He gathered them and the workers in related trades and said, “Men, you know that our prosperity comes from this business.
19:26 You see and hear that not only in Ephesus but in almost all Asia this Paul has persuaded and turned away a considerable crowd, saying that things made by hands are not gods.
19:27 There is danger not only that this trade of ours will fall into disrepute, but also that the temple of the great goddess Artemis will be reckoned as nothing, and that she whom all Asia and the inhabited world worship will be stripped of her magnificence.”
19:28 When they heard this, they became full of rage and cried out, “Great is Artemis of the Ephesians!”
19:29 The city was filled with confusion. They rushed together into the theater, seizing Gaius and Aristarchus, Macedonians who traveled with Paul.
19:30 Paul wanted to enter among the populace, but the disciples would not let him.
19:31 Even some of the Asiarchs, who were his friends, sent to him and urged him not to venture into the theater.
19:32 Some shouted one thing and some another, for the assembly was confused, and most did not know why they had come together.
19:33 They prompted Alexander from the crowd, the Jews having put him forward. Alexander motioned with his hand and wished to make a defense before the populace.
19:34 When they recognized that he was a Jew, one cry arose from them all for about two hours: “Great is Artemis of the Ephesians!”
19:35 The city secretary quieted the crowd and said, “Men of Ephesus, who among human beings does not know that the city of the Ephesians is temple-keeper of the great Artemis and of what fell from Zeus?
19:36 Since these things are undeniable, you must remain calm and do nothing rash.
19:37 For you have brought these men here, though they are neither temple-robbers nor blasphemers of our goddess.
19:38 If Demetrius and the craftsmen with him have a charge against anyone, court sessions are held and proconsuls are available; let them bring charges against one another.
19:39 But if you seek anything further, it will be settled in the lawful assembly.
19:40a For we are in danger of being charged with rioting over today’s events, since there is no cause for it; we will not be able to give an account of this gathering.”
19:40b After saying these things, he dismissed the assembly.

ACTS 20

20:1 After the uproar ceased, Paul summoned the disciples and encouraged them. Taking leave of them, he departed for Macedonia.
20:2 He passed through those regions and encouraged them with many words, then came to Greece
20:3 and spent three months there. When a plot was made against him by the Jews as he was about to sail for Syria, he decided to return through Macedonia.
20:4 He was accompanied by Sopater son of Pyrrhus from Berea; Aristarchus and Secundus from Thessalonica; Gaius from Derbe, and Timothy; and Tychicus and Trophimus from Asia.
20:5 These men went ahead and waited for us in Troas.
20:6 We sailed from Philippi after the days of Unleavened Bread and came to them at Troas in five days, where we stayed seven days.
20:7 On the first day of the week, when we had gathered to break bread, Paul spoke with them. He intended to leave the next day and prolonged his message until midnight.
20:8 There were many lamps in the upper room where we had gathered.
20:9 A young man named Eutychus sat in the window and was sinking into a deep sleep as Paul continued speaking at length. Overcome by sleep, he fell down from the third story and was picked up dead.
20:10 Paul went down, fell upon him, embraced him, and said, “Do not be alarmed, for his life is in him.”
20:11 He went back up, broke the bread and ate, and talked for a long time until dawn. Then he departed.
20:12 They brought the boy away alive and were greatly comforted.
20:13 We went ahead to the ship and sailed for Assos, intending to take Paul aboard there, for he had arranged it this way, intending himself to travel on foot.
20:14 When he met us at Assos, we took him aboard and came to Mitylene.
20:15 Sailing from there, the next day we arrived opposite Chios; on the following day we put in at Samos, and on the next we came to Miletus.
20:16 For Paul had decided to sail past Ephesus so that he would not spend time in Asia. He was hurrying, if possible, to be in Jerusalem on the day of Pentecost.
20:17 From Miletus he sent to Ephesus and summoned the elders of the Church.
20:18 When they came to him, he said, “You know how I was with you the whole time, from the first day I set foot in Asia,
20:19 serving the Lord with all humility, tears, and trials that came upon me through the plots of the Jews.
20:20 I did not hold back from declaring anything beneficial to you and teaching you publicly and from house to house,
20:21 bearing solemn witness to both Jews and Greeks of repentance toward God and faith in our Lord Jesus.
20:22 And now, look, bound in spirit, I am going to Jerusalem, not knowing what will meet me there,
20:23 except that in every city the Holy Spirit bears witness to me, saying that bonds and afflictions await me.
20:24 But I consider my life of no value to myself, so that I may complete my course and the ministry I received from the Lord Jesus: to bear solemn witness to the good news of God’s grace.
20:25 And now, look, I know that none of you among whom I went about proclaiming the kingdom will see my face again.
20:26 Therefore I testify to you this very day that I am clean from the blood of all,
20:27 for I did not hold back from declaring to you the whole purpose of God.
20:28 Pay attention to yourselves and to all the flock, among whom the Holy Spirit has placed you as overseers, to shepherd the Church of God, which He acquired through His own blood.
20:29 I know that after my departure fierce wolves will enter among you, not sparing the flock.
20:30 Even from among you yourselves men will arise, speaking distorted things to draw the disciples away after themselves.
20:31 Therefore stay awake, remembering that for three years, night and day, I never ceased admonishing each one with tears.
20:32 And now I entrust you to God and to the word of His grace, which is able to build you up and give you the inheritance among all who have been sanctified.
20:33 I coveted no one’s silver or gold or clothing.
20:34 You yourselves know that these hands served my needs and those of the people with me.
20:35 In everything I showed you that by working in this way we must support the weak and remember the words of the Lord Jesus, that He Himself said, ‘It is more blessed to give than to receive.’”
20:36 After saying these things, he knelt down with them all and prayed.
20:37 They all wept greatly, threw their arms around Paul’s neck, and kissed him repeatedly,
20:38 grieving most of all over the word he had spoken, that they would no longer see his face. They accompanied him to the ship.

ACTS 21

21:1 When we had torn ourselves away from them and set sail, we made a straight course to Cos, the next day to Rhodes, and from there to Patara.
21:2 We found a ship crossing to Phoenicia, went aboard, and set sail.
21:3 We sighted Cyprus and passed it on our left, sailed toward Syria, and landed at Tyre, for the ship was to unload its cargo there.
21:4 We sought out the disciples and stayed there seven days. Through the Spirit they told Paul not to set foot in Jerusalem.
21:5 When our days there were completed, we left and went on our way. They all accompanied us, with wives and children, as far as outside the city. We knelt on the beach and prayed,
21:6 then said farewell to one another. We boarded the ship, and they returned to their homes.
21:7 Completing the voyage from Tyre, we arrived at Ptolemais, greeted the brothers, and stayed with them for one day.
21:8 The next day we departed and came to Caesarea. We entered the house of Philip the evangelist, who was one of the seven, and stayed with him.
21:9 He had four daughters who were virgins and prophesied.
21:10 While we stayed for several days, a prophet named Agabus came down from Judea.
21:11 He came to us, took Paul’s belt, bound his own feet and hands, and said, “Thus says the Holy Spirit: ‘The Jews in Jerusalem will bind the man whose belt this is in this way and hand him over into Gentile hands.’”
21:12 When we heard these things, both we and the local people urged him not to go up to Jerusalem.
21:13 Then Paul answered, “What are you doing, weeping and breaking my heart? For I am ready not only to be bound, but also to die in Jerusalem for the name of the Lord Jesus.”
21:14 Since he would not be persuaded, we became silent and said, “Let the Lord’s will be done.”
21:15 After these days we made our preparations and went up to Jerusalem.
21:16 Some disciples from Caesarea also went with us, taking us to a certain Mnason of Cyprus, an early disciple, with whom we were to lodge.
21:17 When we arrived in Jerusalem, the brothers welcomed us gladly.
21:18 The next day Paul went with us to James, and all the elders were present.
21:19 After greeting them, he related one by one the things God had done among the Gentiles through his ministry.
21:20 When they heard, they glorified God and said to him, “You see, brother, how many tens of thousands of Jews have believed, and they are all zealous for the law.
21:21 They have been informed about you that you teach all the Jews among the Gentiles to forsake Moses, telling them not to circumcise their children or walk in the customs.
21:22 What, then, is to be done? They will certainly hear that you have come.
21:23 Therefore do what we tell you. We have four men who have taken a vow upon themselves.
21:24 Take these men, purify yourself with them, and pay their expenses so that they may shave their heads. Then everyone will know that there is nothing to what they have been told about you, but that you yourself also walk in step, keeping the law.
21:25 But concerning the Gentiles who have believed, we have sent word, judging that they should keep themselves from what is sacrificed to idols, blood, what is strangled, and sexual immorality.”
21:26 Then Paul took the men, and on the next day, after purifying himself with them, he entered the temple, giving notice of the completion of the days of purification, up to the presentation of the offering for each one of them.
21:27 When the seven days were about to be completed, the Jews from Asia saw him in the temple, stirred up the whole crowd, and laid hands on him,
21:28 crying, “Men of Israel, help! This is the man who teaches everyone everywhere against the people, the law, and this place. Moreover, he has even brought Greeks into the temple and defiled this holy place.”
21:29 For they had previously seen Trophimus the Ephesian with him in the city and supposed that Paul had brought him into the temple.
21:30 The whole city was stirred, and the people ran together. They seized Paul and dragged him outside the temple, and immediately the doors were shut.
21:31 While they were seeking to kill him, a report reached the tribune of the cohort that all Jerusalem was in confusion.
21:32 He immediately took soldiers and centurions and ran down to them. When they saw the tribune and the soldiers, they stopped beating Paul.
21:33 Then the tribune approached, seized him, and ordered him bound with two chains. He asked who he was and what he had done.
21:34 Some in the crowd shouted one thing and some another. Since he could learn nothing certain because of the uproar, he ordered Paul to be brought into the barracks.
21:35 When he reached the steps, the soldiers had to carry him because of the crowd’s violence,
21:36 for the multitude of the people followed, crying, “Away with him!”
21:37 As Paul was about to be brought into the barracks, he said to the tribune, “May I say something to you?” He said, “Do you know Greek?
21:38 Then are you not the Egyptian who some time ago stirred up a revolt and led the four thousand men of the Sicarii into the wilderness?”
21:39 Paul said, “I am a Jewish man from Tarsus in Cilicia, a citizen of no insignificant city. I ask you, allow me to speak to the people.”
21:40 When he gave permission, Paul stood on the steps and motioned with his hand to the people. When a great silence fell, he addressed them in the Hebrew language, saying:

ACTS 22

22:1 “Men, brothers and fathers, hear the defense I now make before you.”
22:2 When they heard that he addressed them in the Hebrew language, they became still quieter. He said:
22:3 “I am a Jewish man, born in Tarsus of Cilicia, but brought up in this city at the feet of Gamaliel, educated in the strict observance of the ancestral law, zealous for God as all of you are today.
22:4 I persecuted this Way to the point of death, binding and handing over to prisons both men and women,
22:5 as the high priest and the whole council of elders can testify for me. From them I also received letters to the brothers, and I went toward Damascus to bring those there bound to Jerusalem for punishment.
22:6 As I was traveling and drawing near Damascus, about noon, a brilliant light from heaven suddenly flashed around me.
22:7 I fell to the ground and heard a voice saying to me, ‘Saul, Saul, why are you persecuting Me?’
22:8 I answered, ‘Who art Thou, Lord?’ He said to me, ‘I am Jesus the Nazarene, whom you are persecuting.’
22:9 Those with me saw the light, but they did not hear the voice of the One speaking to me.
22:10 I said, ‘What should I do, Lord?’ The Lord said to me, ‘Rise and go into Damascus, and there you will be told about everything appointed for you to do.’
22:11 Since I could not see because of the brightness of that light, I was led by the hand by those with me and came into Damascus.
22:12 A certain Ananias, a man devout according to the law and well spoken of by all the Jews living there,
22:13 came to me, stood beside me, and said, ‘Brother Saul, regain your sight.’ At that very hour I regained my sight and looked at him.
22:14 He said, ‘The God of our fathers appointed you to know His will, to see the Righteous One, and to hear a voice from His mouth,
22:15 for you will be His witness to all people of what you have seen and heard.
22:16 Now why do you delay? Rise, be baptized, and wash away your sins, calling upon His name.’
22:17 After I returned to Jerusalem, while I was praying in the temple, I fell into a trance
22:18 and saw Him saying to me, ‘Hurry, and go out quickly from Jerusalem, because they will not accept your testimony about Me.’
22:19 I said, ‘Lord, they themselves know that I used to imprison and beat in the synagogues those who believe in Thee.
22:20 And when the blood of Stephen, Thy witness, was being poured out, I myself stood by, approving and guarding the garments of those who were killing him.’
22:21 He said to me, ‘Go, for I will send you far away to the Gentiles.’”
22:22 They listened to him until this statement, then raised their voice and said, “Remove such a man from the earth, for he ought not to live.”
22:23 As they shouted, flung their garments, and threw dust into the air,
22:24 the tribune ordered him brought into the barracks and directed that he be examined by scourging, so that he might learn why they were shouting against him in this way.
22:25 As they stretched him out with the straps, Paul said to the centurion standing nearby, “Is it lawful for you to scourge a Roman citizen who has not been condemned?”
22:26 When the centurion heard this, he went to the tribune and reported it, saying, “What are you about to do? This man is a Roman.”
22:27 The tribune came and said to him, “Tell me, are you a Roman?” He said, “Yes.”
22:28 The tribune answered, “I acquired this citizenship for a large sum.” Paul said, “But I was born with it.”
22:29 Those about to examine him immediately withdrew. The tribune too was afraid when he learned that Paul was a Roman and that he had bound him.
22:30 The next day, wishing to learn with certainty what accusation the Jews were bringing against him, he unbound him and ordered the chief priests and the whole council to assemble. He brought Paul down and set him before them.

ACTS 23

23:1 Paul looked intently at the council and said, “Men, brothers, I have conducted my life before God with an entirely good conscience to this day.”
23:2 The high priest Ananias ordered those standing beside him to strike his mouth.
23:3 Then Paul said to him, “God is going to strike you, whitewashed wall! You sit judging me according to the law, yet in violation of the law you order me struck?”
23:4 Those standing nearby said, “Are you insulting God’s high priest?”
23:5 Paul said, “Brothers, I did not know that he was high priest, for it is written, ‘You shall not speak evil of a ruler of your people.’”
23:6 When Paul perceived that one part consisted of Sadducees and the other of Pharisees, he cried out in the council, “Men, brothers, I am a Pharisee, a son of Pharisees. I am being judged concerning the hope and resurrection of the dead.”
23:7 As he said this, dissension arose between the Pharisees and Sadducees, and the multitude was divided.
23:8 For the Sadducees say there is no resurrection, nor angel nor spirit, but the Pharisees acknowledge both.
23:9 A great outcry arose, and some scribes of the Pharisees’ party stood up and argued fiercely, saying, “We find nothing wrong in this man. But what if a spirit or an angel has spoken to him?”
23:10 As the dispute intensified, the tribune feared Paul would be torn apart by them. He ordered the troops to go down, snatch him from their midst, and bring him into the barracks.
23:11 The following night the Lord stood beside him and said, “Take courage, for as you have borne solemn witness about Me in Jerusalem, so you must also testify in Rome.”
23:12 When day came, the Jews formed a conspiracy and bound themselves under a curse, saying they would neither eat nor drink until they had killed Paul.
23:13 More than forty had made this conspiracy.
23:14 They went to the chief priests and elders and said, “We have bound ourselves under a solemn curse to taste nothing until we have killed Paul.
23:15 Now, therefore, you and the council should inform the tribune to bring him down to you, as though you intended to determine his case more accurately. We are ready to kill him before he comes near.”
23:16 Paul’s sister’s son heard about the ambush, came and entered the barracks, and reported it to Paul.
23:17 Paul called one of the centurions and said, “Take this young man to the tribune, for he has something to report to him.”
23:18 So he took him and brought him to the tribune, saying, “The prisoner Paul called me and asked me to bring this young man to you, since he has something to tell you.”
23:19 The tribune took him by the hand, withdrew privately, and asked, “What is it that you have to report to me?”
23:20 He said, “The Jews have agreed to ask you to bring Paul down to the council tomorrow, as though it intended to inquire more accurately about him.
23:21 But do not be persuaded by them, for more than forty men among them are lying in wait for him. They have bound themselves under a curse neither to eat nor to drink until they kill him, and now they are ready, awaiting your agreement.”
23:22 The tribune dismissed the young man, ordering him, “Tell no one that you have disclosed these things to me.”
23:23 He called two of the centurions and said, “Prepare two hundred soldiers to go as far as Caesarea, with seventy horsemen and two hundred spearmen, leaving at the third hour of the night.”
23:24 He also ordered mounts to be provided so that they could put Paul on them and bring him safely to Felix the governor.
23:25 He wrote a letter in this form:
23:26 “Claudius Lysias to the most excellent governor Felix: greetings.
23:27 This man was seized by the Jews and was about to be killed by them when I came with the troops and rescued him, having learned that he was a Roman.
23:28 Wishing to learn the reason they accused him, I brought him down to their council.
23:29 I found him accused over questions of their law, but under no charge deserving death or imprisonment.
23:30 When a plot against the man was disclosed to me, I immediately sent him to you, also ordering his accusers to state their case against him before you.”
23:31 So the soldiers, according to their orders, took Paul and brought him by night to Antipatris.
23:32 The next day they left the horsemen to go on with him and returned to the barracks.
23:33 When the horsemen entered Caesarea, they delivered the letter to the governor and also presented Paul before him.
23:34 He read it, asked what province Paul was from, and learned that he was from Cilicia.
23:35 He said, “I will hear you fully when your accusers also arrive,” and ordered him kept under guard in Herod’s praetorium.

ACTS 24

24:1 After five days the high priest Ananias came down with some elders and an advocate named Tertullus. They presented their case against Paul to the governor.
24:2 When Paul was called, Tertullus began to accuse him, saying, “Through you we enjoy much peace, and reforms are being made for this nation through your foresight.
24:3 In every way and everywhere we acknowledge this, most excellent Felix, with all gratitude.
24:4 But so that I may not detain you further, I ask you in your kindness to hear us briefly.
24:5 For we have found this man to be a plague, stirring up disturbances among all the Jews throughout the inhabited world, and a ringleader of the party of the Nazarenes.
24:6 He even tried to profane the temple, and we seized him.
24:8 By examining him yourself, you will be able to learn about all these things of which we accuse him.”
24:9 The Jews also joined in the attack, asserting that these things were so.
24:10 When the governor motioned for him to speak, Paul answered, “Knowing that you have been a judge of this nation for many years, I cheerfully make my defense concerning myself.
24:11 You can confirm that it is no more than twelve days since I went up to Jerusalem to worship.
24:12 They did not find me disputing with anyone or gathering a crowd, whether in the temple, in the synagogues, or in the city.
24:13 Nor can they present proof to you of the things they now accuse me of.
24:14 But I confess this to you: according to the Way, which they call a sect, I worship the God of our fathers, believing everything in accordance with the law and everything written in the prophets,
24:15 having hope in God—which these people themselves also await—that there will be a resurrection of both the righteous and the unrighteous.
24:16 For this reason I myself strive always to have a blameless conscience toward God and human beings.
24:17 After several years I came to bring alms to my nation and offerings.
24:18 While I was engaged in these, they found me purified in the temple, with neither a crowd nor an uproar.
24:19 But there were some Jews from Asia who ought to be present before you and bring charges, if they had anything against me.
24:20 Or let these men themselves say what wrongdoing they found when I stood before the council,
24:21 except for this one declaration I cried out while standing among them: ‘It is concerning the resurrection of the dead that I am being judged before you today.’”
24:22 Felix, who had more accurate knowledge of the Way, adjourned their case, saying, “When Lysias the tribune comes down, I will decide your case.”
24:23 He ordered the centurion to keep Paul in custody but grant him some freedom, and not prevent any of his own people from attending to him.
24:24 After some days Felix came with his own wife Drusilla, who was Jewish. He summoned Paul and heard him concerning faith in Christ Jesus.
24:25 As Paul reasoned about righteousness, self-control, and the coming judgment, Felix became afraid and answered, “Go away for now. When I find an opportunity, I will summon you.”
24:26 At the same time he hoped that Paul would give him money. Therefore he summoned him more frequently and conversed with him.
24:27 When two years were completed, Felix was succeeded by Porcius Festus. Wishing to gain favor with the Jews, Felix left Paul bound.

ACTS 25

25:1 Three days after arriving in the province, Festus went up from Caesarea to Jerusalem.
25:2 The chief priests and the leading men of the Jews presented their case against Paul to him and urged him,
25:3 asking as a favor against Paul that he summon him to Jerusalem, while planning an ambush to kill him along the road.
25:4 Festus answered that Paul was being kept at Caesarea, and that he himself would soon be going there.
25:5 He said, “Let the influential men among you go down with me, and if there is anything wrong in the man, let them accuse him.”
25:6 After staying among them no more than eight or ten days, he went down to Caesarea. The next day he sat on the tribunal and ordered Paul to be brought in.
25:7 When he arrived, the Jews who had come down from Jerusalem stood around him, bringing many serious accusations that they could not prove.
25:8 Paul made his defense: “I have committed no offense against the law of the Jews, against the temple, or against Caesar.”
25:9 But Festus, wishing to gain favor with the Jews, answered Paul, “Are you willing to go up to Jerusalem and be judged there before me concerning these things?”
25:10 Paul said, “I am standing before Caesar’s tribunal, where I ought to be judged. I have done no wrong to the Jews, as you yourself know very well.
25:11 If, then, I am doing wrong and have done anything deserving death, I do not refuse to die. But if there is nothing to their accusations against me, no one can hand me over to them as a favor. I appeal to Caesar.”
25:12 Then Festus conferred with his council and answered, “You have appealed to Caesar; to Caesar you will go.”
25:13 After some days had passed, King Agrippa and Bernice arrived in Caesarea to greet Festus.
25:14 While they stayed there for several days, Festus laid Paul’s case before the king, saying, “There is a man left as a prisoner by Felix.
25:15 When I was in Jerusalem, the chief priests and elders of the Jews presented their case against him, asking for his condemnation.
25:16 I answered them that it is not Roman custom to hand anyone over as a favor before the accused has met his accusers face to face and received an opportunity to defend himself concerning the charge.
25:17 So when they gathered here, I made no delay, but on the next day sat on the tribunal and ordered the man brought in.
25:18 When his accusers stood up, they brought no charge of the evil things I had expected.
25:19 Instead, they had certain disputes with him concerning their own religion and a certain Jesus who had died, whom Paul asserted to be alive.
25:20 Uncertain how to investigate these matters, I asked whether he wished to go to Jerusalem and be judged there concerning them.
25:21 But when Paul appealed to be kept for Augustus’s decision, I ordered him held until I could send him to Caesar.”
25:22 Agrippa said to Festus, “I would like to hear the man myself.” He said, “Tomorrow you will hear him.”
25:23 The next day Agrippa and Bernice came with great pomp and entered the audience hall with the tribunes and the city’s prominent men. At Festus’s order, Paul was brought in.
25:24 Festus said, “King Agrippa and all you men present with us, you see this man concerning whom the whole multitude of the Jews petitioned me, both in Jerusalem and here, shouting that he ought not to live any longer.
25:25 I found that he had done nothing deserving death, but since he himself appealed to Augustus, I decided to send him.
25:26 I have nothing certain to write to the sovereign about him. Therefore I have brought him before you all, and especially before you, King Agrippa, so that after the examination I may have something to write.
25:27 For it seems unreasonable to me to send a prisoner without also indicating the charges against him.”

ACTS 26

26:1 Agrippa said to Paul, “You are permitted to speak for yourself.” Then Paul stretched out his hand and made his defense:
26:2 “Concerning everything of which I am accused by the Jews, King Agrippa, I consider myself fortunate to make my defense before you today,
26:3 especially since you are familiar with all the Jewish customs and disputes. Therefore I ask you to hear me patiently.
26:4 All the Jews know my manner of life from my youth, lived from the beginning among my nation and in Jerusalem.
26:5 They have known me from the first, if they are willing to testify, that I lived as a Pharisee according to the strictest party of our religion.
26:6 Now I stand on trial because of hope in the promise made by God to our fathers,
26:7 which our twelve tribes, earnestly worshiping night and day, hope to attain. It is concerning this hope that I am accused by Jews, O king.
26:8 Why do you consider it unbelievable that God raises the dead?
26:9 I myself thought that I must do many things against the name of Jesus the Nazarene.
26:10 This I did in Jerusalem: I shut up many of the saints in prisons, having received authority from the chief priests, and when they were being killed, I cast my vote against them.
26:11 I punished them often in all the synagogues and tried to compel them to blaspheme. In my intense rage against them, I pursued them even to foreign cities.
26:12 In this pursuit I was traveling to Damascus with authority and commission from the chief priests,
26:13 when at midday, O king, I saw along the road a light from heaven, brighter than the sun, shining around me and those traveling with me.
26:14 When we had all fallen to the ground, I heard a voice speaking to me in the Hebrew language: ‘Saul, Saul, why are you persecuting Me? It is hard for you to kick against goads.’
26:15 I said, ‘Who art Thou, Lord?’ The Lord said, ‘I am Jesus, whom you are persecuting.
26:16 But rise and stand on your feet, for I have appeared to you for this purpose: to appoint you a servant and witness of the things in which you have seen Me and those in which I will appear to you,
26:17 rescuing you from the people and from the Gentiles, to whom I am sending you,
26:18 to open their eyes, so that they may turn from darkness to light and from Satan’s authority to God, so that they may receive forgiveness of sins and an allotted share among those sanctified by faith in Me.’
26:19 Therefore, King Agrippa, I was not disobedient to the heavenly vision,
26:20 but proclaimed first to those in Damascus and Jerusalem, throughout all the country of Judea, and to the Gentiles that they should repent and turn to God, doing works worthy of repentance.
26:21 For these reasons the Jews seized me in the temple and tried to kill me.
26:22 Having therefore received help from God, I stand to this day, testifying to small and great, saying nothing beyond what the prophets and Moses said would happen:
26:23 that the Christ would suffer and, as the first to rise from the dead, would proclaim light both to the people and to the Gentiles.”
26:24 As he was making this defense, Festus said with a loud voice, “You are mad, Paul! Your great learning is turning you to madness.”
26:25 Paul said, “I am not mad, most excellent Festus, but I speak words of truth and sound judgment.
26:26 For the king knows about these things, and I speak freely to him. I am persuaded that none of these things has escaped his notice, for this has not been done in a corner.
26:27 King Agrippa, do you believe the prophets? I know that you believe.”
26:28 Agrippa said to Paul, “With so little, you are trying to persuade me to become a Christian.”
26:29 Paul said, “Whether with little or with much, I would pray to God that not only you but also all who hear me today might become as I am, except for these bonds.”
26:30 The king rose, along with the governor, Bernice, and those seated with them.
26:31 After withdrawing, they spoke to one another, saying, “This man is doing nothing deserving death or imprisonment.”
26:32 Agrippa said to Festus, “This man could have been released if he had not appealed to Caesar.”

ACTS 27

27:1 When it was decided that we should sail to Italy, they handed Paul and some other prisoners over to a centurion named Julius, of the Augustan cohort.
27:2 We boarded a ship from Adramyttium that was about to sail to the ports along Asia, and set sail. Aristarchus, a Macedonian from Thessalonica, was with us.
27:3 The next day we put in at Sidon. Julius treated Paul kindly and allowed him to go to his friends and receive care.
27:4 From there we set sail and sailed under the shelter of Cyprus because the winds were contrary.
27:5 We crossed the open sea off Cilicia and Pamphylia and came to Myra in Lycia.
27:6 There the centurion found an Alexandrian ship sailing to Italy and put us aboard it.
27:7 We sailed slowly for many days and with difficulty came off Cnidus. Since the wind would not permit us to proceed, we sailed under the shelter of Crete off Salmone.
27:8 Coasting along it with difficulty, we came to a place called Fair Havens, near which was the city of Lasea.
27:9 Much time had passed, and sailing was already dangerous because even the Fast had now gone by. Paul advised them,
27:10 saying, “Men, I perceive that the voyage will bring damage and great loss, not only of the cargo and the ship but also of our lives.”
27:11 But the centurion was more persuaded by the pilot and the shipowner than by what Paul said.
27:12 Since the harbor was unsuitable for wintering, the majority decided to sail from there, hoping somehow to reach Phoenix, a harbor of Crete facing southwest and northwest, and winter there.
27:13 When a south wind blew gently, they supposed they had gained their purpose, weighed anchor, and sailed close along Crete.
27:14 But not long afterward a hurricane wind called Euraquilo rushed down from the island.
27:15 The ship was seized and could not face the wind, so we gave way and were driven along.
27:16 Running under the shelter of a small island called Cauda, we were barely able to secure the boat.
27:17 After hoisting it aboard, they used supports to hold the ship together from beneath. Fearing they would run aground on the Syrtis, they lowered the gear and were driven along in this way.
27:18 As we were violently battered by the storm, the next day they began throwing cargo overboard.
27:19 On the third day they threw the ship’s tackle overboard with their own hands.
27:20 When neither sun nor stars appeared for many days, and a fierce storm kept battering us, all remaining hope of our being saved was taken away.
27:21 After a long time without food, Paul stood among them and said, “Men, you ought to have listened to me and not sailed from Crete, and so avoided this damage and loss.
27:22 Now I urge you to take courage, for there will be no loss of life among you, but only of the ship.
27:23 For this night an angel of the God to whom I belong and whom I worship stood beside me,
27:24 saying, ‘Do not fear, Paul. You must stand before Caesar, and look, God has granted you all who sail with you.’
27:25 Therefore take courage, men, for I believe God that it will be just as I have been told.
27:26 But we must run aground on some island.”
27:27 When the fourteenth night came, as we were being driven across the Adria, about midnight the sailors suspected that some land was approaching them.
27:28 They took a sounding and found twenty fathoms. After going a little farther, they sounded again and found fifteen fathoms.
27:29 Fearing we might run aground somewhere on rocky places, they dropped four anchors from the stern and prayed for day to come.
27:30 The sailors sought to flee from the ship and lowered the boat into the sea, pretending that they were about to lay out anchors from the bow.
27:31 Paul said to the centurion and the soldiers, “Unless these men remain in the ship, you cannot be saved.”
27:32 Then the soldiers cut the boat’s ropes and let it fall away.
27:33 As day was about to come, Paul urged everyone to take food, saying, “Today is the fourteenth day you have continued waiting without food, taking nothing.
27:34 Therefore I urge you to take food, for this is for your safety. Not a hair from the head of any of you will be lost.”
27:35 After saying these things, he took bread, gave thanks to God before everyone, broke it, and began to eat.
27:36 They all took courage and themselves took food.
27:37 There were two hundred and seventy-six persons in all aboard the ship.
27:38 When they had eaten enough, they lightened the ship by throwing the grain into the sea.
27:39 When day came, they did not recognize the land, but noticed a bay with a beach and planned, if they could, to drive the ship onto it.
27:40 They cast off the anchors and left them in the sea, while also loosening the lashings of the steering oars. They raised the foresail to the wind and headed for the beach.
27:41 But they struck a place between two seas and ran the ship aground. The bow stuck fast and remained immovable, while the stern was breaking apart under the force of the waves.
27:42 The soldiers planned to kill the prisoners so that none might swim away and escape.
27:43 But the centurion, wishing to save Paul, prevented them from carrying out their plan. He ordered those able to swim to jump overboard first and make for land,
27:44 and the rest to follow, some on planks and some on pieces from the ship. In this way everyone reached land safely.

ACTS 28

28:1 After reaching safety, we learned that the island was called Melite.
28:2 The people who did not speak Greek showed us extraordinary kindness. They kindled a fire and welcomed us all because of the rain that had begun and because of the cold.
28:3 Paul gathered a bundle of brushwood and put it on the fire. A viper came out because of the heat and fastened onto his hand.
28:4 When the people who did not speak Greek saw the creature hanging from his hand, they said to one another, “This man is certainly a murderer. Although he escaped from the sea, Justice has not allowed him to live.”
28:5 But he shook the creature into the fire and suffered no harm.
28:6 They expected him to swell up or suddenly fall down dead. After waiting a long time and seeing nothing unusual happen to him, they changed their minds and said that he was a god.
28:7 Near that place were lands belonging to the island’s chief man, named Publius. He welcomed us and hospitably gave us lodging for three days.
28:8 Publius’s father lay sick with fevers and dysentery. Paul went in to him, prayed, laid his hands upon him, and healed him.
28:9 After this happened, the other sick people on the island also came and were healed.
28:10 They honored us with many honors, and when we sailed, they supplied what we needed.
28:11 After three months we sailed in an Alexandrian ship that had wintered at the island, with the Dioscuri as its emblem.
28:12 We put in at Syracuse and stayed three days.
28:13 From there we cast off and reached Rhegium. After one day a south wind arose, and on the second day we came to Puteoli.
28:14 There we found brothers and were invited to stay with them seven days. And so we came to Rome.
28:15 From there the brothers, having heard about us, came to meet us as far as the Forum of Appius and Three Taverns. When Paul saw them, he thanked God and took courage.
28:16 When we entered Rome, Paul was permitted to stay by himself with the soldier guarding him.
28:17 After three days he called together the leading Jews. When they gathered, he said to them, “Men, brothers, although I had done nothing against the people or the ancestral customs, I was handed over as a prisoner from Jerusalem into Roman hands.
28:18 After examining me, they wished to release me, because there was no cause for death in my case.
28:19 But when the Jews objected, I was compelled to appeal to Caesar, though I had no accusation to bring against my nation.
28:20 For this reason I asked to see you and speak with you, for it is because of Israel’s hope that I wear this chain.”
28:21 They said to him, “We have received no letters from Judea about you, nor has any of the brothers who arrived reported or spoken anything evil about you.
28:22 But we consider it right to hear from you what you think, for concerning this sect we know that it is opposed everywhere.”
28:23 They appointed a day with him and came to his lodging in greater numbers. From morning until evening he explained to them, bearing solemn witness to the kingdom of God and seeking to persuade them concerning Jesus from both the law of Moses and the prophets.
28:24 Some were persuaded by what was said, while others did not believe.
28:25 Disagreeing with one another, they began to leave after Paul made one statement: “The Holy Spirit spoke well through Isaiah the prophet to your fathers,
28:26 saying, ‘Go to this people and say: “You will hear and hear, yet never understand; you will look and look, yet never see.
28:27 For this people’s heart has grown dull, their ears have become hard of hearing, and they have closed their eyes, lest they see with their eyes and hear with their ears and understand with their heart and turn back, and I would heal them.”’
28:28 Therefore let it be known to you that this salvation of God has been sent to the Gentiles; they will listen.”
28:30 He remained two whole years in his own rented lodging and welcomed all who came to him,
28:31 proclaiming the kingdom of God and teaching the things concerning the Lord Jesus Christ with all boldness, unhindered.
