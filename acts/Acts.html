Acts 1

Read this chapter on staging

The Promise of the Holy Spirit

1:1In my first account, Theophilus, I wrote about all that Jesus began both to do and to teach

1:2until the day when, after giving instructions through the Holy Spirit to the apostles whom He had chosen, He was taken up.

1:3To them He also presented Himself alive after His suffering, with many proofs, appearing to them over forty days and speaking about the kingdom of God.

1:4While gathered with them, He ordered them not to leave Jerusalem, but to wait for the Father’s promise, “which,” He said, “you heard from Me;

1:5for John baptized with water, but you will be baptized in the Holy Spirit not many days from now.”

The Ascension

1:6So those who had come together asked Him, “Lord, is this the time when Thou art restoring the kingdom to Israel?”

1:7He said to them, “It is not for you to know times or seasons that the Father has set by His own authority.

1:8But you will receive power when the Holy Spirit comes upon you, and you will be My witnesses in Jerusalem and [in] all Judea and Samaria, and to the end of the earth.”

1:9After saying these things, He was lifted up as they watched, and a cloud took Him from their sight.

1:10As they were gazing into heaven while He went, look—two men stood beside them in white garments.

1:11They said, “Men of Galilee, why do you stand looking into heaven? This Jesus, who was taken up from you into heaven, will come in the same way as you saw Him go into heaven.”

The Upper Room in Jerusalem

1:12Then they returned to Jerusalem from the mountain called Olivet, which is near Jerusalem, a Sabbath’s journey away.

1:13When they entered, they went up to the upper room where they were staying: Peter and John and James and Andrew, Philip and Thomas, Bartholomew and Matthew, James son of Alphaeus, Simon the Zealot, and Judas son of James.

1:14All these devoted themselves with one accord to prayer, together with women, Mary the mother of Jesus, and His brothers.

The Place of Judas

1:15In those days Peter stood up among the brothers and said—about a hundred and twenty people were gathered together—

1:16“Men, brothers, the Scripture had to be fulfilled, which the Holy Spirit spoke beforehand through David’s mouth concerning Judas, who became a guide to those who arrested Jesus;

1:17for he was counted among us and received his allotted share in this ministry.”

1:18Now this man acquired a field from the wages of wrongdoing, and falling headlong, he burst open in the middle, and all his entrails poured out.

1:19This became known to all who lived in Jerusalem, so that the field was called in their own language Hakeldamach—that is, Field of Blood.

1:20“For it is written in the book of Psalms: ‘Let his dwelling become deserted, and let there be no one living in it,’ and, ‘Let another take his office of oversight.’

1:21Therefore, of the men who accompanied us throughout the time when the Lord Jesus went in and out among us,

1:22beginning with John’s baptism until the day when He was taken up from us, one of these must become with us a witness of His resurrection.”

Matthias Chosen

1:23They put forward two: Joseph called Barsabbas, who was also named Justus, and Matthias.

1:24They prayed and said, “Thou, Lord, who knowest everyone’s heart, show which one of these two Thou hast chosen

1:25to take the place in this ministry and apostleship from which Judas turned aside to go to his own place.”

1:26They cast lots for them, and the lot fell on Matthias, and he was counted with the eleven apostles.

Acts 2

Read this chapter on staging

The Day of Pentecost

2:1When the day of Pentecost arrived, they were all together in the same place.

2:2Suddenly a sound came from heaven like a violent wind rushing along, and it filled the whole house where they were sitting.

2:3Tongues like fire appeared to them, separating and settling on each one of them.

2:4They were all filled with the Holy Spirit and began to speak in other tongues, as the Spirit gave them utterance.

The Crowd Hears in Their Own Languages

2:5Now there were Jews living in Jerusalem, devout men from every nation under heaven.

2:6When this sound occurred, the crowd came together and was bewildered, because each one heard them speaking in his own language.

2:7They were astonished and amazed, saying, “Look, are not all these people who are speaking Galileans?

2:8How is it, then, that each of us hears in our own native language?

2:9Parthians, Medes, and Elamites; those living in Mesopotamia, Judea and Cappadocia, Pontus and Asia,

2:10Phrygia and Pamphylia, Egypt and the parts of Libya around Cyrene; visitors from Rome,

2:11both Jews and converts; Cretans and Arabs—we hear them speaking in our own tongues about the mighty deeds of God.”

2:12They were all astonished and perplexed, saying to one another, “What does this mean?”

2:13But others mocked them and said, “They are full of sweet wine.”

Peter’s Address at Pentecost

2:14Peter stood with the eleven, raised his voice, and addressed them: “Men of Judea and all who live in Jerusalem, let this be known to you, and listen closely to my words.

2:15These people are not drunk, as you suppose, for it is the third hour of the day.

2:16Rather, this is what was spoken through the prophet Joel:

2:17‘In the last days, God says, I will pour out of My Spirit upon all flesh. Your sons and your daughters will prophesy; your young men will see visions, and your elders will dream dreams.

2:18Even upon My male slaves and My female slaves in those days I will pour out of My Spirit, and they will prophesy.

2:19I will give wonders in heaven above and signs on the earth below: blood and fire and a cloud of smoke.

2:20The sun will be changed into darkness and the moon into blood before the great and manifest day of the Lord comes.

2:21And everyone who calls upon the name of the Lord will be saved.’

Jesus Raised Up by God

2:22Men of Israel, hear these words: Jesus the Nazarene was a man attested to you by God through mighty works, wonders, and signs that God did through Him in your midst, as you yourselves know.

2:23This man, handed over according to God’s determined purpose and foreknowledge, you nailed up and killed through the hands of lawless men.

2:24God raised Him, releasing Him from the pangs of death, because it was impossible for Him to be held by it.

2:25For David says concerning Him, ‘I kept the Lord before me always, for He is at my right hand so that I will not be shaken.

2:26Therefore my heart rejoiced and my tongue exulted; moreover, my flesh will dwell in hope,

2:27because Thou wilt not abandon my soul to Hades, nor allow Thy Holy One to see decay.

2:28Thou hast made known to me the paths of life; Thou wilt fill me with gladness in Thy presence.’

David Foresaw the Resurrection

2:29Men, brothers, I may speak openly to you about the patriarch David: he both died and was buried, and his tomb is among us to this day.

2:30Since he was a prophet and knew that God had sworn an oath to him to seat one from the fruit of his loins upon his throne,

2:31he foresaw and spoke about the resurrection of the Christ: He was neither abandoned to Hades, nor did His flesh see decay.

2:32God raised this Jesus, and all of us are witnesses of it.

2:33Therefore, exalted to God’s right hand and having received from the Father the promised Holy Spirit, He has poured out what you both see and hear.

2:34For David did not ascend into the heavens, but he himself says, ‘The Lord said to my Lord, “Sit at My right hand

2:35until I make Thy enemies a footstool for Thy feet.”’

2:36Therefore let the whole house of Israel know with certainty that God has made Him both Lord and Christ—this Jesus whom you crucified.”

Three Thousand Baptized

2:37When they heard this, they were pierced to the heart and said to Peter and the other apostles, “Men, brothers, what should we do?”

2:38Peter said to them, “Repent, and let each one of you be baptized upon the name of Jesus Christ for the forgiveness of your sins, and you will receive the gift of the Holy Spirit.

2:39For the promise is for you, for your children, and for all who are far away—as many as the Lord our God calls to Himself.”

2:40With many other words he bore solemn witness and urged them, saying, “Be saved from this crooked generation.”

2:41So those who welcomed his word were baptized, and about three thousand persons were added that day.

They Devoted Themselves to the Teaching

2:42They devoted themselves to the apostles’ teaching and to fellowship, to the breaking of bread and to the prayers.

2:43Awe came upon everyone, and many wonders and signs took place through the apostles.

2:44All who believed were together and held everything in common.

2:45They would sell their properties and possessions and distribute the proceeds to all, as anyone had need.

2:46Day by day they devoted themselves with one accord to gathering in the temple, and breaking bread in their homes, they shared food with gladness and simplicity of heart,

2:47praising God and enjoying favor with all the people. And day by day the Lord added those who were being saved to their gathering.

Acts 3

Read this chapter on staging

The Lame Man at the Beautiful Gate

3:1Peter and John were going up to the temple at the ninth hour, the hour of prayer.

3:2A man unable to walk from his mother’s womb was being carried there. They used to place him each day at the temple gate called Beautiful, to ask for alms from those entering the temple.

3:3When he saw Peter and John about to enter the temple, he asked to receive alms.

3:4Peter, together with John, looked intently at him and said, “Look at us.”

3:5He paid attention to them, expecting to receive something from them.

3:6But Peter said, “Silver and gold I do not have, but what I have I give you: in the name of Jesus Christ the Nazarene, get up and walk.”

3:7Taking him by the right hand, he raised him up. At once his feet and ankles were strengthened.

3:8Leaping up, he stood and began walking. He entered the temple with them, walking and leaping and praising God.

3:9All the people saw him walking and praising God.

3:10They recognized him as the one who used to sit for alms at the temple’s Beautiful Gate, and they were filled with wonder and astonishment at what had happened to him.

Peter Speaks in Solomon’s Portico

3:11As he held on to Peter and John, all the people ran together to them at the portico called Solomon’s, utterly astonished.

3:12When Peter saw this, he addressed the people: “Men of Israel, why do you marvel at this, or why do you stare at us as though we had made him walk by our own power or godliness?

3:13The God of Abraham and the God of Isaac and the God of Jacob, the God of our fathers, has glorified His Servant Jesus, whom you handed over and denied before Pilate, when he had decided to release Him.

3:14But you denied the Holy and Righteous One and asked for a murderer to be granted to you.

3:15You killed the Author of life, whom God raised from the dead; we are witnesses of this.

3:16Through faith in His name, His name has strengthened this man whom you see and know; and the faith that is through Him has given him this complete health before all of you.

Repent and Turn Back

3:17And now, brothers, I know that you acted in ignorance, as did your rulers.

3:18But what God announced beforehand through the mouth of all the prophets—that His Christ would suffer—He has fulfilled in this way.

3:19Repent, therefore, and turn back, so that your sins may be wiped away,

3:20so that times of refreshing may come from the presence of the Lord, and He may send the Christ appointed for you, Jesus,

3:21whom heaven must receive until the times of restoration of all things of which God spoke through the mouth of His holy prophets from long ago.

Moses Said, the Lord God Will Raise Up a Prophet

3:22Moses said, ‘The Lord your God will raise up for you a prophet like me from among your brothers. You shall listen to him in everything he says to you.

3:23Every person who does not listen to that prophet will be destroyed from among the people.’

3:24And all the prophets, from Samuel and those who followed—as many as spoke—also announced these days.

3:25You are sons of the prophets and of the covenant God made with your fathers, saying to Abraham, ‘In your seed all the families of the earth will be blessed.’

3:26God raised up His Servant and sent Him first to you, to bless you by turning each of you away from your wicked deeds.”

Acts 4

Read this chapter on staging

Peter and John Arrested

4:1While they were speaking to the people, the priests, the captain of the temple, and the Sadducees came upon them,

4:2greatly troubled because they were teaching the people and proclaiming in Jesus the resurrection from the dead.

4:3They laid hands on them and placed them in custody until the next day, for it was already evening.

4:4But many of those who heard the word believed, and the number of the men came to about five thousand.

Before the Rulers and Elders

4:5The next day their rulers, elders, and scribes gathered in Jerusalem,

4:6together with Annas the high priest, Caiaphas, John, Alexander, and all who belonged to the high-priestly family.

4:7They made them stand in their midst and asked, “By what power or by what name did you do this?”

No Other Name

4:8Then Peter, filled with the Holy Spirit, said to them, “Rulers of the people and elders,

4:9if we are being examined today concerning a good deed done to a disabled man, and by what means he has been made well,

4:10let it be known to all of you and to all the people of Israel that in the name of Jesus Christ the Nazarene, whom you crucified, whom God raised from the dead—in Him this man stands before you in good health.

4:11He is the stone despised by you, the builders, which has become the cornerstone.

4:12Salvation is in no one else, for there is no other name under heaven given among human beings by which we must be saved.”

Forbidden to Speak in His Name

4:13When they observed Peter’s and John’s boldness and realized that they were unlettered, ordinary men, they were amazed. They also recognized that they had been with Jesus.

4:14Seeing the man who had been healed standing with them, they had nothing to say against it.

4:15They ordered them to leave the council and conferred with one another,

4:16saying, “What should we do with these men? That a notable sign has taken place through them is evident to everyone living in Jerusalem, and we cannot deny it.

4:17But so that it spreads no further among the people, let us warn them with threats not to speak to anyone in this name again.”

4:18They called them in and ordered them neither to speak nor to teach at all in the name of Jesus.

4:19Peter and John answered them, “Judge whether it is right before God to listen to you rather than to God;

4:20for we cannot keep from speaking about what we have seen and heard.”

4:21After further threats, they released them, finding no way to punish them because of the people, since everyone was glorifying God for what had happened.

4:22For the man in whom this sign of healing had occurred was more than forty years old.

The Prayer for Boldness

4:23After their release, they went to their own people and reported all that the chief priests and elders had said to them.

4:24On hearing it, they lifted their voice together to God and said, “Master, Thou art the One who made heaven and earth and the sea and everything in them,

4:25who through the Holy Spirit, by the mouth of our father David, Thy servant, said, ‘Why did nations rage and peoples devise empty things?

4:26The kings of the earth took their stand, and the rulers gathered together against the Lord and against His Christ.’

4:27For truly, in this city, both Herod and Pontius Pilate, with the Gentiles and the peoples of Israel, gathered against Thy holy Servant Jesus, whom Thou didst anoint,

4:28to do whatever Thy hand and Thy purpose had determined beforehand should happen.

4:29And now, Lord, look upon their threats, and grant Thy servants to speak Thy word with all boldness,

4:30while Thou stretchest out Thy hand for healing, and signs and wonders take place through the name of Thy holy Servant Jesus.”

4:31When they had prayed, the place where they were gathered was shaken. They were all filled with the Holy Spirit and spoke God’s word with boldness.

All Things in Common

4:32The multitude of those who believed was of one heart and soul. Not one of them claimed that anything he possessed was his own, but they held everything in common.

4:33With great power the apostles gave their testimony to the resurrection of the Lord Jesus, and great grace was upon them all.

4:34For there was not a needy person among them: all who owned lands or houses would sell them and bring the proceeds of what was sold,

4:35laying them at the apostles’ feet; distribution was made to each as anyone had need.

Barnabas Sells a Field

4:36Joseph, whom the apostles called Barnabas—which means Son of Encouragement—a Levite and a native of Cyprus,

4:37sold a field he owned, brought the money, and laid it at the apostles’ feet.

The Voyage to Rome Begins

27:1When it was decided that we should sail to Italy, they handed Paul and some other prisoners over to a centurion named Julius, of the Augustan cohort.

27:2We boarded a ship from Adramyttium that was about to sail to the ports along Asia, and set sail. Aristarchus, a Macedonian from Thessalonica, was with us.

27:3The next day we put in at Sidon. Julius treated Paul kindly and allowed him to go to his friends and receive care.

27:4From there we set sail and sailed under the shelter of Cyprus because the winds were contrary.

27:5We crossed the open sea off Cilicia and Pamphylia and came to Myra in Lycia.

27:6There the centurion found an Alexandrian ship sailing to Italy and put us aboard it.

27:7We sailed slowly for many days and with difficulty came off Cnidus. Since the wind would not permit us to proceed, we sailed under the shelter of Crete off Salmone.

27:8Coasting along it with difficulty, we came to a place called Fair Havens, near which was the city of Lasea.

Much Time Had Passed, and Sailing Was Dangerous

27:9Much time had passed, and sailing was already dangerous because even the Fast had now gone by. Paul advised them,

27:10saying, “Men, I perceive that the voyage will bring damage and great loss, not only of the cargo and the ship but also of our lives.”

27:11But the centurion was more persuaded by the pilot and the shipowner than by what Paul said.

27:12Since the harbor was unsuitable for wintering, the majority decided to sail from there, hoping somehow to reach Phoenix, a harbor of Crete facing southwest and northwest, and winter there.

The Storm at Sea

27:13When a south wind blew gently, they supposed they had gained their purpose, weighed anchor, and sailed close along Crete.

27:14But not long afterward a hurricane wind called Euraquilo rushed down from the island.

27:15The ship was seized and could not face the wind, so we gave way and were driven along.

27:16Running under the shelter of a small island called Cauda, we were barely able to secure the boat.

27:17After hoisting it aboard, they used supports to hold the ship together from beneath. Fearing they would run aground on the Syrtis, they lowered the gear and were driven along in this way.

27:18As we were violently battered by the storm, the next day they began throwing cargo overboard.

27:19On the third day they threw the ship’s tackle overboard with their own hands.

27:20When neither sun nor stars appeared for many days, and a fierce storm kept battering us, all remaining hope of our being saved was taken away.

Paul Encourages the Ship’s Company

27:21After a long time without food, Paul stood among them and said, “Men, you ought to have listened to me and not sailed from Crete, and so avoided this damage and loss.

27:22Now I urge you to take courage, for there will be no loss of life among you, but only of the ship.

27:23For this night an angel of the God to whom I belong and whom I worship stood beside me,

27:24saying, ‘Do not fear, Paul. You must stand before Caesar, and look, God has granted you all who sail with you.’

27:25Therefore take courage, men, for I believe God that it will be just as I have been told.

27:26But we must run aground on some island.”

The Fourteenth Night

27:27When the fourteenth night came, as we were being driven across the Adria, about midnight the sailors suspected that some land was approaching them.

27:28They took a sounding and found twenty fathoms. After going a little farther, they sounded again and found fifteen fathoms.

27:29Fearing we might run aground somewhere on rocky places, they dropped four anchors from the stern and prayed for day to come.

27:30The sailors sought to flee from the ship and lowered the boat into the sea, pretending that they were about to lay out anchors from the bow.

27:31Paul said to the centurion and the soldiers, “Unless these men remain in the ship, you cannot be saved.”

27:32Then the soldiers cut the boat’s ropes and let it fall away.

Paul Urges Them to Eat

27:33As day was about to come, Paul urged everyone to take food, saying, “Today is the fourteenth day you have continued waiting without food, taking nothing.

27:34Therefore I urge you to take food, for this is for your safety. Not a hair from the head of any of you will be lost.”

27:35After saying these things, he took bread, gave thanks to God before everyone, broke it, and began to eat.

27:36They all took courage and themselves took food.

27:37There were two hundred and seventy-six persons in all aboard the ship.

27:38When they had eaten enough, they lightened the ship by throwing the grain into the sea.

The Shipwreck

27:39When day came, they did not recognize the land, but noticed a bay with a beach and planned, if they could, to drive the ship onto it.

27:40They cast off the anchors and left them in the sea, while also loosening the lashings of the steering oars. They raised the foresail to the wind and headed for the beach.

27:41But they struck a place between two seas and ran the ship aground. The bow stuck fast and remained immovable, while the stern was breaking apart under the force of the waves.

27:42The soldiers planned to kill the prisoners so that none might swim away and escape.

27:43But the centurion, wishing to save Paul, prevented them from carrying out their plan. He ordered those able to swim to jump overboard first and make for land,

27:44and the rest to follow, some on planks and some on pieces from the ship. In this way everyone reached land safely.

Malta

28:1After reaching safety, we learned that the island was called Melite.

28:2The people who did not speak Greek showed us extraordinary kindness. They kindled a fire and welcomed us all because of the rain that had begun and because of the cold.

28:3Paul gathered a bundle of brushwood and put it on the fire. A viper came out because of the heat and fastened onto his hand.

28:4When the people who did not speak Greek saw the creature hanging from his hand, they said to one another, “This man is certainly a murderer. Although he escaped from the sea, Justice has not allowed him to live.”

28:5But he shook the creature into the fire and suffered no harm.

28:6They expected him to swell up or suddenly fall down dead. After waiting a long time and seeing nothing unusual happen to him, they changed their minds and said that he was a god.

Near That Place Were Lands Belonging to Publius

28:7Near that place were lands belonging to the island’s chief man, named Publius. He welcomed us and hospitably gave us lodging for three days.

28:8Publius’s father lay sick with fevers and dysentery. Paul went in to him, prayed, laid his hands upon him, and healed him.

28:9After this happened, the other sick people on the island also came and were healed.

28:10They honored us with many honors, and when we sailed, they supplied what we needed.

Arrival at Rome

28:11After three months we sailed in an Alexandrian ship that had wintered at the island, with the Dioscuri as its emblem.

28:12We put in at Syracuse and stayed three days.

28:13From there we cast off and reached Rhegium. After one day a south wind arose, and on the second day we came to Puteoli.

28:14There we found brothers and were invited to stay with them seven days. And so we came to Rome.

28:15From there the brothers, having heard about us, came to meet us as far as the Forum of Appius and Three Taverns. When Paul saw them, he thanked God and took courage.

28:16When we entered Rome, Paul was permitted to stay by himself with the soldier guarding him.

Paul and the Jewish Leaders in Rome

28:17After three days he called together the leading Jews. When they gathered, he said to them, “Men, brothers, although I had done nothing against the people or the ancestral customs, I was handed over as a prisoner from Jerusalem into Roman hands.

28:19But when the Jews objected, I was compelled to appeal to Caesar, though I had no accusation to bring against my nation.

28:21They said to him, “We have received no letters from Judea about you, nor has any of the brothers who arrived reported or spoken anything evil about you.

28:22But we consider it right to hear from you what you think, for concerning this sect we know that it is opposed everywhere.”

Paul Testifies to the Kingdom of God

28:23They appointed a day with him and came to his lodging in greater numbers. From morning until evening he explained to them, bearing solemn witness to the kingdom of God and seeking to persuade them concerning Jesus from both the law of Moses and the prophets.

28:24Some were persuaded by what was said, while others did not believe.

28:25Disagreeing with one another, they began to leave after Paul made one statement: “The Holy Spirit spoke well through Isaiah the prophet to your fathers,

28:26saying, ‘Go to this people and say: “You will hear and hear, yet never understand; you will look and look, yet never see.

28:27For this people’s heart has grown dull, their ears have become hard of hearing, and they have closed their eyes, lest they see with their eyes and hear with their ears and understand with their heart and turn back, and I would heal them.”’

28:28Therefore let it be known to you that this salvation of God has been sent to the Gentiles; they will listen.”

Two Years in Rome

28:30He remained two whole years in his own rented lodging and welcomed all who came to him,

28:31proclaiming the kingdom of God and teaching the things concerning the Lord Jesus Christ with all boldness, unhindered.
