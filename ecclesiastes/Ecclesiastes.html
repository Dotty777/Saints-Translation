Ecclesiastes 1

Read this chapter on staging

The Words of Ecclesiastes

1:1 The words of Ecclesiastes, son of David, king of Israel in Jerusalem.

1:2 Futility of futilities, said Ecclesiastes; futility of futilities, everything is futility.

1:3What surplus has a person in all his toil at which he toils beneath the sun?

A Generation Goes and a Generation Comes

1:4A generation goes, and a generation comes, but the earth stands for ever.

1:5The sun rises, and the sun sets, and draws toward its place.

1:6Rising there, it goes toward the south and circles toward the north. Round and round the wind goes, and on its circuits the wind returns.

1:7 All the streams go to the sea, yet the sea will not be filled. To the place where the streams go, there they return to go again.

1:8 All words are wearisome; a man will not be able to speak. The eye will not be filled with seeing, nor the ear filled with hearing.

1:9What has come to be is what will come to be, and what has been done is what will be done; there is nothing fresh beneath the sun.

1:10If anyone speaks and says, “Behold, this is new,” it has already come to be in the ages that were before us.

1:11 There is no remembrance of the first ones, nor will the last ones who have come to be have remembrance among those who will come at the end.

I Set My Heart to Seek Out by Wisdom

1:12 I, Ecclesiastes, became king over Israel in Jerusalem.

1:13 I set my heart to seek out and examine by wisdom everything that happens beneath heaven, for God gave human beings a grievous distraction to be distracted by.

1:14 I saw all the works done beneath the sun, and behold, everything is futility and resolve of spirit.

1:15 What is twisted cannot be put in order, and what is lacking cannot be counted.

1:16 I spoke in my heart, saying: Behold, I became great and added wisdom beyond everyone who was in Jerusalem before me, and my heart saw much—wisdom and knowledge.

1:17 I set my heart to know wisdom and knowledge; I came to know comparisons and understanding, for this too is resolve of spirit.

1:18For in much wisdom is much knowledge, and whoever increases knowledge will increase pain.

Saints English wording notes

1:2 · futility

Meaning. Emptiness or lack of lasting gain in the book's opening judgment.

Wording. Retain the emphatic repetition. Futility gives a clear modern connection; vanity is permitted only with its older emptiness sense explained. Neither word should make personal conceit the book's governing theme.

English comparison. All three historical witnesses use vanity of vanities. The Saints proposal chooses futility while retaining the repeated formula and the statement about all things.

1:3 · surplus

Meaning. Gain or advantage remaining from the described toil.

Wording. Keep the question and the repeated toil language. Surplus expresses what remains beyond expenditure; profit is a valid connection but should not restrict the inquiry to business accounts.

English comparison. KJV has profit, Brenton advantage, and DRC what hath a man more. These illuminate the gain sense of the Saints wording.

1:9 · fresh

Meaning. Something newly occurring within the poem's recurring pattern.

Wording. Fresh preserves the Greek adjective; new is equally intelligible in this context. Retain the repeated relation of what has happened to what will happen without expanding the line into a scientific claim.

English comparison. The historical witnesses use new. DRC places the final no-new-thing clause at 1:10, so its 1:9 and 1:10 are shown separately within the comparison.

Ecclesiastes 2

Read this chapter on staging

I Will Test You with Gladness

2:1I said in my heart: Come now, I will test you with gladness; experience good. But behold, this too was futility.

2:2Of laughter I said, “Delirium!” And to gladness, “What are you doing?”

2:3 I examined in my heart how to draw my flesh toward wine—while my heart guided me with wisdom—and how to take hold of folly, until I might see what good human beings should do beneath the sun during the numbered days of their lives.

Great Works, Wealth, and Pleasure Tested

2:4 I enlarged my work; I built houses for myself and planted vineyards for myself,

2:5 I made gardens and parks for myself and planted in them every kind of fruit tree.

2:6 I made pools of water for myself to irrigate from them a woodland sprouting trees.

2:7 I acquired male and female slaves, and I had slaves born in my house. I also had a great possession of herds and flocks, beyond everyone who was before me in Jerusalem.

2:8 I also gathered silver and gold for myself, and the riches of kings and regions. I provided male and female singers for myself, and human delights—a male cupbearer and female cupbearers.

2:9 I grew great and surpassed everyone who was before me in Jerusalem; my wisdom also remained with me.

2:10Whatever my eyes asked for, I did not withhold from them. I did not keep my heart from any gladness, for my heart rejoiced in all my toil, and this was my portion from all my toil.

2:11 Then I looked upon all my works that my hands had done and the toil with which I had labored to do them, and behold, everything was futility and resolve of spirit, with no surplus beneath the sun.

The Wise and the Fool Share One Fate

2:12 Then I looked to consider wisdom, delirium and folly. For who is the person who will come after the counsel—all the things with which he made it?

2:13I saw that wisdom has an advantage over folly, like the advantage light has over darkness.

2:14The wise person’s eyes are in his head, but the fool walks in darkness. Yet I too knew that one event will happen to them all.

2:15I said in my heart: The fool’s fate will happen to me too. Why have I been wise? Then I spoke excessively in my heart, because a fool speaks from excess, for this too is futility.

2:16 For there is no enduring remembrance of the wise person along with the fool; in the coming days everything will already be forgotten. How will the wise person die along with the fool?

2:17 I hated life, because the work done beneath the sun was grievous to me, for everything is futility and resolve of spirit.

Toil Left to the One Who Comes After

2:18I hated all my toil with which I toil beneath the sun, because I leave it to the person who comes after me.

2:19Who knows whether he will be wise or foolish? Yet he has authority over all the toil with which I labored and acted wisely beneath the sun. This too is futility.

2:20 I turned to bid my heart farewell concerning all the toil with which I had labored beneath the sun,

2:21 for a person may toil with wisdom, knowledge and courage, yet will give his portion to someone who did not toil for it. This too is futility and a great evil.

2:22For what comes to a person from all his toil and the resolve of his heart with which he toils beneath the sun?

2:23For all his days are pain, and his distraction is anger; even at night his heart does not sleep. This too is futility.

To Eat and Drink and See Good

2:24There is no good in a person. What he will eat and drink and let his soul see as good in his toil—I saw that this too is from the hand of God.

2:25 For who will eat, and who will abstain, apart from Him?

2:26For to the person who is good before Him He gave wisdom, knowledge and gladness; but to the sinner He gave the distraction of adding and gathering, to give to the one who is good before God. This too is futility and resolve of spirit.

Saints English wording notes

2:24 · good

Meaning. Good denied in a difficult clause preceding eating and drinking.

Wording. Keep the difficult opening provisionally and preserve the following clauses as a separate syntax problem. The Greek adjective is not morphologically comparative; do not silently supply better than to resolve it.

English comparison. KJV supplies nothing better than; DRC frames a question about what is better; Brenton connects the negation to what a man has to eat. These are competing construals, not identical English styles.

Ecclesiastes 3

Read this chapter on staging

A Time for Every Matter Beneath Heaven

3:1 For all things there is a time, and a season for every matter beneath heaven.

3:2 a time to give birth and a time to die, a time to plant and a time to uproot what is planted,

3:3 a time to kill and a time to heal, a time to pull down and a time to build,

3:4 a time to weep and a time to laugh, a time to beat the breast in mourning and a time to dance,

3:5 a time to throw stones and a time to gather stones, a time to embrace and a time to keep far from embracing,

3:6 a time to seek and a time to lose, a time to keep and a time to throw away,

3:7 a time to tear and a time to sew, a time to be silent and a time to speak,

3:8 a time to love and a time to hate, a time of war and a time of peace.

He Has Made Everything Beautiful in Its Time

3:9What surplus does the worker gain from the things for which he toils?

3:10I saw the distraction God has given human beings to be distracted by.

3:11He has made everything beautiful in its time. He has also put the age in their hearts, so that human beings may not find out the work God has done from beginning to end.

3:12 I knew that there is no good for them except to rejoice and do good during one’s life.

3:13Moreover, whenever a person eats and drinks and sees good in all his toil, this is God’s gift.

3:14I knew that everything God has done will endure for ever. Nothing can be added to it, and nothing taken from it; God has done this so that people may fear before Him.

3:15What has come to be already is, and whatever is to come has already come to be; God will seek the one who is pursued.

In the Place of Judgment

3:16 Again I saw beneath the sun: in the place of judgment, the ungodly person was there; in the righteous person’s place, the ungodly person was there.

3:17 I said in my heart: God will judge the righteous and the ungodly, for there is a time for every matter and for every work.

3:18 There I said in my heart concerning human beings’ speech: God will distinguish them and show that they themselves are beasts, even to themselves.

3:19For the fate of human beings and the fate of the beast is one fate for them: as one dies, so the other dies, and all have one breath. What advantage has a human being over the beast? None, for everything is futility.

3:20 All go to one place; all came from dust, and all return to dust.

3:21Who knows whether the spirit of human beings goes upward, and whether the spirit of the beast goes down into the earth?

3:22 I saw that there is no good except for a person to rejoice in his works, for that is his portion. For who will bring him to see what may happen after him?

Saints English wording notes

3:1 · season

Meaning. Time and a fitting occasion within the poem's sequence.

Wording. Prefer a time for everything and a season for every matter, preserving the paired nouns. Keep the sequence's acts distinct; the existence of a season does not itself command each act.

English comparison. Saints and KJV put season first; Brenton puts time first. DRC paraphrases the second clause. The proposal keeps the Greek order without requiring identical English words for these nouns everywhere.

3:11 · age

Meaning. A span or totality of time in a difficult statement about human understanding.

Wording. Retain age with eternity and world recorded as interpretive alternatives. Preserve the limitation on finding out God's work; no one English gloss resolves the whole verse's syntax and scope.

English comparison. KJV, Brenton, and DRC use world, with different accounts of its placement or consideration. Saints exposes the temporal reading. A claim that the source unambiguously says eternity would overstate this review.

3:19 · breath

Meaning. Life-breath shared by human beings and animals in the mortality comparison.

Wording. Use breath in this life-and-death setting and retain the common fate described. Spirit remains a lexical alternative elsewhere; this passage alone does not settle every question about human and animal destiny.

English comparison. Saints, KJV, and Brenton say one breath; DRC states that all breathe alike. The Greek's repeated one and its rhetorical question should remain audible.

Ecclesiastes 4

Read this chapter on staging

The Extortions Beneath the Sun

4:1 Again I turned and saw all the extortions committed beneath the sun. Behold, the tears of those being extorted, with no one to comfort them! Power was in their extorters’ hands, and they had no one to comfort them.

4:2 I praised the dead, who had already died, above the living, who are alive until now.

4:3Better than these two is the one who has not yet come to be, who has not seen the evil work done beneath the sun.

Rivalry and the Handful of Rest

4:4 I saw all toil and all courage in work: it is a person’s rivalry with his companion. This too is futility and resolve of spirit.

4:5 The fool folded his hands and ate his own flesh.

4:6 Better a handful of rest than two handfuls of toil and resolve of spirit.

4:7 Again I turned and saw futility beneath the sun.

4:8 There is one person with no second, with neither son nor brother. There is no end to all his toil, and his eye is not filled with wealth. For whom am I toiling and depriving my soul of goodness? This too is futility and a grievous distraction.

Two Are Better Than One

4:9 Two are better than one, because they have a good reward for their toil.

4:10For if they fall, one will lift his partner. But woe to the one who falls with no second person to lift him!

4:11 Also, if two lie down, they have warmth; but how can one be warm alone?

4:12If the one gains strength, the two will stand against him; a threefold cord is not quickly torn apart.

The Poor and Wise Youth

4:13Better a poor and wise youth than an older and foolish king who no longer knows how to pay attention,

4:14 for he will come from the prisoners’ house to reign, though he was born poor in his kingdom.

4:15 I saw all the living who walk beneath the sun with the second young man who will stand in his place,

4:16 there was no end to all the people, all who came before them; yet those who come last will not rejoice in him. This too is futility and resolve of spirit.

Guard Your Foot When You Go to God's House

4:17Guard your foot when you go to God’s house, and be near to listen. Your sacrifice is better than the fools’ gift, for they do not know how to do evil.

Saints English wording notes

4:12 · threefold

Meaning. A cord consisting of three strands in the strength comparison.

Wording. Retain the material image and not quickly broken. Explain the three strands plainly; applications to marriage or the Trinity belong to attributed reception, not to the noun phrase.

English comparison. All witnesses preserve the threefold cord. DRC has not easily rather than not quickly. The preceding one-and-two clause has its own difficult relations and should not be silently rewritten.

Ecclesiastes 5

Read this chapter on staging

Do Not Be Hasty with Your Mouth

5:1Do not be hasty with your mouth, and do not let your heart hurry to bring out a word before God, for God is in heaven and you are on earth. Therefore let your words be few.

5:2For a dream comes with much distraction, and a fool’s voice with many words.

5:3Whenever you vow a vow to God, do not delay paying it, for there is no delight in fools. Pay whatever you vow.

5:4Better that you do not vow than that you vow and do not pay.

5:5Do not let your mouth cause your flesh to sin, and do not say before God, “It was ignorance,” lest God become angry at your voice and destroy the works of your hands.

5:6 For with many dreams come futilities and many words; therefore fear God.

Extortion of the Poor

5:7If you see extortion of the poor and robbery of judgment and righteousness in a region, do not be astonished at the matter, for a high official is above another to keep watch, and higher ones are above them.

5:8 The land’s surplus is in everything—a king of a cultivated field.

Riches That Do Not Satisfy

5:9Whoever loves silver will not be filled with silver; and who has loved a yield in their abundance? This too is futility.

5:10When goodness increased, those who consumed it multiplied. What strength has its possessor except to look at it with his eyes?

5:11Sweet is the slave’s sleep, whether he eats little or much; but being filled with riches does not let him sleep.

5:12 There is a sickness I have seen beneath the sun: wealth kept by its possessor to his harm,

5:13 and that wealth will be lost through a grievous distraction, and he fathered a son with nothing in his hand.

5:14 As he came naked from his mother’s womb, he will return to depart as he came. He will take nothing from his toil to carry in his hand.

5:15This too is a grievous sickness: just as he came, so he will depart. What surplus has he from toiling for the wind?

5:16 All his days are in darkness and mourning, with much anger, sickness and wrath.

What I Have Seen to Be Good and Fitting

5:17Behold, what I have seen to be good and fitting is to eat and drink and see goodness in all one’s toil beneath the sun during the numbered days of life God has given, for that is one’s portion.

5:18Moreover, every person to whom God has given wealth and possessions, and whom He has empowered to eat from them, take his portion and rejoice in his toil—this is God’s gift.

5:19For he will not often remember the days of his life, because God distracts him with the gladness of his heart.

Saints English wording notes

5:3 · vow

Meaning. Make the solemn promise whose fulfilment the verse requires.

Wording. Preserve the cognate repetition and the command to fulfil what was vowed. Paying a vow need not mean transferring money; the vow's content governs what fulfilment entails.

English comparison. Greek, Saints, Brenton, and DRC 5:3 align with KJV 5:4. The verse numbering must be mapped before comparison.

Ecclesiastes 6

Read this chapter on staging

An Evil Great upon Human Beings

6:1 There is an evil I have seen beneath the sun, and it is great upon human beings:

6:2a person to whom God will give wealth, possessions and glory, so that his soul lacks nothing it desires, yet God will not empower him to eat from it, for a stranger will eat it. This is futility and a grievous sickness.

6:3If a man fathers a hundred children and lives many years, however numerous the days of his years, but his soul is not filled with goodness and he has no burial, I said a stillborn child is better off than he,

6:4for it came in futility and goes in darkness, and in darkness its name will be covered,

6:5and though it has not seen the sun or known anything, it has more rest than he.

6:6 Even if he lived through courses of a thousand years but saw no goodness, do not all go to one place?

All a Person's Toil Is for His Mouth

6:7 All a person’s toil is for his mouth, yet his appetite will not be filled.

6:8For what advantage has the wise person over the fool? For the poor person knows how to walk before life.

6:9 Better a sight for the eyes than wandering in appetite. This too is futility and resolve of spirit.

6:10Whatever has come to be has already been named, and it is known what a human being is; he will not be able to contend in judgment with one stronger than himself.

6:11For many words increase futility; what advantage has a person?

6:12For who knows what is good for a person in life, during the numbered days of his life of futility? He has spent them in a shadow. Who will tell a person what will be after him beneath the sun?

Saints English wording notes

Wording. Singular address uses thou, thee, thy, and thine; plural address uses ye, you, and your. Source-sensitive images, inherited numbering, and uncertainties are retained.

Ecclesiastes 7

Read this chapter on staging

The Day of Death and the House of Mourning

7:1 A name is better than good oil, and the day of death than the day of one’s birth.

7:2 Better to go to a house of mourning than to a house of drinking, for this is the end of every human being, and the living will take it to heart.

7:3 Anger is better than laughter, for through a troubled face the heart will become better.

7:4 The heart of the wise is in the house of mourning, but the heart of fools is in the house of gladness.

7:5 Better to hear a wise person’s rebuke than to be someone listening to the song of fools,

7:6 for like the sound of thorns beneath a cauldron, so is the laughter of fools. This too is futility.

7:7For extortion drives a wise person astray and destroys the heart of his vigor.

The End of Words Is Better Than Their Beginning

7:8 The end of words is better than their beginning; a patient person is better than one high in spirit.

7:9Do not be hasty in your spirit to become angry, for anger will rest in the bosom of fools.

7:10Do not say, “What happened that the former days were better than these?” For it was not in wisdom that you asked about this.

7:11 Wisdom is good with an inheritance, and an advantage to those who behold the sun.

7:12 For wisdom in its shade is like the shade of silver, and the advantage of the knowledge of wisdom will give life to its possessor.

Consider God's Works

7:13Consider God’s works, for who can put in order the one whom God has twisted?

7:14On a day of goodness, live in good; on a day of evil, consider: God has made this in accord with that concerning speech, so that human beings may find out nothing that comes after them.

Do Not Become Overly Righteous

Read this chapter on staging

One Event Comes to All

9:1For I set all this upon my heart, and my heart saw all this: the righteous, the wise and their works are in God’s hand. A person knows neither love nor hatred; everything lies before them.

9:7Come, eat your bread with gladness and drink your wine with a good heart, for God has already favored your deeds.

9:8Let your garments be white at all times, and let oil not be lacking on your head.

9:9Enjoy life with the woman whom you have loved, all the days of your futile life that have been given you under the sun, all your futile days; for this is your share in life and in your toil at which you toil under the sun.

9:10Whatever your hand finds to do, do it with your strength, for there is no deed, reckoning, knowledge or wisdom in Hades, where you are going.

The Race Is Not to the Swift

9:11Again I saw under the sun that the race is not to the swift, nor battle to the mighty, nor bread to the wise, nor wealth to those with understanding, nor favor to those with knowledge; for time and encounter will happen to them all.

9:13 This too I saw as wisdom under the sun, and it seemed great to me:

9:16 I said, Wisdom is better than strength; yet the poor man’s wisdom is despised, and his words are not heard.

9:17 The words of the wise, heard quietly, are better than the shouting of rulers in follies.
