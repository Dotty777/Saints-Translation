Judges 1

Read this chapter on staging

Judah Goes Up First

1:1After Joshua’s death, the sons of Israel inquired of the Lord, saying, “Who shall go up for us against the Canaanites, to lead the battle against them?”

1:2The Lord said, “Judah shall go up. Behold, I have given the land into his hand.”

1:3And Judah said to Simeon his brother, “Go up with me into my allotted territory, and let us fight against the Canaanites; and I also will go with you into your allotted territory.” And Simeon went with him.

1:4Judah went up, and the Lord gave the Canaanites and the Perizzites into his hand. He struck them at Bezek: ten thousand men.

1:5They found Adoni-bezek at Bezek, fought against him, and struck the Canaanites and the Perizzites.

1:6Adoni-bezek fled, but they pursued him, seized him, and cut off the extremities of his hands and his feet.

1:7Adoni-bezek said, “Seventy kings, with the extremities of their hands and feet cut off, used to gather what was beneath my table. As I have done, so God has repaid me.” They brought him to Jerusalem, and he died there.

1:8The sons of Judah fought against Jerusalem and captured it. They struck it with the edge of the sword and burned the city with fire.

Hebron, Debir, and Achsah’s Request

1:9After these things the sons of Judah went down to fight against the Canaanites dwelling in the hill country, the south, and the plain.

1:10Judah went against the Canaanites dwelling in Hebron, and Hebron came out against him. Hebron’s former name was Kariatharboksepher. He struck Sheshai, Ahiman, and Talmai, descendants of Anak.

1:11From there they went against the inhabitants of Debir. Debir’s former name was City of Letters.

1:12And Caleb said, “Whoever strikes the City of Letters and takes possession of it, I will give him Achsah my daughter as wife.”

1:13Othniel son of Kenaz, Caleb’s younger brother, took possession of it, and Caleb gave him Achsah his daughter as wife.

1:14And when she entered, he urged her to ask her father for the field. She murmured on the beast of burden and cried out from it, “You have given me away into a southern land.” And Caleb said to her, “What do you desire?”

1:15And Achsah said to him, “Give me a blessing; for you have given me away into a southern land. Give me a redemption of water.” And Caleb gave her, according to her heart, the redemption of the heights and the redemption of the low places. †

The Cities Judah Could Not Take

1:16The descendants of Jobab the Kenite, Moses’ father-in-law, went up from the City of Palms to the sons of Judah, into the wilderness in the south, on the descent to Arad. He went and dwelt with the people.

1:17Judah went with Simeon his brother. They struck the Canaanites dwelling in Sepheth, placed it under the ban, and destroyed it. They called the city’s name Destruction.

1:18Judah did not possess Gaza and its territory, Ashkelon and its territory, Ekron and its territory, or Ashdod and its surrounding lands.

1:19The Lord was with Judah, and he inherited the mountain, because he could not inherit the inhabitants of the valley, because Rechab had separated it. †

1:20He gave Hebron to Caleb, as Moses had said, and Caleb inherited the three cities from there and removed from there the three sons of Anak.

1:21The sons of Benjamin did not remove the Jebusites dwelling in Jerusalem. The Jebusites dwelt with the sons of Benjamin to this day.

The House of Joseph Takes Bethel

1:22The sons of Joseph also went up to Bethel, and Judah was with them.

1:23The house of Israel camped against Bethel. The city’s former name was Luz.

1:24The watchers saw a man coming out of the city, seized him, and said to him, “Show us the way into the city, and we will show you mercy.”

1:25He showed them the way into the city. They struck the city with the edge of the sword, but let the man and his kin go.

1:26The man went to the land of the Hittites, built a city there, and called its name Luz. That is its name to this day.

The Tribes That Did Not Drive Out the Canaanites

1:27Manasseh did not possess Beth-shan, which is the City of the Scythians, or its dependent towns or surrounding lands; nor Ekthanaad and its dependent towns, the inhabitants of Dor and its dependent towns, the inhabitants of Balaam and its dependent towns, the inhabitants of Magedon and its dependent towns, or the inhabitants of Jeblaam and its dependent towns. The Canaanites began to dwell in this land.

1:28When Israel became strong, it subjected the Canaanites to tribute, but did not completely remove them.

1:29Ephraim did not remove the Canaanites dwelling in Gezer. The Canaanites dwelt among them in Gezer and became subject to tribute.

1:30Zebulun did not remove the inhabitants of Kedron or the inhabitants of Enaala. The Canaanites dwelt among them and became subject to tribute.

1:31Asher did not remove the inhabitants of Acco, and they became subject to his tribute; nor the inhabitants of Dor, the inhabitants of Sidon, the inhabitants of Aalaph, Achazib, Helba, Aphek, or Roob.

1:32Asher dwelt among the Canaanites inhabiting the land, because he was unable to remove them.

1:33Naphtali did not remove the inhabitants of Beth-shemesh or the inhabitants of Beth-anath. Israel dwelt among the Canaanites inhabiting the land, but the inhabitants of Beth-shemesh and Beth-anath became subject to their tribute.

1:34The Amorites pressed the sons of Dan into the mountain, for they did not allow them to come down into the valley.

1:35The Amorites began to dwell on the Mountain of the Myrtle Grove, where the bears and the foxes are. The hand of the house of Joseph became heavy upon the Amorites, and they became subject to tribute.

1:36The Amorite boundary—the Idumean—was above Akrabin, at the Rock and above.

Judges 2

Read this chapter on staging

The Messenger at the Place of Weeping

2:1The messenger of the Lord went up from Gilgal to the Place of Weeping, to Bethel, and to the house of Israel. He said to them, “The Lord, the Lord brought you up from Egypt and brought you into the land that He swore to your fathers to give you. He said to you, ‘I will never scatter My covenant with you.

2:2You shall make no covenant with those dwelling in this land, nor shall you bow down to their gods; but you shall shatter their graven images and pull down their altars.’ Yet you did not obey My voice when you did these things.

2:3And I said, ‘I will no longer remove the people whom I said I would destroy before you. They shall become constraints upon you, and their gods shall become a stumbling block to you.’”

2:4When the messenger of the Lord spoke these words to all Israel, the people raised their voice and wept.

2:5For this reason that place was called the Place of Weeping, and there they sacrificed to the Lord.

The Death of Joshua and His Generation

2:6Joshua sent the people away, and the sons of Israel went, each to his house and his inheritance, to inherit the land.

2:7The people served the Lord all the days of Joshua and all the days of the elders who outlived Joshua, who knew all the great work the Lord had done for Israel.

2:8Joshua son of Nun, the servant of the Lord, died at the age of one hundred ten years.

2:9They buried him within the territory of his inheritance at Timnath-heres, in the mountain of Ephraim, north of Mount Gaash.

2:10That whole generation too was added to its fathers. Another generation arose after them, who did not know the Lord or the work He had done for Israel.

Israel Serves the Baalim

2:11The sons of Israel did evil before the Lord and served the Baalim.

2:12They forsook the Lord, the God of their fathers, who had brought them out of the land of Egypt. They followed other gods from among the gods of the peoples around them, bowed down to them, and provoked the Lord.

2:13They forsook the Lord and served Baal and the Astartes.

2:14The Lord was greatly angered against Israel. He delivered them into the hand of plunderers, who plundered them; and He sold them into the hand of their enemies all around, so they could not stand before their enemies.

2:15In everything in which they prostituted themselves, the Lord’s hand was against them for harm, as the Lord had spoken and as the Lord had sworn. He greatly afflicted them.

The Lord Raises Up Judges

2:16The Lord raised up judges for them and saved them from the hand of their plunderers.

2:17Yet they did not listen even to their judges, for they prostituted themselves after other gods, bowed down to them, and provoked the Lord. They quickly turned aside from the way their fathers had walked in obeying the Lord’s commandments. They did not do as their fathers had done.

2:18Whenever the Lord raised up judges for them, the Lord was with the judge and saved them from the hand of their enemies all the days of the judge. For the Lord was moved by their groaning before those who besieged and harmed them.

2:19When the judge died, they turned back and again acted more corruptly than their fathers, following other gods to serve them and bow down to them. They did not cast away their practices or depart from their hard way.

The Nations Left Behind

2:20And the Lord was greatly angered against Israel, and said, “Because this nation has forsaken My covenant which I commanded their fathers, and has not obeyed My voice,

2:21I too will no longer remove before them anyone from the nations that Joshua left and let remain.”

2:22This was to test Israel through them: would they keep the way of the Lord and walk in it as their fathers had kept it, or not?

2:23The Lord left these nations, not removing them quickly, and did not give them into Joshua’s hand.

Judges 3

The Nations That Remained

3:1These are the nations Joshua left to test Israel through them—all those who had not known any of the wars of Canaan—

3:2only for the sake of the generations of the sons of Israel, to teach them warfare; only those before them had not known these things:

3:3the five satrapies of the foreigners, all the Canaanites, the Sidonians, and the Hivites dwelling in Lebanon, from Mount Balaermon to Loboemath.

3:4This was to test Israel through them, to know whether they would obey the Lord’s commandments that He commanded their fathers through Moses.

3:5The sons of Israel dwelt among the Canaanites, Hittites, Amorites, Perizzites, Hivites, and Jebusites.

3:6They took their daughters as wives for themselves, gave their own daughters to their sons, and served their gods.

Othniel Delivers Israel

3:7The sons of Israel did evil before the Lord, forgot the Lord their God, and served the Baalim and the groves.

3:8And the Lord was greatly angered against Israel, and sold them into the hands of Chousarsathom king of Syria of the Rivers; and they served him eight years.

3:9The sons of Israel cried out to the Lord. The Lord raised up a saviour for Israel and saved them: Othniel son of Kenaz, Caleb’s younger brother. And he heard him.

3:10The Spirit of the Lord came upon him. He judged Israel and went out to war. The Lord gave Chousarsathom king of Syria into his hand, and his hand became strong against Chousarsathom.

3:11The land was quiet for fifty years. Then Othniel son of Kenaz died.

Eglon of Moab Oppresses Israel

3:12The sons of Israel again did evil before the Lord. The Lord strengthened Eglon king of Moab against Israel because they had done evil before the Lord.

3:13He brought all the sons of Ammon and Amalek to himself, went and struck Israel, and took possession of the City of Palms.

3:14The sons of Israel served Eglon king of Moab for eighteen years.

Ehud and the Double-Edged Dagger

3:15The sons of Israel cried out to the Lord. The Lord raised up a saviour for them: Ehud son of Gera, son of Jemeni, a man skilled with both hands. Through him the sons of Israel sent gifts to Eglon king of Moab.

3:16Ehud made himself a double-edged dagger, a span long, and strapped it beneath his cloak upon his right thigh.

3:17He presented the gifts to Eglon king of Moab. Eglon was a very handsome man.

3:18When Ehud finished presenting the gifts, he sent away those who had carried them.

3:19Eglon returned from the graven images near Gilgal. And Ehud said, “I have a secret word for you, O king.” And Eglon said to all, “Depart!” And all his attendants went out from him.

3:20Ehud went in to him as he sat all alone in his summer upper room, and said, “I have a word from God for you, O king.” And Eglon rose from his throne near him.

3:21As he rose, Ehud stretched out his left hand, took the dagger from his right thigh, and drove it into Eglon’s belly,

3:22and drove even the handle in after the blade, and the fat closed over the blade, because he did not pull the dagger out of his belly.

3:23Ehud went out into the vestibule, closed the upper-room doors upon him, and fastened them,

3:24and he went out. The king’s attendants entered, saw that the upper-room doors were closed, and said, “Perhaps he is sitting on the privy in the bedroom’s retreat?”

3:25They waited in embarrassment, but no one opened the upper-room doors. They took the key and opened them, and there was their lord, fallen dead on the ground.

Moab Subdued at the Fords

3:26Ehud escaped while they were in confusion, and no one was paying attention to him. He passed the carved images and escaped to Seirotha.

3:27When he arrived, he sounded a horn in the mountain of Ephraim. The sons of Israel went down with him, and he went before them.

3:28He said to them, “Come down after me, for the Lord God has given your enemies, Moab, into your hand.” They went down after him and seized Moab’s Jordan crossings. They allowed no one to cross.

3:29At that time they struck about ten thousand men of Moab, all their fighters and every man of strength. Not one escaped.

3:30Moab was put to shame that day under Israel’s hand, and the land was quiet for eighty years. Ehud judged them until he died.

Shamgar Son of Anath

3:31After him Shamgar son of Anath arose and struck as many as six hundred foreign men, apart from the calves of the oxen. He too saved Israel. †

Jabin and Sisera Oppress Israel

4:1The sons of Israel again did evil before the Lord.

4:2The Lord sold them into the hand of Jabin king of Canaan, who reigned in Hazor. His army commander was Sisera, who dwelt in Arisoth of the Nations.

4:3The sons of Israel cried out to the Lord, because he had nine hundred iron chariots, and he oppressed Israel mightily for twenty years.

Deborah Summons Barak

4:4Now Deborah, a prophetess, the wife of Lappidoth, was judging Israel at that time.

4:5She sat beneath Deborah’s palm between Ramah and Bethel in the hill country of Ephraim. The sons of Israel went up to her there to be judged.

4:6Deborah sent and called Barak son of Abinoam from Kedesh in Naphtali, and said to him, “Has not the Lord, the God of Israel, commanded you, ‘Go to Mount Tabor and take with you ten thousand men of the sons of Naphtali and of the sons of Zebulun,

4:7and I will draw Sisera, the commander of Jabin’s army, with his chariots and his multitude, to you at the Kishon torrent, and I will deliver him into your hand’?”

4:8Barak said to her, “If you go with me, I will go; but if you do not go with me, I will not go, for I do not know the day on which the Lord gives success to the messenger with me.”

4:9Deborah said to him, “I will surely go with you. Yet know that the advantage will not be yours on the way you go, for the Lord will sell Sisera into the hands of a woman.” And Deborah arose and went with Barak to Kedesh.

4:10Barak summoned Zebulun and Naphtali to Kedesh. Ten thousand men went up at his feet, and Deborah went up with him.

Sisera Draws Up at the Kishon

4:11The neighbours of the Kenite had separated from the sons of Jobab, Moses’ in-law. He pitched his tent near the Oak of the Resters, near Kedesh.

4:12They told Sisera that Barak son of Abinoam had gone up Mount Tabor.

4:13Sisera summoned all his chariots—nine hundred iron chariots—and all the people with him, from Arisoth of the Nations to the Kishon torrent.

4:14And Deborah said to Barak, “Arise! For this is the day on which the Lord has delivered Sisera into your hand. Behold, shall not the Lord go before you?” And Barak went down from Mount Tabor, and ten thousand men followed him.

4:15The Lord threw Sisera, all his chariots, and all his camp into confusion before Barak at the edge of the sword. Sisera descended from his chariot and fled on foot.

4:16Barak pursued the chariots and the camp as far as the Woodland of the Nations. All Sisera’s camp fell by the edge of the sword; not even one remained.

Jael and the Tent Peg

4:17Sisera fled on foot to the tent of Jael, the wife of Heber the Kenite, for there was peace between Jabin king of Hazor and the house of Heber the Kenite.

4:18Jael went out to meet Sisera and said to him, “Turn aside, my lord; turn aside to me. Do not be afraid.” He turned aside to her into the tent, and she covered him with her hide covering.

4:19Sisera said to her, “Please give me a little water to drink, for I am thirsty.” She opened a skin of milk, gave him a drink, and covered his face.

4:20And he said to her, “Stand at the entrance of the tent. And if anyone comes and asks you, ‘Is there a man here?’ say, ‘No.’” And she covered him with her hide covering.

4:21Jael, Heber’s wife, took a tent peg and put the mallet in her hand. She came to him quietly and drove the peg into his jaw, and it passed through into the ground. He convulsed between her knees, breathed his last, and died.

4:22And behold, Barak was pursuing Sisera. Jael went out to meet him and said, “Come, and I will show you the man you seek.” He entered with her, and there lay Sisera, dead, with the peg in his jaw.

Jabin Subdued

4:23On that day the Lord God subdued Jabin king of Canaan before the sons of Israel.

4:24The hand of the sons of Israel grew stronger and stronger against Jabin king of Canaan, until they destroyed him.

The Song of Deborah and Barak

5:1On that day Deborah and Barak son of Abinoam sang and said:

5:2“When leaders began to lead in Israel, when the people chose willingly, bless the Lord!

5:3Hear, O kings; give ear, you mighty satraps! I will sing to the Lord; I will make music to the God of Israel.

5:4O Lord, when Thou didst go forth from Seir, when Thou didst depart from the field of Edom, the earth shook; heaven was astonished, and the clouds dropped water.

5:5Mountains were shaken before the Lord—this Sinai, before the Lord, the God of Israel.

5:6In the days of Shamgar son of Anath, in the days of Jael, kings ceased, and they travelled paths; they travelled twisted roads.

5:7Phrazōn ceased in Israel; it ceased until Deborah arose, because a mother arose in Israel. †

5:8They chose new gods like barley bread. Would I see a covering of lances among forty thousand? †

5:9My heart is with the things appointed for Israel. You mighty ones of the people, bless the Lord!

5:10You who ride upon beasts of burden, you who sit in covered carriages—

5:11utter the sound of musicians among those rejoicing. There they will ascribe righteousness to the Lord. Righteous people grew strong in Israel; then the Lord’s people went down to His cities.

5:12Awake, awake, Deborah! Rouse myriads with the people! Awake, awake, speak with song! Arise in strength, Barak; and strengthen Barak, Deborah! Take your captives, son of Abinoam!

5:13When was his strength magnified? Lord, humble for me those stronger than I am.

5:14The people of Ephraim punished them in the valley of your brother Benjamin, among your peoples. From me, Machir, investigators came down; and from Zebulun the Lord fought for me among mighty ones from there, with a sceptre of one strengthening leadership. †

5:15In Issachar, with Deborah, he sent his foot soldiers into the valley. Why do you dwell between lips? He stretched out at his feet. Among the divisions of Reuben were great scrutinies of heart.

5:16Why do you sit for me between the mosphathaim, to hear the whistling of those who rouse? To pass into the territory of Reuben were great searchings of heart.

5:17Gilead camped beyond the Jordan; and Dan—why does he stay with ships? Asher stayed by the seashore and camped by its breaches.

5:18Zebulun was a people that scorned its life to the point of death, and Naphtali on the heights of the field.

5:19Kings came and drew up in battle; then Canaan’s kings fought at Taanach by Megiddo’s water. They took no greedy gain of silver.

5:20From heaven the stars fought; from their appointed order they fought against Sisera.

5:21The Kishon torrent cast them out—the torrent Kadēmim, the Kishon torrent. My mighty soul will trample them.

5:22Then the horse’s heels were cut off—Amadarōth of his mighty ones. †

5:23‘Curse Meroz,’ said the Lord’s messenger, ‘utterly curse its inhabitants, because they did not come to the Lord’s help. The Lord is our helper, mighty among warriors.’

5:24Blessed among women be Jael, the wife of Heber the Kenite; among women in a tent may she be blessed.

5:25He asked her for water, and she gave him milk. In a bowl of mighty ones she brought butter.

5:26She stretched her left hand toward a peg, her right toward the cuttings of the exhausted; she cut down Sisera, crushed his head, shattered and pierced his jaw. †

5:27Between her feet he bent down and fell; he lay between her feet. Where he bent down, there he fell, wretched.

5:28Through the window Sisera’s mother leaned out, looking through the lattice toward those returning with Sisera: ‘Why is his chariot last to arrive? Why have the tracks of his chariots been delayed?’

5:29The wise among her noble ladies answered her, and she herself answered in her own words:

5:30‘Will they not find him dividing spoils, befriending friends for the head of a mighty man? Spoils of dyed cloth for Sisera, spoils of dyed cloth, embroidery; a dyed embroidered piece around his neck as spoil.’ †

5:31So perish all Thine enemies, O Lord! And let those who love Him be as the sun going forth in its might.” And the land was quiet for forty years.

Readings requiring further review

Judges 5:7. Phrazōn. Phrazōn is an unresolved Greek form and remains transliterated. Brenton has mighty men, KJV inhabitants of the villages, and DRC the valiant. Retain the Greek third-person statement that Deborah arose, unlike KJV’s first-person I. The opaque term is a review item, not a settled English meaning. ↩ 5:7

Judges 5:8. Barley bread. Retain the difficult barley bread wording of the selected Greek text provisionally. The historical versions instead speak of new gods and war or gates. The draft must not be treated as approved at this point; importing a coherent familiar verse would conceal the textual problem rather than solve it. ↩ 5:8

Judges 5:14. God fought and the sceptre. The governing Greek includes God fought, investigators, and the sceptre. The historical versions speak differently of rulers, writers, or leading an army. Preserve the distinctive terms while leaving their syntactic relation open to review. The KJV pen of the writer is not supplied from memory. ↩ 5:14

Judges 5:22. Amadarōth. The draft preserves Amadarōth as an unresolved form and follows the Greek statement about horses’ heels being cut off. Brenton has entangled feet, KJV broken hoofs and pransings, and DRC broken hoofs as the strong ones fled. No definite meaning is assigned to the obscure form without further evidence. ↩ 5:22

Judges 5:26. The difficult right hand clause. The Greek distinguishes the left hand and then gives an obscure right-hand phrase involving cuttings and exhaustion. The historical versions provide a hammer. Judges 4:21 supplies a clear prose account, but this poem must not be silently rewritten from it. The present literal wording is provisional and requires review before publication. ↩ 5:26

Judges 5:30. Friends and the division of spoil. The selected Greek uses a difficult expression about befriending friends. Brenton also differs from the captive women in KJV and DRC. Keep the unresolved phrase visible and do not import captives into its translation solely from those versions. The relation of the clauses and spoil imagery needs further Greek review. ↩ 5:30

16:3Samson slept until midnight. About midnight he arose, seized the city gate’s doors and its two posts, lifted them with the bar, and put them on his shoulder. He carried them up to the top of the mountain facing Hebron and set them there.

Delilah and the Satraps’ Offer

16:4Afterward he loved a woman by the Sorek torrent whose name was Delilah.

16:5The foreigners’ satraps came up to her and said, “Deceive him and learn where his great strength lies, and how we may prevail against him and bind him to humble him. Each of us will give you eleven hundred pieces of silver.”

Three Times Deceived

16:6Delilah said to Samson, “Tell me where your great strength lies, and how you may be bound to humble you.”

16:7Samson said to her, “If they bind me with seven moist sinew-strings that have not dried, I will become weak and be like any other man.”

16:8The foreigners’ satraps brought her seven moist sinew-strings that had not dried, and she bound him with them.

16:9Now those lying in ambush for him waited in the inner room. She said to him, “The foreigners are upon you, Samson!” He broke the sinew-strings as a thread of flax waste breaks when it smells fire; and his strength was not known.

16:10Delilah said to Samson, “Behold, you have deceived me and spoken falsehoods to me. Please tell me now how you may be bound.”

16:11He said to her, “If they bind me tightly with seven new cords that have never been used for work, I will become weak and be like any other man.”

16:12Delilah took new cords for him and bound him with them, and said to him, “The foreigners are upon you, Samson!” Those lying in ambush waited in the inner room. He tore the cords from his arms like a thread.

16:13Delilah said to Samson, “Until now you have deceived me and spoken falsehoods to me. Please tell me how you may be bound.” He said to her, “If you weave the seven strands of my head into the warp and drive it with the peg into the wall, I shall be weak as any other man.”

16:14And Delilah put him to sleep, and wove the seven curls of his head into the stretched fabric, and drove it with pegs into the wall. And she said to him, “The foreigners are upon you, Samson!” And he awoke from his sleep, and tore the pegs with the woven fabric from the wall, and the warp; and his strength was not known.

The Secret Told

16:15Delilah said to him, “How can you say, ‘I love you,’ when your heart is not with me? This is the third time you have deceived me and have not told me where your great strength lies.”

16:16She wore him down with her words all night and troubled him until he grew weary to the point of death.

16:17He told her everything from his heart and said to her, “No razor shall come upon my head, for I am a Nazirite of God from my mother’s womb. If I am shaved, my strength will depart from me; I will become weak and be like everyone else.”

16:18Delilah saw that he had told her everything from his heart. She sent and called all the foreigners’ satraps, saying, “Come up this time, for he has told me all his heart.” All the foreigners’ satrapies came up to her, bringing the silver in their hands.

16:19She put him to sleep between her knees, called the barber, and had the seven curls of his head shaved. He began to be humbled, and his strength departed from him.

16:20Delilah said to him, “The foreigners are upon you, Samson!” He awoke from his sleep and said, “I will go out as at other times and shake myself free.” But he did not know that the Lord had departed from him.

Samson Blinded and Bound

16:21The foreigners seized him and dug out his eyes. They brought him down to Gaza, bound him with bronze fetters, and he ground grain in the prison house.

16:22The hair of his head began to grow again after he had been shaved.

The House of Dagon Falls

16:23The foreigners’ satraps gathered to offer a great sacrifice to Dagon their god and to rejoice. They said, “Our god has given Samson our enemy into our hand.”

16:24The people saw him and praised their gods, saying, “Our god has given our enemy into our hand, the one who devastated our land and multiplied our slain.”

16:25When their hearts were merry, they said, “Call Samson from the prison house, so that he may perform before us.” They called Samson from the prison house, mocked him, and placed him between the two pillars.

16:26Samson said to the boy leading him by the hand, “Please let me rest and feel the pillars on which the house is supported, so that I may lean upon them.” The boy did so.

16:27The house was full of men and women, and all the foreigners’ satraps were there. On the roof were about three thousand men and women watching Samson being mocked.

16:28Samson cried out to the Lord and said, “Lord, Lord, remember me and strengthen me just this once more, so that I may take one vengeance on the foreigners for my two eyes.”

16:29Samson embraced the two middle pillars on which the house was supported and leaned against them, one with his right hand and one with his left.

16:30Samson said, “Let my soul die with the foreigners.” He bent with strength, and the house fell upon the satraps and all the people in it. Those Samson killed in his death were more than those he killed in his life.

16:31His brothers and all his father’s household came down, took him, brought him up, and buried him between Zorah and Eshtaol, in the tomb of his father Manoah. He had judged Israel for twenty years.

Dan Seeks an Inheritance

18:1In those days there was no king in Israel. In those days the tribe of Dan was seeking an inheritance in which to dwell, because until those days no inheritance had fallen to it among Israel’s tribes.

18:2The sons of Dan sent five men from their kindred, from among their number, men of strength from Zorah and Eshtaol, to inspect and explore the land. They said to them, “Go, search out the land.” They came to Ephraim’s hill country, to Micah’s house, and rested there.

18:3While they were near Micah’s house, they recognised the voice of the young man, the younger Levite. They turned aside there and said to him, “Who brought you here? What are you doing here? And what do you have here?”

18:4He said to them, “Micah has done such and such for me. He hired me, and I became his priest.”

18:5They said to him, “Please inquire of God, so that we may know whether the journey we are making will succeed.”

18:6And the priest said to them, “Go in peace. The way you go is before the Lord.”

The Spies at Laish

18:7The five men went and came to Laish. They saw the people dwelling there living in confidence, after the Sidonians’ pattern, resting in confidence and unable to speak a word, because they were far from Sidon and had no dealings with Syria.

18:8The five men came to their brothers at Zorah and Eshtaol. Their brothers said to them, “Why are you sitting here?”

18:9They said, “Arise, and let us go up against them; for we entered and walked through the land as far as Laish, and saw the people dwelling there in confidence, after the manner of the Sidonians, far from Sidon and without dealings with Syria. But arise, let us go up against them; for we have found the land, and behold, it is very good. Do you remain silent? Do not delay to go in and inherit the land.

Six Hundred Men Pass Micah’s House

18:11Six hundred men from Dan’s kindred set out from Zorah and Eshtaol, girded with equipment for war.

18:12They went up and camped at Kiriath-jearim in Judah. Therefore that place was called Dan’s Camp to this day; look, it is behind Kiriath-jearim.

18:13They passed from there, came to Ephraim’s hill country, and reached Micah’s house.

18:14The five men who had gone to inspect the land answered their brothers, saying, “Do you know that in these houses are an ephod and teraphim, a graven image and a cast image? Now consider what you shall do.”

18:15They turned aside there and entered the young Levite’s house, Micah’s house, and greeted him.

The Image and the Priest Taken

18:16The six hundred men from the sons of Dan, girded with equipment for war, stood firmly at the gateway entrance.

18:17The five men who had gone to inspect the land went up, entered there, and took the carved image, the ephod, the teraphim, and the cast image. The priest stood firmly at the gateway entrance with the six hundred men girded with equipment for war.

18:19They said to him, “Be silent, put your hand over your mouth, and come with us. Be a father and priest to us. Is it better for you to be priest to one man’s house, or to a tribe and kindred in Israel?”

18:20The priest’s heart was glad. He took the ephod, the teraphim, the carved image, and the cast image, and went into the midst of the people.

18:21They turned and left, placing the whole household and his valuable property ahead of them.

Micah Pursues and Turns Back

18:25And the sons of Dan said to him, “Let not your voice be heard among us, lest men bitter in soul fall upon you, and you add your life and the lives of your household.”

18:26The sons of Dan went their way. Micah saw that they were stronger than he, so he withdrew and returned to his house.

Laish Taken and Renamed Dan

18:27They took what Micah had made and the priest who had belonged to him, and came to Laish, against a quiet and confident people. They struck them with the edge of the sword and burned the city.

18:28There was no rescuer, because it was far from the Sidonians and they had no dealings with people. It was in the valley belonging to the house of Rehob. They built the city and dwelt in it.

18:29They called the city Dan, after the name of their father, who was born to Israel. Previously the city’s name was Laish.

18:30The sons of Dan set up Micah’s carved image for themselves. Jonathan son of Gershom, son of Moses, and his sons were priests to the tribe of Dan until the day of the land’s deportation.

18:31They established for themselves Micah’s carved image that he had made, throughout the days when God’s house was in Shiloh.

The Levite Goes After His Concubine

19:1In those days, when there was no king in Israel, a Levite was sojourning in the remote parts of Ephraim’s hill country. The man took for himself a concubine from Bethlehem in Judah.

19:2His concubine became angry with him and went away from him to her father’s house, to Bethlehem in Judah. She remained there four months.

19:3Her husband arose and went after her, with his servant and a pair of pack animals, to speak to her heart, reconcile her to himself, and bring her back to him. He went to her father’s house. The young woman’s father saw him and came to meet him.

19:4His father-in-law, the young woman’s father, brought him in, and he stayed with him for three days. They ate, drank, and slept there.

19:5And on the fourth day they rose early in the morning, and he arose to depart. And the father of the young woman said to his son-in-law, “Strengthen your heart with a morsel of bread, and afterward go.”

19:6And they sat down, and the two ate and drank together. And the father of the young woman said to the man, “Begin by lodging here this night, and let your heart be glad.”

19:7The man arose to leave, but his father-in-law pressed him, and he lodged there again.

19:8On the fifth day he rose early in the morning to depart. The young woman’s father said, “Strengthen your heart with bread, and remain until the day declines.” And the two ate and drank.

19:9And the man arose to depart, he and his concubine and his servant. And his father-in-law, the father of the young woman, said to him, “Behold, the day has declined toward evening. Lodge here today also, and let your heart be glad. Tomorrow you may rise early for your journey, and go to your dwelling.”

The Road to Gibeah

19:10The man was unwilling to lodge. He arose and left, and they came opposite Jebus—that is, Jerusalem—with a pair of saddled pack animals and his concubine.

19:11They were still near Jebus, and it was late in the day. The servant said to his master, “Please come, let us turn aside into this Jebusite city and lodge in it.”

19:12His master said to him, “I will certainly not turn aside into a foreigner’s city that does not belong to the sons of Israel. We will pass on to Gibeah.”

19:13He said to his servant, “Come, let us enter one of the places and lodge in Gibeah or Ramah.”

19:14They passed on and went their way, for the sun set near Gibeah, which belongs to Benjamin.

19:15They turned aside there to enter and lodge in Gibeah. They entered and sat in the city square, but no one took them into a house to lodge.

The Old Man Takes Them In

19:16Behold, an old man came in from his work in the field that evening. The man was from Ephraim’s hill country and was sojourning in Gibeah; the people of the place were sons of Benjamin.

19:17He lifted his eyes and saw the traveller in the city square. The old man said, “Where are you going, and where do you come from?”

19:18He said to him, “We are traveling from Bethlehem in Judah to the remote parts of Ephraim’s hill country. I am from there. I went to Bethlehem in Judah, and I am going back to my house. No one takes me into a house.

19:19And we have straw and fodder for our donkeys, and bread and wine for me and your maidservant and the young man, your servants. We lack nothing.”

19:20The old man said, “Peace be to you. Let all your need fall on me; only do not lodge in the square.”

19:21He brought him into his house and set fodder before his pack animals. They washed their feet, ate, and drank.

The Outrage at Gibeah

19:22While their hearts were glad, the men of the city, sons of lawless men, surrounded the house and struck the door. They said to the old man, the master of the house, “Bring out the man who entered your house, that we may know him.”

19:23The master of the house went out to them and said, “No, my brothers, please do not act wickedly. Since this man has entered my house, do not commit this folly.

19:24Behold, here are my virgin daughter and his concubine. I will bring them out; violate them and do to them what is good in your eyes. But do not do this deed of folly to this man.”

19:25The men were unwilling to listen to him. The man seized his concubine and brought her outside to them. They raped her and mocked her all night until morning, and let her go as dawn rose.

19:26The woman came toward morning and fell by the gateway entrance of the man’s house where her master was, until day broke.

19:27Her master arose in the morning, opened the house’s doors, and went out to go on his way. Behold, the woman, his concubine, lay fallen by the door, her hands upon the threshold.

19:28He said to her, “Get up, and let us go.” She did not answer him, for she had died. He lifted her onto the pack animal, and the man arose and went to his place.

The Body Sent Through Israel

19:29He entered his house, took the knife, seized his concubine, and cut her according to her bones into twelve portions. He sent them to all Israel’s tribes.

19:30Everyone who saw it said, “Nothing like this has happened or been seen since the day the sons of Israel came up from Egypt until this day.” He commanded the men he sent, saying, “Say this to every man of Israel: ‘Has anything like this happened since the day the sons of Israel came up from Egypt until this day? Take counsel about her among yourselves and speak.’”

Israel Assembles at Mizpah

20:2The division of all the people, all Israel’s tribes, stood in the assembly of God’s people: four hundred thousand foot soldiers who drew the sword.

20:3The sons of Benjamin heard that the sons of Israel had gone up before the Lord at Mizpah. The sons of Israel said, “Tell us where this wickedness happened.”

20:4The Levite, the husband of the murdered woman, answered, “I came with my concubine to Gibeah in Benjamin to lodge.

20:5The men from Gibeah rose against me and surrounded the house against me by night. They wanted to kill me, and they violated my concubine and mocked her, and she died.

20:6I seized my concubine, cut her into pieces, and sent her throughout Israel’s inherited territory, because they committed folly in Israel.

20:7Behold, all you sons of Israel, give your word and counsel.”

Israel Resolves to Act

20:9Now this is what we will do to Gibeah: we will go up against it by lot,

20:10and take ten men out of a hundred, a hundred out of a thousand, and a thousand out of ten thousand, to obtain provisions for the people going in to deal with Gibeah in Benjamin according to all the folly they committed in Israel.”

Benjamin Refuses to Give Up the Men

20:12Israel’s tribes sent men throughout the whole tribe of Benjamin, saying, “What is this wickedness that has happened among you?

20:13Now hand over the impious men in Gibeah, the sons of Belial, so that we may put them to death and remove wickedness from Israel.” But the sons of Benjamin were unwilling to listen to their brothers, the sons of Israel.

20:14The sons of Benjamin gathered from their cities to Gibeah to go out to battle against the sons of Israel.

Two Days of Defeat

20:18They arose and went up to Bethel and inquired of God. The sons of Israel said, “Who shall go up leading us to fight Benjamin?” The Lord said, “Judah shall go up as leader.”

20:19The sons of Israel arose and camped against Gibeah.

20:20All the men of Israel went out to war against Benjamin. The men of Israel drew up against them for war at Gibeah.

20:21The sons of Benjamin came out of the city and struck down twenty-two thousand men of Israel to the ground that day.

20:22The men of Israel strengthened themselves and again drew up for battle in the place where they had drawn up on the first day.

20:23The sons of Israel went up and wept before the Lord until evening. They inquired of the Lord, saying, “Shall I again approach for battle against Benjamin my brother?” The Lord said, “Go up against him.”

20:24The sons of Israel approached Benjamin on the second day.

20:25Benjamin came out from Gibeah to meet them on the second day and struck down eighteen thousand men of the people to the ground, all men who drew the sword.

Fasting and Inquiry at Bethel

The Ambush at Gibeah

20:29The sons of Israel set ambushes all around Gibeah.

20:30Israel deployed against Benjamin on the third day and drew up against Gibeah as on the previous occasions.

20:31The sons of Benjamin came out to meet the people and were drawn away from the city. As before, they began to strike some of the people on the roads—one going up to Bethel and one going up to Gibeah in the field—about thirty men of Israel.

20:32The sons of Benjamin said, “They are stumbling before us as before.” But the sons of Israel said, “Let us flee and draw them out of the city to the roads.”

20:33All the men of Israel arose from their place and drew up at Baal-tamar. Israel’s ambush struggled forward from its place, from west of Gibeah.

20:34Ten thousand chosen men from all Israel came opposite Gibeah, and the battle grew heavy. They did not know that disaster had reached them.

20:35The Lord routed Benjamin before Israel. That day the sons of Israel struck down twenty-five thousand one hundred men of Benjamin, all men who drew the sword.

20:36Benjamin saw that it was routed. The men of Israel had given ground to Benjamin because they trusted the ambush they had set against Gibeah.

20:37The ambush rushed forward and poured toward Gibeah. The ambush went on, and they struck the whole city with the edge of the sword.

Benjamin Routed

Judges 21

Read this chapter on staging

The Oath at Mizpah and Israel’s Grief

21:1The men of Israel had sworn at Mizpah, saying, “None of us shall give his daughter to Benjamin as a wife.”

21:2All the people came to Mizpah and Bethel and sat there before God until evening. They raised their voices and wept bitterly.

21:3They said, “Why, Lord, God of Israel, has this happened in Israel, that one tribe should be visited today in Israel?” †

21:4The next day the people rose early, built an altar there, and offered whole burnt offerings of deliverance.

21:5The sons of Israel said, “Who among all the tribes of Israel did not come up in the assembly to the Lord?” For there was a great oath concerning anyone who did not come up to the Lord at Mizpah, saying, “He shall surely die.”

21:6The sons of Israel were comforted concerning Benjamin their brother and said, “Today one tribe has been removed from Israel.

21:7What shall we do for wives for those who remain? We have sworn by the Lord not to give them any of our daughters as wives.”

Jabesh-Gilead Struck for Its Absence

21:8They said, “Which one of the tribes of Israel did not come up to the Lord at Mizpah?” And behold, no man from Jabesh-gilead had come to the camp, to the assembly.

21:9When the people were inspected, behold, no man from the inhabitants of Jabesh-gilead was there.

21:10The congregation sent twelve thousand mighty men there and commanded them, saying, “Go and strike all the inhabitants of Jabesh-gilead with the edge of the sword, including the women and the people.

21:11This is what you shall do: put under the ban every male, and every woman who has known the bed of a male.”

21:12Among the inhabitants of Jabesh-gilead they found four hundred young virgin women who had not known a man in the bed of a male. They brought them to the camp at Shiloh, which is in the land of Canaan.

Benjamin Recalled from the Rock

21:13The whole congregation sent word and spoke to Benjamin at the rock of Rimmon, and called them to peace.

21:14Benjamin returned to the sons of Israel at that time, and they gave them the women who were from the women of Jabesh-gilead. And this pleased them.

21:15The people were comforted concerning Benjamin, because the Lord had made a breach in the tribes of Israel.

Wives Taken at the Feast of Shiloh

21:16The elders of the congregation said, “What shall we do for wives for those who remain, since the women have been made to disappear from Benjamin?”

21:17They said, “An inheritance is preserved for Benjamin, and a tribe shall certainly not be blotted out from Israel.

21:18But we cannot give them wives from our daughters, for we, the sons of Israel, have sworn, saying, ‘Cursed is the one who gives a wife to Benjamin.’”

21:19They said, “There is a feast for the Lord at Shiloh year after year. It is north of Bethel, toward the sunrise on the road that goes up from Bethel to Shechem, and south of Lebanon, of Lebona.”

21:20They commanded the sons of Benjamin, saying, “Go over and lie in ambush in the vineyards.

21:21Watch, and when the daughters of the inhabitants of Shiloh come out in Shiloh to dance in the dances, come out of the vineyards and seize, each man for himself, a wife from the daughters of Shiloh. Then go to the land of Benjamin.

21:22When their fathers or brothers come to contend with you, we shall say to them, ‘Have mercy on them, for they did not each take a wife in the war. For you have not given the women to them; at that time you committed an offence.’” †

21:23The sons of Benjamin did so and took wives according to their number from the dancers whom they snatched away. They went and returned to their inheritance, built cities for themselves, and lived in them.

21:24The sons of Israel went away from there at that time, each to his tribe and his kindred. They departed from there, each to his inheritance.

No King in Israel

21:25In those days there was no king in Israel. Everyone did what was right in his own eyes.

Readings requiring further review

Judges 21:3. The tribe that should be visited. The Greek visitation verb is difficult here and may relate to reckoning or mustering. The historical comparisons explicitly describe a missing or removed tribe. The draft’s visited remains provisional and does not mean an ordinary social call. This wording requires independent review for intelligibility and context. ↩ 21:3

Judges 21:22. The argument and the offence. The Greek shifts among you, they, and an affirmative statement about an offence. Brenton’s pronouns differ; KJV makes guilt conditional, while DRC assigns fault differently. Preserve the selected grammar provisionally. The argument remains difficult and must not be made clear by silently borrowing another version’s logic. ↩ 21:22
