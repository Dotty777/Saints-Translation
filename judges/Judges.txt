JUDGES 1

1:1 After Joshua’s death, the sons of Israel inquired of the Lord, saying, “Who shall go up for us against the Canaanites, to lead the battle against them?”
1:2 The Lord said, “Judah shall go up. Behold, I have given the land into his hand.”
1:3 And Judah said to Simeon his brother, “Go up with me into my allotted territory, and let us fight against the Canaanites; and I also will go with you into your allotted territory.” And Simeon went with him.
1:4 Judah went up, and the Lord gave the Canaanites and the Perizzites into his hand. He struck them at Bezek: ten thousand men.
1:5 They found Adoni-bezek at Bezek, fought against him, and struck the Canaanites and the Perizzites.
1:6 Adoni-bezek fled, but they pursued him, seized him, and cut off the extremities of his hands and his feet.
1:7 Adoni-bezek said, “Seventy kings, with the extremities of their hands and feet cut off, used to gather what was beneath my table. As I have done, so God has repaid me.” They brought him to Jerusalem, and he died there.
1:8 The sons of Judah fought against Jerusalem and captured it. They struck it with the edge of the sword and burned the city with fire.
1:9 After these things the sons of Judah went down to fight against the Canaanites dwelling in the hill country, the south, and the plain.
1:10 Judah went against the Canaanites dwelling in Hebron, and Hebron came out against him. Hebron’s former name was Kariatharboksepher. He struck Sheshai, Ahiman, and Talmai, descendants of Anak.
1:11 From there they went against the inhabitants of Debir. Debir’s former name was City of Letters.
1:12 And Caleb said, “Whoever strikes the City of Letters and takes possession of it, I will give him Achsah my daughter as wife.”
1:13 Othniel son of Kenaz, Caleb’s younger brother, took possession of it, and Caleb gave him Achsah his daughter as wife.
1:14 And when she entered, he urged her to ask her father for the field. She murmured on the beast of burden and cried out from it, “You have given me away into a southern land.” And Caleb said to her, “What do you desire?”
1:15 And Achsah said to him, “Give me a blessing; for you have given me away into a southern land. Give me a redemption of water.” And Caleb gave her, according to her heart, the redemption of the heights and the redemption of the low places.
1:16 The descendants of Jobab the Kenite, Moses’ father-in-law, went up from the City of Palms to the sons of Judah, into the wilderness in the south, on the descent to Arad. He went and dwelt with the people.
1:17 Judah went with Simeon his brother. They struck the Canaanites dwelling in Sepheth, placed it under the ban, and destroyed it. They called the city’s name Destruction.
1:18 Judah did not possess Gaza and its territory, Ashkelon and its territory, Ekron and its territory, or Ashdod and its surrounding lands.
1:19 The Lord was with Judah, and he inherited the mountain, because he could not inherit the inhabitants of the valley, because Rechab had separated it.
1:20 He gave Hebron to Caleb, as Moses had said, and Caleb inherited the three cities from there and removed from there the three sons of Anak.
1:21 The sons of Benjamin did not remove the Jebusites dwelling in Jerusalem. The Jebusites dwelt with the sons of Benjamin to this day.
1:22 The sons of Joseph also went up to Bethel, and Judah was with them.
1:23 The house of Israel camped against Bethel. The city’s former name was Luz.
1:24 The watchers saw a man coming out of the city, seized him, and said to him, “Show us the way into the city, and we will show you mercy.”
1:25 He showed them the way into the city. They struck the city with the edge of the sword, but let the man and his kin go.
1:26 The man went to the land of the Hittites, built a city there, and called its name Luz. That is its name to this day.
1:27 Manasseh did not possess Beth-shan, which is the City of the Scythians, or its dependent towns or surrounding lands; nor Ekthanaad and its dependent towns, the inhabitants of Dor and its dependent towns, the inhabitants of Balaam and its dependent towns, the inhabitants of Magedon and its dependent towns, or the inhabitants of Jeblaam and its dependent towns. The Canaanites began to dwell in this land.
1:28 When Israel became strong, it subjected the Canaanites to tribute, but did not completely remove them.
1:29 Ephraim did not remove the Canaanites dwelling in Gezer. The Canaanites dwelt among them in Gezer and became subject to tribute.
1:30 Zebulun did not remove the inhabitants of Kedron or the inhabitants of Enaala. The Canaanites dwelt among them and became subject to tribute.
1:31 Asher did not remove the inhabitants of Acco, and they became subject to his tribute; nor the inhabitants of Dor, the inhabitants of Sidon, the inhabitants of Aalaph, Achazib, Helba, Aphek, or Roob.
1:32 Asher dwelt among the Canaanites inhabiting the land, because he was unable to remove them.
1:33 Naphtali did not remove the inhabitants of Beth-shemesh or the inhabitants of Beth-anath. Israel dwelt among the Canaanites inhabiting the land, but the inhabitants of Beth-shemesh and Beth-anath became subject to their tribute.
1:34 The Amorites pressed the sons of Dan into the mountain, for they did not allow them to come down into the valley.
1:35 The Amorites began to dwell on the Mountain of the Myrtle Grove, where the bears and the foxes are. The hand of the house of Joseph became heavy upon the Amorites, and they became subject to tribute.
1:36 The Amorite boundary—the Idumean—was above Akrabin, at the Rock and above.

JUDGES 2

2:1 The messenger of the Lord went up from Gilgal to the Place of Weeping, to Bethel, and to the house of Israel. He said to them, “The Lord, the Lord brought you up from Egypt and brought you into the land that He swore to your fathers to give you. He said to you, ‘I will never scatter My covenant with you.
2:2 You shall make no covenant with those dwelling in this land, nor shall you bow down to their gods; but you shall shatter their graven images and pull down their altars.’ Yet you did not obey My voice when you did these things.
2:3 And I said, ‘I will no longer remove the people whom I said I would destroy before you. They shall become constraints upon you, and their gods shall become a stumbling block to you.’”
2:4 When the messenger of the Lord spoke these words to all Israel, the people raised their voice and wept.
2:5 For this reason that place was called the Place of Weeping, and there they sacrificed to the Lord.
2:6 Joshua sent the people away, and the sons of Israel went, each to his house and his inheritance, to inherit the land.
2:7 The people served the Lord all the days of Joshua and all the days of the elders who outlived Joshua, who knew all the great work the Lord had done for Israel.
2:8 Joshua son of Nun, the servant of the Lord, died at the age of one hundred ten years.
2:9 They buried him within the territory of his inheritance at Timnath-heres, in the mountain of Ephraim, north of Mount Gaash.
2:10 That whole generation too was added to its fathers. Another generation arose after them, who did not know the Lord or the work He had done for Israel.
2:11 The sons of Israel did evil before the Lord and served the Baalim.
2:12 They forsook the Lord, the God of their fathers, who had brought them out of the land of Egypt. They followed other gods from among the gods of the peoples around them, bowed down to them, and provoked the Lord.
2:13 They forsook the Lord and served Baal and the Astartes.
2:14 The Lord was greatly angered against Israel. He delivered them into the hand of plunderers, who plundered them; and He sold them into the hand of their enemies all around, so they could not stand before their enemies.
2:15 In everything in which they prostituted themselves, the Lord’s hand was against them for harm, as the Lord had spoken and as the Lord had sworn. He greatly afflicted them.
2:16 The Lord raised up judges for them and saved them from the hand of their plunderers.
2:17 Yet they did not listen even to their judges, for they prostituted themselves after other gods, bowed down to them, and provoked the Lord. They quickly turned aside from the way their fathers had walked in obeying the Lord’s commandments. They did not do as their fathers had done.
2:18 Whenever the Lord raised up judges for them, the Lord was with the judge and saved them from the hand of their enemies all the days of the judge. For the Lord was moved by their groaning before those who besieged and harmed them.
2:19 When the judge died, they turned back and again acted more corruptly than their fathers, following other gods to serve them and bow down to them. They did not cast away their practices or depart from their hard way.
2:20 And the Lord was greatly angered against Israel, and said, “Because this nation has forsaken My covenant which I commanded their fathers, and has not obeyed My voice,
2:21 I too will no longer remove before them anyone from the nations that Joshua left and let remain.”
2:22 This was to test Israel through them: would they keep the way of the Lord and walk in it as their fathers had kept it, or not?
2:23 The Lord left these nations, not removing them quickly, and did not give them into Joshua’s hand.

JUDGES 3

3:1 These are the nations Joshua left to test Israel through them—all those who had not known any of the wars of Canaan—
3:2 only for the sake of the generations of the sons of Israel, to teach them warfare; only those before them had not known these things:
3:3 the five satrapies of the foreigners, all the Canaanites, the Sidonians, and the Hivites dwelling in Lebanon, from Mount Balaermon to Loboemath.
3:4 This was to test Israel through them, to know whether they would obey the Lord’s commandments that He commanded their fathers through Moses.
3:5 The sons of Israel dwelt among the Canaanites, Hittites, Amorites, Perizzites, Hivites, and Jebusites.
3:6 They took their daughters as wives for themselves, gave their own daughters to their sons, and served their gods.
3:7 The sons of Israel did evil before the Lord, forgot the Lord their God, and served the Baalim and the groves.
3:8 And the Lord was greatly angered against Israel, and sold them into the hands of Chousarsathom king of Syria of the Rivers; and they served him eight years.
3:9 The sons of Israel cried out to the Lord. The Lord raised up a saviour for Israel and saved them: Othniel son of Kenaz, Caleb’s younger brother. And he heard him.
3:10 The Spirit of the Lord came upon him. He judged Israel and went out to war. The Lord gave Chousarsathom king of Syria into his hand, and his hand became strong against Chousarsathom.
3:11 The land was quiet for fifty years. Then Othniel son of Kenaz died.
3:12 The sons of Israel again did evil before the Lord. The Lord strengthened Eglon king of Moab against Israel because they had done evil before the Lord.
3:13 He brought all the sons of Ammon and Amalek to himself, went and struck Israel, and took possession of the City of Palms.
3:14 The sons of Israel served Eglon king of Moab for eighteen years.
3:15 The sons of Israel cried out to the Lord. The Lord raised up a saviour for them: Ehud son of Gera, son of Jemeni, a man skilled with both hands. Through him the sons of Israel sent gifts to Eglon king of Moab.
3:16 Ehud made himself a double-edged dagger, a span long, and strapped it beneath his cloak upon his right thigh.
3:17 He presented the gifts to Eglon king of Moab. Eglon was a very handsome man.
3:18 When Ehud finished presenting the gifts, he sent away those who had carried them.
3:19 Eglon returned from the graven images near Gilgal. And Ehud said, “I have a secret word for you, O king.” And Eglon said to all, “Depart!” And all his attendants went out from him.
3:20 Ehud went in to him as he sat all alone in his summer upper room, and said, “I have a word from God for you, O king.” And Eglon rose from his throne near him.
3:21 As he rose, Ehud stretched out his left hand, took the dagger from his right thigh, and drove it into Eglon’s belly,
3:22 and drove even the handle in after the blade, and the fat closed over the blade, because he did not pull the dagger out of his belly.
3:23 Ehud went out into the vestibule, closed the upper-room doors upon him, and fastened them,
3:24 and he went out. The king’s attendants entered, saw that the upper-room doors were closed, and said, “Perhaps he is sitting on the privy in the bedroom’s retreat?”
3:25 They waited in embarrassment, but no one opened the upper-room doors. They took the key and opened them, and there was their lord, fallen dead on the ground.
3:26 Ehud escaped while they were in confusion, and no one was paying attention to him. He passed the carved images and escaped to Seirotha.
3:27 When he arrived, he sounded a horn in the mountain of Ephraim. The sons of Israel went down with him, and he went before them.
3:28 He said to them, “Come down after me, for the Lord God has given your enemies, Moab, into your hand.” They went down after him and seized Moab’s Jordan crossings. They allowed no one to cross.
3:29 At that time they struck about ten thousand men of Moab, all their fighters and every man of strength. Not one escaped.
3:30 Moab was put to shame that day under Israel’s hand, and the land was quiet for eighty years. Ehud judged them until he died.
3:31 After him Shamgar son of Anath arose and struck as many as six hundred foreign men, apart from the calves of the oxen. He too saved Israel.

JUDGES 4

4:1 The sons of Israel again did evil before the Lord.
4:2 The Lord sold them into the hand of Jabin king of Canaan, who reigned in Hazor. His army commander was Sisera, who dwelt in Arisoth of the Nations.
4:3 The sons of Israel cried out to the Lord, because he had nine hundred iron chariots, and he oppressed Israel mightily for twenty years.
4:4 Now Deborah, a prophetess, the wife of Lappidoth, was judging Israel at that time.
4:5 She sat beneath Deborah’s palm between Ramah and Bethel in the hill country of Ephraim. The sons of Israel went up to her there to be judged.
4:6 Deborah sent and called Barak son of Abinoam from Kedesh in Naphtali, and said to him, “Has not the Lord, the God of Israel, commanded you, ‘Go to Mount Tabor and take with you ten thousand men of the sons of Naphtali and of the sons of Zebulun,
4:7 and I will draw Sisera, the commander of Jabin’s army, with his chariots and his multitude, to you at the Kishon torrent, and I will deliver him into your hand’?”
4:8 Barak said to her, “If you go with me, I will go; but if you do not go with me, I will not go, for I do not know the day on which the Lord gives success to the messenger with me.”
4:9 Deborah said to him, “I will surely go with you. Yet know that the advantage will not be yours on the way you go, for the Lord will sell Sisera into the hands of a woman.” And Deborah arose and went with Barak to Kedesh.
4:10 Barak summoned Zebulun and Naphtali to Kedesh. Ten thousand men went up at his feet, and Deborah went up with him.
4:11 The neighbours of the Kenite had separated from the sons of Jobab, Moses’ in-law. He pitched his tent near the Oak of the Resters, near Kedesh.
4:12 They told Sisera that Barak son of Abinoam had gone up Mount Tabor.
4:13 Sisera summoned all his chariots—nine hundred iron chariots—and all the people with him, from Arisoth of the Nations to the Kishon torrent.
4:14 And Deborah said to Barak, “Arise! For this is the day on which the Lord has delivered Sisera into your hand. Behold, shall not the Lord go before you?” And Barak went down from Mount Tabor, and ten thousand men followed him.
4:15 The Lord threw Sisera, all his chariots, and all his camp into confusion before Barak at the edge of the sword. Sisera descended from his chariot and fled on foot.
4:16 Barak pursued the chariots and the camp as far as the Woodland of the Nations. All Sisera’s camp fell by the edge of the sword; not even one remained.
4:17 Sisera fled on foot to the tent of Jael, the wife of Heber the Kenite, for there was peace between Jabin king of Hazor and the house of Heber the Kenite.
4:18 Jael went out to meet Sisera and said to him, “Turn aside, my lord; turn aside to me. Do not be afraid.” He turned aside to her into the tent, and she covered him with her hide covering.
4:19 Sisera said to her, “Please give me a little water to drink, for I am thirsty.” She opened a skin of milk, gave him a drink, and covered his face.
4:20 And he said to her, “Stand at the entrance of the tent. And if anyone comes and asks you, ‘Is there a man here?’ say, ‘No.’” And she covered him with her hide covering.
4:21 Jael, Heber’s wife, took a tent peg and put the mallet in her hand. She came to him quietly and drove the peg into his jaw, and it passed through into the ground. He convulsed between her knees, breathed his last, and died.
4:22 And behold, Barak was pursuing Sisera. Jael went out to meet him and said, “Come, and I will show you the man you seek.” He entered with her, and there lay Sisera, dead, with the peg in his jaw.
4:23 On that day the Lord God subdued Jabin king of Canaan before the sons of Israel.
4:24 The hand of the sons of Israel grew stronger and stronger against Jabin king of Canaan, until they destroyed him.

JUDGES 5

5:1 On that day Deborah and Barak son of Abinoam sang and said:
5:2 “When leaders began to lead in Israel, when the people chose willingly, bless the Lord!
5:3 Hear, O kings; give ear, you mighty satraps! I will sing to the Lord; I will make music to the God of Israel.
5:4 O Lord, when Thou didst go forth from Seir, when Thou didst depart from the field of Edom, the earth shook; heaven was astonished, and the clouds dropped water.
5:5 Mountains were shaken before the Lord—this Sinai, before the Lord, the God of Israel.
5:6 In the days of Shamgar son of Anath, in the days of Jael, kings ceased, and they travelled paths; they travelled twisted roads.
5:7 Phrazōn ceased in Israel; it ceased until Deborah arose, because a mother arose in Israel.
5:8 They chose new gods like barley bread. Would I see a covering of lances among forty thousand?
5:9 My heart is with the things appointed for Israel. You mighty ones of the people, bless the Lord!
5:10 You who ride upon beasts of burden, you who sit in covered carriages—
5:11 utter the sound of musicians among those rejoicing. There they will ascribe righteousness to the Lord. Righteous people grew strong in Israel; then the Lord’s people went down to His cities.
5:12 Awake, awake, Deborah! Rouse myriads with the people! Awake, awake, speak with song! Arise in strength, Barak; and strengthen Barak, Deborah! Take your captives, son of Abinoam!
5:13 When was his strength magnified? Lord, humble for me those stronger than I am.
5:14 The people of Ephraim punished them in the valley of your brother Benjamin, among your peoples. From me, Machir, investigators came down; and from Zebulun the Lord fought for me among mighty ones from there, with a sceptre of one strengthening leadership.
5:15 In Issachar, with Deborah, he sent his foot soldiers into the valley. Why do you dwell between lips? He stretched out at his feet. Among the divisions of Reuben were great scrutinies of heart.
5:16 Why do you sit for me between the mosphathaim, to hear the whistling of those who rouse? To pass into the territory of Reuben were great searchings of heart.
5:17 Gilead camped beyond the Jordan; and Dan—why does he stay with ships? Asher stayed by the seashore and camped by its breaches.
5:18 Zebulun was a people that scorned its life to the point of death, and Naphtali on the heights of the field.
5:19 Kings came and drew up in battle; then Canaan’s kings fought at Taanach by Megiddo’s water. They took no greedy gain of silver.
5:20 From heaven the stars fought; from their appointed order they fought against Sisera.
5:21 The Kishon torrent cast them out—the torrent Kadēmim, the Kishon torrent. My mighty soul will trample them.
5:22 Then the horse’s heels were cut off—Amadarōth of his mighty ones.
5:23 ‘Curse Meroz,’ said the Lord’s messenger, ‘utterly curse its inhabitants, because they did not come to the Lord’s help. The Lord is our helper, mighty among warriors.’
5:24 Blessed among women be Jael, the wife of Heber the Kenite; among women in a tent may she be blessed.
5:25 He asked her for water, and she gave him milk. In a bowl of mighty ones she brought butter.
5:26 She stretched her left hand toward a peg, her right toward the cuttings of the exhausted; she cut down Sisera, crushed his head, shattered and pierced his jaw.
5:27 Between her feet he bent down and fell; he lay between her feet. Where he bent down, there he fell, wretched.
5:28 Through the window Sisera’s mother leaned out, looking through the lattice toward those returning with Sisera: ‘Why is his chariot last to arrive? Why have the tracks of his chariots been delayed?’
5:29 The wise among her noble ladies answered her, and she herself answered in her own words:
5:30 ‘Will they not find him dividing spoils, befriending friends for the head of a mighty man? Spoils of dyed cloth for Sisera, spoils of dyed cloth, embroidery; a dyed embroidered piece around his neck as spoil.’
5:31 So perish all Thine enemies, O Lord! And let those who love Him be as the sun going forth in its might.” And the land was quiet for forty years.

JUDGES 6

6:1 The sons of Israel did evil before the Lord, and the Lord gave them into Midian’s hand for seven years.
6:2 Midian’s hand prevailed against Israel. Because of Midian, the sons of Israel made enclosures for themselves in the mountains, caves, and strongholds.
6:3 Whenever an Israelite man sowed, Midian, Amalek, and the sons of the east came up; they came up against him.
6:4 They camped against them and ruined the land’s produce as far as Gaza. They left no means of life in Israel, neither flock nor calf nor donkey.
6:5 For they and their livestock came up, bringing their tents; they arrived like locusts in multitude. They and their camels were beyond numbering, and they entered Israel’s land to ruin it.
6:6 Israel became very poor because of Midian, and the sons of Israel cried out to the Lord.
6:7 When the sons of Israel cried out to the Lord because of Midian,
6:8 the Lord sent a man who was a prophet to the sons of Israel. He said to them, “Thus says the Lord, the God of Israel: ‘I am the One who brought you up from Egypt, and I brought you out of the house of slavery.
6:9 I rescued you from Egypt’s hand and from the hand of all who oppressed you. I cast them out before you and gave you their land.
6:10 And I said to you, “I am the Lord your God. You shall not fear the gods of the Amorites, in whose land you dwell.” But you have not obeyed My voice.’”
6:11 The Lord’s messenger came and sat beneath the oak in Ephrathah that belonged to Joash, father of Abiezri. His son Gideon was beating out wheat in a winepress to escape from Midian.
6:12 The Lord’s messenger appeared to him and said, “The Lord is with you, mighty man of strength.”
6:13 Gideon said to him, “Please, my lord, if the Lord is with us, why have all these evils found us? Where are all His wonders that our fathers recounted to us, saying, ‘Did not the Lord bring us up from Egypt?’ But now He has rejected us and given us into Midian’s hand.”
6:14 And the messenger of the Lord looked toward him and said, “Go in your strength, and you shall save Israel. Behold, I have sent you.”
6:15 Gideon said to him, “Please, my lord, with what shall I save Israel? Behold, my thousand is the lowliest in Manasseh, and I am small in my father’s house.”
6:16 And the messenger of the Lord said to him, “The Lord shall be with you, and you shall strike Midian as one man.”
6:17 Gideon said to him, “If I have found favour in your eyes, give me a sign that it is you speaking with me.
6:18 Do not leave here until I come to you, bring my sacrifice, and set it before you.” And he said, “I am here; I will sit until you return.”
6:19 Gideon went in and prepared a young goat and unleavened cakes from an oiphi of flour. He put the meat on the basket and poured the broth into a pot. He brought it out to him beneath the oak and bowed down.
6:20 The Lord’s messenger said to him, “Take the meat and the unleavened loaves, set them by that rock, and pour out the broth.” He did so.
6:21 The Lord’s messenger stretched out the end of the staff in his hand and touched the meat and the unleavened cakes. Fire was kindled from the rock and consumed the meat and the unleavened cakes. The Lord’s messenger went from his sight.
6:22 Gideon saw that he was the Lord’s messenger. Gideon said, “Ah, ah, Lord, Lord! For I have seen the Lord’s messenger face to face.”
6:23 The Lord said to him, “Peace be to you. Do not be afraid that you will die.”
6:24 Gideon built an altar there to the Lord and called it Peace of the Lord. To this day it is still there in Ephrathah of the father of Ezri.
6:25 And that night the Lord said to him, “Take your father's fattened bull, the bull of seven years. Pull down your father's altar of Baal, and cut down the grove beside it.
6:26 Build an altar to the Lord your God, who appeared to you, on the summit of this Mount Maoz, in battle array. Take the bull and offer it as a whole burnt offering with the wood of the grove you will cut down.”
6:27 Gideon took thirteen men from his servants and did as the Lord had told him. Because he feared his father’s household and the men of the city too much to do it by day, he did it by night.
6:28 The men of the city rose early in the morning. Behold, Baal’s altar had been demolished, the grove beside it had been cut down, and the fattened bull had been offered as a whole burnt offering on the newly built altar.
6:29 Each said to his neighbour, “Who did this thing?” They examined and searched, and said, “Gideon son of Joash did this thing.”
6:30 The men of the city said to Joash, “Bring out your son, and let him die, for he has demolished Baal’s altar and cut down the grove beside it.”
6:31 Joash said to the men who stood against him, “Do you now plead for Baal? Do you save him? Whoever disputed with him shall die by morning. If he is a god, he himself shall avenge him, for one has demolished his altar.”
6:32 On that day he called it Tribunal of Baal, because he demolished his altar.
6:33 All Midian, Amalek, and the sons of the east gathered together, crossed over, and camped in the valley of Jezreel.
6:34 The Spirit of God clothed Gideon. He sounded a horn trumpet, and Abiezer cried out behind him.
6:35 He sent messengers throughout Manasseh, and it too cried out behind him. He sent messengers through Asher, Zebulun, and Naphtali, and they came up to meet him.
6:36 And Gideon said to God, “If Thou art saving Israel by my hand, as Thou hast spoken,
6:37 behold, I lay a fleece of wool on the threshing floor. If dew comes upon the fleece alone, while all the ground is dry, then I shall know that Thou art saving Israel by my hand, as Thou hast spoken.”
6:38 It was so. Gideon rose early the next day and squeezed the fleece. The dew flowed from the fleece, a basin full of water.
6:39 Gideon said to God, “Let not Thy wrath be kindled against me, and let me speak once more. Let me test once more with the fleece: let the fleece alone be dry, and let dew be upon all the ground.”
6:40 God did so that night. The fleece alone was dry, and dew was on all the ground.

JUDGES 7

7:1 Jerubbaal—that is, Gideon—and all the people with him rose early and camped in the land of Aroed. The camp of Midian and Amalek was north of him, by the hill of Abor in the valley.
7:2 The Lord said to Gideon, “The people with you are too many for Me to deliver Midian into their hand, lest Israel boast against Me, saying, ‘My own hand saved me.’”
7:3 The Lord said to him, “Speak now in the people’s hearing, saying, ‘Who is cowardly and afraid? Let him turn back.’” They departed from Mount Gilead, and twenty-two thousand of the people turned back; ten thousand remained.
7:4 The Lord said to Gideon, “The people are still too many. Bring them down to the water, and there I will test them for you. If I say to you of someone, ‘This one shall go with you,’ he shall go with you; and if I say he shall not go with you, he shall not go with you.”
7:5 He brought the people down to the water. And the Lord said to Gideon, “Everyone who laps water with his tongue, as a dog laps, set him apart; and everyone who bends down on his knees to drink, set him aside by himself.”
7:6 The whole number of those who lapped with their tongue was three hundred men. All the rest of the people bent upon their knees to drink water.
7:7 The Lord said to Gideon, “By the three hundred men who lapped I will save you all and deliver Midian into your hand. Let all the other people depart, each to his place.”
7:8 They took the people’s provisions and their horn trumpets into their hands. He sent every man of Israel to his dwelling, but retained the three hundred men. Midian’s camp was below him in the valley.
7:9 That night the Lord said to him, “Arise, go down quickly from here into the camp, for I have delivered it into your hand.
7:10 But if you fear to go down, go down to the camp with Purah your servant,
7:11 and hear what they say. Then your hands shall be strengthened, and you shall go down into the camp.” And he and Purah his servant went down to a section of the fifty in the camp.
7:12 Midian, Amalek, and all the sons of the east were encamped in the valley like locusts in multitude. Their camels were beyond numbering, like sand on the seashore in multitude.
7:13 Gideon entered, and look, a man was explaining his dream to his neighbour. He said, “Look at the dream I dreamed: a cake of barley bread was rolling through Midian’s camp. It came to Midian’s tent, struck it, and overturned it, and the tent fell.”
7:14 His neighbour answered, “This is nothing but the sword of Gideon son of Joash, a man of Israel. The Lord has given Midian and the whole camp into his hand.”
7:15 When Gideon heard the dream’s account and its interpretation, he worshiped the Lord. He returned to Israel’s camp and said, “Arise, for the Lord has given Midian’s camp into your hands.”
7:16 He divided the three hundred men into three companies and put horn trumpets into all their hands, and empty water jars with torches inside the jars.
7:17 He said to them, “Look at me and do likewise. Behold, I enter the midst of the camp; as I do, so shall you do.
7:18 When I and all who are with me sound the horn trumpet, you also shall sound the horn trumpets all around the camp and say, ‘For the Lord and for Gideon!’”
7:19 Gideon and the hundred men with him entered a section of the camp as the middle watch began. He thoroughly roused the guards, and they sounded the horn trumpets and dashed the jars in their hands.
7:20 The three companies sounded the horn trumpets and shattered the jars. They grasped the torches in their left hands, with the horn trumpets in their right hands to sound them, and cried, “A sword for the Lord and for Gideon!”
7:21 Each stood by himself around the camp. The whole camp ran; they sounded the alarm and fled.
7:22 The three hundred horn trumpets sounded, and the Lord set each man’s sword against his neighbour throughout the camp. The camp fled as far as Beth-shittah and, gathered together, as far as the edge of Abel-meholah and toward Tabbath.
7:23 The men of Israel cried out from Naphtali, Asher, and all Manasseh, and they pursued Midian.
7:24 Gideon sent messengers throughout Ephraim’s territory, saying, “Go down to meet Midian and seize for yourselves the water as far as Beth-barah, and the Jordan.” All the men of Ephraim cried out, and they seized the water beforehand as far as Beth-barah, and the Jordan.
7:25 They captured Midian’s two rulers, Oreb and Zeeb. They killed Oreb in Sourin and Zeeb in Jakephzeeb, and pursued Midian. They brought the heads of Oreb and Zeeb to Gideon from beyond the Jordan.

JUDGES 8

8:2 He said to them, “What have I now done compared with you? Are not Ephraim’s gleanings better than Abiezer’s vintage?
8:3 The Lord gave Midian’s rulers, Oreb and Zeeb, into your hand. What could I do compared with you?” And they stopped. Then their spirit relaxed toward him when he said this.
8:4 Gideon came to the Jordan and crossed over, he and the three hundred men with him, fainthearted and hungry.
8:5 He said to the men of Succoth, “Please give bread to the people with me, for they are hungry, and I am pursuing Zebah and Zalmunna, Midian’s kings.”
8:6 And the rulers of Succoth said, “Is the hand of Zebah and Zalmunna already in your hand, that we should give bread to your army?”
8:7 Gideon said, “Not so! When the Lord gives Zebah and Zalmunna into my hand, I will tear your flesh with the wilderness thorns and the barkonnim.”
8:8 He went up from there to Penuel and spoke to them in the same way. The men of Penuel answered him as the men of Succoth had answered.
8:9 He said to the men of Penuel, “When I return in peace, I will demolish this tower.”
8:10 Zebah and Zalmunna were in Karkar, with their camp of about fifteen thousand—those left in the whole camp of the sons of the east. Those who had fallen were one hundred twenty thousand men who drew the sword.
8:11 Gideon went up by the route of the tent-dwellers, east of Nabeth, opposite Zebah. He struck the camp while the camp was confident.
8:12 Zebah and Zalmunna fled. He pursued them and seized Midian’s two kings, Zebah and Zalmunna, and wiped out their whole camp.
8:13 Gideon son of Joash returned from the battle by the ascent of Ares.
8:14 They captured a young man from Succoth, and he questioned him. The young man recorded for them Succoth’s rulers and elders, seventy-seven men.
8:15 And Gideon came to the rulers of Succoth and said, “Behold Zebah and Zalmunna, concerning whom you reproached me, saying, ‘Is the hand of Zebah and Zalmunna already in your hand, that we should give bread to your weary men?’”
8:16 He took the city’s rulers and elders and tore them with the wilderness thorns and the barakēnim; with these he tore the men of Succoth.
8:17 He demolished Penuel’s tower and killed the men of the city.
8:18 And he said to Zebah and Zalmunna, “Where are the men whom you killed at Tabor?” And they said, “Like you, resembling you, resembling them, in appearance as the form of the sons of kings.”
8:19 And Gideon said, “They were my brothers, the sons of my mother.” And he swore to them, “As the Lord lives, if you had kept them alive, I would not kill you.”
8:20 He said to Jether his firstborn, “Arise, kill them.” But his boy did not draw his sword, because he was afraid, for he was young.
8:21 Zebah and Zalmunna said, “You arise and fall upon us; for as the man is, so is his strength.” Gideon arose and killed Zebah and Zalmunna, and took the crescents from their camels’ necks.
8:22 And the men of Israel said to Gideon, “Rule among us, you and your sons; for you have saved us from the hand of Midian.”
8:23 Gideon said to them, “I will not rule over you, and my son will not rule over you. The Lord will rule over you.”
8:24 Gideon said to them, “Let me ask one thing of you: each of you give me an earring from his spoils.” For they had many gold earrings, because they were Ishmaelites.
8:25 They said, “We will certainly give.” He spread out his cloak, and each threw there a gold earring from his spoils.
8:26 The weight of the gold earrings he requested was one thousand seven hundred shekels of gold, apart from the sirōnes, the small necklaces called enphōth, and the purple cloaks worn by Midian’s kings, and apart from the gold collars on their camels’ necks.
8:27 Gideon made it into an ephod and set it up in his city, Ephrathah. All Israel prostituted themselves after it there, and it became a stumbling block to Gideon and his household.
8:28 Midian was put to shame before the sons of Israel and no longer lifted its head. The land was quiet for forty years in Gideon’s days.
8:29 Jerubbaal son of Joash went and dwelt in his house.
8:30 Gideon had seventy sons who came from his loins, for he had many wives.
8:31 His concubine in Shechem also bore him a son, and he gave him the name Abimelech.
8:32 Gideon son of Joash died at a good old age and was buried in his father Joash’s tomb, in Ephrathah of the father of Abiezri.
8:33 When Gideon died, the sons of Israel turned back and prostituted themselves after the Baalim. They set Baal-berith in a covenant for themselves, to be their god.
8:34 The sons of Israel did not remember the Lord their God, who rescued them from the hand of all their surrounding enemies.
8:35 They did not show mercy to the household of Jerubbaal—Gideon—in return for all the goodness he had shown toward Israel.

JUDGES 9

9:1 Abimelech son of Jerubbaal went to Shechem, to his mother’s brothers, and spoke to them and to all the kindred of his mother’s household, saying,
9:2 “Speak now in the hearing of the men of Shechem: ‘Which is better, that seventy men, all the sons of Jerubbaal, should rule over you, or that one man should be your lord?’ And remember that I am your flesh and your bone.”
9:3 His mother’s brothers spoke all these words about him in the hearing of all Shechem’s men. Their hearts inclined toward Abimelech, for they said, “He is our brother.”
9:4 They gave him seventy pieces of silver from the house of Baal of the Covenant. With them Abimelech hired empty and startled men, who followed him.
9:5 He entered his father’s house at Ephrathah and killed his brothers, Jerubbaal’s sons, seventy men upon one stone. But Jotham, Jerubbaal’s youngest son, remained, because he hid himself.
9:6 All Shechem’s men and the whole house of Maallon gathered, went, and made Abimelech king beside the oak of the standing-place in Shechem.
9:7 They told Jotham. He went and stood on Mount Gerizim’s summit, raised his voice, and called out, saying to them, “Listen to me, men of Shechem, and may God listen to you.
9:8 The trees went out to anoint a king for themselves. They said to the olive tree, ‘Reign over us.’
9:9 The olive tree said to them, ‘Should I leave my richness, which God and human beings have glorified in me, and go rule the trees?’
9:10 The trees said to the fig tree, ‘Come, reign over us.’
9:11 The fig tree said to them, ‘Should I leave my sweetness and my good produce and go rule the trees?’
9:12 The trees said to the vine, ‘Come, reign over us.’
9:13 The vine said to them, ‘Should I leave my wine, human beings’ gladness from God, and go rule the trees?’
9:14 The trees said to the bramble, ‘You come and reign over us.’
9:15 And the bramble said to the trees, ‘If in truth you anoint me king over you, come and trust in my shelter. But if not, let fire come forth from the bramble and consume the cedars of Lebanon.’
9:16 And now, if you dealt in truth and integrity when you made Abimelech king, and if you dealt well with Jerubbaal and his house, and did to him according to the recompense of his hand—
9:17 for my father fought for you, cast his life into danger, and rescued you from Midian’s hand,
9:18 yet you rose against my father's house today and killed his sons, seventy men upon one stone, and made Abimelech, the son of his slave girl, king over the men of Shechem, because he is your brother—
9:19 if then you have dealt in truth and integrity with Jerubbaal and his house this day, may you be blessed and rejoice in Abimelech, and may he also rejoice in you.
9:20 But if not, may fire come out from Abimelech and consume Shechem’s men and the house of Maallon; and if not, may fire come out from Shechem’s men and the house of Maallon and consume Abimelech.”
9:21 Jotham ran away, went on the road, and fled to Rara. He dwelt there because of Abimelech his brother.
9:22 Abimelech ruled over Israel for three years.
9:23 God sent an evil spirit between Abimelech and Shechem’s men, and Shechem’s men acted faithlessly toward Abimelech’s house,
9:24 so as to bring back the wrong against Jerubbaal’s seventy sons and lay their blood upon Abimelech their brother, who killed them, and upon Shechem’s men, who strengthened his hands to kill his brothers.
9:25 Shechem’s men set an ambush against him on the mountaintops. They plundered everyone who passed them on the road, and Abimelech was told.
9:26 Gaal son of Abed and his brothers came to Shechem, and Shechem’s men trusted him.
9:27 They went into the field, harvested their vineyards, trod the grapes, and held dances. They entered their god’s house, ate and drank, and cursed Abimelech.
9:28 Gaal son of Abed said, “What is Abimelech, and who is the son of Shechem, that we should serve him? Is he not Jerubbaal’s son, and is not Zebul his overseer, his slave together with the men of Hamor, Shechem’s father? Why should we serve him?
9:29 Who would give this people into my hand? I will remove Abimelech and say to him, ‘Increase your army and come out!’”
9:30 Zebul, the city’s ruler, heard the words of Gaal son of Abed, and he grew furious.
9:31 He sent messengers to Abimelech with gifts, saying, “Behold, Gaal son of Abed and his brothers have come to Shechem, and these men besiege the city against you.
9:32 Now arise by night, you and the people with you, and lie in ambush in the field.
9:33 In the morning, as the sun rises, rise early and advance on the city. Behold, he and the people with him will come out against you; then do to him whatever your hand finds possible.”
9:34 Abimelech and all the people with him arose by night and lay in ambush against Shechem in four companies.
9:35 Morning came, and Gaal son of Abed went out and stood at the entrance of the city gate. Abimelech and the people with him arose from the ambushes.
9:36 Gaal son of Abed saw the people and said to Zebul, “Behold, people are coming down from the mountaintops.” And Zebul said to him, “You see the mountains’ shadow as men.”
9:37 Gaal spoke again, saying, “Behold, people are coming down toward the sea from beside the Navel of the Land, and one company is arriving by the way of the Watchers’ Oak.”
9:38 Zebul said to him, “Where now is your mouth that said, ‘Who is Abimelech, that we should serve him?’ Behold, are these not the people you counted as nothing? Go out now and fight them.”
9:39 Gaal went out from before Shechem’s men and fought Abimelech.
9:40 Abimelech pursued him, and he fled before him. Many fell slain as far as the city’s entrances.
9:41 Abimelech stayed in Arima. Zebul drove Gaal and his brothers out so that they could not dwell in Shechem.
9:42 The next day the people went out into the plain, and Abimelech was told.
9:43 He took the people, divided them into three companies, and lay in ambush there. He saw the people coming out of the city, rose against them, and struck them.
9:44 Abimelech and the companies with him advanced and stood beside the city gate. The two companies poured out upon everyone in the field, and he struck them.
9:45 Abimelech fought in the city all that day. They captured the city, and he killed the people in it, demolished the city, and sowed it with salt.
9:46 All the men of Shechem’s Tower heard and entered the stronghold of the house of Baal of the Covenant.
9:47 Abimelech was told that all the men of Shechem’s Tower had gathered.
9:48 And Abimelech went up Mount Zalmon, he and all the people with him. And Abimelech took an axe in his hand, and cut a load of wood, and lifted it upon his shoulders. And he said to the people with him, “What you have seen me do, do quickly as I have done.”
9:49 They too each cut a load, lifted it, and followed Abimelech. They put it against the stronghold and burned the stronghold with fire over those inside. All the people of Shechem’s Tower died, about a thousand men and women.
9:50 Abimelech went to Thebez, besieged it, and captured it.
9:51 There was a fortified tower inside the city. All the men and women and all the city’s leaders fled there, shut themselves in, and climbed onto the tower’s roof.
9:52 Abimelech came to the tower, and they fought against him. Abimelech approached the tower entrance to burn it with fire.
9:53 A woman threw a fragment of a millstone upon Abimelech’s head and crushed his skull.
9:54 And he called quickly to the young man who bore his armour, and said to him, “Draw your sword and kill me, lest they say, ‘A woman killed him.’” And his young man pierced him, and he died.
9:55 The men of Israel saw that Abimelech was dead, and each went to his place.
9:56 God returned the wickedness Abimelech had done to his father by killing his seventy brothers.
9:57 God returned all the wickedness of Shechem’s men upon their own heads, and the curse of Jotham son of Jerubbaal came upon them.

11:16 when they came up from Egypt. Instead, Israel went through the wilderness as far as the Red Sea and came as far as Kadesh.

11:20 Sihon was unwilling for Israel to pass through his territory. Sihon gathered all his people, camped at Jahaz, and fought Israel.
11:21 The Lord, the God of Israel, gave Sihon and all his people into Israel’s hand, and Israel struck them. Israel inherited all the land of the Amorites who dwelt in the land.
11:22 Israel inherited all the Amorites’ territory, from the Arnon to the Jabbok and from the wilderness to the Jordan.
11:23 And now the Lord, the God of Israel, has removed the Amorites before His people Israel. Will you inherit it for yourself?
11:24 Will you not inherit whatever Chemosh your god has allotted you? And all that the Lord our God has allotted before us, we will inherit.
11:25 And now, are you better than Balak the son of Zippor, king of Moab? Did he ever contend with Israel, or fight against them?
11:26 With the house of Israel in Heshbon and its dependent towns, in Jazer and its dependent towns, and in all the cities beside the Jordan for three hundred years, why did they not rescue them during that time?
11:27 I have not sinned against you, but you do wickedly toward me by fighting me. May the Lord, who judges, judge this day between the sons of Israel and the sons of Ammon.”

11:29 The Spirit of the Lord came upon Jephthah. He passed through the land of Gilead and Manasseh, passed through Gilead’s Watchpost, and from Gilead’s Watchpost crossed over toward the sons of Ammon.
11:30 And Jephthah vowed a vow to the Lord, and said, “If Thou wilt indeed deliver the sons of Ammon into my hand,

11:32 Jephthah crossed toward the sons of Ammon to fight them, and the Lord gave them into his hand.
11:33 He struck them from Aroer as far as Semoith—twenty cities—and as far as Abel of the Vineyards, with a very great blow. The sons of Ammon were put to shame before the sons of Israel.
11:34 Jephthah came to his house at Mizpah, and look, his daughter was coming out to meet him with tambourines and dances. She was his only, beloved child; apart from her he had neither son nor daughter.
11:35 When he saw her, he tore his garments and said, “Alas, my daughter! You have entangled my feet; you have become a thorn in my eyes. I have opened my mouth concerning you to the Lord, and I cannot turn back.”
11:36 She said to him, “My father, if you have opened your mouth concerning me to the Lord, do to me according to what has gone out from your mouth, in return for the vengeance the Lord has taken for you on your enemies, the sons of Ammon.”
11:37 She said to her father, “Do this thing for me: leave me for two months, so that I may go down upon the mountains and weep over my virginity, I and my companions.”
11:38 He said, “Go,” and sent her away for two months. She and her companions went, and she wept over her virginity upon the mountains.
11:39 At the end of two months she returned to her father, and Jephthah fulfilled the vow he had vowed. She had not known a man. It became an ordinance in Israel:
11:40 year by year, Israel’s daughters went together to lament the daughter of Jephthah the Gileadite, four days in the year.

JUDGES 13

13:1 The sons of Israel again did evil before the Lord, and the Lord gave them into the foreigners’ hand for forty years.
13:2 There was a man from Zorah, from the tribe of Dan, whose name was Manoah. His wife was barren and had not borne a child.
13:3 And the messenger of the Lord appeared to the woman and said to her, “Behold, you are barren and have borne no child; but you shall conceive and bear a son.
13:4 Now take care: do not drink wine or sikera, and do not eat anything unclean.
13:5 For behold, you shall conceive and bear a son, and no iron shall come upon his head; for the child shall be a sanctified Nazirite to God from the womb, and he shall begin to save Israel from the hand of the foreigners.”
13:6 The woman came and told her husband, saying, “A man of God came to me. His appearance was like the appearance of God’s messenger, very glorious. I asked where he was from, and he did not tell me his name.
13:7 And he said to me, ‘Behold, you shall conceive and bear a son. Now drink no wine or sikera, and eat nothing unclean; for the child shall be a Nazirite of God from the womb until the day of his death.’”
13:8 And Manoah entreated the Lord, saying, “I beseech Thee, Lord, let the man of God whom Thou didst send to us come to us and enlighten us concerning what we should do for the child who shall be born.”
13:9 God listened to Manoah’s voice, and God’s messenger came again to the woman while she was sitting in the field. Her husband Manoah was not with her.
13:10 The woman hurried and ran out to tell her husband. She said to him, “Behold, the man who came to me that day has appeared to me.”
13:11 And Manoah arose and followed his wife to the man, and said to him, “Are you the man who spoke to the woman?” And the messenger said, “I am.”
13:12 Manoah said, “Now, when your word comes to pass, what shall the rule for the child be, and what shall his deeds be?”
13:13 The Lord’s messenger said to Manoah, “Let her guard against everything I told the woman.
13:14 She shall not eat anything that comes from the vine; she must not drink wine or sikera, nor eat anything unclean. She must observe everything I commanded her.”
13:15 Manoah said to the Lord’s messenger, “Please let us urge you to stay and prepare a young goat before you.”
13:16 The Lord’s messenger said to Manoah, “Though you urge me to stay, I will not eat your loaves. But if you prepare a whole burnt offering, offer it to the Lord.” For Manoah did not know that he was the Lord’s messenger.

15:4 Samson went and caught three hundred foxes. He took torches, tied tail to tail, and put one torch in the middle between each pair of tails.
15:5 He set fire to the torches and released them into the foreigners’ sheaves. He burned the grain ears and the harvested crop, from the heaps to the standing grain, and to the vineyard and olive trees.
15:6 The foreigners said, “Who did these things?” They said, “Samson, the Timnite’s son-in-law, because the man took his wife and gave her to his companion.” The foreigners went up and burned her father’s house, her, and her father with fire.
15:7 Samson said to them, “If you do this, I shall not be pleased; I will take my vengeance from each and every one of you.”

15:9 The foreigners went up, camped against Judah, and spread out at Lehi.
15:10 All the men of Judah said to them, “Why have you come up against us?” The foreigners said, “To bind Samson and do to him as he did to us.”

15:14 He came as far as Jawbone, and the foreigners shouted and ran to meet him. The Spirit of the Lord came straight upon him, and the cords on his arms became like flax fibre when it smells fire; the bonds came loose from his arms.
15:15 He found a donkey’s jawbone thrown on the road, reached out his hand, took it, and struck a thousand men with it.
15:16 Samson said, “With a donkey’s jawbone I utterly wiped them out, for with a donkey’s jawbone I struck a thousand men.”
15:17 When he finished speaking, he threw the jawbone from his hand and called that place Lifting of the Jawbone.
15:18 And he thirsted greatly and cried to the Lord, saying, “Thou hast given this great salvation by the hand of Thy servant. And now shall I die of thirst, and fall into the hand of the uncircumcised?”
15:19 God opened the wound of the jawbone, and water came out of it. He drank; his spirit returned within him, and he was refreshed. Therefore its name was called Invoked Spring of the Jawbone, to this day.
15:20 He judged Israel in the foreigners’ days for twenty years.

JUDGES 16

16:5 The foreigners’ satraps came up to her and said, “Deceive him and learn where his great strength lies, and how we may prevail against him and bind him to humble him. Each of us will give you eleven hundred pieces of silver.”
16:6 Delilah said to Samson, “Tell me where your great strength lies, and how you may be bound to humble you.”
16:7 Samson said to her, “If they bind me with seven moist sinew-strings that have not dried, I will become weak and be like any other man.”
16:8 The foreigners’ satraps brought her seven moist sinew-strings that had not dried, and she bound him with them.
16:9 Now those lying in ambush for him waited in the inner room. She said to him, “The foreigners are upon you, Samson!” He broke the sinew-strings as a thread of flax waste breaks when it smells fire; and his strength was not known.
16:10 Delilah said to Samson, “Behold, you have deceived me and spoken falsehoods to me. Please tell me now how you may be bound.”
16:11 He said to her, “If they bind me tightly with seven new cords that have never been used for work, I will become weak and be like any other man.”
16:12 Delilah took new cords for him and bound him with them, and said to him, “The foreigners are upon you, Samson!” Those lying in ambush waited in the inner room. He tore the cords from his arms like a thread.
16:13 Delilah said to Samson, “Until now you have deceived me and spoken falsehoods to me. Please tell me how you may be bound.” He said to her, “If you weave the seven strands of my head into the warp and drive it with the peg into the wall, I shall be weak as any other man.”
16:14 And Delilah put him to sleep, and wove the seven curls of his head into the stretched fabric, and drove it with pegs into the wall. And she said to him, “The foreigners are upon you, Samson!” And he awoke from his sleep, and tore the pegs with the woven fabric from the wall, and the warp; and his strength was not known.
16:15 Delilah said to him, “How can you say, ‘I love you,’ when your heart is not with me? This is the third time you have deceived me and have not told me where your great strength lies.”
16:16 She wore him down with her words all night and troubled him until he grew weary to the point of death.
16:17 He told her everything from his heart and said to her, “No razor shall come upon my head, for I am a Nazirite of God from my mother’s womb. If I am shaved, my strength will depart from me; I will become weak and be like everyone else.”
16:18 Delilah saw that he had told her everything from his heart. She sent and called all the foreigners’ satraps, saying, “Come up this time, for he has told me all his heart.” All the foreigners’ satrapies came up to her, bringing the silver in their hands.
16:19 She put him to sleep between her knees, called the barber, and had the seven curls of his head shaved. He began to be humbled, and his strength departed from him.
16:20 Delilah said to him, “The foreigners are upon you, Samson!” He awoke from his sleep and said, “I will go out as at other times and shake myself free.” But he did not know that the Lord had departed from him.
16:21 The foreigners seized him and dug out his eyes. They brought him down to Gaza, bound him with bronze fetters, and he ground grain in the prison house.
16:22 The hair of his head began to grow again after he had been shaved.
16:23 The foreigners’ satraps gathered to offer a great sacrifice to Dagon their god and to rejoice. They said, “Our god has given Samson our enemy into our hand.”
16:24 The people saw him and praised their gods, saying, “Our god has given our enemy into our hand, the one who devastated our land and multiplied our slain.”
16:25 When their hearts were merry, they said, “Call Samson from the prison house, so that he may perform before us.” They called Samson from the prison house, mocked him, and placed him between the two pillars.
16:26 Samson said to the boy leading him by the hand, “Please let me rest and feel the pillars on which the house is supported, so that I may lean upon them.” The boy did so.
16:27 The house was full of men and women, and all the foreigners’ satraps were there. On the roof were about three thousand men and women watching Samson being mocked.
16:28 Samson cried out to the Lord and said, “Lord, Lord, remember me and strengthen me just this once more, so that I may take one vengeance on the foreigners for my two eyes.”
16:29 Samson embraced the two middle pillars on which the house was supported and leaned against them, one with his right hand and one with his left.
16:30 Samson said, “Let my soul die with the foreigners.” He bent with strength, and the house fell upon the satraps and all the people in it. Those Samson killed in his death were more than those he killed in his life.
16:31 His brothers and all his father’s household came down, took him, brought him up, and buried him between Zorah and Eshtaol, in the tomb of his father Manoah. He had judged Israel for twenty years.

20:31 The sons of Benjamin came out to meet the people and were drawn away from the city. As before, they began to strike some of the people on the roads—one going up to Bethel and one going up to Gibeah in the field—about thirty men of Israel.
20:32 The sons of Benjamin said, “They are stumbling before us as before.” But the sons of Israel said, “Let us flee and draw them out of the city to the roads.”
20:33 All the men of Israel arose from their place and drew up at Baal-tamar. Israel’s ambush struggled forward from its place, from west of Gibeah.
20:34 Ten thousand chosen men from all Israel came opposite Gibeah, and the battle grew heavy. They did not know that disaster had reached them.
20:35 The Lord routed Benjamin before Israel. That day the sons of Israel struck down twenty-five thousand one hundred men of Benjamin, all men who drew the sword.
20:36 Benjamin saw that it was routed. The men of Israel had given ground to Benjamin because they trusted the ambush they had set against Gibeah.
20:37 The ambush rushed forward and poured toward Gibeah. The ambush went on, and they struck the whole city with the edge of the sword.
20:38 The arrangement between Israel’s men and the ambush was for them to send up a smoke signal from the city.
20:39 The men of Israel turned in the battle, and Benjamin began to strike down men of Israel, killing about thirty, saying, “Surely they are being routed before us as in the earlier battle.”
20:40 The signal began to ascend from the city, a pillar of smoke. Benjamin looked behind them, and look, the city’s destruction rose to heaven.
20:41 The men of Israel turned back, and the men of Benjamin hurried and saw that disaster had reached them.
20:42 They turned before Israel’s men toward the wilderness road, but the battle overtook them. Those from the cities destroyed them in their midst.
20:43 They cut down Benjamin to put them to rest, and trampled them as far as opposite Gibeah on the east.
20:44 Eighteen thousand men of Benjamin fell; with all these were mighty men.
20:45 They turned and fled into the wilderness, to the rock of Rimmon. The Israelites gleaned five thousand men on the roads, pressed after them as far as Gadaam, and struck down two thousand of them.
20:46 All those of Benjamin who fell that day were twenty-five thousand men who drew the sword; with all these were mighty men.
20:47 Six hundred men turned and fled into the wilderness, to the rock of Rimmon. They stayed at the rock of Rimmon for four months.
20:48 The men of Israel hemmed in the sons of Benjamin and struck them with the edge of the sword, from one city to the next, down to the livestock and everything found in all the cities. They set fire to the cities they found.

JUDGES 21

21:1 The men of Israel had sworn at Mizpah, saying, “None of us shall give his daughter to Benjamin as a wife.”
21:2 All the people came to Mizpah and Bethel and sat there before God until evening. They raised their voices and wept bitterly.
21:3 They said, “Why, Lord, God of Israel, has this happened in Israel, that one tribe should be visited today in Israel?”
21:4 The next day the people rose early, built an altar there, and offered whole burnt offerings of deliverance.
21:5 The sons of Israel said, “Who among all the tribes of Israel did not come up in the assembly to the Lord?” For there was a great oath concerning anyone who did not come up to the Lord at Mizpah, saying, “He shall surely die.”
21:6 The sons of Israel were comforted concerning Benjamin their brother and said, “Today one tribe has been removed from Israel.
21:7 What shall we do for wives for those who remain? We have sworn by the Lord not to give them any of our daughters as wives.”
21:8 They said, “Which one of the tribes of Israel did not come up to the Lord at Mizpah?” And behold, no man from Jabesh-gilead had come to the camp, to the assembly.
21:9 When the people were inspected, behold, no man from the inhabitants of Jabesh-gilead was there.
21:10 The congregation sent twelve thousand mighty men there and commanded them, saying, “Go and strike all the inhabitants of Jabesh-gilead with the edge of the sword, including the women and the people.
21:11 This is what you shall do: put under the ban every male, and every woman who has known the bed of a male.”
21:12 Among the inhabitants of Jabesh-gilead they found four hundred young virgin women who had not known a man in the bed of a male. They brought them to the camp at Shiloh, which is in the land of Canaan.
21:13 The whole congregation sent word and spoke to Benjamin at the rock of Rimmon, and called them to peace.
21:14 Benjamin returned to the sons of Israel at that time, and they gave them the women who were from the women of Jabesh-gilead. And this pleased them.
21:15 The people were comforted concerning Benjamin, because the Lord had made a breach in the tribes of Israel.
21:16 The elders of the congregation said, “What shall we do for wives for those who remain, since the women have been made to disappear from Benjamin?”
21:17 They said, “An inheritance is preserved for Benjamin, and a tribe shall certainly not be blotted out from Israel.
21:18 But we cannot give them wives from our daughters, for we, the sons of Israel, have sworn, saying, ‘Cursed is the one who gives a wife to Benjamin.’”
21:19 They said, “There is a feast for the Lord at Shiloh year after year. It is north of Bethel, toward the sunrise on the road that goes up from Bethel to Shechem, and south of Lebanon, of Lebona.”
21:20 They commanded the sons of Benjamin, saying, “Go over and lie in ambush in the vineyards.
21:21 Watch, and when the daughters of the inhabitants of Shiloh come out in Shiloh to dance in the dances, come out of the vineyards and seize, each man for himself, a wife from the daughters of Shiloh. Then go to the land of Benjamin.
21:22 When their fathers or brothers come to contend with you, we shall say to them, ‘Have mercy on them, for they did not each take a wife in the war. For you have not given the women to them; at that time you committed an offence.’”
21:23 The sons of Benjamin did so and took wives according to their number from the dancers whom they snatched away. They went and returned to their inheritance, built cities for themselves, and lived in them.
21:24 The sons of Israel went away from there at that time, each to his tribe and his kindred. They departed from there, each to his inheritance.
21:25 In those days there was no king in Israel. Everyone did what was right in his own eyes.
