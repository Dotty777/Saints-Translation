Sirach — Translator’s Prologue

Read this chapter on staging

Prologue 1 Since many great things have been given to us through the law, the prophets, and the others who followed after them, for which Israel ought to be praised for instruction and wisdom, and since the readers themselves ought not only to become knowledgeable,

Prologue 1a but lovers of learning should also be able to benefit those outside, both by speaking and by writing, my grandfather Jesus, having devoted himself extensively to reading the law and the prophets

Prologue 1b and the other ancestral books, and having acquired considerable familiarity with them, was himself moved to write something concerning instruction and wisdom, so that lovers of learning, by becoming engaged with these things too, might advance much further through a life according to the law.

Prologue 1c You are therefore urged to read with goodwill and attention, and to be forgiving wherever we may seem

Prologue 1d to fall short in some expressions despite the labor devoted to translation. For things expressed in Hebrew in their own form do not have equal force when transferred into another language. Not only these things, but even the law itself, the prophecies,

Prologue 1e and the remaining books show no small difference when expressed in their own form. For in the thirty-eighth year, under King Euergetes, I arrived in Egypt and stayed for some time; finding a likeness of no small learning,

Prologue 1f I myself considered it most necessary to contribute some diligence and hard work toward translating this book. During that time I devoted many wakeful hours and much skill to completing the book and publishing it also for those living abroad who wish to learn,

Prologue 1g preparing their character to live according to the law.

Sirach — Chapter 1

Read this chapter on staging

1:1 All wisdom is from the Lord and is with Him for ever.

1:2 Who can count the sand of the seas, the drops of rain, and the days of eternity?

1:3 Who can trace the height of heaven, the breadth of the earth, the abyss, and wisdom?

1:4 Wisdom was created before all things, and discerning understanding from eternity.

1:6 To whom was the root of wisdom revealed? Who knew her skillful works?

1:8 There is One who is wise, greatly to be feared, seated upon His throne,

1:9 the Lord Himself. He created her, saw her, counted her out, and poured her upon all His works,

1:10 with all flesh according to His giving; He supplied her to those who love Him.

1:11 The fear of the Lord is glory and a boast, gladness and a crown of rejoicing.

1:12 The fear of the Lord will delight the heart and give gladness, joy, and length of days.

1:13 It will go well at the end with the one who fears the Lord, and on the day of his death he will be blessed.

1:14 To fear the Lord is the beginning of wisdom; she was created together with the faithful in the womb.

1:15 Among human beings she nested on an eternal foundation, and among their offspring she will be trusted.

1:16 To fear the Lord is the fullness of wisdom, and she intoxicates them with her fruits.

1:17 She will fill their whole house with desirable things and their storehouses with her produce.

1:18 The fear of the Lord is the crown of wisdom, making peace and the health of healing flourish.

1:19 He saw her and counted her out; He rained down skill and discerning knowledge and raised high the glory of those who hold her fast.

1:20 To fear the Lord is the root of wisdom, and her branches are length of days.

1:22 Unjust anger cannot be justified, for the weight of a man’s anger brings about his fall.

1:23 A patient person will hold out until the right time, and afterward gladness will spring up for him.

1:24 Until the right time he will conceal his words, and the lips of many will recount his understanding.

1:25 In wisdom’s treasuries are sayings of knowledge, but reverence for God is an abomination to a sinner.

1:26 If you desire wisdom, keep the commandments, and the Lord will supply her to you.

1:27 For wisdom and instruction are the fear of the Lord, and faithfulness and gentleness are His good pleasure.

1:28 Do not disobey the fear of the Lord, and do not approach Him with a divided heart.

1:29 Do not play the hypocrite in people’s talk, and keep watch over your lips.

1:30 Do not exalt yourself, lest you fall and bring dishonor upon your soul. The Lord will uncover your hidden things and cast you down in the midst of the assembly, because you did not approach the fear of the Lord and your heart is full of deceit.

Sirach — Chapter 2

Read this chapter on staging

2:1 Child, if you come to serve the Lord, prepare your soul for testing.

2:2 Set your heart straight and endure; do not rush in a time of calamity.

2:3 Cling to Him and do not withdraw, so that you may grow in your last days.

2:4 Accept whatever is brought upon you, and be patient amid the changes of your humiliation.

2:5 For gold is tested in fire, and acceptable people in the furnace of humiliation.

2:6 Trust Him, and He will support you. Set your ways straight and hope in Him.

2:7 You who fear the Lord, wait for His mercy, and do not turn aside, lest you fall.

2:8 You who fear the Lord, trust Him, and your reward will certainly not fail.

2:9 You who fear the Lord, hope for good things, for eternal gladness and mercy.

2:10 Look at the generations of old and see: Who trusted in the Lord and was put to shame? Who remained in His fear and was abandoned? Who called upon Him and was disregarded by Him?

2:11 For the Lord is compassionate and merciful; He forgives sins and saves in a time of affliction.

2:12 Woe to cowardly hearts and slack hands, and to the sinner who walks on two paths!

2:13 Woe to the slack heart, because it does not trust; therefore it will not be sheltered.

2:14 Woe to you who have lost endurance! What will you do when the Lord visits?

2:15 Those who fear the Lord will not disobey His words, and those who love Him will keep His ways.

2:16 Those who fear the Lord will seek His good pleasure, and those who love Him will be filled with the law.

2:17 Those who fear the Lord will prepare their hearts and humble their souls before Him.

2:18 Let us fall into the hands of the Lord and not into human hands, for as His greatness is, so also is His mercy.

Saints English wording notes

2:1 · testing

Meaning. Trial or testing anticipated in the call to serve the Lord.

English comparison. KJV, Brenton, and DRC have temptation. Brenton adds God after Lord; DRC includes additional standing-in-justice-and-fear wording. These do not become automatic additions to the Greek trial.

3:1 Listen to me, your father, children, and act accordingly, so that you may be saved.

3:2 For the Lord has honored a father over his children and confirmed a mother’s authority over her sons.

3:3 The one who honors a father atones for sins,

3:4 and the one who glorifies his mother is like someone storing up treasure.

3:5 The one who honors a father will be gladdened by children, and on the day of his prayer he will be heard.

3:6 The one who glorifies a father will have length of days, and the one who obeys the Lord will give his mother rest;

3:7 he will serve those who brought him into being as masters.

3:8 Honor your father in deed and word, so that a blessing may come upon you from him.

3:9 For a father’s blessing establishes the houses of children, but a mother’s curse uproots foundations.

3:10 Do not glory in your father’s dishonor, for a father’s dishonor is no glory to you.

3:11 For a person’s glory comes from his father’s honor, and a mother in disrepute is a reproach to her children.

3:12 Child, support your father in old age, and do not grieve him during his life.

3:13 Even if he loses understanding, bear with him, and do not dishonor him in all your strength.

3:14 For mercy toward a father will not be forgotten; in place of sins it will be built up for you.

3:15 In the day of your affliction you will be remembered; as fair weather upon frost, so your sins will dissolve.

3:16 The one who abandons a father is like a blasphemer, and the one who provokes his mother is cursed by the Lord.

3:17 Child, carry out your works with gentleness, and you will be loved by an acceptable person.

3:18 The greater you are, the more you must humble yourself, and you will find favor before the Lord,

3:20 because the Lord’s dominion is great, and He is glorified by the humble.

3:21 Do not seek things too difficult for you, and do not investigate things beyond your strength.

3:22 Consider the things commanded you, for you have no need of hidden things.

3:23 Do not busy yourself with matters beyond your tasks, for more than human understanding has been shown to you.

3:24 For their assumption has misled many, and an evil suspicion has caused their thoughts to slip.

3:26 A hard heart will suffer harm at the end, and the one who loves danger will perish in it.

3:27 A hard heart will be weighed down with troubles, and the sinner will add sin upon sins.

3:28 There is no healing for a proud person’s calamity, for a plant of wickedness has taken root in him.

3:29 An understanding heart will consider a proverb, and a listening ear is the desire of a wise person.

3:30 Water will extinguish a blazing fire, and almsgiving will atone for sins.

3:31 The one who repays favors takes thought for what comes afterward, and at the time of his fall he will find support.

Saints English wording notes

3:30 · almsgiving

Meaning. Merciful giving described as making atonement for sins.

Wording. Preserve the atoning statement and the parallel with water extinguishing fire. A translation should neither weaken the verb nor insert a complete explanation of salvation; doctrinal interpretation belongs in commentary.

English comparison. KJV and Brenton say alms maketh an atonement; DRC 3:33 says alms resisteth sins. Greek and Saints number the statement 3:30.

Sirach — Chapter 4

Read this chapter on staging

4:1 Child, do not deprive the poor person of his livelihood, and do not keep needy eyes waiting.

4:2 Do not grieve a hungry soul, and do not provoke a man in his helplessness.

4:3 Do not add trouble to an embittered heart, and do not delay a gift to someone in need.

4:4 Do not reject an afflicted supplicant, and do not turn your face away from a poor person.

4:5 Do not turn your eye away from someone in need, and do not give anyone occasion to curse you.

4:6 For if he curses you in the bitterness of his soul, his Maker will listen to his petition.

4:7 Make yourself welcome to the assembly, and bow your head to a person of high rank.

4:8 Incline your ear to the poor person, and answer him peaceably with gentleness.

4:9 Rescue the one being wronged from the hand of the wrongdoer, and do not lose courage when you judge.

4:10 Be like a father to orphans and in place of a husband to their mother. Then you will be like a son of the Most High, and He will love you more than your mother does.

4:11 Wisdom has raised her sons high and takes hold of those who seek her.

4:12 The one who loves her loves life, and those who rise early to seek her will be filled with gladness.

4:13 The one who holds her fast will inherit glory; wherever he enters, the Lord blesses.

4:14 Those who serve her will minister to the Holy One, and the Lord loves those who love her.

4:15 The one who obeys her will judge nations, and the one who attends to her will dwell confidently.

4:16 If he trusts, he will possess her as an inheritance, and his descendants will hold her in possession.

4:17 For at first she will walk with him on winding paths; she will bring fear and dread upon him and torment him with her discipline until she trusts his soul, and she will test him by her ordinances.

4:18 Then she will return straight to him, gladden him, and reveal her hidden things to him.

4:19 If he goes astray, she will abandon him and hand him over into the grip of his downfall.

4:20 Watch for the right time and guard against evil, and do not be ashamed concerning your soul.

4:21 For there is shame that brings sin, and there is shame that is glory and favor.

4:22 Do not show partiality against your own soul, and do not defer to someone to your own downfall.

4:23 Do not withhold a word at a time of need,

4:24 for wisdom will be known through speech, and instruction through the tongue’s utterance.

4:25 Do not speak against the truth, and be ashamed of your lack of instruction.

4:26 Do not be ashamed to confess your sins, and do not force your way against a river’s current.

4:27 Do not lay yourself down beneath a foolish person, and do not show partiality to someone powerful.

4:28 Struggle for the truth until death, and the Lord God will fight for you.

4:29 Do not be rash with your tongue and sluggish and slack in your deeds.

4:30 Do not be like a lion in your house, indulging vain fancies among your household slaves.

4:31 Do not let your hand be stretched out to receive, but drawn back when repaying.

Saints English wording notes

4:1 · livelihood

Meaning. The means of sustaining life which the command protects.

Wording. Use livelihood as the contextual proposal while recording life as the closer noun. Preserve the second command about keeping needy eyes waiting; it concerns the other person's need.

English comparison. KJV and Brenton have living; DRC narrows the object to alms and changes the eyes clause. The Greek does not name a coin or fixed financial payment.
