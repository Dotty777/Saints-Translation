2 MACCABEES 1

1:1 To the brothers, the Jews throughout Egypt: greetings from the brothers, the Jews in Jerusalem and in the land of Judea, and good peace.
1:2 May God do good to you and remember His covenant with Abraham, Isaac and Jacob, His faithful servants.
1:3 May He give you all a heart to revere Him and do His wishes with a generous heart and a willing soul.
1:4 May He open your heart to His law and His ordinances, and make peace.
1:5 May He hear your petitions, be reconciled to you, and not abandon you in an evil time.
1:6 And now we are here praying for you.
1:7 In the reign of Demetrius, in the one hundred sixty-ninth year, we Jews wrote to you in the affliction and the crisis that came upon us in those years, from the time Jason and those with him revolted from the holy land and the kingdom.
1:8 They burned the gateway and poured out innocent blood. We petitioned the Lord and were heard; we offered sacrifice and fine flour, lit the lamps, and set out the loaves.
1:9 And now, see that you observe the days of the Festival of Booths in the month of Chislev. In the one hundred eighty-eighth year.
1:10 Those in Jerusalem and those in Judea, the council of elders and Judas, to Aristobulus, teacher of King Ptolemy and a member of the family of the anointed priests, and to the Jews in Egypt: greetings and good health.
1:11 Saved by God from great dangers, we give Him great thanks, as those drawn up against a king;
1:12 for He Himself drove out those arrayed against us in the holy city.
1:13 For when the ruler came to Persia with his force, which seemed irresistible, they were cut down in the temple of Nanea, the priests of Nanea having used a deception.
1:14 Antiochus and the friends with him came to the place as though he were to marry her, in order to take the greater part of the money as a dowry.
1:15 The priests of Nanea’s temple laid it out, and he entered the enclosure of the sacred precinct with a few men. Once Antiochus had gone inside, they shut the temple.
1:16 Opening the concealed door in the coffered ceiling, they hurled stones and struck the ruler down. They cut them into pieces, removed their heads, and threw them to those outside.
1:17 Blessed in every way is our God, who handed over those who acted impiously.
1:18 As we are about to observe the purification of the temple on the twenty-fifth of Chislev, we thought it necessary to explain this to you, so that you yourselves may observe the festival of booths and of the fire, when Nehemiah, who rebuilt the temple and the altar, offered sacrifices.
1:19 For when our fathers were being taken to Persia, the devout priests of that time secretly took some of the altar’s fire and hid it in the hollow of a well-like place without water. They secured it there so that the place was unknown to everyone.
1:20 After many years had passed, when it pleased God, Nehemiah was sent by the king of Persia. He sent the descendants of the priests who had hidden it to fetch the fire. When they reported to us that they had found no fire but thick water, he ordered them to draw some and bring it.
1:21 When the sacrificial materials had been brought up, Nehemiah ordered the priests to sprinkle the water on the wood and on what lay upon it.
1:22 When this was done and some time had passed, the sun, previously hidden by clouds, shone out. A great fire was kindled, so that everyone marveled.
1:23 While the sacrifice was being consumed, the priests offered prayer—the priests and everyone—with Jonathan beginning and the others responding, as Nehemiah did.
1:24 The prayer was in this form: “Lord, Lord God, Creator of all, fearsome, strong, righteous and merciful, the only King and good,
1:25 the only Provider, the only righteous One, Almighty and eternal, who dost preserve Israel from every evil, who didst choose the fathers and sanctify them,
1:26 accept the sacrifice on behalf of all Thy people Israel. Protect Thy portion and make it holy.
1:27 Gather our scattered people together. Free those enslaved among the nations. Look upon the despised and abhorred, and let the nations know that Thou art our God.
1:28 Torment those who oppress us and outrage us in arrogance.
1:29 Plant Thy people in Thy holy place, as Moses said.”
1:30 The priests sang the hymns in response.
1:31 When the sacrificial materials had been consumed, Nehemiah ordered that the remaining water also be poured over larger stones.
1:32 When this was done, a flame was kindled, but it was consumed by the light shining back from the altar.
1:33 When the matter became known, the king of the Persians was informed that water had appeared in the place where the deported priests had hidden the fire, and that Nehemiah and his companions had purified the sacrificial materials with it.
1:34 The king fenced off the place and made it sacred, after examining the matter.
1:35 The king received many different revenues and shared them with those whom he favoured.
1:36 Nehemiah and his companions called this “Nephthar,” which is interpreted “purification”; but most people call it “Nephthai.”

2 MACCABEES 2

2:1 In the records it is found that Jeremiah the prophet ordered those who were being deported to take some of the fire, as has been indicated,
2:2 and that the prophet, after giving the law to those being deported, commanded them not to forget the Lord’s ordinances, nor to go astray in their thoughts when they saw gold and silver statues and the adornment surrounding them.
2:3 Saying these and other such things, he urged them not to let the law depart from their heart.
2:4 It was also in the writing that, after receiving an oracle, the prophet ordered the tent and the ark to accompany him, as he went out to the mountain where Moses had gone up and viewed God’s inheritance.
2:5 Jeremiah arrived and found a cave-like chamber. He brought the tent, the ark and the altar of incense inside, and blocked the entrance.
2:6 Some of his companions came to mark the way, but could not find it.
2:7 When Jeremiah learned this, he rebuked them and said, “The place will remain unknown until God gathers His people together and becomes merciful.
2:8 Then the Lord will reveal these things, and the Lord’s glory and the cloud will appear, as they were shown in the time of Moses, and as Solomon also prayed that the place might be greatly sanctified.”
2:9 It was also explained how, being wise, Solomon offered a sacrifice for the dedication and completion of the temple.
2:10 Just as Moses prayed to the Lord, and fire came down from heaven and consumed the sacrificial materials, so Solomon also prayed, and the fire came down and consumed the whole burnt offerings.
2:11 Moses said, “Because the sin offering was not eaten, it was consumed.”
2:12 In the same way Solomon also observed the eight days.
2:13 These same things were related in the records and in the memoirs concerning Nehemiah, and also how, in establishing a library, he gathered together the books concerning the kings and prophets, the writings of David, and kings’ letters concerning dedicated gifts.
2:14 In the same way Judas also gathered all the writings that had been scattered because of the war that befell us, and they are with us.
2:15 Therefore, if you need any of them, send people to bring them to you.
2:16 As we are about to observe the purification, we have written to you. You will do well, then, to observe the days.
2:17 Now God, who saved all His people and restored to all the inheritance, the kingship, the priesthood and the sanctification,
2:18 as He promised through the law—for we hope in God that He will soon have mercy on us and gather us from under heaven into the holy place, for He rescued us from great evils and purified the place.
2:19 The events concerning Judas Maccabeus and his brothers, the purification of the greatest temple and the dedication of the altar,
2:20 and also the wars against Antiochus Epiphanes and his son Eupator,
2:21 and the manifestations from heaven to those who zealously performed brave deeds for Judaism, so that, although few, they plundered the whole land and pursued the barbarian multitudes,
2:22 recovered the temple renowned throughout the inhabited world, freed the city, and restored the laws that were about to be abolished, the Lord having become merciful to them with all gentleness—
2:23 these things, set forth by Jason of Cyrene in five books, we shall attempt to abridge into one composition.
2:24 For, seeing the abundance of figures and the difficulty caused by the mass of material for those who wish to enter into the narratives of the history,
2:25 we have taken thought for the pleasure of those who wish to read, for the ease of those eager to commit it to memory, and for the benefit of everyone who encounters it.
2:26 For us who have undertaken the hard work of abridgement, the task is not easy; it involves sweat and sleeplessness.
2:27 Just as it is not easy for someone preparing a banquet and seeking others’ benefit, nevertheless, for the gratitude of the many, we will gladly bear the hard work.
2:28 We leave the precise treatment of each matter to the historian, while labouring to follow the outlines of the abridgement.
2:29 For just as the architect of a new house must consider its whole foundation, while the person undertaking its encaustic work and painting must examine what is suitable for decoration, so, I think, it is with us.
2:30 To enter deeply into matters, range widely in the discussion, and investigate the particulars belongs to the original historian.
2:31 But the person making a recasting should be permitted to pursue concise expression and set aside an exhaustive treatment of the subject.
2:32 Let us therefore begin the narrative here, adding only this much to what has already been said: it would be foolish to expand the preface to the history while abridging the history itself.

2 MACCABEES 3

3:1 While the holy city was inhabited in complete peace and the laws were being kept most carefully because of the high priest Onias’s piety and hatred of wickedness,
3:2 even the kings themselves honoured the place and glorified the temple with their finest gifts,
3:3 so that Seleucus, king of Asia, also supplied from his own revenues all the expenses required for the service of the sacrifices.
3:4 But a certain Simon, of the tribe of Benjamin, who had been appointed overseer of the temple, disagreed with the high priest over the administration of the city’s market.
3:5 Unable to overcome Onias, he went to Apollonius son of Thraseas, who at that time was governor of Coele-Syria and Phoenicia.
3:6 He reported that the treasury in Jerusalem was full of indescribable riches, so that the amount of money was beyond counting, and that it did not belong to the account for the sacrifices; these funds, he said, could fall under the king’s authority.
3:7 Apollonius met the king and informed him about the money reported to him. The king chose Heliodorus, his chief minister, and sent him with orders to remove that money.
3:8 Heliodorus immediately began his journey, ostensibly to inspect the cities of Coele-Syria and Phoenicia, but actually to carry out the king’s intention.
3:9 When he arrived in Jerusalem and was cordially received by the city’s high priest, he related the information that had been given and explained why he was there. He asked whether these things were really so.
3:10 The high priest explained that they were deposits belonging to widows and orphans,
3:11 and that some also belonged to Hyrcanus son of Tobias, a man of very high standing. Thus the impious Simon had misrepresented matters. In all there were four hundred talents of silver and two hundred of gold.
3:12 It was utterly impossible, he said, that those who had trusted in the holiness of the place and in the dignity and inviolability of the temple honoured throughout the whole world should suffer injustice.
3:13 But Heliodorus, because of his royal instructions, insisted that the money must certainly be taken into the royal treasury.
3:14 He appointed a day and went in to arrange the inspection of these things. There was no small anguish throughout the city.
3:15 The priests threw themselves down before the altar in their priestly robes, calling to heaven upon the One who had legislated about deposits, that He would keep them safe for those who had entrusted them.
3:16 Whoever saw the high priest’s appearance was wounded in mind, for his face and altered complexion revealed his soul’s anguish.
3:17 Dread and bodily shuddering had come over the man, making the pain lodged in his heart plain to those who looked at him.
3:18 People also rushed out of their houses in crowds for a public supplication, because the place was about to be treated with contempt.
3:19 Women, girded with sackcloth beneath their breasts, thronged the streets. Of the secluded virgins, some ran together to the gateways, some to the walls, and others leaned out through the windows.
3:20 All of them stretched their hands toward heaven and offered their entreaty.
3:21 It was pitiful to see the mingled multitude falling prostrate, and the high priest waiting in great anguish.
3:22 So they called on the all-powerful Lord to keep the entrusted property safe and fully secure for those who had entrusted it.
3:23 Heliodorus, however, was carrying out his decision.
3:24 When he was already there at the treasury with his bodyguards, the Sovereign of spirits and of all authority made a great manifestation. All who had dared accompany him were struck with awe at God’s power and were overcome by faintness and cowardice.
3:25 For there appeared to them a horse with a fearsome rider, adorned with the finest harness. Rushing forcefully forward, it struck at Heliodorus with its forehooves; its rider appeared to wear a full suit of golden armour.
3:26 Two other young men appeared to him, remarkable in strength, most beautiful in glory, and splendid in clothing. They stood on either side of him and flogged him without ceasing, raining many blows upon him.
3:27 He suddenly fell to the ground, and deep darkness enveloped him. They picked him up and placed him on a litter.
3:28 The man who had just entered that treasury with a large retinue and all his bodyguard was carried out, unable to help himself. They plainly recognized God’s sovereignty.
3:29 Through the divine action he lay speechless, deprived of every hope and means of deliverance.
3:30 But they blessed the Lord, who had wonderfully honoured His own place. The temple that a little earlier had been full of dread and turmoil was filled with joy and gladness when the Almighty Lord appeared.
3:31 Some of Heliodorus’s companions quickly begged Onias to call upon the Most High to grant life to the man who lay at his last breath.
3:32 The high priest, fearing that the king might form the opinion that the Jews had perpetrated some wrongdoing against Heliodorus, offered a sacrifice for the man’s deliverance.
3:33 While the high priest was making propitiation, the same young men again appeared to Heliodorus, clothed in the same garments. They stood and said, “Give many thanks to Onias the high priest, for it is because of him that the Lord has granted you life.
3:34 You, having been scourged from heaven, proclaim to everyone God’s majestic might.” Having said this, they became invisible.
3:35 Heliodorus offered a sacrifice to the Lord and made very great vows to the One who had preserved his life. He warmly took leave of Onias and departed with his company to the king.
3:36 He bore witness to everyone about the deeds of the greatest God that he had seen with his own eyes.
3:37 When the king asked Heliodorus what sort of person would be suitable to send once more to Jerusalem, he said,
3:38 “If you have an enemy or someone plotting against your government, send him there. You shall receive him back scourged, if indeed he survives, because a certain power of God is truly present about that place.
3:39 For He who has His dwelling in heaven is the Overseer and Helper of that place, and He strikes down and destroys those who come to harm it.”
3:40 This was how the events concerning Heliodorus and the preservation of the treasury unfolded.

2 MACCABEES 4

4:1 The same Simon, who had informed against the funds and his homeland, spoke evil of Onias, alleging that he himself had instigated the attack on Heliodorus and had become the author of the troubles.
4:2 He dared to call the city’s benefactor, the guardian of his fellow countrymen and the zealous defender of the laws a conspirator against the government.
4:3 The hostility advanced so far that murders were committed through one of Simon’s approved supporters.
4:4 Onias saw the danger of the rivalry and that Apollonius son of Menestheus, governor of Coele-Syria and Phoenicia, was increasing Simon’s wickedness.
4:5 He went to the king, not to accuse his fellow citizens but to seek the common and individual benefit of the entire people.
4:6 For he saw that without royal intervention public affairs could no longer find peace and Simon would not cease his folly.
4:7 When Seleucus departed this life and Antiochus, called Epiphanes, took over the kingdom, Jason, Onias’s brother, obtained the high priesthood by fraud.
4:8 In an audience he promised the king three hundred sixty talents of silver, and eighty talents from another source of revenue.
4:9 Beyond these he promised to pay a further one hundred fifty if he were permitted by royal authority to establish a gymnasium and a training institution for young men, and to enroll the people in Jerusalem as Antiochenes.
4:10 When the king consented and Jason gained office, he immediately turned his fellow countrymen toward the Greek manner of life.
4:11 He set aside the benevolent royal concessions established for the Jews through John, the father of Eupolemus, who had undertaken the embassy to the Romans for friendship and alliance. Abolishing the lawful forms of civic life, he introduced unlawful customs.
4:12 For he eagerly established a gymnasium immediately beneath the citadel and brought the most distinguished young men under his direction to wear the broad-brimmed hat.
4:13 There was such an upsurge of Hellenism and advance of foreign ways because of the surpassing impurity of the impious Jason, who was no high priest,
4:14 that the priests were no longer eager for the services of the altar. Despising the temple and neglecting the sacrifices, they hurried to take part in the unlawful provision of exercises in the wrestling ground after the signal for the discus.
4:15 They regarded their ancestral honours as nothing and considered Greek distinctions the finest.
4:16 For this reason a difficult crisis overtook them: those whose ways they admired and whom they wished to resemble in everything became their enemies and punishers.
4:17 For acting impiously against the divine laws is no light matter; the ensuing time will show this.
4:18 When the games held every fifth year were being celebrated in Tyre and the king was present,
4:19 the defiled Jason sent spectators from Jerusalem, as Antiochenes, carrying three hundred silver drachmas for the sacrifice to Heracles. The bearers themselves asked that these not be used for sacrifice, because it was unfitting, but assigned to another expense.
4:20 Thus, although intended by the sender for the sacrifice to Heracles, the money was applied, through the bearers, to the construction of triremes.
4:21 When Apollonius son of Menestheus was sent to Egypt for King Philometor’s accession celebration, Antiochus learned that Philometor had become hostile to his affairs and took thought for his own security. Therefore he came to Joppa and proceeded to Jerusalem.
4:22 Received magnificently by Jason and the city, he was welcomed with torches and shouts. Afterward he departed with his army into Phoenicia.
4:23 After a period of three years, Jason sent Menelaus, brother of the previously mentioned Simon, to convey the money to the king and present memoranda on necessary matters.
4:24 When introduced to the king, Menelaus impressed him by the appearance of authority and secured the high priesthood for himself by outbidding Jason by three hundred talents of silver.
4:25 Having received the royal orders, he returned, bringing nothing worthy of the high priesthood, but possessing the temper of a cruel tyrant and the rage of a savage beast.
4:26 Jason, who had fraudulently displaced his own brother, was himself displaced by another and driven as a fugitive into the land of the Ammonites.
4:27 Menelaus held the office, but made no regular payment of the funds promised to the king,
4:28 although Sostratus, commander of the citadel, demanded them, since collection of the funds belonged to him. For this reason the two men were summoned by the king.
4:29 Menelaus left his own brother Lysimachus as his deputy in the high priesthood, while Sostratus left Crates, the commander of the Cypriots.
4:30 While these things were taking place, the people of Tarsus and Mallus happened to revolt because they had been given as a gift to Antiochis, the king’s concubine.
4:31 The king therefore went quickly to settle the matter, leaving Andronicus, one of the men of high rank, as his deputy.
4:32 Menelaus, thinking he had found a favourable opportunity, stole some of the temple’s gold objects and gave them to Andronicus. He had also sold others in Tyre and the surrounding cities.
4:33 Onias, who had learned these things clearly, denounced them, having withdrawn to a place of asylum at Daphne near Antioch.
4:34 Therefore Menelaus took Andronicus aside and urged him to do away with Onias. Andronicus came to Onias, having been persuaded to act deceitfully, greeted him and gave his right hand with oaths. Although under suspicion, he persuaded Onias to leave the asylum and immediately killed him, showing no regard for justice.
4:35 For this reason not only Jews but also many of the other nations were indignant and distressed at the man’s unjust murder.
4:36 When the king returned from the regions of Cilicia, the Jews in the city petitioned him about the senseless killing of Onias; the Greeks shared their hatred of the wickedness.
4:37 Antiochus was deeply grieved, moved to pity, and wept because of the deceased man’s self-restraint and admirable conduct.
4:38 Burning with anger, he immediately stripped Andronicus of his purple robe, tore off his tunics, and led him around the whole city to the very place where he had committed the impious act against Onias. There he removed the murderer from life, the Lord rendering him his deserved punishment.
4:39 Many sacrilegious thefts were committed in the city by Lysimachus with Menelaus’s consent, and the report spread abroad. The multitude gathered against Lysimachus, many gold objects having already been carried off.
4:40 As the crowds rose up and were filled with anger, Lysimachus armed about three thousand men and began unjust acts of violence, led by a certain Auranus, advanced in age and no less advanced in folly.
4:41 Seeing Lysimachus’s attack, some seized stones, others thick pieces of wood, and some handfuls of the ashes lying nearby, and hurled them in confusion at Lysimachus’s men.
4:42 In this way they wounded many of them, struck down some, and drove them all into flight. They killed the temple-robber himself beside the treasury.
4:43 A trial concerning these matters was brought against Menelaus.
4:44 When the king arrived in Tyre, three men sent by the council of elders presented their case before him.
4:45 Menelaus, already losing, promised a large sum of money to Ptolemy son of Dorymenes to persuade the king.
4:46 Ptolemy therefore took the king aside into a colonnade, as though for refreshment, and changed his mind.
4:47 The king acquitted Menelaus, the cause of the entire evil, of the charges, but condemned to death the unfortunate men who would have been released uncondemned even if they had pleaded before Scythians.
4:48 Those who had spoken for the city, the communities and the sacred vessels quickly suffered the unjust penalty.
4:49 For this reason even the Tyrians, hating the wickedness, generously provided for their burial.
4:50 But through the greed of those in power, Menelaus remained in office, growing in wickedness and becoming a great conspirator against his fellow citizens.

2 MACCABEES 5

5:1 About this time Antiochus organized his second expedition into Egypt.
5:2 Throughout the whole city, for nearly forty days, horsemen were seen racing through the air, wearing robes interwoven with gold, armed with lances and arrayed in squadrons; there were drawn swords,
5:3 formations of cavalry, attacks and countercharges on either side, movements of shields, masses of spears, volleys of missiles, flashes of golden ornaments, and armour of every kind.
5:4 Therefore everyone prayed that the manifestation had come for good.
5:5 When a false rumor arose that Antiochus had departed this life, Jason took no fewer than a thousand men and suddenly attacked the city. Those on the wall were driven back, and when the city was finally being taken, Menelaus fled to the citadel.
5:6 Jason slaughtered his own fellow citizens without mercy, not realizing that success against his kinsmen was the greatest misfortune. He imagined he was setting up trophies over enemies rather than his own countrymen.
5:7 He did not gain control of the government; receiving shame as the outcome of his plot, he again went away as a fugitive into the Ammonite country.
5:8 In the end he met a disastrous conclusion. Accused before Aretas, ruler of the Arabs, he fled from city to city, pursued by everyone, hated as a deserter of the laws and abhorred as the executioner of his homeland and fellow citizens, until he was driven into Egypt.
5:9 He who had made many strangers to their homeland perished in a foreign land, having sailed to the Lacedaemonians in the hope of obtaining shelter because of their kinship.
5:10 He who had thrown out multitudes unburied went unmourned and received neither any funeral care whatever nor a place in his ancestral tomb.
5:11 When news of these events reached the king, he concluded that Judea was in revolt. He therefore broke camp in Egypt, savage in spirit, took the city by force of arms,
5:12 and ordered the soldiers to cut down without mercy those they met and slaughter those who went up into the houses.
5:13 There was killing of young and old, destruction of boys, women and children, and slaughter of virgins and infants.
5:14 In the space of three days eighty thousand were lost: forty thousand in the acts of violence, and no fewer than those slaughtered were sold.
5:15 Not content with this, he dared to enter the holiest temple in all the earth, with Menelaus as guide—the man who had become a traitor to both the laws and his homeland.
5:16 With defiled hands he took the sacred vessels, and with profane hands swept away the gifts other kings had dedicated for the enhancement, glory and honour of the place.
5:17 Antiochus was exalted in his mind, not seeing that the Master had been briefly angered because of the sins of those inhabiting the city, and therefore had disregarded the place.
5:18 Had they not happened to be involved in many sins, he too, like Heliodorus, whom King Seleucus sent to inspect the treasury, would have been scourged immediately upon advancing and thrown back from his rashness.
5:19 But the Lord did not choose the nation for the sake of the place; He chose the place for the sake of the nation.
5:20 Therefore the place itself, having shared in the nation’s misfortunes, afterward shared in its benefits. What had been abandoned in the Almighty’s anger was restored again in all glory when the great Master became reconciled.
5:21 Antiochus, then, carried off eighteen hundred talents from the temple and quickly withdrew to Antioch. In his arrogance he imagined he could make the land navigable and the sea passable on foot, because of the haughtiness of his heart.
5:22 He also left overseers to afflict the people: in Jerusalem, Philip, a Phrygian by birth, whose character was more barbarous than that of the man who appointed him;
5:23 and at Gerizim, Andronicus; besides these, Menelaus, who exalted himself over his fellow citizens more wickedly than the others. Having a hateful disposition toward the Jewish citizens,
5:24 he sent Apollonius, commander of the Mysians, with an army of twenty-two thousand, ordering him to slaughter all the men in their prime and sell the women and the younger people.
5:25 On arriving in Jerusalem, Apollonius pretended to be peaceable and waited until the holy day of the Sabbath. Finding the Jews at rest, he ordered those under him to parade under arms.
5:26 He stabbed all who came out to watch, and rushing armed into the city, struck down considerable multitudes.
5:27 But Judas, also called Maccabeus, withdrew into the wilderness with about nine others. He and those with him lived in the mountains like wild animals, continually eating plants so as not to share in the defilement.

2 MACCABEES 6

6:1 Not long afterward the king sent an elderly Athenian to compel the Jews to depart from their ancestral laws and cease conducting their lives according to God’s laws,
6:2 and also to defile the temple in Jerusalem and name it for Olympian Zeus, and the one at Gerizim for Zeus the Protector of Strangers, in keeping with those who inhabited the place.
6:3 The intensification of the evil was harsh and wholly grievous.
6:4 For the temple was filled by the nations with debauchery and revels. They dallied with prostitutes, had intercourse with women in the sacred enclosures, and also brought inside things that were unfit.
6:5 The altar was filled with forbidden things excluded by the laws.
6:6 It was impossible to keep the Sabbath, observe ancestral festivals, or even profess simply to be a Jew.
6:7 With bitter compulsion they were led to partake of sacrificial entrails on the king’s monthly birthday celebration. When the festival of Dionysus came, they were compelled to carry ivy and march in procession for Dionysus.
6:8 At Ptolemy’s suggestion a decree also went out to the neighboring Greek cities, to follow the same policy against the Jews and make them partake of sacrificial entrails,
6:9 and to slaughter those who would not choose to adopt Greek customs. Thus one could see the misery that had come upon them.
6:10 Two women were brought forward for circumcising their children. They hung the infants from the women’s breasts, paraded the women publicly through the city, and hurled them down from the wall.
6:11 Others, who had gathered in nearby caves to observe the seventh day secretly, were reported to Philip and burned together, because their reverence for the most holy day’s dignity restrained them from defending themselves.
6:12 I therefore urge those who encounter this book not to shrink back because of the disasters, but to consider these punishments as intended for the discipline of our people, not their destruction.
6:13 For it is a sign of great kindness that those who act impiously are not left alone for long, but immediately encounter penalties.
6:14 For the Master has not determined to deal with us as with the other nations, patiently waiting to punish them until they have reached the full measure of sins,
6:15 so that He may not take vengeance on us afterward, when we have reached the end of our sins.
6:16 Therefore He never withdraws His mercy from us. Though He disciplines through disaster, He does not abandon His own people.
6:17 Let these words serve only as a reminder; we must now return briefly to the narrative.
6:18 A certain Eleazar, one of the foremost scribes, a man already advanced in age and most handsome in appearance, was being forced to open his mouth and eat pork.
6:19 Choosing a glorious death rather than a defiled life, he went of his own accord to the torture frame,
6:20 having spat it out, as those ought to approach who persevere in refusing things it is unlawful to taste, even for love of life.
6:21 Those appointed to oversee the unlawful eating of sacrificial entrails, because of their long acquaintance with the man, took him aside and urged him to bring meat prepared by himself that he could lawfully use, but to pretend that he was eating the sacrificial meat ordered by the king.
6:22 By doing this, they said, he would be released from death and receive humane treatment because of his old friendship with them.
6:23 But he adopted a noble resolve, worthy of his age, the dignity of his old age, his acquired and distinguished gray hair, his excellent conduct from childhood, and still more the holy legislation established by God. Accordingly he declared at once that they should send him to Hades.
6:24 “For it is unworthy of our age to pretend, lest many of the young suppose that Eleazar, at ninety years of age, has gone over to foreign ways,
6:25 and through my pretence, for the sake of a brief and momentary life, they themselves be led astray because of me, and I acquire defilement and a stain upon my old age.
6:26 For even if I now escape the punishment inflicted by human beings, neither living nor dead shall I escape the hands of the Almighty.
6:27 Therefore, by departing this life courageously now, I shall show myself worthy of my old age,
6:28 and leave the young a noble example of dying willingly and nobly for the revered and holy laws.” After saying this, he went immediately to the torture frame.
6:29 Those who led him changed their goodwill of a little earlier into hostility, because they considered his preceding words madness.
6:30 As he was about to die under the blows, he groaned and said, “It is clear to the Lord, who possesses holy knowledge, that although I could have been released from death, I endure harsh bodily pains under the scourging. Yet in my soul I gladly suffer these things because I fear Him.”
6:31 Thus he departed in this way, leaving his own death as an example of nobility and a memorial of virtue, not only to the young but also to the greater part of the nation.

2 MACCABEES 7

7:1 It also happened that seven brothers were arrested with their mother and compelled by the king to partake of forbidden pork, while being tortured with whips and sinew lashes.
7:2 One of them became their spokesman and said, “What do you intend to ask and learn of us? We are ready to die rather than transgress our ancestral laws.”
7:3 The king became furious and ordered pans and cauldrons to be heated.
7:4 When they were immediately heated, he ordered the tongue of their spokesman cut out, and that he be scalped and his extremities cut off while the other brothers and their mother watched.
7:5 When he had been rendered completely helpless, the king ordered him brought to the fire, still breathing, and fried in a pan. As the vapor from the pan spread far, they encouraged one another with their mother to die nobly, saying,
7:6 “The Lord God watches, and in truth He has compassion on us, as Moses explained in the song that bears witness against us to our face, saying, ‘And He will have compassion on His servants.’”
7:7 After the first had departed in this manner, they brought the second for their mockery. Tearing the skin from his head together with his hair, they asked, “Will you eat before your body is punished limb by limb?”
7:8 He answered in his ancestral language, “No.” Therefore he too received the ensuing torture, as the first had.
7:9 And when he was at his last breath, he said, “You, accursed one, release us from this present life; but the King of the world shall raise us, who die for His laws, unto everlasting renewal of life.”
7:10 After him the third was mocked. When asked for his tongue, he quickly put it out and fearlessly stretched forth his hands.
7:11 He nobly said, “I received these from heaven, and for His laws I disregard them; from Him I hope to receive them again.”
7:12 The king himself and those with him were astonished at the young man’s spirit, for he regarded his pains as nothing.
7:13 When he too had departed, they tortured the fourth in the same manner, tormenting him.
7:14 As he was about to die, he said, “It is preferable, when dying at human hands, to look for the hopes given by God, to be raised again by Him. But for you there shall be no resurrection to life.”
7:15 Next they brought forward the fifth and tormented him.
7:16 Looking at the king, he said, “Although mortal, you have authority among human beings and do what you will. But do not suppose our people have been abandoned by God.
7:17 Wait and behold His majestic might, how He will torment you and your offspring.”
7:18 After him they brought the sixth, and as he was about to die he said, “Do not deceive yourself vainly. We suffer these things because of ourselves, having sinned against our own God; astonishing things have happened.
7:19 But do not imagine that you shall go unpunished after attempting to fight against God.”
7:20 Their mother was exceedingly admirable and worthy of a good remembrance. Although she saw seven sons perish within the span of a single day, she bore it courageously because of her hopes in the Lord.
7:21 Filled with a noble resolve, she encouraged each of them in their ancestral language, rousing her womanly reasoning with a man’s courage, and said to them,
7:22 “I do not know how you appeared in my womb. It was not I who granted you spirit and life, nor did I arrange the composition of each of you.
7:23 Therefore the Creator of the world, who fashioned humanity’s beginning and devised the beginning of all things, in mercy gives you again both spirit and life, since you now disregard yourselves for the sake of His laws.”
7:24 Antiochus thought himself despised and suspected her words were reproaches. As the youngest was still alive, he not only urged him with words but assured him with oaths that, if he turned away from his ancestral ways, he would make him rich and enviable, regard him as a friend, and entrust him with public responsibilities.
7:25 When the young man paid no attention at all, the king summoned the mother and urged her to advise the boy to save himself.
7:26 After he had urged her at length, she agreed to persuade her son.
7:27 Bending toward him, she mocked the cruel tyrant by speaking in their ancestral language: “Son, have mercy on me, who carried you in my womb for nine months, nursed you for three years, nourished you, brought you to this age, and sustained you.
7:28 I beseech you, child, look up at heaven and earth and see everything in them. Recognize that God made them not from things that were, and that the human race comes into being in the same way.
7:29 Do not fear this executioner. Prove worthy of your brothers and accept death, so that in mercy I may receive you back with your brothers.”
7:30 While she was still finishing, the young man said, “What are you waiting for? I do not obey the king’s ordinance; I obey the ordinance of the law given to our fathers through Moses.
7:31 But you, who have devised every evil against the Hebrews, shall certainly not escape God’s hands.
7:32 For we are suffering because of our own sins.
7:33 Although our living Lord has been angered briefly for rebuke and discipline, He will again be reconciled to His own servants.
7:34 But you, unholy and most defiled of all human beings, do not be vainly exalted, raging with uncertain hopes as you raise your hand against Heaven’s children.
7:35 For you have not yet escaped the judgment of the Almighty, the all-seeing God.
7:36 Our brothers, after enduring a short pain, have now fallen under God’s covenant of everlasting life; but you, by God’s judgment, shall bear the just penalties of your arrogance.
7:37 I, like my brothers, give up both body and soul for our ancestral laws, calling upon God to become merciful quickly to the nation, and that through trials and scourges you may confess that He alone is God.
7:38 May the Almighty’s anger, justly brought upon our whole people, come to rest in me and my brothers.”
7:39 The king became furious and treated this one more cruelly than the others, bitterly resenting the mockery.
7:40 Thus he too departed undefiled, trusting entirely in the Lord.
7:41 Last of all, after her sons, the mother died.
7:42 Let this account suffice concerning the sacrificial eating and the surpassing tortures.

2 MACCABEES 8

8:1 Judas, also called Maccabeus, and those with him slipped secretly into the villages, called their kinsmen to join them, and gathered those who had remained in Judaism, assembling about six thousand.
8:2 They called upon the Lord to look upon the people trampled by everyone, and to have pity on the temple profaned by impious men,
8:3 to have mercy also on the city being destroyed and about to be levelled, and to listen to the blood crying out to Him,
8:4 to remember the unlawful destruction of innocent infants and the blasphemies committed against His name, and to show His hatred of wickedness.
8:5 Once Maccabeus had formed his company, he became irresistible to the nations, the Lord’s anger having turned to mercy.
8:6 Coming unexpectedly upon cities and villages, he burned them; seizing strategic places, he put not a few enemies to flight.
8:7 He especially used the nights as allies in such attacks. The report of his courage resounded everywhere.
8:8 Philip saw that the man was gradually advancing and making increasingly frequent progress in his successes. He wrote to Ptolemy, governor of Coele-Syria and Phoenicia, to assist the king’s affairs.
8:9 Ptolemy quickly selected Nicanor son of Patroclus, one of the foremost friends, and sent him, placing under him no fewer than twenty thousand men from nations of every kind, to eradicate the whole people of Judea. He also associated with him Gorgias, a general experienced in military affairs.
8:10 Nicanor determined to discharge the king’s tribute to the Romans, amounting to two thousand talents, from the captivity of the Jews.
8:11 He immediately sent to the coastal cities, inviting people to buy Jewish bodies and promising to give ninety bodies for a talent, not expecting the judgment that was about to follow upon him from the Almighty.
8:12 News of Nicanor’s advance reached Judas. When he informed those with him of the army’s approach,
8:13 the cowardly and those who distrusted God’s judgment slipped away and left the place.
8:14 The others sold everything they had left and together begged the Lord to rescue those whom the impious Nicanor had sold before even encountering them,
8:15 if not for their own sake, then because of the covenants with their fathers and because His revered and magnificent name had been invoked over them.
8:16 Maccabeus gathered those with him, six thousand in number, and urged them not to be terrified of their enemies or fear the multitude of nations unjustly coming against them, but to fight nobly,
8:17 keeping before their eyes the unlawful outrage they had committed against the holy place, the maltreatment of the mocked city, and also the overthrow of their ancestral civic order.
8:18 “For they trust in weapons and audacity,” he said, “but we trust in the Almighty God, who can strike down both those coming against us and the whole world with a single nod.”
8:19 He further recounted to them the assistance given to their ancestors, including what happened under Sennacherib, how one hundred eighty-five thousand perished,
8:20 and the battle fought in Babylonia against the Galatians: how eight thousand in all came to the engagement, with four thousand Macedonians; when the Macedonians were at a loss, the eight thousand destroyed one hundred twenty thousand through the help that came to them from heaven, and took much spoil.
8:21 Having thus made them confident and ready to die for the laws and their homeland, he divided the army into four sections.
8:22 He appointed his brothers Simon, Joseph and Jonathan to lead the respective divisions, placing fifteen hundred under each,
8:23 and also Eleazar. Having read aloud the sacred book and given “God’s help” as the watchword, he himself led the first company and engaged Nicanor.
8:24 With the Almighty as their ally, they slaughtered more than nine thousand of the enemy, wounded and disabled the greater part of Nicanor’s army, and forced them all to flee.
8:25 They took the money of those who had come to buy them. After pursuing them for a considerable distance, they returned, constrained by the hour,
8:26 for it was the day before the Sabbath. For this reason they did not prolong their pursuit.
8:27 After collecting their enemies’ weapons and stripping off their spoils, they occupied themselves with the Sabbath, abundantly blessing and giving thanks to the Lord who had preserved them to that day and appointed for them a beginning of mercy.
8:28 After the Sabbath they gave a share of the spoils to the maltreated, the widows and the orphans, and divided the rest among themselves and their children.
8:29 Having done this, they made a common supplication and begged the merciful Lord to become fully reconciled to His own servants.
8:30 They also fought the forces of Timothy and Bacchides, killed more than twenty thousand of them, and gained firm possession of very high fortresses. They distributed much plunder, giving equal shares to themselves, the maltreated, the orphans and widows, and also the elderly.
8:31 They collected the weapons and carefully deposited them all in strategic places, but brought the remaining spoils to Jerusalem.
8:32 They killed the tribal commander of Timothy’s forces, a most unholy man who had caused the Jews much suffering.
8:33 As they celebrated victory in their homeland, they burned those who had set fire to the sacred gateways, and Callisthenes, who had fled into a little house; he received the reward worthy of his impiety.
8:34 But the thrice-accursed Nicanor, who had brought the thousand merchants for the sale of the Jews,
8:35 was humbled, with the Lord’s help, by those whom he considered of the least account. He took off his splendid clothing and went alone through the interior like a runaway, reaching Antioch most fortunate despite his army’s destruction.
8:36 He who had undertaken to provide the Romans’ tribute from the captivity of Jerusalem’s inhabitants now proclaimed that the Jews had a Defender, and that for this reason the Jews were invulnerable, because they followed the laws laid down by Him.

2 MACCABEES 9

9:1 About that time Antiochus happened to have retreated in disorder from the regions of Persia.
9:2 For he had entered the city called Persepolis and attempted to rob its temple and oppress the city. The multitudes therefore rushed to defend themselves with weapons, and his men were routed. Antiochus, put to flight by the inhabitants, had to make a disgraceful retreat.
9:3 While he was at Ecbatana, news reached him of what had happened to Nicanor and Timothy’s forces.
9:4 Exalted in anger, he thought he would make the Jews bear even the harm inflicted by those who had driven him away. He therefore ordered his charioteer to drive without stopping and finish the journey, though judgment from heaven was already accompanying him. For he arrogantly said, “When I arrive there, I will make Jerusalem a mass grave for Jews.”
9:5 But the all-seeing Lord, the God of Israel, struck him with an incurable and unseen plague. He had only just finished speaking when an unrelenting pain in his bowels and bitter internal torments seized him—
9:6 most justly, since he had tormented the bowels of others with many strange afflictions.
9:7 Yet he did not cease his haughtiness at all, but was still filled with arrogance, breathing fire in his anger against the Jews and ordering the journey hastened. He happened to fall from the chariot as it rushed along, and in the severe fall all the members of his body were wrenched.
9:8 He who just now, in arrogance beyond human measure, thought he could command the waves of the sea and imagined he could weigh the mountain heights in a balance, was brought down to the ground and carried on a litter, plainly showing God’s power to everyone.
9:9 Worms swarmed up from the impious man’s body, and while he was still alive in pain and anguish, his flesh fell away. The whole army was burdened by the stench of his decay.
9:10 No one could carry the man who a little earlier thought he could touch the stars of heaven, because of the unbearable weight of his smell.
9:11 Then, broken, he began to cease much of his arrogance and come to understanding, his pains increasing every moment under the divine scourge.
9:12 Unable even to endure his own smell, he said, “It is right to submit to God and, being mortal, not to think oneself equal to God.”
9:13 The defiled man made vows to the Master who would no longer have mercy on him, speaking in this way:
9:14 he would declare free the holy city which he was hurrying to level and build into a mass grave;
9:15 and the Jews, whom he had decided were unworthy even of burial, but whom he would cast out with their infants to be eaten by birds and wild animals, he would make all equal to Athenians;
9:16 he would adorn the holy temple he had previously plundered with the finest dedicated gifts, restore all the sacred vessels many times over, and supply from his own revenues the necessary contributions for the sacrifices.
9:17 Besides these things, he would become a Jew and go through every inhabited place, proclaiming God’s might.
9:18 But his pains did not cease at all, for God’s just judgment had come upon him. Despairing of his own situation, he wrote the following letter to the Jews, having the form of a supplication and containing these words:
9:19 “King and general Antiochus to the good Jewish citizens: many greetings, good health and prosperity.
9:20 If you and your children are well and your affairs are as you wish, placing my hope in heaven,
9:21 I remembered your honour and goodwill affectionately. As I return from the regions of Persia, having fallen into a troublesome illness, I have considered it necessary to take thought for the common security of all.
9:22 I am not despairing of my condition, but have great hope of escaping the illness.
9:23 Yet I observe that my father also, on the occasions when he campaigned in the upper regions, designated his successor,
9:24 so that if something unexpected occurred or some troubling news was reported, those in the country might know to whom the government had been left and not be disturbed.
9:25 Moreover, observing that the nearby rulers who border the kingdom watch for opportunities and await what will happen, I have appointed my son Antiochus as king. I often entrusted and commended him to most of you when travelling to the upper satrapies; I have also written to him what is set down here.
9:26 I therefore urge and ask you, remembering the benefits shown to all and to each individually, to maintain your existing goodwill toward me and my son.
9:27 For I am persuaded that he, following my policy with fairness and humanity, will accommodate himself to you.”
9:28 Thus the murderer and blasphemer, suffering the worst as he had treated others, ended his life by a most pitiful death in the mountains of a foreign land.
9:29 Philip, who had been brought up with him, conveyed his body. Fearing Antiochus’s son, he went to Ptolemy Philometor in Egypt.

2 MACCABEES 10

10:1 Maccabeus and those with him, the Lord leading them, recovered the temple and the city.
10:2 They tore down the altars built by the foreigners in the marketplace, and also the sacred precincts.
10:3 Having purified the temple, they made another altar. They heated stones, took fire from them, and offered sacrifices after a period of two years. They offered incense, lit lamps and set out the loaves.
10:4 When they had done this, they fell on their faces and begged the Lord that they might no longer fall into such evils, but that, if they should ever sin, they might be disciplined by Him with gentleness and not handed over to blasphemous and barbarous nations.
10:5 On the very day the temple had been profaned by foreigners, the temple’s purification took place: the twenty-fifth of the same month, which is Chislev.
10:6 They celebrated joyfully for eight days in the manner of the Festival of Booths, remembering how a short time before, during the Festival of Booths, they had roamed the mountains and caves like wild animals.
10:7 Therefore, carrying garlanded staffs, beautiful branches and palm fronds, they offered hymns to the One who had prospered the purification of His own place.
10:8 By a common ordinance and decree they determined that the entire Jewish nation should observe these days every year.
10:9 Thus ended the matters concerning the death of Antiochus, called Epiphanes.
10:10 Now we shall explain the events under Antiochus Eupator, the son of the impious man, abridging the principal evils of the wars.
10:11 For when he took over the kingdom, he appointed a certain Lysias as chief minister and chief governor of Coele-Syria and Phoenicia.
10:12 Ptolemy, called Macron, had taken the lead in maintaining justice toward the Jews because of the wrong done to them, and attempted to conduct relations with them peacefully.
10:13 Therefore the friends accused him before Eupator, and at every turn he was called a traitor because he had abandoned Cyprus, which Philometor had entrusted to him, and gone over to Antiochus Epiphanes. Having failed to make his office honourable, he poisoned himself and departed this life.
10:14 Gorgias, having become governor of the regions, maintained foreign troops and at every turn fostered war against the Jews.
10:15 Along with him, the Idumeans, who held strategic fortresses, harassed the Jews. Taking in those driven from Jerusalem, they attempted to maintain the war.
10:16 Maccabeus and his men offered an entreaty and asked God to become their ally; then they rushed against the Idumean fortresses.
10:17 Attacking them vigorously, they gained control of the places, drove off all who fought on the wall, and slaughtered those they encountered. They killed no fewer than twenty thousand.
10:18 No fewer than nine thousand fled together into two very strong towers, which had everything necessary to withstand a siege.
10:19 Maccabeus left Simon and Joseph, and also Zacchaeus with his men, a sufficient force to besiege them, and himself departed for places requiring urgent attention.
10:20 But Simon’s men, in their love of money, were persuaded with silver by some of those in the towers. Receiving seventy thousand drachmas, they let some slip away.
10:21 When Maccabeus was informed of what had happened, he gathered the people’s leaders and accused them of selling their brothers for money by releasing enemies against them.
10:22 He therefore put these men to death as traitors and immediately captured the two towers.
10:23 Succeeding in everything by the weapons in his hands, he destroyed more than twenty thousand in the two fortresses.
10:24 Timothy, who had previously been defeated by the Jews, gathered very numerous foreign forces and assembled not a few horses from Asia. He arrived intending to take Judea by force of arms.
10:25 As he approached, Maccabeus and his men sprinkled earth on their heads and girded their waists with sackcloth in supplication to God.
10:26 Falling down on the platform opposite the altar, they asked Him to become merciful to them, to be an enemy to their enemies and oppose their opponents, as the law declares.
10:27 Rising from their petition, they took up their weapons and advanced some distance from the city. When they had approached the enemy, they remained by themselves.
10:28 Just as the sunrise spread, the two sides attacked: the one had, together with courage, their refuge in the Lord as a guarantee of success and victory, while the other appointed anger as the leader of their battles.
10:29 As the battle became fierce, five splendid men appeared to the enemy from heaven on horses with golden bridles, leading the Jews.
10:30 They took Maccabeus into their midst and sheltered him with their own armour, keeping him unwounded. They hurled arrows and thunderbolts at the enemy, who, confused by blindness and filled with turmoil, were cut down.
10:31 Twenty thousand five hundred were slaughtered, and six hundred horsemen.
10:32 Timothy himself fled into a stronghold called Gazara, a very strong fortress, where Chaereas was commander.
10:33 Maccabeus and his men eagerly besieged the fortress for four days.
10:34 Those inside, trusting in the strength of the place, blasphemed excessively and uttered unlawful words.
10:35 As the fifth day dawned, twenty young men of Maccabeus’s company, burning with anger because of the blasphemies, attacked the wall courageously and cut down whoever they met with a beast-like fury.
10:36 Others likewise climbed up while those inside were distracted, set the towers on fire, and kindled fires that burned the blasphemers alive. Others broke through the gates, admitted the rest of the division, and took possession of the city.
10:37 They slaughtered Timothy, who was hiding in a cistern, and his brother Chaereas and Apollophanes.
10:38 Having done these things, they blessed the Lord with hymns and thanksgiving, for He greatly benefited Israel and gave them victory.

2 MACCABEES 11

11:1 After a very short interval, Lysias, the king’s guardian, kinsman and chief minister, was deeply distressed at what had happened.
11:2 Gathering about eighty thousand men and all the cavalry, he came against the Jews, intending to make the city a dwelling for Greeks,
11:3 to make the temple subject to taxation like the other nations’ sacred precincts, and to put the high priesthood up for sale each year.
11:4 He took no account at all of God’s might, but was elated by his tens of thousands of infantry, his thousands of horsemen, and his eighty elephants.
11:5 Entering Judea, he approached Beth-zur, a fortified place about five stadia from Jerusalem, and pressed it hard.
11:6 When Maccabeus and his men learned that he was besieging the strongholds, they, together with the crowds, implored the Lord with lamentations and tears to send a good angel for Israel’s deliverance.
11:7 Maccabeus himself was the first to take up his weapons. He urged the others to share the danger with him and help their brothers. Together and eagerly they set out.
11:8 While they were there near Jerusalem, a horseman in white clothing appeared leading them, brandishing golden armour.
11:9 Together they all blessed the merciful God and grew strong in spirit, ready to pierce not only human beings but the fiercest wild animals and iron walls.
11:10 They advanced in battle array with their Ally from heaven, the Lord having shown them mercy.
11:11 Rushing like lions upon the enemy, they struck down eleven thousand of them and sixteen hundred horsemen; they forced all the rest to flee.
11:12 Most of them escaped wounded and stripped; Lysias himself also escaped by fleeing disgracefully.
11:13 Being no fool, he considered the defeat that had befallen him and realized that the Hebrews were invincible while the mighty God was their ally.
11:14 He sent to persuade them to settle on every just condition, declaring that he would also persuade the king and compel him to become their friend.
11:15 Maccabeus consented to everything Lysias proposed, taking thought for what was beneficial. For the king granted all the requests concerning the Jews that Maccabeus submitted to Lysias in writing.
11:16 The letters written to the Jews contained these words. From Lysias: “Lysias to the multitude of the Jews, greetings.
11:17 John and Absalom, your envoys, presented the document appended below and requested the matters indicated in it.
11:18 Whatever also needed to be brought before the king, I explained; and he granted what was possible.
11:19 If, then, you maintain your goodwill toward the government, I will also attempt in future to help bring about good things for you.
11:20 Concerning these matters and their particulars, I have instructed both these men and my own representatives to speak with you.
11:21 Farewell. In the one hundred forty-eighth year, on the twenty-fourth of Dios Corinthios.”
11:22 The king’s letter contained these words: “King Antiochus to his brother Lysias, greetings.
11:23 Since our father has departed to the gods, we desire those in the kingdom to be free from disturbance and attend to their own affairs.
11:24 We have heard that the Jews do not consent to our father’s change to Greek customs, but choose their own way of life and ask that their lawful practices be permitted.
11:25 Wishing therefore that this nation also be free from disturbance, we decide that their temple be restored to them and that they conduct their lives according to their ancestors’ customs.
11:26 You will therefore do well to send to them and give pledges, so that, knowing our policy, they may be of good heart and happily devote themselves to the management of their own affairs.”
11:27 The king’s letter to the nation was as follows: “King Antiochus to the council of elders of the Jews and the other Jews, greetings.
11:28 If you are well, it is as we wish. We ourselves are also in good health.
11:29 Menelaus has informed us that you wish to return and attend to your own affairs.
11:30 Those who return by the thirtieth of Xanthicus will have a pledge of safe conduct,
11:31 permitting the Jews to use their own foods and laws as before. None of them shall be troubled in any way for offences committed in ignorance.
11:32 I have also sent Menelaus to encourage you.
11:33 Farewell. In the one hundred forty-eighth year, on the fifteenth of Xanthicus.”
11:34 The Romans also sent them a letter with these words: “Quintus Memmius and Titus Manius, ambassadors of the Romans, to the people of the Jews, greetings.
11:35 Regarding what Lysias, the king’s kinsman, has granted you, we also give our approval.
11:36 As for the matters he decided should be referred to the king, consider them and send someone immediately, so that we may set them forth as is appropriate for you, for we are proceeding to Antioch.
11:37 Therefore hurry and send some men, so that we too may know what your judgment is.
11:38 Be in good health. In the one hundred forty-eighth year, on the fifteenth of Xanthicus.”

2 MACCABEES 12

12:1 When these agreements had been made, Lysias went away to the king, and the Jews turned to farming.
12:2 But among the local governors, Timothy and Apollonius son of Gennaeus, as well as Hieronymus and Demophon, and besides them Nicanor, commander of Cyprus, would not allow them to remain settled and live quietly.
12:3 The people of Joppa committed this great impiety. They invited the Jews living among them to board boats that they had provided, with their wives and children, as though there were no hostility toward them,
12:4 in accordance with the city’s common decree. The Jews accepted, wishing to live peaceably and suspecting nothing; but the people took them out to sea and drowned no fewer than two hundred.
12:5 When Judas learned of the cruelty committed against his fellow countrymen, he gave orders to the men with him.
12:6 Calling upon God, the righteous Judge, he came against his brothers’ murderers. By night he burned the harbour and set the boats ablaze, and stabbed those who had fled there.
12:7 Since the town was shut, he withdrew, intending to return and uproot the whole community of Joppa.
12:8 Learning also that the people of Jamnia intended to act in the same way toward the Jews living among them,
12:9 he attacked the Jamnites by night and set fire to the harbour with its fleet. The radiance of the blaze was visible in Jerusalem, two hundred forty stadia away.
12:10 After they had withdrawn nine stadia from there, on their way toward Timothy, Arabs attacked him, no fewer than five thousand, with five hundred horsemen.
12:11 A fierce battle took place, and Judas’s men succeeded through God’s help. Defeated, the nomads asked Judas to give them pledges, promising to give livestock and assist them in other matters.
12:12 Judas, thinking that they could indeed be useful in many ways, agreed to make peace with them. They received pledges and departed to their tents.
12:13 He also attacked a city strong with earthworks and enclosed by walls, inhabited by a mixture of nations, and named Caspin.
12:14 Those inside trusted in the strength of the walls and the stock of provisions. They treated Judas’s men rudely, insulting them, and moreover blaspheming and saying things that were unlawful.
12:15 Judas’s men called upon the great Sovereign of the world, who in Joshua’s time threw down Jericho without battering rams or mechanical engines, and rushed like wild animals against the wall.
12:16 Taking the city by God’s will, they carried out indescribable slaughter, so that the adjacent lake, two stadia wide, appeared filled with flowing blood.
12:17 After withdrawing seven hundred fifty stadia from there, they reached Charax, among the Jews called Toubiani.
12:18 They did not find Timothy in those regions, for he had then left without accomplishing his purpose, but had left a very strong garrison in one place.
12:19 Dositheus and Sosipater, commanders under Maccabeus, marched out and destroyed those whom Timothy had left in the stronghold, more than ten thousand men.
12:20 Maccabeus arranged his army in companies, placed those men over the companies, and rushed against Timothy, who had with him one hundred twenty thousand infantry and twenty-five hundred horsemen.
12:21 When Timothy learned of Judas’s advance, he sent the women, children and remaining baggage ahead to the place called Carnion, for the place was difficult to besiege and difficult to approach because of the narrowness of all the approaches.
12:22 When Judas’s first company appeared, dread came upon the enemy. Fear fell upon them through the manifestation of the One who watches all things, and they rushed into flight, scattering in different directions, so that they were often injured by their own men and pierced by the points of their swords.
12:23 Judas pursued them with greater vigor, stabbing the accursed men and destroying about thirty thousand.
12:24 Timothy himself fell into the hands of Dositheus’s and Sosipater’s men. With much deceitful persuasion he begged them to release him unharmed, because he held the parents of many and the brothers of some, and these would come to be disregarded.
12:25 After he had guaranteed in many ways his undertaking to restore them unharmed, they released him for their brothers’ deliverance.
12:26 Judas went out against Carnion and the sanctuary of Atargatis and slaughtered twenty-five thousand people.
12:27 After their defeat and destruction, he also marched against Ephron, a fortified city where Lysias and mixed multitudes lived. Strong young men stood before the walls and defended themselves vigorously; there were also large stores of engines and missiles there.
12:28 Calling upon the Sovereign who powerfully shatters the strength of enemies, they brought the city under their control and struck down about twenty-five thousand of those inside.
12:29 Departing from there, they rushed toward Scythopolis, six hundred stadia from Jerusalem.
12:30 The Jews living there testified to the goodwill the Scythopolitans had shown them and their humane treatment in times of misfortune.
12:31 They thanked them and further urged them to remain favourable to their people in future, then came to Jerusalem as the Festival of Weeks drew near.
12:32 After the festival called Pentecost, they rushed against Gorgias, governor of Idumea.
12:33 He came out with three thousand infantry and four hundred horsemen.
12:34 When they drew up for battle, a few of the Jews happened to fall.
12:35 A certain Dositheus, one of Bacenor’s men, a strong cavalryman, caught hold of Gorgias. Grasping his cloak, he dragged him forcefully, wishing to capture the accursed man alive. But one of the Thracian horsemen rushed upon him and struck away his shoulder, and Gorgias escaped to Marisa.
12:36 When Esdris’s men had been fighting for a long time and were exhausted, Judas called upon the Lord to appear as their ally and leader of the war.
12:37 Beginning the cry with hymns in their ancestral language, he rushed unexpectedly upon Gorgias’s forces and put them to flight.
12:38 Judas took his army and came to the city of Adullam. As the seventh day was approaching, they purified themselves according to custom and observed the Sabbath there.
12:39 On the following day, when it had become necessary, Judas’s men came to recover the bodies of the fallen and place them with their kinsmen in the ancestral tombs.
12:40 Under the tunics of each of the dead they found sacred objects of the idols from Jamnia, things from which the law excludes the Jews. It became clear to everyone that these men had fallen for this reason.
12:41 They all therefore blessed the deeds of the Lord, the righteous Judge who makes hidden things manifest,
12:42 and turned to supplication, asking that the sin committed might be completely blotted out. The noble Judas urged the multitude to keep themselves free from sin, having seen with their own eyes what had happened because of the sin of those who had fallen.
12:43 He took up a collection from each man, amounting to two thousand drachmas of silver, and sent it to Jerusalem to offer a sacrifice for sin. He acted very well and nobly, considering the resurrection.
12:44 For if he did not expect those who had fallen to rise again, it would have been superfluous and foolish to pray for the dead.
12:45 But if he was looking to the finest reward laid up for those who fall asleep in piety, the thought was holy and devout. Therefore he made atonement for the dead, so that they might be released from their sin.

2 MACCABEES 13

13:1 In the one hundred forty-ninth year, word reached Judas and his men that Antiochus Eupator had come against Judea with multitudes,
13:2 and with him Lysias, his guardian and chief minister, each having a Greek force of one hundred ten thousand infantry, fifty-three hundred horsemen, twenty-two elephants, and three hundred scythe-bearing chariots.
13:3 Menelaus also joined them and urged Antiochus on with much deceit, not for his homeland’s deliverance, but thinking that he would be restored to office.
13:4 But the King of kings stirred Antiochus’s anger against the accursed man. When Lysias pointed out that he was the cause of all the evils, Antiochus ordered that he be taken to Beroea and destroyed according to the custom of that place.
13:5 In that place there is a tower fifty cubits high, full of ashes. It had a revolving device with a sheer drop on every side into the ashes.
13:6 There they all push to destruction anyone guilty of temple robbery or notorious for certain other evils.
13:7 By such a fate the lawless Menelaus happened to die, receiving not even a place in the earth.
13:8 And most justly: since he had committed many sins against the altar, whose fire and ashes were pure, he received his death in ashes.
13:9 The king, made barbarous in his intentions, was coming to show the Jews evils worse than those that had occurred in his father’s time.
13:10 When Judas learned these things, he ordered the multitude to call upon the Lord day and night, that if ever He had helped them before, He would help them now as they were about to be deprived of the law, homeland and holy temple,
13:11 and would not permit the people, who had only just gained a little respite, to fall under the power of blasphemous nations.
13:12 All did the same together and entreated the merciful Lord with weeping, fasts and prostration for three days without ceasing. Judas encouraged them and ordered them to assemble.
13:13 Meeting privately with the elders, he resolved that before the king’s army entered Judea and gained control of the city, they should go out and decide matters with God’s help.
13:14 Entrusting the outcome to the Creator of the world, he urged those with him to fight nobly to the death for laws, temple, city, homeland and civic life. He made his encampment near Modein.
13:15 Giving his men “God’s victory” as the watchword, he attacked the royal quarters in the camp by night with selected young men of the finest quality. He killed about two thousand men and stabbed the leading elephant together with the man in its tower.
13:16 Finally they filled the camp with dread and confusion and departed successfully.
13:17 This had taken place just as day was dawning, because of the Lord’s protection that helped him.
13:18 The king, having tasted the Jews’ boldness, tried to take the places by stratagem.
13:19 He advanced against Beth-zur, a strong fortress of the Jews; he was routed, repulsed and defeated.
13:20 Judas sent necessary supplies to those inside.
13:21 Rhodocus, one of the Jewish ranks, disclosed the secrets to the enemy. He was sought out, caught and imprisoned.
13:22 The king negotiated a second time with those in Beth-zur, gave a pledge, received one, and went away. He attacked Judas’s men and was defeated.
13:23 He learned that Philip, whom he had left in charge of affairs in Antioch, had lost his senses. Disturbed, he appealed to the Jews, submitted and swore to all just conditions. He made a settlement, offered sacrifice, honoured the temple, and treated the place kindly.
13:24 He warmly received Maccabeus and left Hegemonides as governor from Ptolemais to the territory of the Gerrhenes.
13:25 He came to Ptolemais. The people of Ptolemais were distressed about the agreements, for they were so indignant that they wished to annul the provisions.
13:26 Lysias went up to the platform, offered such a defence as was possible, persuaded them, calmed them, won their goodwill, and departed for Antioch. Thus unfolded the events of the king’s expedition and withdrawal.

2 MACCABEES 14

14:1 After a period of three years, word reached Judas and his men that Demetrius son of Seleucus had sailed into the harbour at Tripolis with a strong force and a fleet,
14:2 had gained control of the country, and had done away with Antiochus and his guardian Lysias.
14:3 A certain Alcimus, who had formerly been high priest but had willingly defiled himself during the times of separation, realized that there was no way for him to obtain deliverance or regain access to the holy altar.
14:4 About the one hundred fifty-first year he came to King Demetrius, bringing him a golden crown and a palm, and also some of the customary temple branches. That day he remained quiet.
14:5 He found an opportunity to assist his own folly when Demetrius summoned him to a council and asked what disposition and policy the Jews had adopted. He answered,
14:6 “Those Jews called Hasideans, whose leader is Judas Maccabeus, foster war and rebellion, preventing the kingdom from obtaining stability.
14:7 Therefore, having been deprived of my ancestral honour—I mean the high priesthood—I have now come here,
14:8 first because I am sincerely concerned for the king’s interests, and second because I seek the good of my own fellow citizens. For through the thoughtlessness of those men, our whole people suffer no small misfortune.
14:9 Now that you know each of these things, O king, take thought for the country and our distressed people, in accordance with your readily bestowed humanity toward everyone.
14:10 For as long as Judas survives, public affairs cannot find peace.”
14:11 When he had said such things, the other friends who were hostile to Judas quickly inflamed Demetrius further.
14:12 He immediately selected Nicanor, the former commander of the elephants, appointed him governor of Judea, and sent him out,
14:13 with orders to kill Judas himself, scatter those with him, and appoint Alcimus high priest of the greatest temple.
14:14 The nations in Judea who had driven Judas into exile joined Nicanor in crowds, thinking the Jews’ misfortunes and disasters would become their own successes.
14:15 When Judas’s men heard of Nicanor’s advance and the attack of the nations, they sprinkled earth on themselves and entreated the One who established His own people for ever and always assists His own portion by a manifestation.
14:16 When their leader gave the command, he immediately set out from there and met them at a village called Dessau.
14:17 Simon, Judas’s brother, had engaged Nicanor, but had faltered for a time because of the opponents’ sudden silence.
14:18 Nevertheless, Nicanor heard of the courage of Judas’s men and their spirit in struggles for their homeland, and was wary of deciding the matter through bloodshed.
14:19 Therefore he sent Posidonius, Theodotus and Mattathias to give and receive pledges.
14:20 After prolonged consideration of these matters, the leader consulted the multitudes. A unanimous opinion emerged, and they consented to the agreements.
14:21 They appointed a day on which they would meet privately in the same place. A chariot came forward from each side, and they set out seats.
14:22 Judas stationed armed men ready at strategic places, lest the enemy suddenly attempt some treachery. They held an appropriate conference.
14:23 Nicanor remained in Jerusalem and did nothing out of place; he dismissed the crowds that had assembled in throngs.
14:24 He kept Judas continually in his presence, having become deeply attached to the man.
14:25 He urged him to marry and have children. Judas married, settled down and shared in ordinary life.
14:26 Alcimus saw their goodwill toward one another and obtained the agreements they had made. He went to Demetrius and said that Nicanor’s intentions were disloyal to the government, for he had appointed Judas, the kingdom’s conspirator, as his successor.
14:27 The king became furious and, provoked by the thoroughly wicked man’s slanders, wrote to Nicanor, declaring his distress over the agreements and ordering him to send Maccabeus in chains to Antioch immediately.
14:28 When these instructions reached Nicanor, he was dismayed and distressed at the prospect of annulling the settlement when the man had done no wrong.
14:29 But since he could not oppose the king, he watched for a suitable moment to accomplish this by stratagem.
14:30 Maccabeus saw that Nicanor was dealing more harshly with him and that their customary meeting had become less courteous. Recognizing that the harshness had no good cause, he gathered not a few of his men and concealed himself from Nicanor.
14:31 The other man realized that Judas had skillfully outmaneuvered him. He went to the greatest and holy temple while the priests were offering the required sacrifices and ordered them to hand over the man.
14:32 When they declared with oaths that they did not know where the man being sought was,
14:33 he stretched his right hand toward the temple and swore, “Unless you hand Judas over to me in chains, I will make this enclosure of God a level plain, tear down the altar, and erect here a splendid temple for Dionysus.”
14:34 Having said this, he departed. The priests stretched their hands toward heaven and called upon the One who continually defends our nation, saying,
14:35 “Thou, Lord of all, hast need of nothing, yet Thou wast pleased that a temple for Thy dwelling should come into being among us.
14:36 And now, holy Lord of all sanctification, preserve this recently purified house undefiled for ever.”
14:37 A certain Razis, one of Jerusalem’s elders, was reported to Nicanor: a man who loved his fellow citizens, was very well spoken of, and because of his goodwill was called a father of the Jews.
14:38 For in the earlier times of separation he had incurred a judgment for Judaism, risking both body and soul for Judaism with complete earnestness.
14:39 Nicanor, wishing to make his hostility toward the Jews evident, sent more than five hundred soldiers to arrest him,
14:40 for he thought that by arresting him he would inflict a disaster upon them.
14:41 As the multitudes were about to take the tower, forcing the courtyard door and ordering fire to be brought to set the doors alight, Razis, surrounded, thrust his sword into himself,
14:42 wishing to die nobly rather than fall under the power of the accursed men and be outraged in a manner unworthy of his own nobility.
14:43 But because of the haste of the struggle he did not strike accurately. As the crowds rushed inside the doorways, he ran bravely up onto the wall and hurled himself courageously down into the crowds.
14:44 They quickly drew back, leaving a space, and he fell into the middle of the empty area.
14:45 Still breathing and burning with anger, he rose. Although his blood streamed out and his wounds were grievous, he ran through the crowds and stood on a projecting rock.
14:46 By now entirely drained of blood, he pulled out his entrails, took them in both hands and hurled them at the crowds. Calling upon the Master of life and spirit to give these back to him again, he departed in this manner.

2 MACCABEES 15

15:1 Nicanor learned that Judas and his men were in the regions of Samaria and planned to attack them in complete security on the day of rest.
15:2 The Jews who were compelled to accompany him said, “Do not destroy so savagely and barbarously, but assign honour to the day that the One who watches all things has especially honoured with holiness.”
15:3 The thrice-accursed man asked whether there was a Sovereign in heaven who had commanded the observance of the Sabbath day.
15:4 They declared, “The living Lord Himself is the Sovereign in heaven, who commanded that the seventh day be observed.”
15:5 But he said, “And I am a sovereign on earth, who commands that weapons be taken up and the king’s business carried out.” Nevertheless he did not succeed in accomplishing his cruel intention.
15:6 Nicanor, holding his neck high in all arrogance, had determined to erect a common trophy over Judas and his men.
15:7 But Maccabeus remained continually confident, with every hope of receiving help from the Lord.
15:8 He urged those with him not to fear the nations’ advance, but to remember the help they had previously received from heaven and now expect the victory that would come to them from the Almighty.
15:9 Encouraging them from the law and the prophets, and reminding them also of the struggles they had completed, he made them more eager.
15:10 Arousing their spirits, he gave orders while also pointing out the nations’ faithlessness and violation of their oaths.
15:11 Arming each of them with the encouragement of good words more than with the security of shields and lances, he also related a trustworthy dream, a kind of waking vision, and gladdened them all.
15:12 Its vision was as follows: Onias, the former high priest, a noble and good man, modest in his encounters, gentle in character, speaking fittingly, and practiced from childhood in everything belonging to virtue, stretched out his hands and prayed for the whole community of the Jews.
15:13 Then another man appeared, distinguished by gray hair and glory, surrounded by a wondrous and most magnificent majesty.
15:14 Onias spoke and said, “This is the lover of the brothers, who prays much for the people and the holy city: Jeremiah, God’s prophet.”
15:15 Jeremiah stretched out his right hand and handed Judas a golden sword. As he gave it, he addressed him in these words:
15:16 “Take this holy sword, a gift from God, with which you will shatter the adversaries.”
15:17 Encouraged by Judas’s very fine words, which could incite them to virtue and strengthen the young men’s souls, they decided not to campaign, but to charge nobly and, engaging with all courage, decide the matter, because the city, the holy things and the temple were in danger.
15:18 For concern for wives and children, and also brothers and kinsmen, occupied a lesser place with them; greatest and foremost was their fear for the consecrated temple.
15:19 Those confined within the city also had no minor anguish, troubled about the attack in the open country.
15:20 Everyone was now awaiting the coming decision. The enemy had already approached, the army had been drawn up, the beasts positioned at a suitable point, and the cavalry stationed on the wing.
15:21 Maccabeus saw the arrival of the multitudes, their varied equipment of weapons, and the beasts’ savagery. Stretching his hands toward heaven, he called upon the wonder-working Lord, knowing that victory does not come through weapons, but that He obtains it for the worthy as He judges.
15:22 He called upon Him in this way: “Thou, Master, didst send Thine angel in the time of Hezekiah, king of Judea, and he killed about one hundred eighty-five thousand in Sennacherib’s camp.
15:23 Now also, Sovereign of the heavens, send a good angel before us to bring dread and trembling.
15:24 By the greatness of Thine arm, may those who come with blasphemy against Thy holy people be struck with terror.” With these words he ended.
15:25 Nicanor’s men advanced with trumpets and battle hymns.
15:26 But Judas’s men met the enemy with invocation and prayers.
15:27 Fighting with their hands and praying to God with their hearts, they struck down no fewer than thirty-five thousand, greatly gladdened by God’s manifestation.
15:28 When they had finished the engagement and were returning with joy, they recognized that Nicanor had fallen in his full armour.
15:29 Amid shouting and commotion, they blessed the Sovereign in their ancestral language.
15:30 The man who had fought foremost for his fellow citizens with his whole body and soul, preserving the goodwill of his youth toward his countrymen, ordered Nicanor’s head and his hand with the shoulder to be cut off and carried to Jerusalem.
15:31 When he arrived there, he called his fellow countrymen together, stationed the priests before the altar, and sent for those in the citadel.
15:32 He displayed the defiled Nicanor’s head and the blasphemer’s hand, which he had stretched out against the holy house of the Almighty while boasting.
15:33 He cut out the impious Nicanor’s tongue and said that it should be given piece by piece to the birds, and that the rewards of his folly should be hung opposite the temple.
15:34 They all blessed the manifested Lord toward heaven, saying, “Blessed is He who has preserved His own place undefiled.”
15:35 He hung Nicanor’s head from the citadel as an evident and visible sign to everyone of the Lord’s help.
15:36 By a common vote they all decreed that this day must never be left unmarked, but that they should specially observe the thirteenth day of the twelfth month, called Adar in the Syrian language, one day before Mordecai’s day.
15:37 Since the matters concerning Nicanor unfolded in this way, and from that time the city was held by the Hebrews, I too will end my account here.
15:38 If it is well composed and to the point, that is what I myself desired; if it is slight and mediocre, that is what I could achieve.
15:39 For drinking wine by itself, and likewise water by itself, is harmful; but just as wine mixed with water is sweet and produces a delightful pleasure, so the arrangement of the account delights the ears of those who encounter the composition. Here shall be the end.
